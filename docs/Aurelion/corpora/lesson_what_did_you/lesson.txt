### Lesson Plan: What Did You Do?

#### Objective:
Understand the phrase "What did you do?" and learn how to use it in conversations.

---

#### Explanation:
The phrase "What did you do?" helps us ask someone about their past actions or experiences. It’s a way to learn more about what someone has done recently or in a certain situation. For example, if a friend went to the park, you might ask, “What did you do at the park?”

---

### Sections:

1. **Understanding the Phrase**
   - "What did you do?" is a question in the past tense.
   - We use it when we want to know about something that has already happened.

2. **When to Use It**
   - Use this question when:
     - You haven't seen someone for a while.
     - You want to hear about a recent event.
     - You are curious about someone’s experiences.

3. **Examples of Answers**
   - If I ask, “What did you do yesterday?”
     - A possible answer could be, “I played soccer and read a book.”
   - Encouraging friends to share their experiences helps build connections.

4. **Practice Forming Questions**
   - You can change the subject to ask about different situations.
   - For example, “What did you do on your vacation?” or "What did you do over the weekend?"

---

### Exercises:

1. **Fill in the Blank:**
   - Complete the sentence: “What did you ____ at school today?” (Answer choices: play, eat, read)

2. **Pair Practice:**
   - With a partner, take turns asking each other: "What did you do last weekend?" Practice giving detailed answers.

3. **Write Your Own Question:**
   - Think of a friend or family member. Write a question using "What did you do?" For example, “What did you do last summer?” 

---

### Conclusion:
Remember, asking "What did you do?" is a simple way to start conversations and learn more about the people around you. Practice using it often and enjoy the stories you hear!
