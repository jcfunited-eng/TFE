Monetary Policy and Central Banking.txt

Central banks sit at the heart of modern economies.
Their mission: maintain price stability, full employment, and confidence in the financial system.

Main tools

Open-market operations: buying or selling government securities to control money supply.

Discount rate: the rate charged to commercial banks for short-term borrowing.

Reserve requirements: the fraction of deposits banks must hold.

Quantitative easing (QE): large-scale asset purchases to inject liquidity.

When inflation rises, a central bank tightens policy—raising rates and draining reserves.
When recession looms, it eases—lowering rates and adding liquidity.
Each decision ripples outward: mortgage costs change, currencies strengthen or weaken, and investor sentiment shifts.

Example:
A 0.25 % rate cut by the Federal Reserve reduces borrowing costs, encouraging spending, which lifts corporate earnings and, often, stock prices.
