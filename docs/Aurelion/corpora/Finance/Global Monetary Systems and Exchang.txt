Global Monetary Systems and Exchange Rates.txt

Currencies are the blood vessels of global trade.

Historical evolution
Gold standard → Bretton Woods → floating rates after 1971.

Exchange-rate regimes

Fixed: currency pegged to another (e.g., HKD ↔ USD).

Managed float: central bank intervenes occasionally.

Free float: market determines price (USD, EUR).

Notation
Pairs such as EUR/USD express “how many USD per 1 EUR.”
Appreciation of USD means fewer dollars buy the same euro—stronger dollar.

Parity relationships

Interest-rate parity: differences in interest rates predict forward exchange rates.

Purchasing-power parity: identical goods should cost the same globally once FX is adjusted.

Example:
When U.S. rates rise faster than Europe’s, capital flows into USD assets, strengthening the dollar and pressuring emerging-market borrowers with dollar debt.
