Investment Vehicles and Portfolio Theory.txt

Investors assemble portfolios much like engineers build structures—balancing load and flexibility.

Asset classes
Equities, fixed income, alternatives (real estate, commodities), and cash.
Each offers a different blend of risk and return.

Diversification reduces unsystematic risk.
When one asset falls, another may rise.
Mathematically, correlation is the glue or the fault line between assets.

Portfolio theory (Markowitz) seeks the efficient frontier: the highest expected return for a given risk.
CAPM adds a market perspective:
expected return = risk-free rate + β × (market premium).

Active vs. passive
Active managers try to beat benchmarks; passive funds track indexes cheaply.
Rebalancing restores intended weights as prices drift.

Analogy: a balanced diet—proteins (bonds) for stability, carbohydrates (equities) for energy, and spices (alternatives) for flavor but potential burn.
