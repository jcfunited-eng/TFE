Financial Crises and Systemic Risk.txt

A crisis begins quietly—too much leverage, too little margin of safety.

Mechanics

Leverage amplifies returns until volatility reverses direction.

Falling prices trigger margin calls → forced sales → further price drops.

Liquidity evaporates as everyone tries to exit simultaneously.

Historic snapshots

1929: margin loans on stocks, no deposit insurance.

1997: Asian contagion through pegged currencies and short-term debt.

2008: mortgage derivatives, opaque counterparty chains.

Central banks act as lenders of last resort, injecting liquidity and guaranteeing critical institutions to halt panic.
Afterward, regulation tightens: higher capital ratios, stress testing, and systemic-risk councils.

Lesson: in complex networks, safety lies not in strength of parts but in slack between them.
