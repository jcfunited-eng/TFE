Financial_Instruments.txt

Financial instruments are contracts transferring value or risk.

Equities give ownership; holders share profits and voting rights.
Debt instruments—bonds, notes, bills—lend capital for fixed interest.
Derivatives derive value from an underlying asset:

Options grant rights but not obligations to buy/sell.

Futures obligate both sides at a fixed future price.

Swaps exchange cash-flow streams.

Commodities (energy, metals, agriculture) and foreign exchange extend diversification.
ETFs bundle baskets of assets that trade like stocks.
Leverage magnifies both gains and losses; margin requirements limit exposure.

Each instrument is a tool—powerful in skilled hands, dangerous in careless ones.
