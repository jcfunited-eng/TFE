## Exact physical relation

Resident membrane charge is an integer count `n` of the SI-defined elementary
charge. The exact ensemble-current phase is carried from the current-to-charge
law. For capacitance `C` in picofarads:

```text
Q_fC = n * e_fC
V_mV = Q_fC / C_pF
n_next = n - outward_elementary_charges
```

The unit identity `fC / pF = mV` is retained explicitly. Inward positive
current is negative under the outward-positive convention, so it increases
the membrane's separated positive charge and depolarizes the cell. Zero
current is exact quiescence. No voltage threshold, clamp, or wrap exists.

The executable reference test uses `5.5 pF`, the reported mean capacitance for
E17-21 chick zone 2/3 type-II vestibular hair cells. The production anatomy is
not yet selected: the implementation requires an explicit positive
capacitance and does not hide a default by sensory label.

## Why ion species remain unresolved

The MET pore carries nonselective cation current. K+ dominates in physiological
endolymph, while Ca2+ carries a smaller but important share and controls
adaptation. That share varies with preparation and local concentrations.
Assigning every emitted elementary charge to K+ would therefore be a false
material-conservation claim. This membrane law updates charge only; it does not
debit or credit an ion species until one declared local fluid preparation
supplies the partition.

## Executable proof

The isolated native tests prove:

- exact potential from signed elementary charge and explicit capacitance;
- inward current adds positive charge and depolarizes;
- zero-current quiescence;
- byte-exact restart preserving the next current transition;
- 100,000 opposing-current pairs with fixed residency and no drift; and
- refusal of zero/negative capacitance and malformed restart state.

The complete isolated D3 physics target passes 60/60 tests; the rejected
direct-winding source-retirement gate passes 2/2. These proofs do not establish
resting equilibrium, recovery, fluid conservation, neuronal settlement,
mounting, deployment, or cognition.
