# Multiple-environment, embodiment, and sensory catalog

## One continuous world relationship

Guala has one continuous organism/body across environments. Each environment is a persistent region of one authenticated world graph or an explicitly separate simulated world with declared provenance. No room, world, device, or UI owns another Guala.

Every transition follows:

`prior body/world -> prepared intent -> at-most-once action -> successor body/world -> complete sensory consequence -> organism re-entry -> persistence`

## Environment topology

| Environment | Physical purpose and contents | Required experience |

| Backyard/play area | Slide, swing, sandbox, garden, open ground, weather, forest edge. | Locomotion, balance, acceleration, touch, temperature, smell, sound direction, digging/carrying, plants/growth, play. |

## World-state requirements

- Exact world identity, revision, causal clock, region/place topology, and body location.
- Objects/materials with physical state, location, quantity, affordances, custody, and predecessor/successor receipts.
- Authenticated agents and source/presence relations without treating tags as theory of mind.
- Light/optical geometry, sound sources/propagation, contact/material state, temperature, airborne and ingested chemistry, gravity/orientation, body internal state, and time/weather only where exact mechanics are ratified.
- Event-driven continuation over elapsed causal time; no busy loop, repeated whole-world copy, or worldfeed pretending to be world physics.
- Bounded retained current state plus compact evidence/formation references; no unbounded frame/audio/world history.

## Complete sensory and body matrix

| Lane | Pre-receptor mechanics | Receptor/neuron consequence | Body/action closure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sight | World light, surfaces, occlusion, viewpoint, eye pose, optics, retina/fovea/periphery, color and temporal change. | Authenticated visual occurrence reaches specialized neurons and later formations. | Gaze, blink, head/body orientation, navigation, manipulation, self-seeing. |
| Hearing | Sound source, medium/propagation, distance/direction, pinna/head geometry, ear canal, middle/inner ear, cochlear temporal-frequency mechanics. | Pressure/sample clock reaches auditory receptors and later sound/word/music formations. | Orienting, self-hearing, vocal correction, playback action, source localization. |
| Touch/pressure/vibration | Body surface geometry, contact patch, material compliance/friction/texture, force and time. | Localized somatosensory neurons and touch formations. | Withdrawal, grasp/carry, comfort, manipulation, tool/object learning. |
| Temperature | Material/air/body heat exchange and receptor location over time. | Thermal receptor trajectories and hot/cold formations. | Regulation, clothing/shelter, approach/avoidance, rest. |
| Smell | Airborne material transport, concentration, airflow, nasal/receptor exposure. | Olfactory receptor/neuron trajectories and odor formations. | Orienting, approach/avoidance, food/place/social association. |
| Taste | Material contact/dissolution in mouth, receptor chemistry, temperature, smell/body interaction. | Gustatory neurons and taste formations. | Mouth action, preference/avoidance, ingestion/body consequence only when modeled. |
| Proprioception | Joint/limb/mouth/face position, muscle/tendon state, movement. | Body-position neurons and pose/action formations. | Learned articulation, posture, locomotion, manipulation, procedural memory. |
| Vestibular | Orientation, acceleration, rotation, gravity relation. | Balance/orientation neurons integrated with vision and proprioception. | Recovery, navigation, slide/swing/dance/gymnastic movement. |
| Interoception | Energy, respiration, circulation, hunger/thirst analogues, fatigue, pain/tension/comfort, fluid/metabolic state. | Internal-body neurons and affect/motivation participation. | Rest, sleep, regulation, action selection, recovery. |
| Effector/self-sense | Motor intent, mouth/breath/larynx, face/eyes, posture/limbs, actuators. | Efference/body state plus returned sensory consequence. | At-most-once action, self-voice, facial/body expression, correction and learning. |

Unavailable biological mechanics must remain unavailable rather than being replaced with descriptor words or static channel values.

## Media and curriculum placement

- Books, PDFs, cards, puzzles, and drawing belong in persistent study/library scenes.
- Gutenberg material becomes a lawful physical book/page, never a corpus voice.
- Khan lessons become visual/auditory/interactive presentations, not snippets.
- YouTube and PBS content become synchronized audiovisual experience on an in-world screen, not metadata.
- Spotify/local music becomes actual sound from an in-world source, not a track title.
- Camera/microphone create authenticated live visual/acoustic occurrences with explicit user permission and start/stop state.
- Social teaching binds participant, referent, environment, sensory presentation, attention, and consequence in one causal occurrence.

## UI observation contract

Both live pages must expose, without invention:

- organism identity and causal clock;
- current environment/place, world revision, body pose/state, and provenance;
- what physical material is available, selected, presented, and accepted;
- what the server proved reached visual, auditory, and other sensory boundaries;
- authenticated attention/orienting if available;
- prepared/executed action and returned consequence;
- neuronal/fractal/formation/cognitive-capital evidence at its actual level;
- autonomous, tutoring, play, sleep/dream/wake, and quiescent state;
- exact unavailability/loss when a stage is absent.

Do not render whole brain/world state. Use bounded event-derived projections keyed by receipt change.

## Acceptance

Prove environment persistence and return, object permanence, navigation, real sensory change across locations, action/consequence, cross-environment recall/transfer, autonomous exploration, sleep/wake continuity, exact restart, and bounded CPU/RAM/storage. Live proof requires the real production body/world, APIs, both pages, and browser behavior—not source files or illustrations.
