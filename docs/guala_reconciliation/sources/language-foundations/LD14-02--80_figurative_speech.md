# 80 FIGURATIVE SPEECH — when words are carried away from home
Say one thing, mean another, be understood anyway. Every figure is
a word taken from its birthplace and still working.

ESSENCE: a figure means something other than what it says — "the
  eyes are the windows to the soul" holds no glass and no glazier;
  reading it flat is the ordinary way people miss each other.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / a handle can be aimed off its thing.
THREAD: law (17: contracts hunt figures out so words can be taken
  flat), teaching (30: told to hold his horses, a child looks up).
ASKED-AS: figure of speech literally means something else not saying eyes windows soul

ESSENCE: metaphor carries a word away from where it was born and
  sets it on new ground; what survives the trip is a shape, never
  a fact. "Her argument collapsed" brings falling, not bricks.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / metaphor is borrowed structure.
THREAD: money (16: liquid, flow, crash, all carried in from water),
  computing (19: files and folders, an office moved into glass).
ASKED-AS: metaphor comparing saying is a picture image carried meaning not literal

ESSENCE: the plain reading fails first, and that failure is the
  alarm — when the words as they stand make no picture that could
  stand up ("he bit my head off"), a carried sense waits beneath.
ROOT: this file / a figure holds two senses; only one works.
THREAD: evidence (36: a reading that predicts nonsense is thrown
  out), fixing things (57: the symptom names where to look).
ASKED-AS: makes no sense weird sentence doesnt add up literal fails hidden meaning

ESSENCE: simile carries in the open — "like" and "as" are a
  receipt saying a comparison is under way. Metaphor hides the
  seam and says the thing simply is. Same trip; one shows papers.
ROOT: this file / every figure carries; only the showing differs.
THREAD: design (28: a frame declares itself or disappears), law
  (17: the disclosed term against the silent one).
ASKED-AS: simile like as comparison metaphor difference which one hidden marked

ESSENCE: an idiom is a figure gone hard — its parts have stopped
  paying their way and only the whole means anything. "Kick the
  bucket" has no kick and no bucket; learn it as one long word.
ROOT: this file / long use fuses a carried phrase into one handle.
THREAD: words and meaning (66: a handle is agreed whole), teaching
  (30: idioms are taught one by one, never worked out).
ASKED-AS: idiom sayings kick the bucket phrase parts dont mean weird expressions

ESSENCE: exaggeration and understatement are one trick facing two
  ways — each says a thing nobody could believe, and the width of
  the gap is the message. "A million times" reports anger.
ROOT: this file / a said sense nobody accepts sends you hunting.
THREAD: persuasion (22: overclaim spends trust), humor (41: "a bit
  damp" in a downpour is a joke about restraint).
ASKED-AS: exaggeration hyperbole million times understatement overstating not literally big small

ESSENCE: irony says the opposite of what it means and trusts you
  to flip it — "lovely weather" in a storm. The flip runs on shared
  ground, so far from home it lands as a lie or a blunder.
ROOT: the other mind (76) / a figure runs on ground both sides hold.
THREAD: people together (21: an in-joke marks who is inside), law
  (17: written words are read without the room's tone).
ASKED-AS: irony sarcasm opposite meaning tone joke missed offended misread serious

ESSENCE: personification hands a thing a will it does not own —
  the storm wants the coast, the engine refuses to start. It is a
  picture, and it is how people truly reason about weather and luck.
ROOT: the mind (20) / minds read intention first and check later.
THREAD: chance (35: a streak is a will read into noise), machines
  (04: "she's being stubborn" is hiding a dry bearing).
ASKED-AS: personification machine hates me luck against weather angry alive treating like person

ESSENCE: a figure used long enough stops being one — the leg of a
  table, the face of a clock, the head of a line. Nobody feels the
  carry now; the borrowed sense has become the plain one.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / use re-signs meaning and moves it.
THREAD: computing (19: "desktop" no longer pictures a desk),
  keeping knowledge (40: terms outlive the world that minted them).
ASKED-AS: dead metaphor table leg clock face used to be picture stopped noticing

ESSENCE: a symbol stands for something by agreement or by long
  company with it — a dove, a flag, a white dress. The link lives
  in the people, not the object, so among strangers it is mute.
ROOT: meaning and ritual (26) / marks are made by repetition.
THREAD: money (16: a bill is paper until all agree it isn't), law
  (17: a seal binds only where seals are honoured).
ASKED-AS: symbol stands for represents dove flag colors meaning shared culture mute

ESSENCE: an analogy claims two unlike things share a shape — the
  heart is a pump, the atom is a solar system. Alone it proves
  nothing; every analogy has a place where the shape stops.
ROOT: this file / a carry moves structure; structure can be wrong.
THREAD: evidence (36: a resemblance is a question, not a finding),
  teaching (30: new ground gets mapped with old maps).
ASKED-AS: analogy comparing similar situation argument proves anything breaks down fails

ESSENCE: each kind of writing has its own appetite for figures — a
  poem reaches for them, a torque table refuses them, a contract
  bans them. The wrong amount is not bad taste; it breaks meaning.
ROOT: language (09) / every saying is cut for a listener and a use.
THREAD: law (17: a definitions clause exists to kill figures),
  engineering (04: a tolerance is a number or it is nothing).
ASKED-AS: when to use metaphor formal technical writing poem tone flowery plain
