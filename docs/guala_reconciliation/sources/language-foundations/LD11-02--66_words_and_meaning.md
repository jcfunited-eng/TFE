# 66 WORDS AND MEANING — how words carry sense
Handles, not things — every word is an agreement that can drift,
collide, or carve the world somewhere else.

ESSENCE: a word is a handle, not the thing — "bread" has no crust,
  and the loaf never picked that noise; sound and thing are joined
  by agreement alone.
ROOT: language (09) / words are agreed tokens, signed by peoples.
THREAD: money (16: a bill is paper until all agree it isn't),
  computing (19: a byte is a letter only by a shared code table).
ASKED-AS: word means name sound thing why called label agree handle noise arbitrary

ESSENCE: one spelling can carry unrelated meanings — the crust on a
  loaf and the crust of the planet share letters and nothing else;
  the words nearby pick which one is meant.
ROOT: this file / handles are few, things many; reuse is forced.
THREAD: computing (19: one file name in two folders opens two
  files), law (17: contracts define their terms for this reason).
ASKED-AS: same word different meanings bank river crust confusing which one context spelling

ESSENCE: words come in families — kinds (a robin is a bird is an
  animal), parts (a wing is part of a bird), and opposites; a word's
  meaning is largely its address in that web.
ROOT: the mind (20) / to know a thing is to know its neighbors.
THREAD: living world (31: the tree of life is one kind-family),
  teaching (30: new words are hung on words already owned).
ASKED-AS: kinds types categories part of opposite bird animal related words belongs sorting

ESSENCE: near-words are not one word — cold, cool, chilly, freezing
  all point at low warmth and disagree about how much; the wrong
  pick changes the claim, not just the flavor.
ROOT: this file / two handles on one spot grip different amounts.
THREAD: measurement (36: near-words are a coarse ruler), medicine
  (06: sore, aching and stabbing send for different tests).
ASKED-AS: synonyms similar words stronger weaker angry furious thesaurus shade difference exact

ESSENCE: words point in company — "small" beside an elephant and
  "small" beside a mouse name very different sizes; the neighbors do
  half the work of every word in a line.
ROOT: this file / a handle is aimed by the hand that holds it.
THREAD: design (28: a color shifts with what surrounds it), music
  (10: one note means different things in different keys).
ASKED-AS: context surrounding words meaning changes depends sentence alone isolated neighbors clue

ESSENCE: naming carves the world — where one tongue sees snow,
  another keeps three snows apart; where we say "he", some tongues
  make you say whether he is near or far. The cuts are choices.
ROOT: language (09) / each people minted tokens over its own ground.
THREAD: living world (31: one naturalist's species is another's
  five), law (17: a statute cuts where the world only slopes).

ESSENCE: abstract words are stacked concrete ones — justice, freedom
  and value are built out of simple felt things: a fair share, an
  open door, a trade you would take again.
ROOT: language (09) / minds map new ground with old maps.
THREAD: teaching (30: explain with examples or you have not
  explained), persuasion (22: big words hide small arguments).
ASKED-AS: abstract concepts justice freedom love vague big words explain examples concrete grasp

ESSENCE: to define is to name the family, then the difference — a
  chair is furniture (family) for one person to sit on, with a back
  (difference); too wide catches strangers, too narrow drops kin.
ROOT: this file / words come in families; a definition is an address.
THREAD: mathematics (11: a proof states its premises first), law
  (17: the definitions clause decides the case).
ASKED-AS: define definition what is a explain word family difference too broad narrow

ESSENCE: metaphor moves a word's home — the leg of a table, the
  mouth of a river, a warm welcome; carrying a word to new ground is
  how vocabulary grows without minting one new sound.
ROOT: language (09) / metaphor is borrowed structure, caught moving.
THREAD: money (16: liquid, flow, crash — all borrowed), computing
  (19: files, folders, windows — an office moved into glass).
ASKED-AS: metaphor table leg river mouth figure speech idioms sayings borrowed literally originally

ESSENCE: ambiguity is normal, not broken — one handle doing many
  jobs is cheap, and the listener settles it by context without
  noticing ("I saw her duck").
ROOT: this file / one spelling carries several meanings by design.
THREAD: humor (41: a pun is ambiguity cashed for a laugh), law (17:
  fortunes turn on one comma's two readings).
ASKED-AS: ambiguous double meaning misunderstanding pun joke confusing headlines unclear reading

ESSENCE: words drift — "nice" once meant foolish and "awful" once
  meant full of awe; use moves a word a step at a time, and only use
  moves it.
ROOT: this file / meaning is agreement, re-signed at every speaking.
THREAD: law (17: custom outranks the letter once everyone ignores
  it), money (16: prices drift by the same daily voting).
ASKED-AS: words change meaning old fashioned literally slang used to mean drift proper

ESSENCE: a dictionary is a map of agreements — it reports how a
  community uses a handle now, the way a chart reports where the
  sandbars sit this year.
ROOT: this file / a record of a moving thing is dated when printed.
THREAD: navigation (12: the chart is not the coastline), keeping
  knowledge (40: every record is a snapshot, dated).
