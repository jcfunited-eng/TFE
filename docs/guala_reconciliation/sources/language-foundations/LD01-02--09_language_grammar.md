# 09 LANGUAGE AND GRAMMAR — arbitrary noises, binding contracts
The oldest human technology, and the one this corpus is written in.
Its deepest laws are contracts wearing sound.

ESSENCE: a word means what it means only because everyone agrees it
  does — the sound is arbitrary, the agreement is binding ("dog"
  carries no dogness; any noise would do, once shared).
ROOT: law+agreements / a contract binds because both sides keep it —
  here signed by whole peoples, enforced by nothing but use.
CANNOT: no one can change a word's meaning alone. No private
  language only its owner could check — a token no one else honors
  buys nothing.
THREAD: money (a bill is paper until everyone agrees it isn't — the
  same mint), computing (a byte is a letter only by shared code
  table), law (all of it stands on this one).
ASKED-AS: slang dictionary definitions made up evolving names labels gibberish nicknames official spelling

ESSENCE: order carries meaning — the same words rearranged say
  different things ("dog bites man" / "man bites dog").
ROOT: words as agreed tokens (this file) — with sounds arbitrary,
  position is the cheapest place to store who did what to whom;
  languages that ignore order pay with word-endings instead.
CANNOT: no orderless heap of words still says who did what to whom —
  strip order and markings both, and a sentence dies into a list.
THREAD: chemistry (same atoms, different arrangement, different
  substance), music (the same notes reordered are a different tune),
  cooking (eggs before or after the heat — scramble or custard;
  order is the dish).
ASKED-AS: sentence structure subject verb scrambled shuffled yoda backwards syntax mixing endings latin

ESSENCE: finite pieces make infinite sentences — a few thousand
  words and a handful of stacking rules generate sentences never
  said before, without end (a sentence can nest inside a sentence).
ROOT: mathematics / counting never ends — a rule that accepts its
  own output never runs out.
CANNOT: no finished dictionary of everything sayable. No longest
  sentence — whatever is offered, a clause extends it.
THREAD: mathematics (ten digits, all numbers), music (twelve notes,
  endless tunes), chemistry (~90 atoms, every substance there is).
ASKED-AS: vocabulary alphabet letters combinations creativity phrases unlimited building blocks novel utterances endless

ESSENCE: ambiguity is a permanent resident — most sentences carry
  more than one legal reading, and listeners settle it by context
  without noticing ("I saw her duck").
ROOT: words as agreed tokens — tokens are few, things are many;
  reuse is forced, so collisions are structural, not sloppy.
CANNOT: no natural language where every sentence has exactly one
  reading — buy total precision and you get contract prose or
  mathematics, which nobody speaks at dinner.
THREAD: law (fortunes turn on one comma's two readings), computing
  (code bans ambiguity — the ban is why programs are precise and
  inhuman at once), humor (a pun is ambiguity cashed for a laugh).
ASKED-AS: misunderstanding double meaning confusion texting sarcasm headlines unclear vague interpretation email tone

ESSENCE: the name is not the thing — words point at the world but
  are not in it; redrawing the map moves no mountain.
ROOT: words as agreed tokens — a pointer is not its target.
CANNOT: no renaming changes the named: calling the tiger "kitten"
  declaws nothing. No magic words — pointing is not pushing.
THREAD: navigation (the chart is not the coastline; ships sink on
  the difference), money (the price is not the worth), medicine (a
  diagnosis names the disease and cures nothing by itself).
ASKED-AS: labels rebranding euphemism spells curses titles slogans propaganda insults sticks stones

ESSENCE: negation is language's strange power — one small word
  builds a statement about what is NOT there, which no photograph
  can do.
ROOT: premise — the world contains only what is; absences live in
  descriptions alone. UNSURE at full depth, but the asymmetry is
  real: cameras assert, only speakers deny.
CANNOT: no picturing "no dog here" directly. And negation cannot
  un-summon what it names — "don't think of an elephant" delivers
  the elephant.
THREAD: mathematics (zero — absence promoted to a symbol that
  calculates), the two fabrics (the white IS negation made
  load-bearing: knowledge of cannot-be), law (proving a not-there
  is famously harder than proving a there).
ASKED-AS: missing absent empty denial opposites invisible proving nobody nowhere blank gone silence

ESSENCE: metaphor is borrowed structure — understanding one thing
  by importing another's skeleton ("time flows", "prices climb"),
  and most abstract talk is dead metaphors still on the payroll.
ROOT: the body / minds map new territory with old maps — up, warm,
  heavy, forward are the first maps everything abstract borrows.
CANNOT: no purely literal language for abstractions — try to
  discuss time or minds with no borrowed structure and speech
  stalls. The loans are permanent.
THREAD: this corpus (every THREAD line is metaphor doing honest
  work), physics teaching (electricity taught as water in pipes),
  medicine (the heart as pump — a metaphor so good it became fact).
ASKED-AS: figure speech comparison symbolism poetry idioms sayings expressions analogy imagery describing feelings

ESSENCE: writing is frozen speech — sound made durable, so a mind
  can address minds not yet born; the technology that lets the
  dead teach.
ROOT: words as agreed tokens, plus a second contract: marks for
  sounds.
CANNOT: without it, nothing said outlives its last rememberer.
  With it, tone freezes out — text cannot smile, which is why
  messages wound where voices wouldn't.
THREAD: histories (the field exists only because of this freeze),
  computing (code is writing a machine thaws and obeys), music
  (scores — we replay the fingers of the dead).
ASKED-AS: books letters diaries records alphabet printing reading preserved paper ink libraries emails

ESSENCE: perfect translation is impossible — languages cut the
  world at different joints, so every crossing keeps some of the
  meaning by spending the rest.
ROOT: words as agreed tokens — each people minted its own tokens
  over its own territory; the maps do not tile the same.
CANNOT: no lossless crossing. Poetry proves it hardest: rhyme,
  rhythm, and sense cannot all cross the bridge — the translator
  chooses which to drown.
THREAD: computing (every file-format conversion loses an edge —
  same law), music (a tune moved to a new instrument keeps the
  notes, loses the throat), cooking (a dish rebuilt abroad from
  local goods is a translation, never the original).
ASKED-AS: subtitles dubbing interpreter bilingual idioms foreign untranslatable nuance lost accents dictionary phrasebook
