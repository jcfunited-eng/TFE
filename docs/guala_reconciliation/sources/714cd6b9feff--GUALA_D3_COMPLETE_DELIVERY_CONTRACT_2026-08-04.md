The neuron retains bounded current physical state. It does not retain every

### 2. Whole-organism sensory wiring

The same organism must receive sight, binaural hearing, touch, temperature,
smell, taste, vestibular state, proprioception, interoception, fluid-brain
state, motor consequences, and articulation consequences. A shared episode
may recruit every modality. Sight and hearing have no privileged cognitive

### 3. Fractal-to-mosaic formation

A mosaic first becomes possible with at least three nonzero retained fractals
from three distinct neurons. Count is insufficient. Admission also requires a
shared or physically connected occurrence, topological connectedness,
preserved distinct perspectives, retained change, later collective recurrence
or recognition, exact lineage, and bounded physical resources.

### 4. Cognitive hierarchy

- **Neuronal fractal:** one neuron's exact sparse retained impression.
- **Mosaic:** learned or recognized object, word, face, sound, taste, smell,
  feeling, body state, or other physical pattern.
- **Mosaic of mosaics:** learned relationship among mosaics.
- **Tapestry:** ordered sentence, episodic moment, navigation, avoidance, or
  other temporal organization.
- **Tapestry of tapestries:** ordered thought or extended expression.
- **Weave:** integrated practiced capability or creative self-expression.

No receipt, label, database row, scalar, or counter is one of these formations.
