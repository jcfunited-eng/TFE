## Biological-equivalent boundary

Primary experiments show that attention acts most strongly when neuronal
populations compete, and that recurrent/prefrontal influence can alter local
sensory transmission through anatomically localized inhibitory circuitry. The
substrate analogue must therefore be physical competition among simultaneous
routes whose outcome changes with prior internal/body state. It must not be a
software winner.

- Reynolds, Chelazzi, and Desimone (1999), competing V2/V4 populations and
  attention-dependent response bias:
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6782185/>.
- Wimmer et al. (2015), causal prefrontal influence through localized thalamic
  reticular feedforward inhibition:
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4626291/>.
- McAlonan, Cavanaugh, and Wurtz (2006), single-neuron attentional modulation
  in primate thalamic reticular circuitry:
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6674014/>.

These papers inform the physical responsibility, not coefficients or a claim
that Guala already reproduces biological attention.
