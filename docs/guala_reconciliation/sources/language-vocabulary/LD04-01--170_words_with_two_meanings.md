# 170 WORDS WITH TWO MEANINGS — the same letters, different things
The commonest words in the language are the ones doing the most jobs,
and a reader who counts letters will hear a river bank in a question
about a loan. Nothing in the spelling settles it. What settles it is
the company: each sense of a word travels with its own crowd, and the
crowd is written out here so it can be looked for.

ESSENCE: crust, pie and jam are kitchen words that each keep a second
  life outside the kitchen — the planet has a crust, a road and a
  printer both have jams, a market is cut into pie slices, and a band
  jams. Flour, oven and fruit nearby mean food; plates, traffic and
  guitars mean anywhere else.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / handles are few and things are many,
  so reuse is forced and the reuse is usually a picture.
THREAD: the deep earth (33: the planet's crust was named by picture),
  music (10: to jam is to play without a written part).
