# 95 DEEP TRADES AND HANDS — the working knowledge of practical work
Middle floors for the trades: what the joiner, the bricklayer, the
sparks, the welder, the butcher and the fitter each know in their
hands. Every entry is a law wearing overalls, and every law forbids
something.

ESSENCE: wood never stops breathing — it swells and shrinks across
  the grain with the room's dampness, a wide tabletop moving a
  quarter inch between February and August; good joinery does not
  stop that movement, it gives it somewhere to go.
ROOT: building / wood is a bundle of straws — cross-grain shrink is
  mandatory, so the joint is the only part left to design.
THREAD: building (a bridge's toothed joints are the same surrender
  at another scale), the body (a cast that allows no swelling
  strangles the limb).
ASKED-AS: table top cracked drawer sticking summer humid door swollen panel gap furniture split

ESSENCE: grain runs uphill or downhill under a blade, and the same
  board planed one way comes off like glass and the other way tears
  out in chunks — the fibers either lie down ahead of the edge or
  get lifted and torn.
ROOT: craft / the material has a will — here the will has a
  direction along the surface, not just an axis through the board.
THREAD: cooking (a fish scaled the wrong way, fur stroked
  backwards), barbering (clippers run one direction), painting (a
  brush dragged against a nap leaves the same lifted mess).
ASKED-AS: planing sanding chipped tearing rough smooth direction feed board carving whittling knots

ESSENCE: glue holds long grain to long grain and almost nothing
  else — end grain is a bundle of open straws that drinks the glue
  inward and leaves the surface bare, which is why every traditional
  joint exists: to turn an end-grain meeting into long-grain gluing
  surface plus a mechanical lock.
ROOT: chemistry / a bond needs both faces wetted and held — a
  surface that swallows the adhesive is never wetted at all.
THREAD: medicine (a graft needs living tissue on both faces),
  people together (an agreement bonded only at one end), building
  (mortar needs a bed, not a smear).
ASKED-AS: glue joint wood breaking apart end grain dowel mortise dovetail clamp weak sticking

ESSENCE: a taper locks and a parallel does not — a shallow wedge
  driven into a gap turns a light tap into an enormous sideways
  squeeze and then holds itself there by friction; axe heads, drill
  tapers, wedged tenons and dovetails all live on that one angle.
ROOT: engineering / a machine trades distance for force — a wedge
  is a ramp, and a shallow ramp multiplies hardest.
THREAD: geology (a landslide holds until the slope passes its
  angle), sports (a slight lean holds a scrum, a steep one collapses
  it), doorstops and boat trailers.
ASKED-AS: wedge axe handle loose hammer head splitting taper dovetail chisel driving tight friction

ESSENCE: a screw does not remove wood, it shoves it aside — so the
  two pieces need two different holes: a narrow pilot in the far
  piece for the thread to bite, and a wider clearance hole in the
  near piece so the screw passes through and pulls the two together
  instead of jacking them apart.
ROOT: physics / matter cannot share space — the displaced fibers go
  outward as a split, or the threads simply hold the top piece off.
THREAD: medicine (a bone screw uses the identical two-hole trick),
  negotiation (a deal held apart by the thing meant to close it),
  sewing (a needle parts cloth rather than cutting it).
ASKED-AS: screw split wood gap between boards pilot hole drilling countersink pulling tight clearance

ESSENCE: a fastener is strong across and weak along — a nail driven
  through two boards resists sliding hugely and pulls out with a
  claw hammer, so framing is arranged so every load pushes across
  the nails and never hangs off their tips.
ROOT: physics / a bar carries load along its length far better than
  it grips sideways along a hole.
THREAD: this file (a duct-tape patch failing to peel and holding to
  a drag), climbing (a piece of gear rated one way only), rope (a
  knot loaded from the wrong end lets go).
ASKED-AS: nails screws pulling out shear hanging joist bracket hanger deck load direction strong

ESSENCE: an edge is not a thing you make, it is the line where two
  flat surfaces meet — so sharpening is flattening: work one face,
  work the other, and the edge appears free of charge when the two
  arrive at the same place.
ROOT: mathematics / two planes meet in a line, and the sharper that
  line, the truer both planes have to be.
THREAD: optics (a lens is polished by truing surfaces, never by
  aiming at the focus), negotiation (agreement is the line where two
  positions meet — move the positions, not the line).
ASKED-AS: sharpening knife stone whetstone honing burr angle blade dull edge chisel steel

ESSENCE: a dull edge is a rounded nose — it cannot part fibers, so
  it crushes and wedges them apart; the force it needs climbs while
  the direction that force resolves into swings sideways, which is
  why it skates across the work before it bites.
ROOT: physics / pressure = force ÷ area — a rounded nose spread the
  area, and every wedge sends part of the push out sideways.
THREAD: driving (a bald tire needs no extra grip until the moment
  it needs all of it), teaching (an argument that will not enter is
  shoved harder and glances off the person entirely).
ASKED-AS: dull knife slipped cut finger pressing hard skated tomato blade sharper safer accident

ESSENCE: a cutting tooth needs somewhere to put what it removes and
  somewhere to go afterward — a saw's teeth are bent alternately
  outward so the slot is wider than the blade and nothing binds, and
  a file cuts on the push only, so dragging it back rounds the teeth
  it just used.
ROOT: physics / friction is a tax — every tooth buried in its own
  waste is paying it, and a blade squeezed by the cut is paying it
  along its whole length.
THREAD: farming (a plow needs a furrow to throw the soil into),
  computing (a full disk stops working long before it is full),
  crowds (a room with no exit route jams at the door).
ASKED-AS: saw stuck binding blade kerf set teeth file pushing dragging jamming cutting slow

ESSENCE: polishing is a queue of scratches — each grade of abrasive
  removes the scratches the last one left and puts in finer ones of
  its own, so a skipped grade leaves coarse marks hiding under the
  fine shine, invisible until the finish goes on and lights them all
  up.
ROOT: craft / the last tenth takes half the time — order is bought
  at rising rates, and every step buys only one step's worth.
THREAD: teaching (a skipped stage shows up two lessons later),
  medicine (a wound closed over debris), writing (a polished
  sentence sitting on an unfixed structure).
ASKED-AS: sanding grit paper scratches swirl marks stain finish shiny smooth steps skipping wood

ESSENCE: steel is hardened by heating it and cooling it fast, which
  freezes it in a brittle state, and then softened a little on
  purpose so it will bend rather than shatter — every edge tool is a
  chosen point on that line, and there is no point on it that is
  both.
ROOT: craft / the material holds the temper it was given — hardness
  and toughness come from the same structure pulled in opposite
  directions.
THREAD: people (the rigid rule and the flexible one, each failing
  the other's way), building (glass has no yield at all — it takes
  hardness to the end and only breaks), rope (stiff or stretchy).
ASKED-AS: hardening tempering quenching steel brittle chipped edge grinding blue burnt spring file

ESSENCE: brittle things are not cut, they are cracked on purpose —
  a scored line is a row of tiny flaws, and a bend puts the stretch
  exactly there, so glass and tile break along a scratch you chose
  rather than one the material chose.
ROOT: building / a crack is a force magnifier — the trick is to
  supply the crack yourself, at the place you want it.
THREAD: paper (tearing along a fold), geology (rock split on its
  bedding plane), governance (an institution breaks at whatever line
  someone has been scoring for years).
ASKED-AS: glass cutting tile score snap breaking clean wandering crack scratch pliers straight edge

ESSENCE: a pencil line has width and the saw eats a slot of its
  own, so the line must be left standing on the piece you are
  keeping — cut beside it, never through it, and decide before you
  start which side is the offcut.
ROOT: engineering / no part is made exact — but this error is not
  random, it is a known thickness you can choose where to spend.
THREAD: tailoring (cut outside the chalk), surgery (a margin taken
  wide of the disease, never through it), budgeting (put the slack
  where losing it is survivable).
ASKED-AS: pencil mark saw blade thickness kerf cutting board short size fit trim line

ESSENCE: a knife line beats a pencil line because it is not
  information, it is a feature — the blade severs the top fibers and
  leaves a tiny wall, and a chisel dropped into it finds that wall
  and cannot wander; the mark has become part of the work.
ROOT: engineering / a controlled machine steers against error — a
  physical stop removes the need to steer at all.
THREAD: craft (a jig is this idea grown large), computing (a rule
  the machine enforces beats a rule in the manual), law (a locked
  gate outperforms a posted notice).
ASKED-AS: marking knife pencil line chisel scoring accurate joint gauge cutting layout wall

ESSENCE: measuring twice does not mean reading the same tape the
  same way again — a repeat by the same road repeats the same
  mistake; the second measurement has to arrive by another route,
  from the far end, or by holding the actual part up against the
  actual hole.
ROOT: evidence / two agreeing measurements are worth something only
  if they could have disagreed — same method, same error, no news.
THREAD: accounting (two books kept by two routes — the whole point
  of double entry), navigation (a position from one instrument is a
  guess), medicine (a second opinion means a second doctor).
ASKED-AS: tape measure ruler wrong twice check mistake cut short mark double reading

ESSENCE: everything built stands on three references — plumb
  (straight down), level (flat across), and square (the corner) —
  and gravity hands out the first two free to anyone with a string
  and a bubble; square is the one nothing in nature provides.
ROOT: physics / gravity points one way everywhere — a hanging weight
  and a trapped bubble are both reading the same arrow.
THREAD: navigation (down and flat are free at sea; direction is the
  costly one), surveying (the whole trade is this triangle of
  checks), law (two references given, the third must be agreed).
ASKED-AS: level bubble spirit plumb bob string square corner crooked wall shelf diagonal

ESSENCE: a wall is set out from one line and one height, and every
  mark is taken from that origin — never from the last piece you
  fitted, because errors measured from a moving start do not cancel
  each other, they add up in one direction.
ROOT: mathematics / small independent errors chained end to end
  wander, and a chain that always measures forward from the last
  wanders without ever coming home.
THREAD: computing (an absolute address against a relative one),
  music (tuning each string from the one before leaves the last
  sour), history (a copy of a copy of a copy).
ASKED-AS: studs spacing layout benchmark string line marking wall origin measuring drift accumulate

ESSENCE: in an old building nothing is square, so a shelf into an
  alcove is not measured, it is scribed — hold the piece close, run
  a compass along the crooked wall, and let the point draw the
  wall's actual shape onto the wood; you have copied reality instead
  of describing it.
ROOT: evidence / a measurement is a summary and every summary drops
  something — transfer keeps what the number threw away.
THREAD: dentistry (an impression taken rather than a tooth
  measured), shoemaking (a last carved to the actual foot),
  photography (a trace beats a description of a face).
ASKED-AS: scribing alcove shelf uneven wall gap fitting compass template tracing old house crooked

ESSENCE: one hand holds the tool and one hand guides it, which
  leaves nobody to hold the work — so a bench, a vise, a clamp or a
  knee is not an accessory, it is the third worker, and most bad
  cutting is a workpiece that moved.
ROOT: physics / no push without push-back — every cutting force has
  an equal one shoving the work away, and something has to take it.
THREAD: surgery (the assistant's whole job is holding), music (a
  cello has a spike because the player has no third hand),
  photography (a tripod buys a shutter speed no arm can).
ASKED-AS: vise clamp bench holding workpiece moved slipped steady jig hands third grip

ESSENCE: the irreversible step gets rehearsed — a glue-up is
  assembled dry first, every joint tried and every clamp laid out,
  because glue sets on a schedule that will not wait while you find
  a missing part.
ROOT: craft / measure twice, cut once — extended from the cut to
  every step that starts a clock you cannot stop.
THREAD: theater (dress rehearsal before an audience that will not
  wait), surgery (the instruments counted and laid out first),
  cooking (everything chopped before the pan is hot).
ASKED-AS: dry fit glue up rehearsal clamps ready assembly practice run panic setting time test

ESSENCE: mortar is not glue, it is a bed and a sacrifice — bricks
  sit in it and shift against it, and the joint is deliberately the
  weakest thing in the wall so that when something has to crack, it
  cracks where a trowel can put it back.
ROOT: engineering / a system fails at its weakest point — so choose
  that point on purpose, and choose a repairable one.
THREAD: cars (crumple zones and fuses — failure sited on purpose),
  fishing (the weak link on a leader saves the rod), governance (an
  office designed to break before the constitution does).
ASKED-AS: brick wall pointing repointing cement crumbling old house mortar joints cracked lime

ESSENCE: the water in a concrete mix is two things at once — the
  reactant that makes it set and the lubricant that makes it easy to
  pour — and every extra splash that buys easier work buys
  permanently weaker concrete, because the surplus leaves voids
  where it evaporates.
ROOT: chemistry / a reaction consumes a fixed share of its reagent
  — anything beyond that share is not chemistry, it is a hole
  waiting to happen.
THREAD: cooking (a wet dough is easier to mix and gives a different
  bread), medicine (a dose diluted for comfort is a dose reduced),
  writing (padding a paragraph to fill a page weakens all of it).
ASKED-AS: concrete mix water sloppy wet strong weak pouring workable crumbling cement ratio

ESSENCE: concrete does not dry, it grows — cement locks water into
  crystals, and the crystals keep knitting for months and creep on
  for years; keep it damp and covered while it works, or the
  reaction stops early and never restarts.
ROOT: chemistry / a reaction needs its reagent present — take the
  water away and the unreacted cement is dead powder inside the slab
  forever.
THREAD: cooking (dough proving under a cloth for the same reason),
  medicine (a wound healing under a moist dressing, not a scab),
  gardening (a seedbed kept damp through germination).
ASKED-AS: concrete slab driveway cracking dusty curing spray plastic sheet pouring setting hot weather

ESSENCE: every pound in a house is traveling — roof to rafter to
  wall to beam to post to footing to soil — and the path is real
  even where nobody drew it; a beam over a window is not holding the
  window up, it is carrying the roof around the hole.
ROOT: building / the chain of squeeze and stretch runs unbroken to
  earth — the trade's job is knowing which members are in the chain.
THREAD: money (a cut budget line moves the cost, it does not remove
  it), computing (traffic rerouted onto a link never sized for it),
  the body (a limp loads the other hip until that one goes too).
ASKED-AS: load bearing wall removing opening beam header sagging floor post basement renovation ceiling

ESSENCE: a roof keeps water out by overlap, not by seal — every
  layer laps over the one below so gravity always finds a downhill
  path across a joint, and the size of the lap is set by how far a
  gale can drive water back uphill.
ROOT: physics / water runs downhill unless something pushes it, and
  wind is that something — so the lap is a wind allowance, not a
  water one.
THREAD: clothing (shingled feathers and overlapping scales), armor
  (lapped plates), record-keeping (overlapping backups so no single
  gap runs all the way through).
ASKED-AS: roof leaking shingles tiles overlap lap flashing pitch wind driven rain sealant

ESSENCE: warm air holds far more water than cold air, so moisture
  inside a heated house drifts outward and meets the cold somewhere
  in the wall — if that meeting happens inside the insulation, the
  water arrives as liquid in a place nobody can see, and the timber
  rots quietly for years.
ROOT: weather / the dew point is where warm damp air is cooled to
  its limit — a wall has a temperature gradient, so it has a dew
  point somewhere along it.
THREAD: clothing (a waterproof coat with no breathability soaks you
  from within), spectacles fogging on entering a warm room,
  refrigeration (every cold pipe drips in summer).
ASKED-AS: condensation walls insulation damp mold vapor barrier attic loft ventilation rot black spots

ESSENCE: a repair puts a strong new piece into a tired old system,
  and the weak point simply moves next door — replace a rotted
  length of pipe and the next failure is at the joint, patch one
  panel and the rust reappears at its edge; the fix relocates the
  fault as much as it removes it.
ROOT: engineering / a system fails at its weakest point — take that
  point away and there is immediately a new one, usually adjacent.
THREAD: medicine (a stiff joint replaced, and the one above it
  starts hurting), roads (a widened junction pushing the jam one
  block on), governance (closing one loophole moving the money).
ASKED-AS: repair patch replaced section pipe rust broke again next joint weak spot fix moved

ESSENCE: every drain holds a small bend full of water that seals the
  sewer out of the room — and that seal survives only because a pipe
  open to the sky lets air in behind the falling water; without the
  vent, a flush sucks its own trap dry and the smell walks in.
ROOT: physics / a moving column of liquid drags the pressure down
  behind it, and the vent is a door for air to answer through.
THREAD: pouring (a can glugs until you punch a second hole), fuel
  tanks (a blocked breather stalls the engine), medicine (a chest
  drain is a trap and a vent making the same argument).
ASKED-AS: drain smell sewer gas gurgling toilet flush sink trap vent pipe empty bathroom

ESSENCE: a drain needs a slope, but a steep one is worse than a
  gentle one — water outruns what it is carrying, and the solids
  are left stranded high and dry in a pipe the water has already
  left.
ROOT: fluids / liquid and solid travel at different speeds down a
  slope, so the pipe must run part full and carry rather than race.
THREAD: rivers (a stream too fast drops its gravel where it slows),
  roads (traffic too fast leaves gaps and carries fewer cars),
  teaching (material delivered faster than the class travels).
ASKED-AS: drain slope blocked toilet waste pipe fall gradient plumbing gurgle backed up smells

ESSENCE: every threaded joint seals at one particular surface and
  nowhere else — a tapered thread seals on the wedging threads
  themselves, a parallel one seals on a flat face against a washer —
  and knowing which surface is doing the work tells you where the
  tape or the paste belongs and where it is useless.
ROOT: engineering / a fit is either interference or a gasket, never
  both at once; sealant is a gap filler and a lubricant, not glue.
THREAD: cooking (a jar seals on its rim, not its thread), diving
  and hydraulics (the O-ring's groove is the whole design),
  agreements (a contract binds at one clause, not everywhere).
ASKED-AS: plumbing thread tape ptfe leaking joint tightening washer olive compression fitting seal drip

ESSENCE: a breaker does not protect the appliance, it protects the
  wire — every thickness of copper can shed only so much heat, and
  the fuse is chosen to give up before the cable buried in the wall
  gets hot enough to light the house.
ROOT: physics / current through resistance makes heat, and heat
  inside a wall has nowhere to go.
THREAD: bridges (a posted weight limit protects the deck, not the
  truck), money (a credit limit sized to income), the body (fever is
  a protective response, not the disease).
ASKED-AS: breaker tripping fuse blown wire gauge extension cord overload amps outlet melted heater

ESSENCE: an earth wire is a deliberate easy road home for
  electricity that has escaped — its job is not to be safe by
  itself, but to make the fault so large and so fast that the
  breaker notices and cuts the supply before a person becomes the
  road instead.
ROOT: physics / current takes every path at once in proportion to
  how easy each one is — so the cure is offering a far easier path
  than flesh.
THREAD: buildings (a lightning rod is the same offer at sky
  voltage), plumbing (an overflow is an earth for water), governance
  (a lawful channel for grievance exists so it does not go elsewhere).
ASKED-AS: earth ground wire shock tingle appliance metal case plug third pin bonding safety

ESSENCE: a dead reading and a dead instrument look exactly alike —
  so the rule is prove the tester on a circuit known to be live,
  test the one you care about, then prove the tester again; two
  proofs bracket the one measurement that matters.
ROOT: evidence / an instrument reporting nothing is reporting two
  possible facts at once, and only a second known input separates
  them.
THREAD: medicine (a negative test from a spoiled kit), computing (a
  monitoring alarm that is itself down), fire alarms (the weekly
  test exists to prove silence means silence).
ASKED-AS: tester voltage stick proving dead live checking meter battery flat safe isolation switched off

ESSENCE: paint holds onto whatever it was put on, not onto the wall
  — so a perfect coat over chalk, grease, rust, damp or gloss peels
  away in sheets carrying the old layer with it; the failure plane
  is always the weakest layer in the stack, never the newest one.
ROOT: craft / contact is everything — adhesion is a handshake with
  the top layer only, and it inherits whatever grip that layer had.
THREAD: medicine (a dressing on unclean skin), building (render
  over a damp wall coming off in slabs), teaching (new understanding
  laid on a misunderstanding comes away with it).
ASKED-AS: paint peeling flaking blistering bubbles wall primer old gloss damp repainting chalky

ESSENCE: in every finishing trade the visible step is the short one
  — washing, filling, sanding, masking and priming take the days,
  while the paint, the tile, the varnish take the hour; the
  invisible work is the work, and it is the part nobody is thanked
  for.
ROOT: this file / the failure plane lies underneath, so the quality
  of a finish is settled before the finish arrives.
THREAD: cooking (everything chopped and laid out — the prep is the
  meal), photography (an hour of lighting for a one-minute shot),
  law (a case is won in preparation, not in the room).
ASKED-AS: sanding washing masking taping primer filler prep painting shortcut rushed finish quality

ESSENCE: the order of trades on a building is fixed by what buries
  what — pipes and cables before the boards, boards before the
  plaster, plaster before the paint — so the schedule is written by
  access, not by convenience, and a step taken out of turn is paid
  for with demolition.
ROOT: craft / one-way doors — burying a thing is a door that closes,
  and it closes for everyone downstream, not just for you.
THREAD: software (a decision baked into a foundation everyone built
  on), surgery (the order of closure), publishing (a correction
  after the print run).
ASKED-AS: order sequence trades plastering wiring plumbing before after schedule chasing walls buried

ESSENCE: getting the body into a position where the hands can work
  is half the job — a painter on proper boards outworks one
  stretching off a ladder three to one, and most bad workmanship is
  good workmanship attempted at arm's length.
ROOT: the body / strength and control both collapse away from the
  center — an arm at full stretch has neither force nor fine
  steering left.
THREAD: sports (footwork buys every shot before the racket moves),
  surgery (positioning and lighting before the first cut), writing
  (a desk and an hour beat willpower).
ASKED-AS: ladder scaffold reaching stretching platform access position awkward corner working height safe

ESSENCE: a weld is not glue between two metals, it is one pool of
  molten metal that freezes as a single grain across the seam — so
  everything that is not metal must leave first: paint, rust, oil,
  damp and zinc all boil into gas and freeze into the bead as holes.
ROOT: chemistry / a contaminant does not vanish under heat, it
  changes state — and gas trapped inside freezing metal is a bubble
  with nowhere left to go.
THREAD: soldering (the same law at a tenth the temperature), gluing
  (the same law with no heat at all), medicine (a wound closed over
  dirt is closed over an abscess).
ASKED-AS: welding weld porosity holes grinding clean rust paint galvanized spatter bead strong

ESSENCE: any two different metals with damp between them make a
  small battery, and a battery is paid for by the weaker metal — so
  a brass fitting on a steel tank, or aluminum against stainless in
  the rain, quietly eats one of them from the contact outward.
ROOT: chemistry / metals differ in how tightly they hold their
  electrons, and a difference plus a wet path is a current whether
  anyone wanted one or not.
THREAD: ships (a zinc block bolted on to be eaten instead of the
  hull — the same law hired), plumbing (copper above steel eats the
  steel), medicine (two different metals in one mouth).
ASKED-AS: dissimilar metals corrosion aluminum stainless brass steel white powder pitting boat anode contact wet

ESSENCE: solder and glue want opposite gaps — solder is a metal
  that wets clean hot metal and is pulled into a tight joint by the
  narrowness itself, so a sloppy gap gives a weak joint; glue holds
  by a thin deliberate film, so a joint clamped bone dry is starved.
ROOT: physics / narrow spaces pull liquids in — solder rides that
  pull, while glue holds by contact over a film that has to survive.
THREAD: cooking (a sauce that must coat, not drown), building (a
  mortar bed of a chosen thickness), friendship (too close and too
  distant fail by different routes).
ASKED-AS: solder flux plumbing pipe joint glue clamp gap wood copper wetting bond starved

ESSENCE: tightening a bolt is stretching it — the bolt is a stiff
  spring and its grip is the pull of that stretch — but stretch
  cannot be seen, so twist is measured instead, and most of the
  twist is eaten by friction. Only about a tenth reaches the clamp.
ROOT: engineering / a proxy is measured because the real quantity is
  hidden, and every proxy has a slippery exchange rate.
THREAD: medicine (blood pressure as a stand-in for what matters),
  economics (a measured target becomes a gamed target), cooking (an
  oven dial against the actual air inside).
ASKED-AS: torque wrench tight bolt snapped stripped thread lubricate oil setting wheel nuts spec

ESSENCE: a bolt can go slack without ever turning — paint, burrs,
  gaskets and rough faces crush down under the clamp, the joint
  settles a few thousandths, and the bolt's stretch is spent paying
  for that settling; nothing unscrewed, and the grip is gone.
ROOT: building / materials remember two ways — the crushed high
  spots went past yield, and past yield nothing pushes back.
THREAD: knots (a new rope's hitches bed in and need retaking),
  guitars (fresh strings drop for days), agreements (terms settle in
  the first weeks and get renegotiated once).
ASKED-AS: wheel nuts retighten loose bolt gasket settled clamp check again head studs slack

ESSENCE: fasteners around one joint are not independent — pulling
  one down tips the part and loosens its neighbors, so nuts go round
  in a criss-cross star and in two or three passes, and the last
  pass finds the first ones slack again.
ROOT: building / a load finds every path — a clamped joint is one
  connected body, so any change at one point redistributes at all
  the others.
THREAD: shoelaces and corsets (tightened in passes for the same
  reason), negotiation (settling one clause reopens two others),
  building (a truss loaded unevenly during assembly).
ASKED-AS: tightening order star pattern criss cross bolts head gasket wheel warped even passes torque

ESSENCE: the stitching line is not the cut line — every piece is
  cut larger than the finished part by a strip the seam eats, and
  that strip is also the garment's whole future: a seam can be let
  out as far as its allowance and not one thread further.
ROOT: craft / joining consumes material in every trade — the join
  has to come from somewhere, and it comes from the pieces.
THREAD: building (the overlap on a roof tile is the same tax),
  writing (margins are a page's allowance), money (a reserve is
  simply the ability to be let out later).
ASKED-AS: sewing seam allowance hem cutting pattern let out taking in altering fabric stitching edge

ESSENCE: woven cloth is stiff along its threads and stretchy on the
  diagonal between them — a piece cut on the slant drapes and
  clings, a piece cut with the threads hangs straight, and every
  pattern piece carries an arrow saying which way it must lie on the
  cloth.
ROOT: mathematics / a grid of unstretchable threads cannot lengthen
  along a thread, but it can change its corner angles — all the give
  is in the corners.
THREAD: engineering (a diagonal brace is the identical geometry,
  used to stop the give instead of to use it), bandaging (crepe
  stretches on the bias by design), nets (a net closes by shearing).
ASKED-AS: fabric grain bias diagonal stretchy cutting pattern arrow twisted seam hangs drape wash

ESSENCE: a bread recipe's numbers are an opening guess — flour
  drinks differently by harvest and by the day's damp, and dough
  runs on temperature, so an hour in a warm kitchen is three in a
  cold one; the dough reports its own state, and the clock never
  does.
ROOT: evidence / measure the thing you care about, not a stand-in —
  rise is the goal, and time is only a rumor about rise.
THREAD: cooking (roast to an internal temperature, not to minutes
  per pound), farming (crops ripen on accumulated warmth, not on
  dates), teaching (finish when the class has it, not at the bell).
ASKED-AS: dough rising bread proving poke test sticky warm kitchen recipe hours flour ready

ESSENCE: knife skill lives in the hand that is not holding the
  knife — curled fingers set the thickness and the knuckles ride the
  blade like a fence, while the cutting hand only draws the edge
  through and listens to what the food is doing.
ROOT: the mind / practiced skill sinks below deciding, which frees
