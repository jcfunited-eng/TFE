# 56 DEEP COLUMN — WEATHER WHERE YOU LIVE
Middle floors for the sky over one house: porch, yard, freezer
door — weather at the sizes a person actually stands in.

ESSENCE: the calm before a storm is the storm's intake stroke — a
  big storm's updraft swallows the low air ahead of it and hands
  some back down warm, dry, and settled, so a hush can fall just
  before the rush (not every storm grants one).
ROOT: physics / lighter rises through heavier — the storm is a
  chimney, and a chimney has a draw.
THREAD: fire (a great blaze makes its own inrushing wind), sea
  (the drawback before a big wave — water gathered into the
  coming crest).
ASKED-AS: calm before storm still quiet birds heavy air muggy eerie sky green waiting

ESSENCE: the smell of coming rain is cargo, not mood — drops
  striking dry ground elsewhere kick up an oil made by soil
  bacteria (geosmin; the nose catches it at parts per trillion),
  and the storm's outrunning wind delivers it ahead of the rain,
  sometimes with an ozone edge pulled down from the heights.
ROOT: chemistry / a smell is molecules in flight — nothing is
  smelled that did not physically travel to you.
THREAD: farming (the after-rain perfume is the soil's own
  workforce), cooking (beets taste of earth — the same molecule).
ASKED-AS: rain smell storm coming fresh dirt earthy summer shower pavement dust drops

ESSENCE: frost picks clear nights because clouds are a blanket —
  under an open sky the ground beams its heat to space unanswered
  and falls colder than the air itself, and the air's moisture
  lands on that overchilled surface as ice fur.
ROOT: cooking / heat moves by touch, flow, and glow — a frost
  night is glow with no reply.
THREAD: farming (orchard fans stir warm air down to fight the
  beam), desert (cloudless lands scorch by day, freeze by night).
ASKED-AS: frost windshield clear night scraping car morning white grass lawn cold snap

ESSENCE: wind has no voice — the howl is manufactured at
  obstacles, where air splitting around a wire, an edge, or a
  gap sheds a steady flutter of eddies; the flutter's beat is
  the pitch, so faster wind cries higher.
ROOT: music / a steady beat on the air IS a tone, whatever drums
  it.
THREAD: music (flutes and organ pipes are tamed howls — edge
  plus tube, tuned), engineering (the flutter that hums a wire
  can shake a bridge to pieces).
ASKED-AS: wind howling whistling windows door crack wires humming chimney eerie night storm

ESSENCE: hail is a summer stone because only summer heat drives
  updrafts violent enough to hold growing ice aloft — lofted
  again and again through freezing heights, ringed like an onion
  each trip (a golf-ball stone needs a rising wind near 90 km/h);
  winter's shallow, gentle clouds cannot lift a pea.
ROOT: physics / lighter rises through heavier, driven to
  violence by summer's hot floor.
THREAD: aviation (these updrafts are why airliners go around
  storms, never through), geology (rings as diary — hailstones
  and tree trunks, one trick).
ASKED-AS: hail summer storm ice balls july dents car roof golf ball why not winter

ESSENCE: morning fog is cloud parked on the grass, born of the
  same clear calm night as frost — and the sun doesn't burn it
  so much as evict it: light reaches the ground, the ground
  warms the air, and the droplets are re-drunk from below by
  noon.
ROOT: this file / frost's clear-night beam — fog is its other
  child, repossessed at daybreak.
THREAD: sea (coastal fog that owns whole summer days), aviation
  (valley airports scheduled around late-morning clearing).
ASKED-AS: fog morning burn off lifting valley mist drive headlights noon clearing airport

ESSENCE: lightning and thunder leave together — light arrives
  almost instantly while sound plods (about 3 seconds per
  kilometer, 5 per mile), so the gap is a tape measure: count
  seconds to the rumble, divide, and the storm is ranged.
ROOT: physics / every messenger has a speed — sound is only a
  shove passed molecule to molecule.
THREAD: sea (the old cannon-flash ranging trick — the same
  arithmetic), fireworks (bloom then boom, the lesson free every
  July).
ASKED-AS: lightning thunder count seconds miles away storm distance flash rumble delay boom

ESSENCE: humid heat feels hotter because the body's cooler is
  evaporation — sweat pays out only when it leaves as vapor, and
  air already crowded with vapor won't take more, so sweat sits
  and drips, spending nothing ("feels like" is exactly this
  correction).
ROOT: deep column 50 / evaporation is the fastest escape — here
  with the door jammed.
THREAD: survival (that line is the hard bound of livable
  climate), desert (dry 45°C is survivable with water — the same
  law's kinder face).
ASKED-AS: humidity muggy sticky heat index feels like sweat dripping swamp summer shade

ESSENCE: cold air is heavier air — open an upright freezer and
  it pours over your feet like water from a tipped tub; a chest
  freezer barely spills, its cold lying in the box like a liquid
  with the lid on top.
ROOT: physics / lighter rises through heavier — read downward.
THREAD: farming (orchards planted off the valley floor, where
  draining cold pools and kills), mountains (evening wind
  pouring off cooling slopes — the same spill at landscape
  size).
ASKED-AS: freezer door cold feet fridge open air falls mist chest freezer basement draft
