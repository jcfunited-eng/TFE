# 119 DEEP PHILOSOPHY OF BEING — what there is
Not what can be known, but what the world is made of and how its
pieces hold together. The oldest questions, opened as machinery.
Nothing here is mystical and nothing here is settled by a slogan.

ESSENCE: a ship has a plank replaced, then another, then all of
  them. Nothing of the original wood remains and the crew never
  stopped calling it the same ship. A thing can survive the total
  replacement of what it is made of, because what makes it one
  thing is the arrangement carrying on, not the stuff.
ROOT: premise — things are patterns that matter passes through, and
  the pattern is what persists while the material is traffic.
THREAD: the body (bones rebuilt over years, the person unbroken),
  rivers (the same river, none of the same water), organisations
  (a club with no original member).
ASKED-AS: same ship planks replaced still identical thing changed parts new all of it

ESSENCE: now suppose someone kept the old planks and rebuilt them.
  Two ships, each with a real claim, and no further fact anywhere
  in the world that decides which is the ship. The question has no
  hidden answer because identity was never a thing in the object —
  it was our way of counting one across time.

ESSENCE: for anything to change, something has to stay. A rotting
