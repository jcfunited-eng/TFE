# 100 DEEP HEALTH PRACTICE — keeping bodies working
File 06 holds the body's laws. This one holds the practice built on
them: what actually keeps people alive, in the order it matters.
Nothing here replaces a trained pair of hands.

ESSENCE: it is cheaper and better to stop a disease than to treat one,
  by a wide margin. The case that never happens costs nothing, needs
  no hospital, and leaves no damage behind. Almost all of the gain in
  human lifespan came from prevention, not from cures.
ROOT: medicine / the body heals fast and crooked — a disease survived
  still leaves its patch; money (a cost avoided beats a cost
  recovered).
CANNOT: no prevention that can point at who it saved — the person who
  did not get sick is invisible and ungrateful, which is why
  prevention is chronically underfunded while dramatic rescues are
  not. And no crediting it after the fact: the counting has to be set
  up before.
THREAD: cleaning + maintenance (the service that stops the breakdown
  nobody then remembers), engineering (maintenance against repair),
  money.
ASKED-AS: checkup screening seat belt why bother healthy diet doctor visit cheaper hospital

ESSENCE: the largest health intervention in history was not a drug. It
  was separating drinking water from sewage. Clean water and a working
  drain have saved more lives than every medicine combined, and where
  they fail the old diseases return within days.
ROOT: medicine / infection compounds — a water supply is the fastest
  multiplier a germ can rent; building (the drain and the trap as
  medical equipment).
CANNOT: no hospital that can outrun a contaminated supply — treatment
  runs one patient at a time and the pipe delivers to everybody at
  once. And no safe water where sewage and supply can touch: one
  cross-connection re-infects the whole line.
THREAD: building (drains, traps, sealed pipe), farming (irrigation and
  runoff carrying the same load), power (public works noticed only
  when they fail).
ASKED-AS: clean water cholera sewage plumbing boil well contaminated toilet outbreak pipes

ESSENCE: a vaccine shows the body's defence force a harmless mugshot
  so it will recognise the real thing on sight. And the protection is
  partly borrowed: when enough people nearby are immune, the germ runs
  out of hosts before it reaches the baby who could not be vaccinated.
ROOT: medicine / the defence force runs on me-or-not-me and keeps a
  list — a vaccine writes an entry without the disease; chance + risk
  (spread dies out once each case infects fewer than one).
CANNOT: no opting out privately — coverage is shared, so the risk of a
  refusal lands on the newborn and the immune-suppressed, who had no
  vote. And no partial herd protection below the threshold: it is a
  chain reaction, and it either dies out or it does not.
THREAD: chance + risk (multiplication below one), computing (a
  network's spread threshold is the same arithmetic), people together.
ASKED-AS: vaccine shots measles herd immunity babies outbreak flu booster why everyone

ESSENCE: hands are the main vehicle. Twenty seconds with soap — which
  pries grease-coated germs off skin and floats them down the drain —
  cuts infection more than nearly anything else a person can do alone.
  It was resisted for decades because it accused the doctors.
ROOT: chemistry / soap has one end that grips grease and one that
  grips water, and a germ's coat is grease; medicine (infection
  compounds, so removal early is worth enormously more than treatment
  later).
CANNOT: no sterile hands and no need for them — the aim is removal,
  not killing. And no skipping the mechanical part: rinsing without
  soap leaves the greasy passengers attached and simply wets them.
THREAD: cooking (the same molecule taking the same fat off the same
  pan), evidence + measurement (the ward with washed hands against the
  ward without — an early controlled comparison, and an early ignored
  one), cleaning + maintenance.
ASKED-AS: wash hands soap twenty seconds sanitizer germs before eating hospital sick spreading

ESSENCE: food poisoning is farming a crop you did not mean to grow.
  Germs double on a schedule set by warmth, so the whole practice is
  time and temperature: keep it cold, cook it hot, and do not leave it
  sitting in the warm middle for hours.
ROOT: medicine / infection is fermentation with a hostile tenant;
  chemistry (reaction and doubling rates climb with heat, until
  cooking's one-way door kills the workers).
CANNOT: no cooking away a poison already made — heat kills the
  bacteria and some of their toxins survive it, which is why reheated
  rice can still put someone in hospital. And no judging by smell or
  taste: the dangerous loads are usually invisible and odourless.
THREAD: cooking (the whole preservation trade — salt, cold, acid,
  smoke), farming, chemistry.
ASKED-AS: food poisoning leftovers fridge chicken raw expired smell fine reheat buffet sick

ESSENCE: a fever, a cough, and a pain are the body's smoke, not its
  fire. Treating them makes the patient comfortable and changes
  nothing about the cause. Sometimes that is exactly the right choice.
  It is never the same choice.
ROOT: medicine / pain is the alarm wire and not the damage — the same
  law widened to cover every symptom.
CANNOT: no diagnosis from a symptom by itself — fever names a hundred
  different fires. And no safety in a quietened alarm: comfort
  treatment removes the very signal the illness was being tracked by.
THREAD: engineering (tape over the warning light and the engine still
  seizes), money (covering a shortfall with a loan), cleaning +
  maintenance (mopping under a leaking pipe forever).
ASKED-AS: fever reduce cough syrup painkiller root cause treating symptoms feels better underlying

ESSENCE: diagnosis is not recognition, it is elimination. Start with
  everything that could produce this picture, sort the list by
  dangerous and by likely, then knock items off with questions and
  tests — dangerous ones first, even when they are unlikely.
ROOT: evidence + measurement / a test's job is to split the
  possibilities; strategy + conflict (fight the nearest deadline
  first).
CANNOT: no ruling something in without ruling the killers out — a
  story that fits is not a diagnosis while a fatal option is still
  standing. And no complete list, ever: the practice is a bounded
  search, so the discipline is what you deliberately excluded rather
  than what happened to occur to you.
THREAD: engineering (fault-finding by swapping one part at a time),
  computing (debugging by halving), law (the alternative explanation
  the defence is there to raise).
ASKED-AS: diagnosis tests doctor ruling out could be anything worried possibilities symptoms wrong

ESSENCE: common things are common. A young adult with a headache
  almost always has an ordinary headache; the identical headache in an
  older patient with a known cancer is a different problem entirely.
  The population a patient walked in from is evidence before anyone
  touches them.
ROOT: chance + risk / rare times many equals often, read backwards —
  how often a disease appears in this crowd sets where to look first;
  evidence + measurement.
CANNOT: no diagnosis from the picture alone — identical symptoms in
  different populations mean different things, and ignoring that is
  the error every medical school teaches against: when you hear
  hoofbeats, think horses.
THREAD: evidence + measurement, chance + risk, computing (a rare alert
  on a common event, and the flood of false ones).
ASKED-AS: common cold rare disease googled symptoms worst case zebras horses likely typical

ESSENCE: a test that is ninety-nine percent accurate, used on a
  disease one person in ten thousand has, throws about a hundred false
  alarms for every true one. The result is not the answer. The result
  updates whatever chance the person already had.
ROOT: this file / base rates; chance + risk (the size of each error
  group depends on how many people were in each group to begin with).
CANNOT: no reading a positive without the before-chance — the same
  positive means near-certainty in a symptomatic patient and nearly
  nothing in a random screen. And no free screening: testing enough
  healthy people guarantees a crowd of false alarms, each one buying
  its own biopsy.
THREAD: evidence + measurement, computing (spam filters run on this
  same arithmetic), law (a database match across millions of records).
ASKED-AS: test positive accurate false alarm screening results scared retest odds actually have

ESSENCE: a sugar pill from a confident doctor genuinely reduces pain,
  nausea, and anxiety — measurably, partly through the body's own
  painkillers, and it still works somewhat when the patient is told it
  is sugar. What it does not do is shrink a tumour or set a bone.
ROOT: the mind / perception is construction and expectation steers it
  — so anything with a large felt component moves, and pure mechanics
  does not.
CANNOT: no placebo effect on a hard measured outcome — belief cannot
  lower a bacterial count or close a fracture. And no trial worth
  reading that does not account for it, since the untreated improve on
  their own as well.
THREAD: evidence + measurement (the control group and blinding exist
  because of this), persuasion (the confident delivery is part of the
  dose), the mind.
ASKED-AS: placebo sugar pill fake real effect mind over matter believe healing alternative

ESSENCE: people get better on their own, seek help at their worst, and
  remember cures far more vividly than failures. A trial's job is not
  to show that patients improved — they always do. It is to show they
  improved more than the untreated ones did.
ROOT: evidence + measurement / the untouched twin; chance + risk (the
  extraordinary returns to the ordinary, so improvement after the
  worst day was already scheduled).
CANNOT: no learning a treatment's worth from grateful patients — the
  ones it failed are not in the room. And no comparison without random
  assignment: doctors choosing who gets the drug will unconsciously
  choose the ones likely to do well anyway.
THREAD: evidence + measurement, chance + risk, money (the strategy
  that looks brilliant on the years it was picked for).
ASKED-AS: clinical trial study works for me testimonial anecdote cured natural remedy control randomized

ESSENCE: the same total amount of a drug does different things
  depending on how it is spread out. Medicines work inside a window —
  too low does nothing, too high poisons — and the schedule exists to
  hold the blood level inside that window, not because six hours is a
  tidy number.
ROOT: medicine / the dose makes the poison, with a clock added: the
  body clears a drug continuously, so the level is a balance between
  what arrives and what leaves.
CANNOT: no dose without a schedule — a day's worth swallowed at once
  overshoots into the poison range and is then gone entirely by
  evening, delivering both failures in one day. And no single window
  shared by all bodies: age, weight, kidney and liver set each
  person's clearance.
THREAD: cooking (heat and time trade against each other, but never
  freely), farming (fertiliser split across a season rather than
  dumped in a week), chemistry.
ASKED-AS: every six hours missed dose double up twice daily with food timing pills

ESSENCE: the survivors of a half-finished course are the toughest
  germs in the body — the ones that needed the last three days to
  kill. Stopping when you feel better selects for exactly the
  population you least want to breed, inside you and in everyone you
  meet afterwards.
ROOT: farming / selection shapes lineages — a drug is a selection
  pressure, and a weak one selects instead of killing; medicine
  (infection compounds, so the survivors do not stay few).
CANNOT: no partial killing without selecting — any dose that kills the
  weak and spares the strong hands the whole population to the strong.
  (The right lengths are under active revision and several infections
  do fine on shorter courses; the principle is the pressure, not the
  number — UNSURE on lengths, firm on the mechanism.)
THREAD: farming (pesticide resistance, identical arithmetic), living
  world (evolution watched over a week), strategy + conflict (a
  half-finished campaign leaves the enemy's best troops in the field).
ASKED-AS: antibiotics finish course feel better stop early resistance superbug leftover pills days

ESSENCE: drugs do not only meet the body, they meet each other. Two
  sharing one exit route through the liver back up together; two that
  each thin the blood a little will empty a patient between them. The
  risk climbs faster than the number of pills does.
ROOT: medicine / a drug touches every lock it fits — now with a second
  drug competing for the same locks and the same door out; chance +
  risk (the pairs to check grow as the square of the count).
CANNOT: no list made safe pill by pill — safety is a property of the
  whole set, and a set of ten contains forty-five pairs. And no one
  prescriber holding the whole set: the interaction lives in the gaps
  between them, which is why the pharmacist keeps the list.
THREAD: engineering (two safe subsystems sharing one bus), computing
  (every module fine alone and the system deadlocked), chemistry.
ASKED-AS: mixing medications pharmacist grapefruit alcohol pills together supplements herbal blood thinner

ESSENCE: pain relief is bought, never granted. Strong painkillers slow
  breathing, cloud judgment, stop the bowel, and lose strength as the
  body adapts — while pain left untreated wrecks sleep, healing, and
  the will to move. Neither side of this trade is free.
ROOT: medicine / painkillers edit the report and never the wound;
  medicine (no remedy has exactly one effect).
CANNOT: no relief without cost in either direction — undertreating is
  a choice with its own bill, not a neutral default. And no dose that
  holds forever: adaptation means the same relief costs more over
  time, which is the shape of the whole trap.
THREAD: money (comfort borrowed now at a rising rate), sports (playing
  through pain spends the alarm as fuel), the mind (habit and
  adaptation).
ASKED-AS: painkillers addiction opioid morphine tolerance dependence surgery recovery suffering strong

ESSENCE: of everything an untrained person can fix, the biggest is
  blood loss. Hard direct pressure on the exact spot, held without
  peeking, for ten unbroken minutes. For a limb that will not stop, a
  tourniquet high and tight — losing a limb is a distant second to
  losing the person.
ROOT: medicine / blood is the delivery network and the pump only works
  with something in it; survival (this clock runs in minutes, not
  days).
CANNOT: no clot forming under a dressing that keeps being lifted — a
  peek at two minutes tears the plug out and restarts the clock at
  zero. And no pressure through soaked padding: layers are added,
  never removed.
THREAD: survival, engineering (stop the leak before servicing the
  pump), money (stop the outflow before doing any planning).
ASKED-AS: bleeding stop pressure tourniquet gauze deep cut spurting bandage soaked wound

ESSENCE: airway, then breathing, then circulation — a chain in which
  each link is worthless without the one above it. Perfect chest
  compressions push unoxygenated blood around if the airway is
  blocked. The order is not a preference; it is the plumbing's own
  sequence.
ROOT: medicine / blood delivers what the lungs load, and nothing can
  be circulated that was never taken in; survival (the rule of threes
  at its three-minute end).
CANNOT: no oxygen past a blocked airway and no delivery without a pump
  — no step substitutes for an earlier one, however well it is
  performed. (Massive bleeding now jumps ahead of all three in
  training: an empty circuit cannot carry oxygen either.)
THREAD: survival, engineering (a process chain fails at its first
  broken stage and everything after it is wasted), computing (no point
  optimising downstream of a stall).
ASKED-AS: cpr airway choking breathing not responsive check pulse rescue order unconscious steps

ESSENCE: shock is the delivery system failing — not enough blood
  reaching the tissues, from bleeding, a weak pump, or vessels gone
  slack. The signs run ahead of the collapse: pale cold clammy skin, a
  fast weak pulse, fast breathing, thirst, and confusion.
ROOT: medicine / every cell must sit within a hair's width of the flow
  — shock is that condition ending; the body's thermostats sacrificing
  the skin to hold the core.
CANNOT: no treating shock by treating the skin — warmth and
  reassurance help and only fixing the cause reverses it. And no
  reassurance from an early normal blood pressure: the body
  compensates hard, holds the number, and then falls off a cliff when
  it can no longer.
THREAD: engineering (a pump losing prime — the gauge reads fine until
  it suddenly does not), money (a business paying every bill right up
  to the day it cannot), oceans + weather (a system that holds and
  then breaks fast).
ASKED-AS: shock pale clammy sweaty faint dizzy accident blood loss cold confused pulse

ESSENCE: a burn keeps burning after the source is gone — heat held in
  the tissue drives the damage deeper for minutes. Cool running water
  for twenty minutes is the treatment. Ice, butter, and popped
  blisters each make it worse, in roughly that order of certainty.
ROOT: physics / heat flows down a gradient until the gradient is gone,
  so cooling is removing stored heat rather than soothing; medicine
  (past about 42°C proteins cook, and that door is one-way).
CANNOT: no undoing cooked protein — the tissue is not injured but
  changed, so treatment can only limit the depth. And no cooling with
  ice: freezing damages the tissue you are saving and shuts down the
  very blood flow that carries heat away.
THREAD: cooking (the one-way door, applied to the wrong meat),
  building + materials, physics.
ASKED-AS: burn cold water ice butter blister pop sunburn scald kitchen hospital

ESSENCE: a splint heals nothing. It stops the broken ends moving, and
  moving ends are what tear the vessels and nerves running past them —
  the damage after the break is often larger than the break. Splint it
  where it lies, capturing the joint above and the joint below.
ROOT: medicine / bone is the frame and the plumbing runs beside it;
  engineering (a fractured member concentrates every bit of motion at
  the break).
CANNOT: no immobilising a bone without capturing both neighbouring
  joints — leave one free and the ends still move. And no
  straightening a cold pulseless limb without training: the manoeuvre
  that restores blood flow is the one that can finish a vessel.
THREAD: engineering (splinting a cracked beam before moving it), craft
  (a clamp holds while the glue sets — motion is the enemy of every
  joint), building + materials.
ASKED-AS: broken arm splint sling cast move straighten swelling deformed leg fracture support

ESSENCE: a concussion is a brain injury with a normal scan — the
  damage is in function, not in a picture, and consciousness need
  never be lost. The danger is a second hit before the first has
  healed, which makes the rule simple and unpopular: out of the game
  today.
ROOT: medicine / the brain floats and can be thrown against its own
  case — energy delivered, structure intact; the mind (judgment is
  among the first things lost, so the patient is the worst available
  judge).
CANNOT: no concussion ruled out by a clear scan or by keeping someone
  awake — the ordinary tools do not see it. And no self-assessment:
  the injured organ is the one being questioned, which is why the
  decision must be taken away from the player.
THREAD: sports (why the rules changed and who changed them),
  engineering (fatigue damage invisible until the failure), law
  (return-to-play statutes).
ASKED-AS: concussion head hit helmet dizzy blacked out symptoms return play football headache

ESSENCE: both ends kill by one route — the thermostat losing its
  argument. In heat the warning is sweating that stops and confusion
  starting; in cold it is shivering that stops and confusion starting.
  When the body quits regulating, minutes matter.
ROOT: medicine / the body is a thousand thermostats and these are the
  two failures of the temperature one; survival (water and wind steal
  heat far faster than still air).
CANNOT: no heat illness beaten by willpower or fitness — the fittest
  people die of it, in training, every summer. And no fast rewarming
  of a deeply cold body by fire or hot bath: cold blood washing back
  from the limbs to the core can stop the heart.
THREAD: survival, physics (heat flows to cold, faster by touch and
  faster still by moving flow), farming (livestock lost the same two
  ways).
ASKED-AS: heat stroke exhaustion sweating stopped hypothermia shivering frostbite warming hot car confused

ESSENCE: drowning is silent and fast. There is no waving and no
  shouting — the mouth is busy and the arms are pressing down.
  Children go under in seconds, in inches of water, feet from adults
  who heard nothing at all. Rescue order: reach, throw, row, and go in
  last.
ROOT: medicine / the three-minute clock; premise — a person fighting
  for breath cannot spend air on a call for help.
CANNOT: no drowning that announces itself — the thrashing picture
  everyone is watching for does not occur. And no safe untrained
  swimming rescue: a panicking person climbs their rescuer, and the
  count becomes two.
THREAD: survival, power (a fence around a pool saves more children
  than a lifeguard, because it works while nobody is watching), chance
  + risk (rare per pool-hour, common per nation-summer).
ASKED-AS: drowning kids pool bathtub silent quiet lifeguard rescue swimming splashing seconds shallow

ESSENCE: the old advice was to make the person vomit. It is wrong. A
  corrosive burns the throat twice on the way back and a fuel or
  solvent goes into the lungs. Read the container, call the poison
  line, keep the packet — the right answer depends entirely on what it
  was.
ROOT: medicine / the dose makes the poison — and the correct response
  is substance-specific, so the identification is the treatment
  decision.
CANNOT: no general antidote and no universal first move — the single
  instruction that fits every poison is the phone call. And no
  reassurance from a well-looking child: several common poisonings,
  including some ordinary painkillers and antifreeze, look fine for a
  day and are lethal by then.
THREAD: chemistry, law (child-resistant caps as prevention engineered
  into the object), cooking (the same cupboard holds both).
ASKED-AS: swallowed poison child bleach pills overdose vomit charcoal poison control label

ESSENCE: move a casualty only when leaving them is more dangerous than
  moving them — fire, water, traffic, or no other way to keep them
  breathing. Otherwise leave them where they are. A spine injury is
  made permanent by the well-meaning drag more often than by the
  crash.
ROOT: this file / immobilisation, at whole-body scale; right + wrong
  (acting against allowing, argued at a roadside in ten seconds).
CANNOT: no assessing a spine at the scene — an untrained person cannot
  rule it out, so the working assumption has to be that it is there.
  And no moving someone gently enough to make it safe: the danger is
  the bending, and care does not remove it.
THREAD: survival (lost means stop — the same urge to move that has to
  be resisted), engineering (do not run a damaged machine to a better
  workshop), strategy + conflict.
ASKED-AS: move injured person car accident neck back spine drag safety helmet roadside

ESSENCE: an untrained person can do four things extremely well: call
  for help, stop bleeding, keep an airway open, and press on a chest.
  Everything past that — resetting bones, giving drugs, diagnosing —
  has a worse record in amateur hands than doing nothing at all.
ROOT: evidence + measurement / worth is measured, and confidence is
  not competence; medicine (half of the field's real knowledge is when
  not to act).
CANNOT: no learning these four from reading — they are physical
  skills, and hands that have never practised do them badly under
  adrenaline. And no shame in the limit: the person who calls early
  and holds pressure outperforms the improviser at every level of
  injury.
THREAD: right + wrong (duty and its limits, at a roadside), craft (the
  apprentice who knows the edge of their own skill), flight
  (checklists exist because trained people freeze too).
ASKED-AS: first aid what can i do call ambulance untrained help hurt someone scared

ESSENCE: when casualties outnumber hands, treating in order of arrival
  or of loudness kills people. Triage sorts by who is dying now and
  can still be saved — which means walking past the screaming to reach
  the silent, and marking some as beyond help.
ROOT: strategy + conflict / fight the nearest deadline; this file (the
  loudest symptom is not the largest danger).
CANNOT: no maximum survival without leaving someone untreated — with
  fewer hands than patients, treating everyone equally treats nobody
  in time. And no sorting by sympathy: the quiet ones are often quiet
  because they are the worst.
THREAD: right + wrong (a tragic choice made correctly still leaves a
  residue), money (a limited budget spent where it changes outcomes
  rather than where the noise is), strategy + conflict.
ASKED-AS: triage emergency room waiting many injured disaster who first tag screaming quiet

ESSENCE: a wound's fate is decided by what stays inside it. Flushing
  with a lot of clean water beats any antiseptic scrub, and closing a
  dirty wound seals the germs in with a warm food supply. Some wounds
  are meant to be left open.
ROOT: this file / hand washing's principle applied to a wound — the
  aim is removal; medicine (infection compounds, and a sealed pocket
  is the ideal place for it).
CANNOT: no disinfecting away what has not been flushed out — strong
  antiseptics kill living tissue as readily as bacteria and leave the
  debris where it was. And no safe closure over contamination: the
  closing is what makes the infection dangerous.
THREAD: cleaning + maintenance (rinse first, polish later), craft (a
  joint glued over dirt fails at the dirt), farming (damp grain sealed
  in a silo — the same warm dark growth).
ASKED-AS: cleaning cut wound peroxide alcohol water rinse dirt stitches infected open bandage

ESSENCE: the small deep wounds are the dangerous ones. A puncture or a
  bite carries germs deep and the skin closes over the top — an animal
  bite on a hand and a nail through a boot both need looking at long
  before they look bad.
ROOT: this file / it is what stays inside that does the damage;
  medicine (a deep sealed pocket is the ideal culture, warm and out of
  reach).
CANNOT: no judging a puncture by its opening — the visible hole
  reports nothing about the depth or what went in with it. And no
  waiting for signs in two cases: tetanus and rabies are prevented in
  the window after exposure and are close to untreatable once symptoms
  start.
THREAD: farming (soil-borne, which is exactly why the boot wound
  matters), living world (a cat's teeth are needles that inject and
  then close), medicine.
ASKED-AS: dog bite cat scratch rusty nail deep puncture tetanus shot rabies infected

ESSENCE: for a blocked heart artery and a blocked brain artery, the
  treatment is a clock. Tissue dies by the minute while it waits, and
  the hospital's best options only work inside a window measured in
  hours. Waiting in order to seem calm costs muscle and brain.
ROOT: medicine / blood delivers, and everything downstream of a
  blocked pipe dies in minutes; evidence + measurement (a treatment's
  effect collapses outside its window).
CANNOT: no recovering tissue that is already dead — the treatment
  saves what is still starving, never what is gone. And no
  waiting-and-seeing with these two: the symptoms often ease while the
  damage continues underneath.
THREAD: engineering (a fire's first minute is worth its next hour),
  money (a margin call answered late), strategy + conflict.

ESSENCE: the test is whether they can make a noise. Coughing or
  speaking means air is still moving, and the best help is to leave
  them to it. Silence with a hand at the throat means nothing is

CANNOT: no useful intervention in someone who is still coughing — a
  slap or a thrust can convert a partial blockage into a complete one.
  And no time to go and fetch help once it has gone silent: the
  three-minute clock has already started.
THREAD: survival (the rule of threes at its shortest end), flight (a
  blocked intake at full power), engineering.

ESSENCE: a severe allergic reaction is the defence force overreacting
  until the airway swells and the vessels leak the blood pressure
  away. The injector into the thigh is the treatment, it works within
  minutes, and giving it early is far safer than waiting to be
  certain.
ROOT: medicine / the defence force runs on me-or-not-me and can be

CANNOT: no waiting out a reaction that has reached the breathing or
  the blood pressure — it accelerates, and antihistamines are far too

  later, so the ambulance is part of the treatment and not a
  formality.
THREAD: chemistry (a lock and key recognised far too
  enthusiastically), law (schools required to stock injectors —
  prevention written as policy), medicine.

ESSENCE: a diabetic who is confused, sweating, and aggressive is far
  more often low on sugar than high — and low kills in minutes where
  high takes hours. If they can swallow, sugar is the safe guess,
  because being wrong about it does almost no harm.
ROOT: medicine / the brain runs on sugar delivered continuously and
  stores none of its own; this file (act on the fastest killer first).
CANNOT: no telling the two apart reliably at a roadside — which is why
  the rule is built on the lopsided costs of being wrong rather than
  on a diagnosis. And nothing by mouth for someone who cannot swallow:

THREAD: chance + risk (choose by the cost of the error, not by the
  odds alone), farming (an engine out of the one fuel it cannot

ESSENCE: the number on the thermometer is a poor guide. What matters

THREAD: evidence + measurement (a number with no meaning attached is

ESSENCE: for centuries children died of diarrhoea, losing water faster
