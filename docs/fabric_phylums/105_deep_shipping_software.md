# 105 DEEP SHIPPING SOFTWARE — getting it into the world and keeping it alive

ESSENCE: a misunderstanding at the start survives every test,
  every review, every careful hour. The team builds the wrong
  thing perfectly, and no amount of quality catches a right answer
  to the wrong question.
ROOT: evidence / a check can only measure against the standard it
  was handed.
CANNOT: no test for "is this what they needed" — tests compare
  the code to the stated want, never the want to the world. No
  cheap discovery of a wrong requirement, because the discovery
  comes from use, and use comes last.
THREAD: building (a house built exactly to the plan for a
  different lot), medicine (a flawless operation on the wrong
  knee), translation (a perfect sentence answering another
  question).
ASKED-AS: wrong thing built not what i wanted asked for misunderstood needs project waste

ESSENCE: people cannot describe a thing they have never used, but
  they can judge one the instant they see it. Wants are found by
  reaction, not by interview — so the fastest road to the real
  requirement is something real enough to be criticised.
ROOT: the mind / recognition is far easier than recall, and much
  more reliable.
CANNOT: no complete requirement gathered by asking alone. No
  honest reaction to a description; only to a thing they can push
  on.
THREAD: tailoring (the fitting tells what the measurements
  cannot), cooking (nobody orders by reading the recipe), music
  (the rough recording settles arguments the description
  started).
ASKED-AS: know it when i see show me mockup try out feedback describe cannot say

ESSENCE: build the smallest complete thing that actually runs end
  to end, then grow it. A working small system teaches what no
  document can, and the large systems that work are nearly always
  grown from small ones that worked.
ROOT: this file / wants are found by reaction, and only a running
  thing can be reacted to.
CANNOT: no large working system designed whole from nothing — the
  parts that were never run hide their disagreements until they
  meet. No learning from a piece that cannot yet be used by
  anyone.
THREAD: the body (grown from one cell, never assembled), farming
  (a seedling, not a delivered tree), building (a shed that
  stands teaches you the house).
ASKED-AS: start small first version simple thing that works grow later minimum build big

ESSENCE: planning pays where the ground is known. Past a certain
  uncertainty, every extra hour of plan is fiction, and the
  cheapest way forward is a short loop: try, look, adjust. The
  length of the loop, not the depth of the plan, sets how fast
  you learn.
ROOT: chance / a forecast is only as good as the stability of
  what it forecasts.
CANNOT: no plan that outruns its own assumptions — a schedule
  built on unknowns is a guess with dates written on it. No
  learning faster than your slowest loop, however clever the
  people in it.
THREAD: navigation (fog means short legs and frequent fixes),
  science (experiment settles what argument cannot), cooking
  (taste and adjust when the ingredient is new).
ASKED-AS: plan ahead or just try short steps adjust unknown figure it out going

ESSENCE: estimates are wrong in one direction. We picture the
  path we imagined — not the interruptions, the surprises, the
  task that turns out to be three tasks. The error is a bias, not
  noise, so it never averages out.
ROOT: the mind / imagining the work is exactly the act that omits
  the unimagined parts.
CANNOT: no unbiased guess produced by picturing the job. No
  fixing it by adding a bit: the correction is a multiplier on
  the whole, and past records beat fresh confidence every time.
THREAD: travel (every journey takes longer than the map says),
  building (renovation and its surprises behind the plaster),
  homework (the last hour that becomes four).
ASKED-AS: how long will it take estimate deadline late always longer than expected guess

ESSENCE: adding people to a late project makes it later. The
  newcomers must be taught by the very people who are already
  behind, and each new head adds a line of talk to everyone
  already there.
ROOT: people together / connections between n people grow as
  pairs, not as persons.
CANNOT: no speed from a hand that must first be taught by someone
  who has no time to teach. No shrinking the talk once the heads
  are added; the lines exist whether or not they are used.
THREAD: birth (nine mothers, one month — no), kitchens (a new
  cook in the middle of service), sport (a substitute who does
  not know the plays).
ASKED-AS: add more people help late project behind hire team faster slower teaching newcomer

ESSENCE: every addition sounds small on its own, and none of them
  arrives carrying the schedule. Scope grows by single reasonable
  steps until the thing that was nearly finished is far away
  again.
ROOT: this file / estimates omit what was not pictured, and each
  addition is pictured alone.
CANNOT: no finishing a target that moves — a project with no edge
  has no end, only an abandonment. No addition that is free: each
  must be built, tested, explained, and carried for the life of
  the system.
THREAD: packing (one more thing, and one more), building (while
  we are in here, we may as well), meetings (each new point is
  fair, and the meeting never ends).
ASKED-AS: one more thing just add feature grew keeps growing never finished endless extra

ESSENCE: the rewrite from scratch promises everything the old
  system lacks and quietly inherits none of what it learned. The
  old code's ugliness is largely the record of real cases met;
  the new one must meet them all again, from zero, while the old
  one keeps running.
ROOT: this file / a working small thing grows into a working
  large one, and a rewrite starts from neither.
CANNOT: no rewrite that carries forward the lessons nobody wrote
  down. No pause in the world while you rebuild: the old system
  keeps gaining features you must now also match.
THREAD: building (demolishing the only bridge before the new one
  stands), farming (ploughing in a crop for one you have not
  grown), language (a reformed spelling nobody switches to).
ASKED-AS: start over from scratch rewrite everything new version never ships second system old

ESSENCE: "done" is a claim by the person who made it. "Shipped"
  means somebody who is not you is using it and it is holding.
  Between those two words sits every assumption that was true
  only on the maker's machine.
ROOT: evidence / a self-report is not a measurement.
CANNOT: no value from work that never reaches anyone — unshipped
  work is pure cost. No knowing a thing works from inside the
  making of it; only use decides.
THREAD: cooking (plated is not served), theatre (a dress
  rehearsal is not an opening night), building (built is not the
  same as moved into).
ASKED-AS: done finished nearly there works on my machine shipped released using it real

ESSENCE: many hands write one text at once. Version control keeps
  each hand's work whole, records who changed what and why, and
  turns the joining of two futures into something done
  deliberately instead of by accident.
ROOT: software development / every change kept with its history;
  people together / shared work needs a shared record.
CANNOT: no parallel work without a way to reconcile it; the only
  alternative is one person at a time. No recovering why a line
  exists from the change alone — the record shows what moved,
  never the reason, unless someone wrote it.
THREAD: surveying (two field books reconciled into one map),
  medicine (a chart every shift writes on), theatre (every
  department's notes merged into one prompt book).
ASKED-AS: many people same file merge conflict who changed what history team edits together

ESSENCE: a branch is a private future. The longer it lives, the
  further it drifts from the future everyone else is living in,
  and the cost of reunion grows faster than the days do. Short
  branches merge; old ones fight.
ROOT: this file / two hands on one text must be reconciled, and
  the reconciling grows with the difference.
CANNOT: no cheap merge of two long divergences — the conflicts
  are not text, they are two different intentions about the same
  thing. No branch that costs nothing; every day apart is a day
  of drift.
THREAD: language (dialects splitting), families (relatives who
  stop visiting), roads (a detour that becomes its own town).
ASKED-AS: branch merge conflicts long time apart diverged catch up reunite separate copy painful

ESSENCE: review catches some faults, but its larger work is that
  a second person now knows this code exists and how it thinks.
  The house style spreads, the newcomer learns from real
  examples, and no corner of the system belongs to one head.
ROOT: teaching / explaining and being explained to are both
  learning.
CANNOT: no shared ownership of code only one person has ever
  read. No treating review as the safety net — it is a lantern,
  not a sieve, and a team that leans on it finds the net empty
  when it matters.
THREAD: medicine (the second opinion that also trains the junior),
  craft (apprentices watching the master's hands), teaching
  (marking teaches the marker).
ASKED-AS: code review someone else reads checks comments learn team knows nitpicking second pair

ESSENCE: join everyone's work to the shared line constantly, and
  let a machine build and test it on every change. Integration
  pain grows with how long it is postponed, so the cure is
  frequency, not effort.
ROOT: this file / branches drift, and drift is what makes joining
  expensive.
CANNOT: no "it works on my machine" surviving a shared, honest
  build. No big-bang integration that goes well — weeks of errors
  arrive at once, tangled, and none of them can be isolated.
THREAD: cleaning (daily tidying versus the annual clear-out),
  accounting (books balanced daily, not in a panic at year end),
  music (rehearsing together often, not once before the concert).
ASKED-AS: build broken every change tested automatically merge often machine checks daily integrate together

ESSENCE: the same source must produce the same program every
  time, on any machine. Where the build depends on what happened
  to be installed that day, nobody can say what is actually
  running, and no failure can be traced to its source.
ROOT: this file / a self-report is not a measurement; the machine
  must be able to repeat the act.
CANNOT: no reasoning about a program you cannot rebuild. No
  bisecting history, no rollback, and no honest security answer
  when the same source gives different programs on different
  days.
THREAD: cooking (a recipe that depends on which shop you used),
  science (a result nobody can repeat), manufacture (parts that
  fit only if made in the same shed).
ASKED-AS: building twice gives identical result differs each time machine dependent recipe locked versions

ESSENCE: most of what you ship was written by strangers, and
  their code runs with your code's privileges. Every library is a
  door into your system left open by someone you never met and
  cannot call.
ROOT: software development / a program stands on other programs,
  and what it stands on can shift.
CANNOT: no trusting what you did not read, and no reading all of
  it — so the discipline is naming exact versions, changing them
  deliberately, and watching for known holes. No security in your
  own code alone.
THREAD: food (a supplier's supplier in your kitchen), building (a
  batch of steel you certify by paperwork), medicine (an
  ingredient sourced from three countries back).
ASKED-AS: libraries someone else wrote update broke security patch versions pinned trust strangers code

ESSENCE: the copy is never the original. It has less data, fewer
  users, kinder timing and cleaner machines. A test environment
  proves that a thing can work; only the live one proves that it
  does.
ROOT: evidence / a model is judged by what it left out.
CANNOT: no test environment identical to the live one — if it
  were identical, it would be the live one, load and history and
  all. No confidence from a rehearsal that had no audience.
THREAD: flight (a simulator cannot make you afraid), theatre (an
  empty house changes every pause), medicine (trial volunteers
  are not the population).
ASKED-AS: worked in testing broke live different environment staging real users load practice copy

ESSENCE: a system that is comfortable at ten does not merely slow
  at ten thousand — it changes character. Queues form, caches
  stop helping, and the part that was never the bottleneck
  becomes the whole problem.
ROOT: computer science / cost grows with size in shapes, and
  shapes have knees.
CANNOT: no learning a system's behaviour under load from its
  behaviour when idle. No smooth degradation that was not
  designed: an overloaded system either sheds work on purpose or
  collapses by accident.
THREAD: roads (a junction that works until the school run),
  shops (a queue that forms suddenly, not gradually), bridges
  (rated for a crowd, not for one person times a thousand).
ASKED-AS: worked with ten broke with thousands load busy traffic spike capacity how many

ESSENCE: every release is the moment a working world becomes an
  untested one. The aim is not courage but boredom — small,
  frequent, automatic, reversible releases, so that nobody's
  heart rate changes when one happens.
ROOT: this file / a rehearsed act is performed reliably; an
  unusual act is performed badly.
CANNOT: no safety in a rare, large release — rarity loses the
  practice, size hides which change caused the harm. No reliable
  act that only one person knows how to perform.
THREAD: flight (checklists make the extraordinary routine),
  surgery (rehearsed steps and counted instruments), fire drills
  (the boredom is the whole point).
ASKED-AS: deploy release push live scary friday night routine boring small often risky moment

ESSENCE: at any moment you must be able to answer, exactly, what
  version is running where — stamped into the thing itself and
  visible from outside. Everything else in an emergency depends
  on that one answer.
ROOT: this file / the same source must give the same program;
  identity is what makes that checkable.
CANNOT: no debugging a live system whose contents are unknown —
  you would be reading one program and running another. No safe
  rollback without knowing what you are rolling back from.
THREAD: manufacture (a batch number on every part), medicine (the
  label on the vial actually in the tray), shipping (a manifest
  that matches the hold).
ASKED-AS: which version is live running now stamp deployed unsure what is actually there

ESSENCE: what differs between one place and another — addresses,
  limits, keys, names — belongs outside the program, handed in at
  the start. Bake them in and the thing you tested is not the
  thing you shipped.
ROOT: software development / what a part is handed can be
  chosen; what it contains cannot.
CANNOT: no one artefact serving many places while each place's
  details are inside it. No secret kept once it is written into
  the source — the history keeps it forever, and everyone with
  the code has it.
THREAD: machines (one appliance, a voltage switch), post (the
  same form, a different address block), theatre (one script,
  local names inserted at the read-through).
ASKED-AS: settings passwords keys config file environment different per place hard coded baked in

ESSENCE: the fastest fix for a bad release is the last good one.
  Being able to go back — quickly, without thought, without
  cleverness — turns a disaster into an inconvenience. It must be
  built and rehearsed before it is needed.
ROOT: this file / deployment is a risk event, and every risk
  event needs an exit.
CANNOT: no rollback of a change that rewrote data on its way
  through; the code returns, the changed records do not. No
  untested return path — a rollback first attempted during an
  emergency is a second emergency.
THREAD: climbing (never go up what you cannot come down), sailing
  (an anchorage you can leave in any wind), surgery (the
  reversible step taken first).
ASKED-AS: undo the release go back previous version revert broken update turn off quickly

ESSENCE: changing the shape of stored data is the one step that
  does not undo. Code can be replaced in a second; a million
  records rewritten cannot be un-rewritten. So migrations go in
  stages — add the new, write both, move readers, then remove the
  old.
ROOT: this file / rollback is the exit, and data changes are
  where the exit is walled off.
CANNOT: no going back through a migration that dropped what it
  replaced. No safe single-step change of shape while old and new
  code are both running — and during any real deployment, both
  are running.
THREAD: building (moving load onto the new beam before cutting
  the old), language (two spellings accepted during a reform),
  transport (the new line opened before the old one closes).
ASKED-AS: changing the data shape upgrade cannot go back old records converted risky step

ESSENCE: a switch that separates shipping the code from turning
  it on. Now the release itself is small and dull, the new thing
  can wake for a few people first, and switching it off takes a
  second instead of a deployment.
ROOT: this file / deployment should be boring, and a flag removes
  the interesting part from the deployment.
CANNOT: no flag without an end date — each one doubles the paths
  through the code, and a hundred forgotten flags is a maze
  nobody can reason about. No trust in a path nobody has run in
  months.
THREAD: theatre (a set built behind a closed curtain), plumbing
  (a valve rather than a cut pipe), electricity (one breaker per
  circuit).
ASKED-AS: switch turn on off new feature some users try hidden behind toggle release

ESSENCE: give the new thing to a few first, watch, then widen.
  Most faults announce themselves in minutes under real use, and
  the only question that matters is how many people were standing
  in the blast radius when they did.
ROOT: this file / only live use proves a thing works, so buy that
  proof in small amounts.
CANNOT: no learning from a gradual rollout without watching
  something during it; slow and blind is just slow. No small
  blast radius for a change to shared data — some changes are all
  or nothing, and those need the other tools.
THREAD: medicine (dose escalation in trials), farming (the new
  seed tried on one field), bridges (load tested with lorries
  before the public drives across).
ASKED-AS: few users first small group slowly wider watch then everyone canary blast radius

ESSENCE: a running system is opaque. You can see only what it was
  built to show — counts, timings, states, and the trail of one
  request through many parts. That showing has to be designed in;
  it cannot be added from outside afterwards.
ROOT: evidence / nothing can be measured that was not made
  measurable.
CANNOT: no seeing inside a system that reports nothing; guessing
  is all that is left. No stitching one user's journey across
  many machines without a shared mark carried through every hop.
THREAD: medicine (instruments on the patient, not on the ward
  door), flight (the recorder installed before the flight), ships
  (gauges on the engine you cannot open at sea).
ASKED-AS: cannot see inside running system what is it doing built to show numbers

ESSENCE: wake a person only for what a person must fix now. An
  alert that needs no action teaches everyone to ignore alerts,
  and then the one that mattered arrives in a crowd of noise and
  is treated exactly like the noise.
ROOT: the mind / attention that is spent falsely is not available
  truly.
CANNOT: no attention that survives repeated false alarms — a
  pager that cries wolf is worse than no pager, because it also
  costs sleep. No alerting on every possible cause; alert on the
  symptom the user feels, then go looking for causes.
THREAD: fire (the detector everyone took the battery out of),
  medicine (a monitor beeping constantly gets muted), farming (a
  fence alarm that fires for every leaf).
ASKED-AS: alert alarm woke me up noise ignored pager warning too many false monitoring

ESSENCE: when something goes wrong in the live world you cannot
  go back and watch it. What was written down at the time is the
  entire evidence — and what was not written did not happen, as
  far as anyone will ever be able to say.
ROOT: evidence / testimony recorded at the time outranks memory
  afterwards.
CANNOT: no reconstructing an event from a record that never
  mentioned it. No forensics at all without a shared identifier
  joining one request's trail across every part it touched.
THREAD: navigation (the ship's log is the whole record), law (the
  transcript, not the recollection), medicine (undocumented care
  did not occur).
ASKED-AS: logs what happened record trace history evidence find out afterwards printed messages missing

ESSENCE: the value of a written procedure is measured at three in
  the morning, by a tired person who did not build the thing.
  Every step spelled out, every command copyable, every decision
  named — because judgement is the first thing exhaustion takes.
ROOT: teaching / a procedure is only as good as the least
  prepared person who must follow it.
CANNOT: no calm response improvised from first principles at
  night. No useful procedure that was never walked through while
  nothing was wrong; the gaps are only visible in the walking.
THREAD: flight (emergency checklists read aloud), lifeboats
  (drills in daylight), hospitals (the crash trolley in the same
  order on every ward).
ASKED-AS: what to do at three in the morning steps written down tired stranger

ESSENCE: a design does not stop at the diagram. It decides who is
  woken, how often, and for how many years. A choice that saves a
  week of building and costs one call a night is not a saving; it
  is a loan against people.
ROOT: this file / what ships must be kept alive, and keeping
  alive is done by humans.
CANNOT: no honest cost for a design that leaves out the nights it
  will cost. No sustained care from an exhausted team — the
  system's reliability is capped by the health of the people
  holding it up.
THREAD: farming (the livestock you choose decides your mornings),
  ships (a design needing constant hand-steering), pets (a puppy
  is a decade of walks).
ASKED-AS: on call night woken weekend support maintenance burden tired team every design costs

ESSENCE: perfect uptime is not a goal, it is a refusal to choose.
  Each further nine costs more than the last, and the money buys
  reliability the user often cannot feel. Name the target, then
  spend the difference on what people actually notice.
ROOT: money / every purchase is measured against what the same
  money would buy elsewhere.
CANNOT: no system that never fails; the parts it stands on fail,
  and it stands on many. No unlimited reliability budget either —
  the effort spent chasing the last fraction is effort not spent
  on the thing itself.
THREAD: engineering (safety factors are chosen numbers, not
  infinity), medicine (screening everyone for everything harms
  more than it saves), building (a house is not built for the
  thousand-year storm).
ASKED-AS: never goes down perfect uptime how reliable enough cost of nines target budget

ESSENCE: when a review hunts for a culprit, people learn to say
  less next time, and the real cause stays hidden. Ask instead
  what made the mistake easy to make and hard to notice. The
  person was the last defence, not the hole.
ROOT: people together / information flows only where it is safe
  to give.
CANNOT: no truth from people who expect punishment for telling
  it. No system made safe by everyone promising to be careful —
  carefulness is a mood, not a control.
THREAD: flight (accident reporting protected on purpose),
  medicine (near-miss registers), sport (reviewing the play, not
  the player).
ASKED-AS: whose fault blame incident outage who broke it review honest report punished hiding

ESSENCE: a review after failure exists to produce a change.
  Understanding is the middle of the work, not the end. If
  nothing about the system is different afterwards, the outage
  bought nothing and will simply be purchased again at full
  price.
ROOT: evidence / a finding that changes no action was not used.
CANNOT: no learning without an owned, dated change to something
  real. No repeat prevented by "we will be more careful", which
  is a feeling with no owner and no deadline.
THREAD: medicine (an autopsy that changes practice), engineering
  (bridges redesigned from the wreck), law (rules written from
  the disaster's own record).
ASKED-AS: postmortem writeup lessons learned after outage change what actually different next time nothing

ESSENCE: a backup nobody has ever restored is not a backup, it is
  a hope with a filename. The only proof is a full restore,
  performed on a schedule, timed — because how long it takes is
  half of what you need to know.
ROOT: evidence / an untested claim is not a fact, however
  reasonable.
CANNOT: no recovery from a copy that was never read back — silent
  corruption, missing pieces and wrong keys are all discovered at
  the worst moment. No restore faster than practised; the first
  attempt is always the slowest.
THREAD: lifeboats (launched in drills, not first at sea), fire
  (extinguishers tested and dated), farming (seed corn tested for
  germination before the season).
ASKED-AS: backup restore tested actually recover lost data copy never tried worked when needed

ESSENCE: writing decays the moment the code moves beneath it, and
  nothing warns you. Only documentation people actually follow
  gets corrected — the unread page becomes confidently wrong,
  which is worse than a blank one.
ROOT: this file / a fact with two homes drifts, and a document is
  a second home for what the code says.
CANNOT: no document that stays true without a reason to be read.
  No trusting an old page whose claims nothing checks — so prefer
  what runs: examples that execute, tests that must pass.
THREAD: maps (a road atlas ages badly), medicine (a label for a
  reformulated drug), museums (a sign left when the exhibit
  moved).
ASKED-AS: docs out of date wrong instructions stale nobody updated manual old guide misleading

ESSENCE: the first page answers four questions: what this is, how
  to run it, how to change it safely, and where to look next. It
  is written for someone who knows nothing — including you, a
  year from now.
ROOT: teaching / the entry is where the most people are, so it
  earns the most care.
CANNOT: no honest entry page that cannot be followed on a clean
  machine, step by step, to a running program. No substitute for
  it living in someone's head; heads leave, and take the first
  step with them.
THREAD: buildings (the lobby directory), travel (the trailhead
  sign with the map and the warnings), books (a preface saying
  who this is for).
ASKED-AS: readme first page how to run this what is it getting started new here

ESSENCE: how long a capable newcomer needs to make a safe first
  change is the most honest measurement of a system's design. It
  cannot be argued with and cannot be faked, and it prices every
  hidden rule at once.
ROOT: evidence / the measurement nobody can massage is the one
  worth keeping.
CANNOT: no clean design that takes months to enter — the delay IS
  the mess, measured in days. No measuring it without newcomers;
  the fluent cannot feel friction they have long since adapted
  to.
THREAD: cities (a stranger finding their way without asking),
  kitchens (a new cook finding the pans), machines (controls that
  explain themselves).
ASKED-AS: new person first week how long to learn confusing hard to start onboarding

ESSENCE: once someone builds on what you published, changing it
  breaks their work, not yours. Their reliance turned your
  convenience into a promise — and the promise is now a cost you
  carry whether you agreed to it or not.
ROOT: law / publication plus reliance creates an obligation.
CANNOT: no unbreaking someone else's system from your side; you
  cannot reach into their code and repair what your change broke.
  No version of "but we announced it" that un-breaks something
  already running.
THREAD: money (a currency cannot be quietly redefined), roads (a
  bridge's weight limit that whole fleets were built around), law
  (contracts binding across a change of management).
ASKED-AS: old version still works broke everyone update breaks others promise depend on us

ESSENCE: to retire something honestly: announce it, provide a
  working replacement, give real time, warn people where they
  actually stand, and then genuinely remove it. Warnings that
  never come true teach everyone that your warnings mean nothing.
ROOT: law / notice must precede enforcement, and enforcement must
  eventually come.
CANNOT: no honest retirement without a path to somewhere else —
  "stop using it" is not a migration. No credibility surviving a
  deadline that passes with nothing happening; the next notice is
  already ignored.
THREAD: transport (a line closed with a replacement bus and a
  date), medicine (a drug withdrawn alongside its substitute),
  buildings (the old exit closed only once the new one opens).
ASKED-AS: removing old feature warning notice replacement deadline retire stop supporting migrate away honestly

ESSENCE: every feature shipped is a feature answered for,
  forever: questions, edge cases, bug reports, and its own small
  weight in every future change. The build is the cheap part; the
  keeping is the rest of its life.
ROOT: software development / every added piece must pay rent, and
  the rent is collected for years.
CANNOT: no shipping without accepting the tail — a feature used
  by three people still gets three people's questions and all of
  the maintenance. No estimating a feature honestly by its build
  time alone.
THREAD: farming (every animal kept is fed all winter), pets (the
  cost is the years, not the purchase), buildings (an extension
  is heated and repaired for its whole life).
ASKED-AS: every feature shipped supported forever questions bugs help requests cost after release keeps

ESSENCE: security is a running fight, not a box ticked. Your
  system holds still while attackers, tools and newly discovered
  weaknesses all move — so something that was safe last year is
  not safe now, without a single line having changed.
ROOT: strategy / an adaptive opponent makes any fixed defence
  temporary.
CANNOT: no "secured" as a finished state, only "not known to be
  broken today". No strength above the weakest way in; the
  attacker chooses the door, and they only need one.
THREAD: farming (pests evolve past every treatment), the body
  (immunity must keep meeting new strains), locks (every lock is
  eventually picked, and replaced).
ASKED-AS: security hacked safe patch updates attackers keep trying never finished ongoing weakest door

ESSENCE: software with no owner does not stand still; it rots.
  The ground beneath it moves, its dependencies age, its
  knowledge leaves with the people, and one day the thing still
  running is a thing nobody understands or dares touch.
ROOT: software development / a program stands on ground that
  shifts, and standing still is therefore falling behind.
CANNOT: no unattended system that stays healthy. No safe change
  to an orphaned system later — by then the people who knew what
  it must not do are gone, and only the code remains, silent
  about its reasons.
THREAD: houses (an empty house decays faster than a lived-in
