# 90 DEEP MAKING THINGS — how anything gets built
No new laws here. This is the argument between what a thing must
do, what materials will actually do, and what a shop can actually
make — settled again for every object, always by paying somebody.

ESSENCE: strong, light, and cheap come bundled in every material
  and are never sold apart — steel is strong, heavy and cheap;
  titanium strong, light and dear; wood cheap, light and weak. You
  shop from a catalogue nature wrote, not from a wish list.
ROOT: chemistry / properties come from the innards — you buy what
  the atoms already agreed to, not what the drawing wanted.
THREAD: money (fast, good, cheap — the same three-cornered menu),
  flight (this list read with a magnifying glass), the body (bone
  chose stiff-and-light and pays for it in brittleness).
ASKED-AS: aluminum steel plastic titanium carbon fiber lightweight expensive strong material choose

ESSENCE: the same steel can be left soft or made glass-hard
  depending only on how it was heated and cooled — properties are
  chosen after the material is, and hard always arrives holding
  hands with brittle.
ROOT: chemistry / the innards decide behaviour, and heating then
  quenching rearranges the innards without changing the recipe.
THREAD: cooking (the same dough, two ovens, two breads), the file
  that shatters when dropped, knives and springs cut from one bar
  and separated only by two different afternoons.
ASKED-AS: hardened tempered steel quench brittle tough knife file spring annealed treatment

ESSENCE: stiffness is geometry far more than material — move the
  material away from the bending line and stiffness climbs with
  the cube of the depth; a limp sheet of paper rolled into a tube
  will hold up a book.
ROOT: mathematics / the outermost fibers travel farthest when a
  thing bends, so they resist most — distance gets counted three
  times over.
THREAD: corrugated roofing, the I-beam (a rectangle with its idle
  middle deleted), hollow long bones, a folded pizza slice
  carrying its own tip.
ASKED-AS: cardboard corrugated tube pipe hollow bending sagging shelf stiff floppy depth

ESSENCE: inside a loaded beam all the work happens at the two
  faces — top squeezed, bottom stretched — while a layer through
  the middle does almost nothing; the beam is mostly filler
  holding two busy surfaces apart.
ROOT: building / two jobs spread across a thickness — the further
  a fiber sits from the middle, the harder it is working.
THREAD: joists drilled by plumbers (permitted near the middle,
  never near the edges), tubes beating solid rods for the same
  weight, a bitten cracker breaking from its underside.
ASKED-AS: joist drilling notch hole beam wood floor sagging bounce span middle edge

ESSENCE: an arch stands only while the invisible line of squeeze
  stays inside the stone, and its ideal shape is the shape a
  hanging chain makes turned upside down — a chain finds pure
  pull, and the flip turns pure pull into pure push.
ROOT: building / stone takes squeeze and refuses stretch, so the
  arch's entire job is keeping that force line off the edges.
THREAD: domes and eggshells, suspension bridges (the same curve
  worn right side up), hanging chain and soap-film models used as
  calculators before anyone could do the sums.
ASKED-AS: arch bridge stone tunnel dome curve shape catenary cathedral collapse span

ESSENCE: a truss is a beam with its lazy middle swapped for
  triangles — they send every load straight down a stick's length,
  where sticks are strongest, instead of across it, where they are
  weakest, so a web of thin sticks out-carries a solid bar.
ROOT: building / a triangle cannot change shape without changing a
  side, so its members get pushed and pulled end to end, never bent.
THREAD: cranes, roof rafters, bicycle frames, the transmission
  tower that is almost entirely air, the wobbly shelf cured by a
  single wire run corner to corner.
ASKED-AS: truss bridge tower crane roof rafter web member girder lattice steel

ESSENCE: a cable is the cheapest carrier there is — every fiber
  pulls at once and nothing can buckle — but it keeps no shape of
  its own: it hangs in whatever curve the load draws, and it must
  be anchored to something that refuses to move.
ROOT: building / pull is the job matter does best, because pulling
  cannot fold a thing sideways.
THREAD: sailboat rigging, bicycle spokes (a wheel is a hub hung
  from its rim), circus tents, a washing line that sags further
  the more it carries.
ASKED-AS: cable wire rope suspension tent guy line spokes anchor stretched hanging

ESSENCE: a joint held permanently squeezed hardly feels the load
  that comes and goes — the working force only cancels part of the
  squeeze already there, so the bolt sees a small change instead of
  the whole swing.
ROOT: engineering / metal dies by repetition, and repetition is
  counted by the SIZE of the swing; shrink the swing and the count
  stops mattering.
THREAD: prestressed concrete (squeezed before it is ever used),
  bicycle spokes (the wheel works because every spoke is already
  tight), a strung bow, the torque wrench and its printed number.
ASKED-AS: torque tight bolt stretched preload spokes tension loose rattling snapped clamped

ESSENCE: every building sinks — soil is a wet sponge squeezing out
  slowly under load — and sinking is harmless while it stays even.
  All the damage comes from one part going down further than
  another and dragging the walls apart between them.
ROOT: building / a footing spreads load onto soil, and soil answers
  by compressing over months and years, never instantly.
THREAD: doorframes that stick on one side only, the leaning tower,
  ships (an unevenly loaded hull sags the same way), old houses
  whose floors all slope in one direction.
ASKED-AS: foundation settling cracks doors sticking uneven slab soil clay addition sinking

ESSENCE: things break where they were joined — a joint is a
  deliberate interruption in the material, and load crossing it
  must detour through bolts, glue, or a re-melted seam. Count the
  joints and you have counted the likely failures.
ROOT: building / cracks feed on corners, and a joint is a corner
  drawn on purpose, with a change of material at the same spot.
THREAD: welded frames failing at the weld and not the tube, the
  body (tendon tears at its bone attachment), clothes going at the
  seams, plumbing that leaks at fittings and never mid-pipe.
ASKED-AS: weld seam joint bolt glue broke apart connection weakest failed corner

ESSENCE: three ways to make two things one — clamp them (bolts:
  openable, but the load crowds into a few holes), spread the load
  (glue: a whole face works, and peel kills it), or melt them
  together (weld: no seam left, but the metal beside it has been
  cooked and is no longer what you specified).
ROOT: premise — every union answers one question before any other:
  will anybody ever need to take this apart?
THREAD: aircraft skins riveted and bonded and never taped; the
  glued chair that cannot be re-glued because the old glue is still
  in the joint; flat-pack furniture sold with a hex key.
ASKED-AS: screws bolts epoxy welding rivets glue holds stronger permanent removable repair

ESSENCE: when two shops build halves that must meet, both sets of
  errors land in the one joint between them — so somebody has to
  own that boundary: its sizes, its reference face, and which side
  the spare gap belongs to.
ROOT: making / errors accumulate along a chain, and an interface is
  the place where two independent chains are asked to agree.
THREAD: law (a contract exists because two intentions must be made
  into one), tunnels bored from both ends, buildings where the
  steelwork and the glazing were ordered from different drawings.
ASKED-AS: interface fits together supplier drawing mismatch bolt pattern mating dimensions integration gap

ESSENCE: most joining failures are dirt failures — a weld, a glue
  line, a paint film, a solder joint, and a bearing all die of what
  was on the surface, not of what was asked of the material.
ROOT: craft / a bond is contact, and a film of oil, dust, or oxide
  keeps the two surfaces from ever actually touching.
THREAD: surgery (the scrub before the cut), painting (nine parts
  sanding to one part brush), engines (a speck of grit in a bearing
  becomes a groove inside a minute).
ASKED-AS: clean surface prep degrease sanding primer weld failed glue peeled dust grease

ESSENCE: a fit is not one number but two parts' misses landing on
  opposite sides — leave a gap and it slides, leave none and it
  presses together for good; the same drawing yields a bearing or a
  permanent joint depending which way the misses fell.
ROOT: engineering / no part is made exact, so a designer sizes the
  permitted misses rather than the parts.
THREAD: door hinges, the freezer trick (chill the pin, warm the
  hole, and an impossible fit walks in), bearings killed by the
  wrong clearance far more often than by too much load.
ASKED-AS: clearance interference shaft hole bearing press fit slides seized snug gap

ESSENCE: exact is not a size a shop can cut or a gauge can read —
  every cut, cast, and reading scatters — so exact names a
  direction and an unlimited bill, and the real question is only
  how much miss the job can live with.
ROOT: evidence / every measurement carries its own error, so no
  ruler can certify what no machine can hit.
THREAD: cooking (a pinch is a tolerance), craft (the last tenth of
  the work eating half the time — the same bill), money (the final
  decimal of anything is the expensive one).
ASKED-AS: precision exact measurement accurate machining tolerance thousandths cost perfect close enough

ESSENCE: the error has to go somewhere, and there are only two
  places to put it — into tight machining that you pay a shop for,
  or into one adjustable part that swallows the leftovers. A shim
  is a tolerance bought cheap.
ROOT: engineering / no part is made exact, so every assembly needs
  either narrow misses or one place where misses are absorbed.
THREAD: hinges with slotted holes, guitar truss rods, surveying
  (one benchmark and everything referred back to it), spectacles
  bent to a face rather than manufactured for one.
ASKED-AS: shim adjustment slotted holes alignment washers spacer packing level tweak calibration lock

ESSENCE: two things rubbing both lose material, so the only real
  choice is which one loses. Good design nominates the cheap,
  reachable, quickly swapped part and makes it the softer one, so
  the damage collects where it is affordable.
ROOT: engineering / friction is a tax paid in matter as well as in
  heat — contact grinds both faces, always.
THREAD: brake pads against discs, the shear pin that snaps before
  the gearbox does, replaceable saw teeth, cutting boards kept
  softer than the knife on purpose, stair treads and doorsills.
ASKED-AS: brake pads worn replace liner bushing shear pin cheap sacrificial rubbing groove

ESSENCE: oil's job is to make sure the two surfaces never touch —
  a film thinner than a hair carries the whole load, and in a plain
  bearing the motion itself drags that film into being, so the film
  exists only while the machine is turning.
ROOT: engineering / a liquid layer converts a scrape between two
  solids into a shear between two skins of oil.
THREAD: the body (joint fluid doing exactly this), the wet floor
  that drops you, engines (the filter and the oil cooler are two
  admissions that oil has other jobs).
ASKED-AS: oil grease lubricant seized dry squeaking bearing engine startup wear film

ESSENCE: metal is always trying to go back to ore, and there are
  only four ways to delay it: cover it, grow a skin that stops
  itself, feed it something cheaper to eat, or shape the thing so
  water never stands anywhere on it.
ROOT: chemistry / refined metal sits uphill; and two different
  metals touching in wet make a battery, in which the poorer one
  pays the bill.
THREAD: galvanised nails in cedar decking, boat hulls hung with
  zinc lumps, aluminium ladders (the dull skin is the armour), cars
  that rust from inside the panel outward.
ASKED-AS: rust corrosion galvanized zinc stainless aluminum boat anode paint pitting drainage

ESSENCE: fatigue life is counted in cycles and only ever spent —
  each flex adds a hair of crack and nothing takes it back. A
  machine parked for ten years is exactly as old as the day it
  stopped, and none of the damage healed while it sat.
ROOT: engineering / metal dies by repetition, and the tally is a
  ledger with no credit column.
THREAD: aircraft retired by flight count rather than by age, the
  body (bone fatigues too, but is alive and repairs), a coathanger
  wire that never recovers between bends.
ASKED-AS: fatigue cycles cracked broke suddenly bending flexing old metal scratch inspection hours

ESSENCE: a material under a steady load does not simply sit there —
  it flows, slowly and permanently, and warmth speeds it up. The
  plastic clip relaxes, the lead roof sags, the bolt loses its
  tension, and nothing at all appears to happen.
ROOT: chemistry / bonds re-seat under sustained strain given time
  and heat, so duration achieves what a much bigger load would.
THREAD: fatigue (the twin — that one counts repetitions, this one
  counts hours), hillsides and glaciers moving by the same
  mechanism, an old shelf that took the shape of its books.
ASKED-AS: sagging plastic relaxed clip lost tension over time bolt loose warm shelf bowed

ESSENCE: every machine turns part of its work into heat, and heat
  is what actually limits it — metal softens, oil thins, insulation
  cooks, and parts swell into each other. Most power ratings are
  cooling ratings wearing a different name.
ROOT: physics / no conversion without waste heat; building /
  everything swells when warmed, and swelling closes the clearances
  the machine needed.
THREAD: laptops slowing when hot, brakes fading downhill,
  transformers humming inside oil baths, the body (a fever is the
  same ceiling met from the other side).
ASKED-AS: overheating fan cooling radiator thermal shutdown hot burning duty cycle rating vents

ESSENCE: a machine that shakes is spending its energy on itself,
  and the shaking travels to whatever is nearly loose — unpicking
  joints, sawing cracks, hunting out the one part that was almost
  right. It is a machine finding its own worst workmanship.
ROOT: waves / repeated pushes in step add up, so a small force at a
  structure's own rhythm builds motion a large one could not.
THREAD: bridges closed to marching soldiers, a washing machine
  walking across the floor on an unbalanced load, singers and
  glasses, engine mounts made of rubber on purpose.
ASKED-AS: vibration shaking rattle resonance humming loose wobble balance damping noise speed

ESSENCE: a seal is a soft thing kept squeezed between two hard
  ones, and it holds only while the squeeze holds — it dies by
  drying, by taking a permanent set, or by the gap moving further
  than the squeeze can follow.
ROOT: building / matter cannot share space, so sealing means
  filling every gap continuously with something pushable.
THREAD: jar lids and gaskets, a diver's watch rated by depth and
  ruined by age, window frames (where the real trick is drainage —
  letting the water that gets in get back out).
ASKED-AS: gasket seal leaking oring rubber hardened weeping waterproof dust cover shaft

ESSENCE: a safety factor is a confession, not a strength — the
  number runs large where knowledge is thin and lives ride on it,
  small where testing is thorough and weight is dear. Ropes that
  carry people run about ten; an airliner runs about one and a half.
ROOT: chance / a margin should match the width of the unknown, not
  the size of the fear.
THREAD: money (a cash reserve is this same number in dollars),
  medicine (the gap between the dose that works and the dose that
  harms), flight (thin margins bought back with testing).
ASKED-AS: safety factor overbuilt rated load limit margin capacity elevator rope tested strength

ESSENCE: a spare earns nothing on every day it is not needed, so it
  reads as waste in any account measuring this year; efficiency
  drives eat margin first, and the bill arrives once, in full, on a
  day nobody put in the calendar.
ROOT: chance / rare-and-ruinous can outweigh common-and-cheap in
  total cost, but only the common events show up in the numbers.
THREAD: hospitals and their empty beds, farming (seed corn held
  through a hungry winter), grids run close to the line, two
  engines fed from one tank.
ASKED-AS: backup spare redundant efficiency lean waste capacity idle just in case failover

ESSENCE: strength is not knowable from arithmetic alone, so samples
  are broken to find it — and every sample broken is spent forever.
  Testing consumes exactly the thing it certifies.
ROOT: evidence / a claim about a population is bought with the
  destruction of members of that population.
THREAD: proof testing (every unit loaded hard but short of failure
  — it proves the individual and quietly spends some of its life),
  medicine (trials run on people who are not you), chains stamped
  with a rating nobody in the shop watched being earned.
ASKED-AS: tested rated proof load certified sample broke laboratory strength guarantee batch inspection

ESSENCE: once parts are made to a shared size, repair stops needing
  the maker — the part is bought, not fitted — and assembly stops
  needing a craftsman. The whole modern habit of owning complicated
  things rests on this one agreement.
ROOT: engineering / a standard is tolerance made public: an agreed
  permitted miss, published so strangers can hit it.
THREAD: money (a currency is a standard for value), shipping
  containers, screw threads, a rail gauge argued over in the 1830s
  and obeyed ever since.
ASKED-AS: standard sizes replacement parts universal fitting metric thread compatible spare adapter

ESSENCE: the first one carries all the thinking, all the tooling,
  and all the mistakes; the thousandth carries material and
  minutes. A thing's price is mostly a question of how many were
  made, and one-of-a-kind is expensive before anyone makes it rare.
ROOT: money / a fixed cost paid once and then divided — the shorter
  the run, the larger each share.
THREAD: medicine (the first pill costs a billion, the second a
  cent), software (copying is free, so nearly all of it is first
  unit), books, films, and every mould ever cut.
ASKED-AS: custom one off prototype mass produced cheaper bulk quantity tooling factory price

ESSENCE: making one and making thousands are different crafts — for
  one you spend skill on the piece; for many you spend it on the
  process and then get the pieces almost free. A shape a skilled
  hand cuts in an hour can be impossible to stamp.
ROOT: making / the first unit carries the thinking, and scale is
  what moves that thinking out of the hands and into the tooling.
THREAD: 3D printing (cheap for one, slow for a thousand — the curve
  crossing over), cooking for two against catering for two hundred,
  teaching one student against writing a textbook.
ASKED-AS: mass production handmade tooling mold injection batch scale manufacturing volume redesign

ESSENCE: a share of everything made comes out wrong, and that share
  sets the real price — at half yield, every good one carries a bad
  one on its back, and the bad ones consumed material, hours, and a
  machine's turn before being thrown away.
ROOT: making / no part is made exact, so some fall outside the
  permitted miss; the tighter the tolerance, the more of them do.
THREAD: chip fabrication (whole industries organised around yield),
  farming (the crop that never reaches the crate), cooking (the
  trimmings), printing, and every glassblower's floor.
ASKED-AS: scrap rejects yield defect rate quality control wasted rework batch failed inspection percentage

ESSENCE: the first build is an instrument for producing surprises,
  not a product — its real output is the list of things the drawing
  did not know — and because those surprises are baked into it,
  correcting it usually costs more than building the second one.
ROOT: evidence / a plan cannot test itself against reality; some
  errors exist only in three dimensions and in the hand.
THREAD: cooking (the first pancake), software (the version whose
  whole purpose is to be discarded), medicine (trials before the
  market), writing (the draft that teaches you what it is).
ASKED-AS: prototype mockup first version test model scrapped rework redesign learn mistakes sample

ESSENCE: the same change costs an eraser on the drawing, a week in
  the prototype, the tooling once tooling exists, and the whole
  fleet after shipping — the price of a fix multiplies at every
  stage it survives.
ROOT: making / an assembly is a stack of one-way doors, and a late
  change has to reopen every door built on top of it.
THREAD: building (moving a wall on paper against moving it in
  concrete), medicine (screening against treating), writing (the
  outline against the rewrite), law (the clause against the suit).
ASKED-AS: change order late redesign recall expensive fix early stage tooling revision costly

ESSENCE: the drawing is a guess about what can be made, and the
  person making it finds out the truth first — so a design improves
  by being built, and a designer who never visits the floor keeps
  re-issuing the same impossibility.
ROOT: evidence / the plan cannot test itself; the test happens where
  hands meet material, and the report has to travel back.
THREAD: people and power (bad news thins as it climbs — this is
  that law, in a workshop), teaching (the student's confusion is
  data about the lesson), farming (the field answers the plan).
ASKED-AS: designer never made one impossible to build machinist feedback drawing revision workshop practical

ESSENCE: a design is not real until every line on it can be bought
  or made — and the exotic alloy, the discontinued chip, or the
  part with one supplier becomes the true weak point long after the
  strength sums were finished.
ROOT: people and power / specialisation raises output and raises
  dependence; a supply chain is that dependence drawn out in parts.
THREAD: engineering (a chain is its worst link, and here the link is
  a company), war (factories stopped for want of one bearing),
  cooking (the recipe needing an ingredient nobody stocks).
ASKED-AS: parts unavailable discontinued supplier lead time backorder single source substitute obsolete stock

ESSENCE: measure everything from one reference face, never end to
  end down a row — errors in a chain add up, so the tenth mark
  carries all nine misses ahead of it as well as its own, while ten
  marks struck from one edge each carry only their own.
ROOT: mathematics / independent errors accumulate along a chain; a
  shared origin refuses to pass them on.
THREAD: tile floors laid from a snapped centre line, navigation
  (dead reckoning drifts; a star fix resets it), music (a metronome
  kept for the room, not a tempo taken from the last bar).
ASKED-AS: measuring tape marking layout cutting spacing drift crooked reference line square accumulate

ESSENCE: some steps are only possible before others — drill before
  you bend, thread before you harden, run the wires before you fit
  the panel that hides them — and an order mistake does not cost one
  step, it costs the undoing of everything done since.
ROOT: physics / disorder wins — an assembly is a stack of one-way
  doors, and reversing one means reopening every door above it.
THREAD: surgery (the order of closing), cooking (sear then simmer,
  never the reverse), computing (the wrong install order and the
  whole machine starts over), packing a car boot.
ASKED-AS: assembly order steps sequence disassemble backwards install first before after take apart

ESSENCE: a part that CAN be fitted backwards will be fitted
  backwards, by somebody, on some tired afternoon — so the fix is
  never a better instruction, it is a shape that refuses the wrong
  way: an offset hole, a keyed plug, a connector with one seating.
ROOT: chance / across enough repetitions every possible error
  happens at its own steady rate, however good the people are.
THREAD: fuel nozzles sized so diesel will not enter a petrol tank,
  medicine (syringes that cannot connect to the wrong line),
  computing (plugs made asymmetric after decades of upside-down).
ASKED-AS: backwards upside down wrong way connector plug keyed foolproof assembly mistake orientation

ESSENCE: whether a thing can be fixed is settled on the drawing,
  long before it breaks — hands need room, tools need swing, and
  the part that wears must be reachable without disturbing the parts
  that are still fine.
ROOT: making / the order of assembly read backwards: whatever went
  in first comes out last.
THREAD: cars with a cheap belt buried under an engine mount,
  buildings with pipes cast into poured concrete, phones glued shut,
  surgeons arguing about access before the operation.
ASKED-AS: serviceable repair access panel reach behind replace battery glued sealed disassembly cost

ESSENCE: everything fails eventually, so the design's only real
  choice is the direction. A part can bend, sag, weep, and warn — or
  it can let go whole, at speed, with no notice. Choosing the first
  is most of what safety actually means.
ROOT: engineering / a system fails at its weakest point, which means
  the designer gets to nominate that point and its manners.
THREAD: brakes that clamp when the air is lost, lift grippers that
  bite when the rope goes slack, pressure vessels built to leak
  before they burst, doors that unlock in a fire.
ASKED-AS: fail safe brakes warning snapped without notice bend leak crack graceful sudden

ESSENCE: a pound added never costs only a pound — it wants a
  stronger bracket, bigger fasteners, a heavier frame, more power
  and more fuel, and every one of those weighs something too.
  Weight grows on weight.
ROOT: building / the chain of squeezed and stretched parts runs
  unbroken to the ground, so each new load is carried by everything
  underneath it.
THREAD: flight (this spiral is why aircraft weight is argued gram by
  gram), the body (heavier animals need disproportionately thicker
  legs), backpacking, rockets carrying fuel to carry fuel.
ASKED-AS: weight heavy lighter grams saving frame stronger fuel adds up spiral design

ESSENCE: nobody can inspect the metallurgy, so people judge a made
  thing by the parts they touch — the door's sound, the seam's
  evenness, the switch's click — and makers who understand this
  spend where the hand lands.
ROOT: the mind / a verdict is formed from the evidence available,
  and the evidence available to a buyer is the surface.
THREAD: image and design (the surface as an argument), medicine (a
  tidy clinic believed before a word is spoken), cooking (the plate
  read before the taste).
ASKED-AS: feels cheap solid built quality panel gaps rattles finish plastic flimsy premium

ESSENCE: the object walks out of the shop and the record stays —
  without a written account of materials, sizes, permitted misses,
  and sequence, the second one cannot be built, the failure cannot
  be explained, and the spare cannot be ordered.
ROOT: keeping knowledge / a record outlives its maker, and what is
  recorded here is a set of decisions the object does not carry
  anywhere on its face.
THREAD: cooking (the recipe outlasting the dinner), law (writing is
  what lets a promise reach strangers), cathedral crafts that had to
  be re-derived from the surviving stones.
ASKED-AS: drawings blueprints documentation specs revision as built records spare parts manual lost

ESSENCE: what happens to a thing after it stops being wanted is
  designed in at the start — materials bonded face to face cannot be
  told apart again, so a glued sandwich of metal, foam, and plastic
  is one indivisible object forever.
ROOT: physics / disorder wins — separating mixed things costs far
  more than mixing them ever did, and glue is mixing at the joint.
THREAD: chemistry (mixtures are cheap to make and dear to undo),
  cars (designed to be dismantled, and largely are), buildings
  (bolted steel comes back, poured composites do not), compost.
ASKED-AS: recycling disposal landfill glued laminated separate materials dismantle waste scrapped reuse

ESSENCE: owning is a subscription, not a purchase — the price tag is
  a deposit, and the true cost is buying plus keeping, totalled over
  the years you hold it. Most things are discarded not because they
  broke but because their keeping cost passed their worth.
ROOT: physics / disorder wins — decay never pauses for ownership, so
  holding anything at all is a standing bill.
THREAD: boats and pools and horses, roads and bridges (whole
  countries deferring the same bill), old buildings saved by finding
  a use that pays for them, software quietly rotting.
ASKED-AS: maintenance upkeep servicing costs owning cheaper repair worth scrapped neglected annual

ESSENCE: a design is a settlement between parties who want different
  things — cheap for the buyer, easy to build for the factory, easy
  to reach for the mechanic, new-looking for the seller — and the
  finished object disappoints every one of them a little.
ROOT: premise — the requirements genuinely conflict, so there is a
  negotiated point but no maximum.
THREAD: law (the same settlement with more parties), engineering (a
  virtue is always bought with a virtue), architecture (client,
  builder, and occupant are three different clients).
ASKED-AS: compromise tradeoff design decisions cheap ugly annoying why did they nobody happy
