# 96 DEEP ARTS — how made things move people
Middle floors under painting, film, music, writing and design: not
taste, but the machinery every maker is working whether they can
name it or not. Soft-looking field, hard bones — and every law here
also forbids something.

ESSENCE: composition decides the order of arrival, not just the
  arrangement — the eye lands somewhere first, then somewhere
  second, then gives up; and the only real compositional decision is
  which one thing gets first place, because there is exactly one of
  those to give away.
ROOT: image+design / the eye tours a picture in jumps — a tour has
  an order, and that order is either authored or accidental.
THREAD: writing (the first sentence spends the reader's only
  arrival), architecture (a facade with two front doors has none),
  meetings (two agendas is no agenda).
ASKED-AS: composition layout painting photo focal point busy arrangement eye first look framing balance

ESSENCE: nothing is perceived in itself, only against its
  surroundings — so visibility is bought from the field, not from
  the mark: a whisper in a silent room carries further than a shout
  in a crowd, and a small difference in a quiet setting beats a
  large one in a noisy one.
ROOT: the mind / perception reports differences, and a difference is
  measured against the local surround, never against nothing.
THREAD: music (the quiet bar before the loud one is what makes it
  loud), advertising (shouting in a shouting market buys nothing),
  survival (an animal hides by matching the field, not by shrinking).
ASKED-AS: contrast standing out quiet loud background noisy busy plain bold subtle attention design

ESSENCE: a dominant element needs elements built to lose — a work
  wants a clear first, a few supporting parts and a majority
  deliberately held down; the usual failure is not too many stars
  but too few servants, every element polished to full strength and
  nothing left to be the ground.
ROOT: this file / a thing is seen only against its field — so a
  figure has to be supplied with a ground, and the ground must be
  made on purpose.
THREAD: theater (a play of nothing but leads), music (a piece where
  every instrument solos), teams (an eleven of strikers loses to
  eleven ordinary players with positions).
ASKED-AS: hierarchy dominant supporting everything competing flat busy equal weight main element background quiet

ESSENCE: the gaps are a second drawing, made whether you meant to
  make it or not — the space between a chair's legs has a shape, and
  it is either right or wrong; trained hands draw the gaps precisely
  because the gaps are unfamiliar and cannot be faked from memory.
ROOT: the mind / recognition swaps a stored symbol for what is
  actually in front of it — and there is no stored symbol for the
  hole between two chair legs.
THREAD: music (a rest has a length and is composed, not skipped),
  sculpture (the mold is the gap and holds all the information),
  listening (what a person leaves out is the shape of the answer).
ASKED-AS: drawing gaps between shapes negative space looks wrong copying seeing accurate sketch outline

ESSENCE: a strong image survives being reduced to three or four flat
  tones — because the maker assigned every part to a tone family and
  then enforced it; a hundred careful gradations with no families is
  mush at any distance, and detail is the first thing distance takes
  away.
ROOT: light+seeing / at small size or low light the eye keeps only
  the large light-dark masses, so those masses are the design.
THREAD: maps (a good one survives being shrunk), camouflage
  (breaking up the mass is the entire trick), teaching (three clear
  headings beat forty facts of even weight).
ASKED-AS: squint thumbnail dark light shading values grey tones muddy reads distance small poster

ESSENCE: colors belong together when something is common to all of
  them — one light falling on the scene, one gray or one pigment
  stirred through every mixture; the way to harmonize a set is not
  to choose better colors but to contaminate them all with the same
  thing.
ROOT: image+design / no color is seen alone — belonging is read as
  shared cause, and a shared light is the plainest shared cause
  there is.
THREAD: photography (white balance imposing one light on
  everything), writing (a consistent voice is the same
  contamination), a room furnished by one person over twenty years
  against one bought in an afternoon.
ASKED-AS: colors clashing palette harmony mixing paint matching room scheme muddy pleasing warm cool

ESSENCE: imitation copies a material's picture and not its behavior
  — printed woodgrain has the pattern but not the depth, and the
  eye, which reads how light goes into a surface and comes back out,
  catches the difference long before anyone can say what is wrong.
ROOT: light+seeing / real materials scatter light through their
  thickness, while a printed surface reflects only off its face.
THREAD: money (a counterfeit fails on feel, not on the printed
  face), music (a sampled instrument holds until it has to respond),
  food (imitation flavor is right at the first taste and wrong by
  the third).
ASKED-AS: fake wood laminate veneer plastic looks cheap real stone marble feels touch quality

ESSENCE: proportions feel settled when one rule divides the whole,
  the parts and the details — a family of related sizes; there is no
  magic number, and a plain ratio repeated everywhere beats a famous
  ratio used once.
ROOT: the mind / the eye hunts pattern, and a repeated ratio is a
  pattern it can find without being told to look.
THREAD: music (one key relates every chord in a piece), building
  (classical orders size every element from one column's diameter),
  typography (a scale of five sizes, not thirty).
ASKED-AS: proportion sizes golden ratio spacing layout pleasing dimensions room furniture scale rule repeated

ESSENCE: repeat a thing three times and the eye stops seeing three
  things and starts seeing one texture — so repetition is how a
  background is built, and the break in it is how a subject is made;
  the odd one out is created by the sameness of everything else.
ROOT: the mind / senses report change and discard the steady —
  repetition manufactures steadiness deliberately, as raw material.
THREAD: music (a riff becomes a floor, and the fill lands on it),
  drill and uniform (an army made into one body so that one signal
  reads), gardens (a hedge is texture, one tree in it is an event).
ASKED-AS: pattern repeating tiles rows texture background odd one out breaking series rhythm wallpaper design

ESSENCE: the frame is a claim that this much is the work — and it
  makes the edges active: a figure touching the edge behaves
  differently from one floating free, and a different crop turns the
  same photograph from a crowd into a portrait.
ROOT: image+design / meaning is read from relationships, and the
  edge is a relationship every element has whether it wanted one or
  not.
THREAD: journalism (a photograph's truthfulness lives mostly in what
  was cropped away), quoting (the sentence taken out of the
  paragraph), maps (where the sheet ends decides what looks central).
ASKED-AS: cropping photo edges framing zoom cut off border picture composition tight wide portrait

ESSENCE: size is felt in the body before it is understood — bigger
  than you is awe or threat, smaller than you invites the hand, and
  the same image on a phone and across a wall are not the same work
  at all.
ROOT: the body / perception is scaled to a human frame, so every
  size is read as a relation to yourself first and a fact second.
THREAD: architecture (cathedral doors built too large on purpose),
  religion (the colossal statue and the pocket icon doing opposite
  jobs), maps (a globe and a pocket atlas make different feelings
  about one planet).
ASKED-AS: size big small huge painting wall phone screen tiny model monument overwhelming intimate

ESSENCE: every image throws almost everything away — a photograph
  discards depth, smell, duration and the entire world outside its
  edge — so the question is never whether to abstract but which few
  properties to carry, and what the discarding buys.
ROOT: mathematics / a model keeps some structure and drops the rest,
  and its usefulness is decided entirely by which it kept.
THREAD: maps (a map the size of the country is useless), language (a
  word is a violent abstraction that works), science (a model is a
  drawing with arithmetic in it).
ASKED-AS: realistic abstract painting cartoon simplified detail photograph likeness style modern picture meaning

ESSENCE: every material forbids something, and the forbidding is
  what gives a form its character — watercolor cannot be undone so
  it has to be decided, marble can only be taken away so it has to
  be seen first, and film cost money by the second, so it taught
  economy to everyone who used it.
ROOT: craft / the material has a will — the will is a set of
  refusals, and a whole way of working grows in the shape they leave.
THREAD: poetry (meter and rhyme as productive obstruction), music
  (the three-minute song shaped by how much fitted on a disc),
  building (stone architecture is the arch's refusal made beautiful).
ASKED-AS: watercolor oils marble digital undo film photography limits constraints medium material forgiving mistakes

ESSENCE: a mark records the movement that made it — a line drawn
  fast carries speed and confidence in it, a line crept along
  carries hesitation, and both are read instantly by people who
  could not name what they are reading.
ROOT: the mind / we are built to read movement in other bodies, and
  a trace of a movement is enough to run that reading.
THREAD: handwriting (a signature is trusted because speed is hard to
  copy), music (an attack played timidly is audible as timid), sport
  (a committed movement and a half-committed one look different from
  the stands).
ASKED-AS: brushstroke line confident hesitant sketchy loose tight drawing gesture quick slow marks energy

ESSENCE: everything in a work must appear to come from one world —
  one light, one level of finish, one degree of stylization — and a
  single element from another world is read as a mistake rather than
  as variety, however good that element is by itself.
ROOT: the mind / a viewer builds one model of what kind of thing
  they are looking at, and anything outside that model is filed as
  error before it is judged at all.
THREAD: film (one bad effect shot damages the whole picture),
  writing (a modern idiom in a period scene), building (a plastic
  window in a stone wall), lying (one wrong detail sinks a whole
  account).
ASKED-AS: consistency style mixed clashing one element wrong world unified odd out place jarring

ESSENCE: ornament earns its keep by doing a job — marking where one
  material ends and another begins, showing which door is the
  important one, celebrating a joint, or taking the wear at a
  corner; the condemned kind is the sort that hides how the thing is
  made.
ROOT: this file / decoration answers to taste and design answers to
  purpose — ornament is the honest overlap, where a thing is
  decorated by being explained.
THREAD: heraldry (decoration that is entirely information),
  bookbinding (raised bands that were once the actual cords),
  uniforms (rank shown in braid — ornament doing government).
ASKED-AS: ornament trim molding fancy carving detail plain decorated fake beam column edging useful

ESSENCE: design has a job and can therefore be wrong — a chair that
  hurts is a bad chair whatever it looks like; decoration has no job
  and can only be liked or disliked. Confusing them makes objects
  that fail the job and then lose the argument about taste as well.
ROOT: evidence / a claim that forbids nothing cannot be tested, and
  a purpose is exactly what makes a made thing testable.
THREAD: architecture (a beautiful building that leaks), software (a
  handsome screen nobody can use), clothing (a coat that is not warm
  is a costume, which is fine if that was the job).
ASKED-AS: design decoration pretty useful chair building looks works function style form purpose ugly

ESSENCE: the first idea is the common idea — it arrives fastest
  because it is the one everybody's associations lead to, so the
  earliest solutions are reliably the most shared ones; the work
  starts after those have been spent.
ROOT: the mind / memory hands back the strongest link first, and the
  strongest link is usually strong because it is common.
THREAD: brainstorming (the rule about quantity before judgment),
  chess (the natural move examined and rejected), science (the first
  explanation is the one everyone already believed).
ASKED-AS: first idea obvious cliche brainstorming ideas coming easy predictable original digging further list

ESSENCE: new work is old parts in an arrangement nobody tried, or an
  old arrangement carried into a material that never held it — so
  the reach of what you can invent is set by the range of parts you
  have collected, not by how hard you strain for novelty.
ROOT: mathematics / the number of combinations explodes with the
  number of elements, so a wider store is a vastly larger space of
  possible works.
THREAD: heredity (sexual reproduction is recombination adopted as a
  strategy), cooking (a fused cuisine is the same arithmetic),
  engineering (most patents are two known things joined).
ASKED-AS: original idea copying inspiration new invention creative stealing combining fresh ideas influences reading

ESSENCE: you are made of what you were soaked in, and the influences
  you can name are rarely the ones doing the work — the operative
  ones are the things so familiar you stopped registering them as
  choices at all.
ROOT: the mind / repeated exposure becomes the default, and defaults
  are invisible precisely because nothing contrasts with them.
THREAD: language (an accent you cannot hear in yourself), farming (a
  field grows whatever its seed bank holds), children (they copy the
  behavior, never the instruction).
ASKED-AS: influence copying unconscious accidentally similar derivative avoiding others work absorbed reading habits taste

ESSENCE: a cliché is a phrase or image that worked so well it wore
  through — it is now processed as one unit and never reaches the
  senses at all, so the failure is not that it is untrue but that it
  is invisible.
ROOT: the mind / anything fully expected is discarded before it is
  examined, and a familiar shape is fully expected.
THREAD: language (a dead metaphor nobody pictures anymore), music (a
  chord progression heard as a genre rather than as chords), manners
  (a greeting so standard it stops being a greeting).
ASKED-AS: cliche tired phrase overused predictable corny stale familiar fresh original writing image worn

ESSENCE: style is what a maker refuses — the color never used, the
  angle never taken, the joke never made; it shows up only across
  several works, as the shape of a set of rules kept so consistently
  that they have stopped being decisions.
ROOT: being something / a self is the pattern in a long run of
  choices, not any single one of them.
THREAD: handwriting (a constraint you could not escape if you
  tried), cooking (a regional cuisine is a pantry's refusals), law (a
  court's character is visible only across a run of judgments).
ASKED-AS: style recognizable artist signature look consistent variety versatile handwriting voice work distinctive

ESSENCE: craft is the distance between what you meant and what came
  out — and closing that distance is what lets you find out whether
  the idea was any good, because until then a bad result cannot be
  blamed on anything in particular.
ROOT: evidence / a test with two possible causes of failure tests
  neither of them; skill removes one cause and leaves the idea alone
  in the dock.
THREAD: science (a null result from a broken instrument is not a
  result), cooking (a recipe cannot be judged by a cook who cannot
  follow it), sport (a strategy is untestable while execution fails).
ASKED-AS: skill technique practice execution beginner idea failed hands ability result matching intended

ESSENCE: taste is not preference but discrimination — the ability to
  see differences most people cannot — and it arrives years before
  the hands can act on it, which is why beginners with good taste
  are miserable and beginners without it are perfectly content.
ROOT: teaching+learning / recognizing is far easier than producing,
  so the two skills grow at different speeds out of the same
  exposure.
THREAD: wine and coffee (trained noticing, often mistaken for
  snobbery), medicine (the experienced eye seeing the shadow on the
  film), music (hearing that you are out of tune long before you can
  stay in it).
ASKED-AS: taste good eye beginner frustrated bad work improving noticing critic judging practice years

ESSENCE: a maker goes blind to their own work — you see the
  intention instead of the object, and familiarity removes exactly
  the freshness you are trying to judge; every remedy is a trick for
  making it strange again, which is why painters use mirrors and
  writers wait a week.
ROOT: the mind / a repeated stimulus stops being reported, and
  knowing what a thing is meant to be fills in what is actually
  there.
THREAD: writing (reading aloud, or in a different typeface),
  proofreading (errors invisible to the author and obvious to
  anybody else), cleaning (you stop seeing your own hallway).
ASKED-AS: see own work fresh eyes mirror upside down break week later obvious feedback

ESSENCE: there is a point past which more work subtracts — a drawing
  worried until the life goes, a mix polished until it is flat, a
  paragraph rewritten into blandness; and the point is invisible
  from inside the effort, because effort feels like improvement all
  the way down.
ROOT: craft / the last tenth costs the most — and past the end of
  the last tenth the same effort is buying damage at the same high
  price.
THREAD: cooking (a sauce beaten past its point, a steak taken past
  rare), gardening (a hedge cut until it is a stick), negotiation (a
  deal renegotiated until nobody wants it).
ASKED-AS: overworked overdone fiddling ruined it stopping knowing when finished polish flat lifeless enough

ESSENCE: making generates material and editing selects it — a film
  keeps a fraction of its footage, a photographer a fraction of the
  frames — so quality is largely set by the ratio of made to kept,
  and the way to raise it is to deliberately make far more than you
  need.
ROOT: chance+risk / the best of many draws beats the average of a
  few; selection is the machine that turns quantity into quality.
THREAD: heredity (variation then selection — the same two-stroke
  engine), science (many experiments, few papers), hiring (a
  shortlist is only as good as the pile it was drawn from).
ASKED-AS: editing cutting drafts footage takes photos rewriting throwing away choosing best more shooting

ESSENCE: the passage hardest to cut is often the one that has to go
  — your attachment records what it cost you or where it came from,
  which are facts about your history and not about the work; the
  audience arrives knowing none of it.
ROOT: the mind / effort already spent is felt as value held, though
  the spending is gone whatever you decide now.
THREAD: money (the sunk cost that keeps bad projects breathing),
  building (the beautiful detail that has to come out for the
  stair), medicine (a surgeon taking healthy tissue at the margin).
ASKED-AS: cutting favorite part scene chapter deleting hurts attached better shorter reluctant killing darlings

ESSENCE: a work is finished inside the person receiving it — the
  maker supplies enough for them to build the rest, and the built
  part belongs to the builder, which is why suggestion outlasts
  statement and the monster you never see is the frightening one.
ROOT: the mind / perception is mostly prediction, so a receiver is
  always filling in — and a maker can choose what to leave for that
  filling.
THREAD: humor (the explained joke dies of exactly this), teaching
  (the struggled answer beats the given one), selling (letting the
  buyer conclude they want it).
ASKED-AS: imagination horror unseen suggestion showing telling reader viewer filling gaps subtle obvious

ESSENCE: a work that does the audience's feeling for them prevents
  the feeling — the character weeping onscreen while the music
  swells tells you the emotion has already been handled, and there
  is nothing left for you to supply.
ROOT: this file / the audience completes the work — sentimentality
  is that law violated, by completing it on their behalf.
THREAD: comforting a friend (a person told they are sad withdraws),
  advertising (the laughing family telling you this product is
  happiness), news (footage that leaves you room hits harder than
  narration).
ASKED-AS: sentimental cheesy manipulative music swelling crying scene restrained understated moving cold subtle emotion

ESSENCE: beauty is one goal among several, not the goal — a work can
  be deliberately ugly and still succeed, because force, truth,
  strangeness and discomfort are separate currencies, and the
  strongest works often trade beauty away to buy one of them.
ROOT: this file / a made thing is judged against its purpose — so
  the question is never whether a work is beautiful but what it was
  spending itself on.
THREAD: architecture (a war memorial that must not be pleasant),
  music (dissonance used on purpose and for real), speech (a blunt
  sentence chosen over an elegant one).
ASKED-AS: ugly beautiful art disturbing difficult unpleasant pretty purpose meant liked shocking judging

ESSENCE: the same object is three different things in a gallery, a
  shop and a skip — the surround supplies the instructions for how
  to read it, and no amount of quality inside the object overrides
  the instruction outside it.
ROOT: people together / status is assigned by a group's frame, and
  the frame arrives and does its work before the object is looked at
  properly.
THREAD: money (the same note is wealth or paper depending entirely
  on who else accepts it), music (a hymn in a stadium), food (the
  same dish in a canteen and on a tasting menu).
ASKED-AS: gallery museum context setting meaning same object street shop label famous surroundings changes

ESSENCE: a performance is negotiated with one room on one night and
  then gone, so it can risk everything; a recording is an object
  that will be met a hundred times, so it has to survive familiarity
  — daring on the night, depth on the disc.
ROOT: chance+risk / a one-shot bet and a repeated one have different
  best play, and repetition punishes the trick while rewarding the
  layer.
THREAD: sport (the live match against the highlights), teaching (a
  lecture against the textbook), conversation (a letter is not a
  talk written down).
ASKED-AS: live concert recording album studio audience atmosphere repeat listening again performance video different

ESSENCE: who pays shapes the form — panel paintings were the size of
  the walls somebody had, symphonies the size of the orchestra a
  court kept, songs the length that fitted a disc, and streaming pays
  by the play, so choruses arrive sooner. The economics are inside
  the work, not around it.
ROOT: money / a maker who must eat produces what can be sold, and
  the buyer's constraints become the medium's conventions within a
  generation.
THREAD: architecture (a cathedral is a tax record in stone), film
  (running time set by how many showings fit in an evening),
  journalism (column width and story length set by paper size).
ASKED-AS: money patron commission funding artists paid selling market size length album streaming shaped

ESSENCE: when a machine takes over a job that art was doing, art
  moves to what the machine cannot do — photography took likeness
  from painting and painting went to the things a lens could not
  reach; recording took permanence from performance and performance
  became about the night itself.
ROOT: money+trade / a capability that becomes cheap stops being
  valuable, so the people who sold it move to whatever is still
  scarce.
THREAD: farming (mechanization moving human work up the chain),
  chess (computers ending one kind of admiration and starting
  another), craft (handmade becoming a statement only after machines
  made it unnecessary).
ASKED-AS: photography replaced painting technology machines artists jobs changed camera recording printing new medium

ESSENCE: the work you inherit is the work that survived, and
  survival is not the same as quality — durable materials, lucky
  owners, the fashion of one century, somebody bothering to write it
  down; so comparing yourself with the canon is comparing yourself
  with a filtered sample and not with the past.
ROOT: chance+risk / a sample selected by anything other than the
  thing you are measuring will mislead about that thing, reliably
  and in one direction.
THREAD: history (buildings of stone survive, buildings of wood do
  not, so the past looks stony), medicine (a treatment judged by the
  patients who came back), business (studying only the firms that
  are still there).
ASKED-AS: old masters everything better past classics survived famous quality comparing modern golden age

ESSENCE: makers explain their own work badly and go on making it
  well — the doing lives in trained judgment, and the explanation is
  a story assembled afterwards, so an artist's account of their
  method is evidence about them and only weak evidence about the
  work.
ROOT: the mind / a skill built from thousands of corrections has no
  sentence inside it, and the part that speaks was not the part that
  decided.
THREAD: sport (a great player coaching badly), language (a native
  speaker who cannot state the rule they never break), medicine (an
  expert diagnosis arriving before the reasoning for it).
ASKED-AS: artist explaining meaning interview statement intended nonsense pretentious method knowing why work talking

ESSENCE: many hands need one authority or the work averages into
  blandness — every taste in the room removes whatever the others
  find strange, and what is left is the part nobody objected to,
  which is also the part nobody remembers.
ROOT: people together / a group decision converges on the least
  objectionable option, and distinctiveness lives entirely in the
  parts somebody objects to.
THREAD: cooking (a dish seasoned by six people), governance (a
  committee document), engineering (a specification with every
  stakeholder's feature bolted on).
ASKED-AS: committee design by group compromise bland safe watered down feedback notes vision decision collaboration

ESSENCE: copying a great work forces you to make every decision its
  maker made, in the order they made them — and you find the dozens
  you never noticed, because looking confirms what you already see
  while copying exposes what you do not.
ROOT: teaching+learning / doing surfaces the gaps that watching
  hides, since a skill is only tested when it has to produce.
THREAD: music (learning a solo note for note before improvising),
  writing (copying paragraphs out by hand, an old and effective
  exercise), medicine (watching a hundred operations does not make a
  surgeon).
ASKED-AS: copying master painting studying learning art lessons imitating practice technique tracing museum understanding
