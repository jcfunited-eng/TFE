# 163 DEEP NEUROSCIENCE — the physical mind
File 20 holds what the mind does and file 86 what a nerve costs to
run. This one holds the tissue: how a cell decides, how the decisions
are wired together, what the parts do, and where the honest edge of
the knowledge is. Every entry carries what it forbids.

ESSENCE: a neuron is not a wire and not a switch. It is an adding
  machine with a trigger. Thousands of inputs arrive, some pushing up
  and some pushing down, they leak away as they travel and as time
  passes, and if what is left at the trigger point crosses a line the
  cell fires. Nothing else about the cell is a decision.
ROOT: engineering / a device that sums many inputs and compares the
  total to a threshold is the smallest thing that can be said to
  decide anything.
THREAD: engineering (a relay that trips at a set current), money (a
  balance that must clear a threshold before anything happens), law
  (a vote counted against a quorum).
ASKED-AS: nerve cell how works adds up inputs threshold fires signal decides neuron brain

ESSENCE: the spike is the same size every time. A weak stimulus and a
  violent one produce the identical pulse, so strength cannot be
  carried by size — it is carried by how often the spikes come and by
  how many cells are sending them. The signal is a rhythm, never a
  volume.
ROOT: physics / a signal travelling a metre without loss must be
  regenerated at every step, and anything regenerated to a standard
  cannot carry a magnitude.
THREAD: computing (a digital pulse that survives a long cable), music
  (loudness carried by how often the drum is struck), engineering.
ASKED-AS: nerve signal strength pain more spikes rate all or nothing same size louder

ESSENCE: immediately after a spike a nerve cannot fire again at any
  price, and for a short while after that only with difficulty. That
  brief deadness does two jobs nobody would guess: it sets a ceiling
  on how fast a nerve can signal, and it stops the spike travelling
  backwards, because the stretch behind it is still recovering.
ROOT: engineering / a device that must be reset between uses has both
  a maximum rate and a built-in direction.
THREAD: engineering (a machine gun's cyclic rate set by its
  mechanism), music, computing.
ASKED-AS: nerve fire again limit fast maximum rate backwards direction reset pain saturate strongest

ESSENCE: wrapping an axon in fat lets the spike skip. Instead of
  being rebuilt at every point along the membrane it jumps from gap
  to gap, which makes it fifty times faster and far cheaper to run.
  Speed in a nervous system can be bought two ways — a thicker fibre
  or a wrapped one — and wrapping is the enormously better deal.
ROOT: physics / an insulated conductor loses less along its length,
  so the signal survives to the next place it is regenerated.
THREAD: electrics (insulation and repeaters on a long line),
  engineering, building.
ASKED-AS: myelin nerve speed insulation multiple sclerosis fast reflexes damage thick fibre jumps gaps

ESSENCE: nerves do not touch. At the junction the electrical signal
  stops, a chemical is released across a gap of a few millionths of a
  millimetre, and a new signal starts on the other side. That gap
  looks like a defect and it is the whole point: a chemical step can
  be made stronger or weaker, and a soldered joint cannot.
ROOT: engineering / adjustability has to live somewhere, and it can
  only live at a step where something is converted.
THREAD: engineering (a clutch rather than a welded shaft), money (a
  price that can move because there is a transaction), teaching.
ASKED-AS: synapse gap chemical nerves connect learning stronger weaker delay memory junction transmitter

ESSENCE: there are direct electrical junctions too, and comparing
  them says exactly what the chemical gap buys. Electrical ones are
  instant, run both ways, and are used where a group must fire as one
  — heart muscle, some escape reflexes. They are also fixed. Every
  system that has to change is built on the slow kind.
ROOT: this file / speed and modifiability are bought with different
  mechanisms, so a nervous system needing both must run both.
THREAD: engineering (a rigid coupling against a gearbox), music (a
  section playing as one), medicine.
ASKED-AS: nerves connected directly electrical fast heart together reflex escape fixed cannot learn

ESSENCE: a handful of chemicals do nearly all of it, and they split
  into two jobs. A few are the workhorses that carry the actual
  traffic — one that mostly excites and one that mostly inhibits, and
  between them they account for the overwhelming majority of all
  junctions in the brain. The famous ones are not traffic at all;
  they are settings.
ROOT: engineering / carrying a message and setting the conditions

  substances do them.
THREAD: engineering (signal wiring against supply voltage), music (a
  note against the key the piece is in), money.
ASKED-AS: brain chemicals dopamine serotonin glutamate gaba what they do traffic settings different places

ESSENCE: every transmitter has two kinds of lock waiting for it. One
  is a hole that opens the instant the chemical lands and shuts again
  in a millisecond — that is the traffic. The other starts a slow
  chain inside the cell that changes how the cell will behave for
  seconds, minutes or longer. Same chemical, same cell, two
  completely different consequences.
ROOT: engineering / a receiver decides what a signal means, so one
  message can be read as an instruction or as a change of policy.
THREAD: law (an order against a change in the standing rules),
  computing (a command against a configuration change), engineering.
ASKED-AS: receptors different types same chemical fast slow effects lasting minutes drug unwanted brain

ESSENCE: a message that is not ended is not a message. The chemical
  in the gap has to be cleared within milliseconds — sucked back into
  the cell that released it, chewed up by an enzyme, or mopped up by a
  neighbouring support cell — or the junction stays on and stops
  carrying information at all.
ROOT: engineering / a channel carries information only through
  changes, so anything that persists is silence at a different level.
THREAD: cleaning (a room that must be cleared between uses),
  computing (a buffer that must be emptied), music (a note that must
  stop).
ASKED-AS: transmitter cleared reuptake enzyme broken down stays on nerve gas drugs blocking cleanup

ESSENCE: for every cell pushing, there is one pushing back. Roughly a
  fifth of cortical cells do nothing but inhibit their neighbours, and
  they are what keeps the rest from running away — a network of
  excitable cells that all excite each other has only one stable
  state, which is everybody firing at once. That state has a name: it
  is a seizure.
ROOT: engineering / positive feedback without a brake has exactly two
  outcomes, silence and saturation, and neither carries information.
THREAD: engineering (a governor that is part of the engine, not an
  accessory), money, power + governance.
ASKED-AS: brain inhibition seizure epilepsy balance excitation runaway spreads control cells calm neighbours

ESSENCE: neighbours suppress each other, and that single arrangement
  manufactures edges. A cell strongly stimulated pushes its
  neighbours down harder than a weakly stimulated one does, so at a
  boundary the bright side reports brighter and the dark side darker
  than either really is. What you see as an outline was largely
  invented by this.
ROOT: this file / a network reports differences rather than levels,
  and mutual suppression is the cheapest way to build that in.
THREAD: image + design (why a border makes two greys look unequal),
  light + seeing, engineering.

ESSENCE: learning is a physical change in the junctions. Use one hard
  and it grows — more receiving locks on the far side, more chemical
  released, and, over hours, the spine it sits on physically enlarges
  and new ones sprout. Nothing is stored anywhere; the storage is the
  altered wiring itself.
ROOT: this file / a network's behaviour is entirely set by its
  connection strengths, so changing behaviour means changing
  strengths and there is no other option.
THREAD: craft (a path worn by walking it), computing (weights rather
  than files), teaching.
ASKED-AS: memory stored where brain connections stronger learning physical change spines grow hours protein

ESSENCE: the rule is about order, not just co-occurrence. If cell A
  fires a few thousandths of a second before cell B, the junction
  between them strengthens. If A fires just after B, it weakens. The
  window is tens of milliseconds wide, and its asymmetry is what lets
  a network learn which things predict which, rather than merely
  which things go together.
ROOT: mathematics / causes precede effects, so a rule sensitive to
  order is a rule that can find causes.
THREAD: teaching (the signal must come before the thing), chance +
  risk, computing.
ASKED-AS: cells fire together wire timing before after order prediction learning window milliseconds cause

ESSENCE: connections weaken as readily as they strengthen, and the
  weakening is not decay — it is an active process with its own
  machinery. A network in which everything can only grow saturates
  within days and can no longer learn anything, because every
  junction is at maximum and no junction can any longer say more than
  any other.
ROOT: mathematics / information lives in differences between weights,

ASKED-AS: forgetting necessary connections weaken pruned learning new interferes old skill worse saturate memory

ESSENCE: a child's brain builds far too much and then cuts. The
  number of junctions peaks in the first years at roughly twice the
  adult figure, and the following decade is spent removing the ones
  that were not used. Development is subtraction as much as growth,
  and what survives is decided by traffic.
ROOT: the living world / it is cheaper to overbuild and select than

THREAD: craft (carving as removal), farming (thinning a crop),

ASKED-AS: baby brain connections too many pruned childhood use it lose experience shapes wiring

ESSENCE: a critical period does not simply lapse. It is opened by the
  arrival of a particular set of inhibiting cells and then actively
  closed — brakes are put on, physical nets are woven around the
  cells, and the machinery for change is bolted down. Which means the
  window is not a stage of growth. It is a decision the tissue makes,
  and one that can in principle be reopened.
ROOT: engineering / a system that must first be shaped by the world
  and then made reliable has to have a mechanism for ending its own
  plastic phase.
THREAD: engineering (a setting jig removed once the glue is cured),
  building, teaching.

ESSENCE: the branching tree a neuron grows is not plumbing, it is
  arithmetic. Inputs landing on the same branch at the same moment
  add up far more than inputs on different branches; inputs near the
  cell body count for more than distant ones; and an inhibiting
  input placed on the stem can veto everything beyond it. Where a
  connection lands is as important as how strong it is.
ROOT: physics / a signal spreading through a branched leaky cable
  interacts with its neighbours locally, so geometry becomes
  computation.
THREAD: mathematics, electrics (a branched circuit where position
  changes the answer), building.
ASKED-AS: dendrites branches inputs where land close far veto shape matters neuron computation tree

ESSENCE: neurons are a minority of the brain. The support cells wrap
  the axons, hold the junctions in shape, supply fuel, mop up spilled
  transmitter, control the local blood supply, and are the brain's own
  immune force. They were called glue for a century. They are not
  passive, and several diseases that look like neuron diseases begin
  in them.
ROOT: this file / the cells that do the signalling are specialised to
  the point of helplessness, so everything else has to be done by
  somebody.
THREAD: people together (the staff without whom the named workers
  cannot work), building, engineering.
ASKED-AS: glial cells brain support neurons minority astrocytes fuel cleaning immune not just glue

ESSENCE: a nerve cell running from your spine to your foot is a
  metre long, and everything it needs is made at the far end, in the
  body. So it runs a freight line: motors walking cargo down the axon
  at a few hundred millimetres a day, and a return service bringing
  material and messages back. The cell's factory and its workplace
  are a very long way apart.
ROOT: physics / random blundering crosses a hair in an instant and a
  metre never, so anything longer than a cell needs deliberate
  freight.
THREAD: movement of goods (a factory and a site connected by one
  slow road), engineering, farming.
ASKED-AS: long nerves feet first numbness diabetes transport axon slow months recovery toxin travels

ESSENCE: cut a nerve in an arm and it regrows, slowly, along the
  tube its own sheath left behind. Cut one in the spinal cord and it
  does not. The difference is not in the cells — it is in the
  surroundings: the central environment is full of molecules that
  actively stop growth, and the scar that forms is a chemical wall as
  well as a physical one.
ROOT: engineering / a system whose wiring encodes decades of learning

THREAD: building (a conduit that survives the cable), farming, law.

ESSENCE: regions do specialise, but not the way the diagrams show. A

ESSENCE: the honest account of the hemispheres is small. The left

ESSENCE: the cortex is a sheet about three millimetres thick, six
  layers deep, folded to fit — and its wiring is nearly the same
  everywhere. The patch handling vision and the patch handling
  movement are built from one repeated circuit, arranged in narrow
  columns running top to bottom. What differs is what is plugged into
  it.

ESSENCE: the body and the visual world are laid out on the cortex as

  the lips and the tongue take up enormous territory and the back
  takes almost none. The centre of vision — a patch you could cover
  with a thumbnail at arm's length — takes up more than half the
  first visual map.

ESSENCE: those maps move. Lose a finger and the neighbouring

  practise an instrument and the trained fingers' territory grows.
  This is also where phantom limbs come from: the face's territory
  creeps into the hand's abandoned patch, so a touch on the cheek is
  felt in a hand that is no longer there.

ESSENCE: almost nothing reaches the cortex without passing through
