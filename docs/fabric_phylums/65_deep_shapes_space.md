# 65 DEEP SHAPES AND SPACE — geometry underfoot
Where and how big — shapes carry laws no maker put in them.
Every entry carries its cannot-twin.

ESSENCE: an angle counts turning — a full spin is the whole,
  degrees its 360 pieces; tear the corners off a paper triangle
  and they lie on a straight edge: half a turn, every time.
ROOT: mathematics (11) / counting — 360 is a chosen bundle; the
  turn itself is the truth.
CANNOT: no flat triangle beats or misses half a turn — 181° means
  a bent side or a bad protractor. On a globe corners CAN fatten:
  the flatness was a premise, not a law.
THREAD: navigation (a compass is one turn cut into 360), building
  (rafters and ramps are angles).
ASKED-AS: angle degrees triangle 180 protractor corner turn compass sum measure add

ESSENCE: the right triangle's law — the square on the long side
  holds exactly the other two squares together; not near, exactly,
  for every right corner ever drawn.
ROOT: proof (11) — granted a flat sheet, forced steps do the rest;
  proved, not surveyed.
CANNOT: no right corner disobeys — nor backward: sides 3, 4, 5
  FORCE the square corner; builders square foundations with a
  knotted 3-4-5 rope.
THREAD: building (the rope that squares a foundation), maps
  (distance between grid points is this law).
ASKED-AS: pythagoras right triangle hypotenuse square corner 3 4 5 rope diagonal distance

ESSENCE: every circle, any size, carries the same ratio of rim to
  width — a little more than three (3.14159…), the same for a coin
  and a planet; no circle escapes it.
ROOT: this file / similar shapes — all circles are one shape at
  different sizes, so rim-to-width is one number for all.
CANNOT: no exact fraction and no final digit — every written pi is
  a rounding (64); and no squaring the circle with ruler and
  compass — proved dead, not unsolved.
THREAD: engineering (wheels, gears, pipes bill by pi), waves
  (whatever turns or repeats hides a pi).
ASKED-AS: pi circle circumference diameter 3.14 radius why forever digits irrational

ESSENCE: perimeter is fencing, area is covering — near strangers:
  forty feet of fence can close a starved strip or a fat square;
  the circle closes the most ground of all.
ROOT: this file / dimension — fence is length once, ground is
  length twice; one cannot dictate the other.
CANNOT: no reading area off perimeter — fence can grow without
  limit while ground shrinks toward none; with a given fence
  nothing beats the circle — proved.
THREAD: farming (fence cost against field gained), bubbles (a soap
  bubble: most air, least skin).
ASKED-AS: area perimeter fence most space same length bigger garden circle square why

ESSENCE: same shape, different size — every length scales by one
  factor while every angle holds; a photo and its billboard are
  one shape at two sizes.
ROOT: premise — shape is what survives resizing; size is what
  resizing changes.
CANNOT: no angle moved by resizing — and no partial resemblance in
  triangles: two matched angles force the third and every
  proportion; a stick's shadow measures a pyramid this way.
THREAD: maps (a similar copy of the land), the square-cube gears
  (64: lengths once, areas twice, volumes thrice).
ASKED-AS: similar triangles scale shadow height measure proportion same shape model zoom map

ESSENCE: a shape's symmetries are the moves that leave it looking
  untouched — turn a square a quarter, flip it on a diagonal,
  nothing shows; that list of moves is its deepest name.
ROOT: mathematics (11) / symmetry is sameness under change —
  standing here on shapes a hand can hold.
CANNOT: no telling which of its eight placements a plain square
  got — the moves erased it; more symmetry, fewer tellable
  positions. Mark one corner and all eight bloom.
THREAD: crystals and snowflakes (11: one bond angle voting six
  times), craft (exactly 17 wallpaper patterns exist, proved).
ASKED-AS: symmetry mirror rotate flip square snowflake pattern wallpaper looks same match

ESSENCE: two numbers pin any point on a flat sheet — over this
  much, up that much — and that marries two kingdoms: every shape
  becomes an equation, every equation a picture.
ROOT: premise — a place IS its pair of distances from two agreed
  lines; the bridge algebra crosses.
CANNOT: no third number needed on a sheet, no pair enough in a
  room — the count is dimension itself (this file).
THREAD: maps (latitude and longitude are the globe's pair),
  computing (every pixel lives at its pair).
ASKED-AS: coordinates graph x y axis plot grid point latitude longitude plane pair

ESSENCE: on a flat sheet nothing beats the straight line between
  two points — a tight string finds it by itself; on a globe the
  honest straight is a great circle, so flights arc over the pole.
ROOT: premise — "shortest" defines "straight"; each surface issues
  its own straight lines.
CANNOT: no flat map draws the globe straight — every flat world
  map must lie somewhere, proved dead; stretch or tear, something
  gives.
THREAD: navigation (the string on a school globe finds the flight
  path), light (the quickest path — a lens is geometry in glass).
ASKED-AS: shortest path straight line flight route globe curve why polar map distance

ESSENCE: hexagons tile a floor with no gap and least wall — bees
  build them; spheres can never fill space: the grocer's orange
  pyramid is the best possible, and still a quarter is air.
ROOT: premise — shapes carry their own filling laws; no
  cleverness outruns them.
CANNOT: no packing spheres past about three-quarters full — the
  grocer's stack is proved unbeatable; and only triangle, square,
  and hexagon tile a floor alone — no fourth, dead.
THREAD: life (honeycomb; a fly's eye), crystals (metal atoms stack
  like the oranges).
ASKED-AS: hexagon honeycomb tiles gaps spheres stack oranges packing bees floor fit why

ESSENCE: each independent direction of travel is one dimension —
  wire, sheet, room: one, two, three; a shadow is a solid's
  information flattened by one.
ROOT: this file / coordinates — dimension is how many numbers a
  point demands, no more, no fewer.
CANNOT: no faithful flattening — countless different solids cast
  the identical shadow, and nothing rebuilds the lost direction
  from one view; two views can — that is how eyes buy depth.
THREAD: the body (two flat pictures rebuild the third dimension),
  medicine (a CT scan braids many shadows back into a solid).
