# 149 DEEP TEXTILES AND SOFT MATERIALS — cloth, leather, rope and thread
Soft materials are the ones that hold their strength in a structure
rather than in a substance. A rope, a jumper and a shoe are all
arrangements of thin things persuaded to act as one thick thing, and
every property they have — warmth, stretch, colour, life — is a
consequence of that arrangement. These are the laws underneath the
fabric shop, the tannery and the chandlery, and every one forbids
something.

ESSENCE: everything a cloth can ever be is settled before a single
  thread exists, by two numbers on the fibre itself — how long each
  one is and how fine it is. Long fine fibres make smooth, strong,
  even yarn; short coarse ones make hairy, weak, uneven yarn, and no
  machine downstream puts back what the fibre did not bring.
ROOT: craft / the smallest part's properties travel all the way up —
  every later stage only arranges what it was handed.
THREAD: metals (crystal size deciding the finished part), food (the
  flour's protein setting the ceiling for the bread), teaching (the
  spread a class arrives with limiting where the term can end).
ASKED-AS: cotton quality long staple egyptian soft rough cheap shirt fibre length smooth expensive

ESSENCE: a fibre either arrives in short pieces that have to be
  twisted into continuity, or it arrives as one endless strand.
  Everything follows from which: short-piece yarn is hairy, warm,
  matt and it pills; endless-strand yarn is smooth, cool, shiny and
  it snags. Silk and every synthetic start endless — and are chopped
  up on purpose when the hairy kind is wanted.
ROOT: craft / a made thing inherits the shape of its raw unit, and
  here there are only two raw units in the world.
THREAD: building (poured concrete against laid brick — continuous
  against assembled), music (a bowed note against a plucked one),
  writing (one long sentence against many short ones).
ASKED-AS: shiny fabric matt fuzzy polyester silk feel smooth warm jumper pills snags difference

ESSENCE: a handful of loose fibres carries nothing at all. Twist
  presses them against each other so that friction makes a pull on
  any one of them a pull on all of them — and strength climbs with
  twist to a peak and then falls again, because past that peak the
  fibres lie at too steep an angle to the load to carry it.
ROOT: physics / friction turns many separate things into one thing,
  and the price is that the load no longer runs straight.
THREAD: rope (the same trick at every scale above), people together
  (a group held by mutual obligation rather than by a leader),
  farming (a rope of straw made by the same motion).
ASKED-AS: spinning thread twist strong yarn breaking wool hand spindle tight loose kinking

ESSENCE: a single spun yarn is always fighting to untwist and kinks
  the moment you let it go slack. Plying two of them together in the
  opposite direction sets the two urges against each other and the
  yarn finally lies dead straight. So ply is not mainly about
  thickness — it is about cancellation.
ROOT: physics / two equal and opposite stored twists sum to nothing,
  which is a way of storing strength without storing restlessness.
THREAD: rope (three strands laid the other way at each level),
  engineering (counter-rotating propellers cancelling torque), music
  (two detuned strings beating against each other).
ASKED-AS: two ply yarn knitting twist kinking single strand wool balanced skew four

ESSENCE: yarn fineness is counted backwards. It is measured as
  length per unit of weight, so a bigger number means a thinner
  yarn — and every fibre kept its own scale, so a cotton count and a
  worsted count and a linen count are three different numbers that
  cannot be compared without conversion.
ROOT: evidence / a unit is defined by the procedure that produced
  it, so two numbers with the same name can be about different
  worlds.
THREAD: money (interest quoted per month against per year), light
  (film speed and lens numbers where bigger means less), cooking
  (oven marks against degrees).
ASKED-AS: yarn count thread weight number bigger finer confusing wool cotton comparing labels metric

ESSENCE: a weave is nothing but a schedule of who goes over and who
  goes under, and one dial sets the whole character: how often the
  threads cross. Cross at every thread and you get plain weave —
  firmest, most stable, dullest. Cross less often and you get twill —
  it drapes, it resists tearing, it hides dirt. Cross least and you
  get satin — smooth, shiny, and it snags.
ROOT: engineering / one variable governing several outcomes means
  the outcomes cannot be chosen separately.
THREAD: building (more fixings means stiffer and heavier), the mind
  (frequent checking makes a task reliable and slow), roads (more
  junctions, more stability, less speed).
ASKED-AS: plain weave twill satin denim difference shiny drapes fabric structure sateen fraying tearing

ESSENCE: the two directions in a woven cloth are not equals. The
  warp is stretched tight on the loom for the entire weaving, so it
  must be strong, smooth and often sized; the weft only has to lie
  down where it is put. The finished cloth is therefore stronger,
  straighter and less stretchy along the warp, and that is the
  direction a garment hangs on.
ROOT: engineering / a manufacturing process leaves its own history
  inside the product, and here the history is a tension.
THREAD: metals (a rolled sheet stronger along the rolling
  direction), wood (the tree's own axis), printing (paper with a
  grain from the way it was made).
ASKED-AS: fabric length width stretchy direction warp weft loom selvedge hangs cutting roll

ESSENCE: a flat woven cloth cannot cover a rounded body, and there
  are only three ways round that: shear the grid on its diagonal,
  cut wedges out and sew the gap shut, or shrink parts of it with
  heat and steam. Every tailored garment in the world is those three
  moves in some combination, and nothing else is available.
ROOT: mathematics / a flat sheet can be rolled onto a tube or a
  cone without stretching, and onto a ball not at all.
THREAD: map-making (every flat map of the earth lying somewhere),
  metals (a car panel pressed by stretching the sheet), building (a
  dome built from wedge-shaped pieces).
ASKED-AS: darts fitting curves shoulder dress pattern flat fabric sewing bust shaping steaming tailor

ESSENCE: a woven cloth gives only by the corners of its grid shifting
  and then stays where you put it. A knit gives because every loop is
  a little spring being pulled out of shape, so it stretches in every
  direction and comes back. That single difference is why a T-shirt
  needs no buttons and why a suit needs a great many.
ROOT: engineering / recovery is stored energy, so a material springs
  back only if the deformation loaded something that wanted to
  return.
THREAD: engineering (a coil spring against a solid block), the body
  (a tendon returning energy where a bone does not), agreements (a
  rule with give and a rule without).
ASKED-AS: jersey knit woven stretch tshirt bags elbows knees recover shape jeans stiff

ESSENCE: what a cut edge does is decided entirely by the structure
  behind it. A woven edge unravels thread by thread because nothing
  holds the ends in; a weft-knit edge curls and, if a loop is cut,
  runs away in a ladder because the whole fabric is one continuous
  thread. Every edge finish ever invented is an answer to one of
  those two facts.
ROOT: engineering / a structure fails in the manner its construction
  allows, and here the two constructions allow opposite failures.
THREAD: computing (one corrupt link losing a whole chained file),
  rope (an unwhipped end unlaying itself), knitting a dropped stitch.
ASKED-AS: fraying edge overlocking hem tights ladder run knit curling raw cut fabric

ESSENCE: wool fibres are covered in overlapping scales all pointing
  the same way, so under warmth, wet and movement they ratchet past
  each other one notch at a time and cannot ratchet back. That is
  felt — cloth made with no spinning and no weaving at all — and it
  is also, in a hot wash, the ruined jumper.
ROOT: physics / a ratchet is any mechanism where the return path is
  harder than the outward one, so random shaking produces one-way
  motion.
THREAD: cleaning (a jam jar lid that only tightens), the living
  world (a burr that goes into fur and not out), money (a cost that
  ratchets up and never back down).
ASKED-AS: wool shrunk washing machine jumper felt hot water matted ruined shrinking scales agitation

ESSENCE: most cloth in the world is now never spun and never woven.
  Fibres are laid down as a mat and bonded by heat, glue or a bed of
  barbed needles. Such a fabric has no grain, no fraying and no
  stretch to recover from — which makes it perfect for the things
  used once and thrown away, and hopeless for anything else.
ROOT: engineering / strength per weight comes from load running
  continuously through a member, and a mat has no continuous member.
THREAD: building (chipboard against sawn timber), paper (made by
  exactly this process, from very short fibres), food (a burger
  against a steak).
ASKED-AS: wipes masks interfacing felt disposable fabric tears cheap bags nonwoven pressed sheet

ESSENCE: wool fibres are naturally crimped like tiny springs, so a
  wool yarn is mostly trapped air held in a cage of fibre — and the
  air is the warmth. The same crimp is a spring in the other sense:
  it pushes a crease back out, which is why a wool garment recovers
  overnight on a hanger and a linen one does not.
ROOT: physics / still air is one of the best insulators there is, so
  every warm material is a scheme for holding air still.
THREAD: building (loft insulation ruined by being trodden on),
  animals (fur fluffed up in cold), food (a foam whose bubbles are
  the whole product).
ASKED-AS: wool warm jumper thin thick air trapped crushed coat sleeping bag loft crease

ESSENCE: wool takes up about a third of its own weight in water
  before it feels wet at all, and it gives off a little heat while
  it does so. That is why wool stays warm when damp, why it does not
  chill you the moment the weather turns, and why it cannot be
  wrung: it is holding the water inside the fibre, not between the
  fibres.
ROOT: chemistry / a fibre with sites that grab water molecules holds
  them where a fibre without such sites cannot.
THREAD: building (a lime wall that breathes and buffers damp), the
  body (sweat evaporating and taking heat with it), weather (the sea
  holding a coast's temperature steady).
ASKED-AS: wool damp still warm wet socks drying slow synthetic base layer sweat clammy

ESSENCE: cotton is the one common fibre that is stronger wet than
  dry, which is why it became the fibre of sails, ropes, bandages
  and laundry. But it swells when it drinks, so it shrinks and it
  dries slowly, and a wet cotton layer against skin pulls heat out
  of a body faster than the body can make it.
ROOT: chemistry / water can strengthen a structure by helping its
  chains slide into better contact, and the same drinking that does
  it is what makes the fibre fat and slow.
THREAD: the body (heat lost to water far faster than to air), farming
  (soil holding water and staying cold in spring), survival (the
  first rule being to get out of wet clothes).
ASKED-AS: cotton wet cold hiking jeans denim heavy dries slowly towel absorbent shrink strong

ESSENCE: flax fibres are stiff and have almost no spring in them, so
  a fold in linen becomes a fold in the fibre and stays there. That
  same stiffness is what makes linen cool, crisp, strong and capable
  of lasting a century. The crease is not a fault in the cloth — it
  is the receipt for everything else linen is.
ROOT: engineering / a design buys a virtue with a virtue, so a
  property complained about is usually the price of one admired.
THREAD: metals (a hard blade that chips and a tough one that dulls),
  people (the blunt friend and the tactful one), building (a rigid
  frame that cracks and a flexible one that sways).
ASKED-AS: linen creases wrinkled shirt ironing crumpled cool summer trousers rumpled crisp fabric

ESSENCE: silk is a single filament a mile long, triangular in
  section so it breaks light into a shifting sheen, and stronger for
  its weight than most steel wire. It is also a protein, so it is
  eaten by alkali, sunlight and sweat — the strength, the sheen and
  the fragility all come from the one substance.
ROOT: chemistry / a protein is a long chain held by many small
  bonds, magnificent in tension and easily unpicked by chemistry.
THREAD: the body (hair and skin, the same protein family with the
  same enemies), metals (something strong in one direction and
  vulnerable to one chemical), light (fading as a slow chemical
  dose).
ASKED-AS: silk washing detergent hand wash shattered curtains sun damage sweat stains shiny delicate

ESSENCE: each synthetic fibre was invented to solve one named
  problem, and each still solves it. Nylon was strength and spring —
  stockings and ropes. Polyester was crease resistance and staying
  the size it was made. Acrylic was wool's look without wool's price
  or wool's moths. Elastane was recoverable stretch. All of them
  inherit one flaw from being plastics: they melt.
ROOT: chemistry / a made molecule can be designed for a property,
  and the family it belongs to comes along whether or not you wanted
  it.
THREAD: pharmacy (a drug designed for one action carrying its class
  effects), computing (a language built for one job dragging its
  whole runtime), engineering (a solved problem with a shipped side
  effect).
ASKED-AS: polyester nylon acrylic fleece melting sparks fire safe clothes synthetic invented why plastic

ESSENCE: a synthetic fibre holds almost no water inside itself, and
  every good and bad thing about it follows. It dries fast, it will
  not shrink and it takes no dye in plain warm water — but it does
  dissolve oil into itself, so an oily mark becomes part of the
  fibre and a body's smell moves in and stays after washing.
ROOT: chemistry / like dissolves like — a fibre that repels water is
  by the same token a fibre that welcomes grease.
THREAD: cleaning (the right solvent for the right soil), cooking
  (aroma living in the fat), medicine (a fat-soluble drug stored in
  fat for weeks).
ASKED-AS: polyester smells sports shirt washing stains grease dry fast dye colour stubborn odour

ESSENCE: a blend does not average two fibres. It gives you the
  strictest care rules of both, the visible virtues of the tougher
  one, and often a new fault neither had alone — poly-cotton pills
  precisely because the polyester is strong enough to keep hold of
  the cotton fuzz that a pure cotton would have shed.
ROOT: engineering / mixing two components mixes their failure modes
  as well as their properties, and failure modes do not cancel.
THREAD: money (a mixed portfolio carrying every asset's tax rules),
  cooking (a sauce inheriting every ingredient's shelf life), law (a
  contract governed by the most restrictive clause in it).
ASKED-AS: poly cotton mix blend pilling washing label wool acrylic shirt bobbles care instructions

ESSENCE: moving sweat away from skin and holding sweat are opposite
  jobs done by opposite fibres. A fibre that absorbs takes the water
  inside itself and keeps it exactly where it landed; a fibre that
  does not absorb lets the water travel along the gaps between
  fibres to somewhere it can evaporate. So the layer touching the
  skin should be the one that will not drink.
ROOT: physics / a liquid moves along narrow channels by its own
  surface pull, and only where it is not being held.
THREAD: building (a vented cavity taking the water a wall lets
  through), farming (a mulch moving water where roots are), the body
  (blood carrying heat to the skin to lose it).
ASKED-AS: base layer wicking sweat sports fabric clammy cotton merino two layers hiking dry

ESSENCE: colour that sits on the surface rubs off. Dyeing means
  getting the colour molecule inside the fibre and then making it
  unable to leave — by being too big to come back out, by bonding
  chemically to the fibre, or by being made insoluble after it
  arrived. Fastness is the story of which of those three was used.
ROOT: chemistry / a thing stays where it is only if leaving is
  harder than staying, so permanence is always a locked door and
  never a preference.
THREAD: paint (a stain that penetrates against a coat that sits on
  top), the mind (a fact understood against a fact memorised), law
  (a right written into the constitution rather than the statute).
ASKED-AS: dye runs colour fades washing bleeding red sock fastness natural dyeing fixing bright

ESSENCE: most natural dyes have no grip on cloth at all. A mordant
  is a metal salt that grabs the fibre at one end and the dye at the
  other — a broker standing between two things that will not shake
  hands. Change the broker and you change the colour: one dye plant
  gives yellow with alum, olive with iron and rust with copper.
ROOT: chemistry / a bond needs both faces willing, so where they are
  not, a third body with two willing faces is the whole answer.
THREAD: welding (flux preparing a surface that will not otherwise
  join), people together (a trusted go-between making a deal two
  parties could not), medicine (a carrier molecule delivering a drug
  that cannot arrive alone).
ASKED-AS: natural dye plants alum iron mordant colour washed out onion skins wool fixing

ESSENCE: fastness is not one number. A colour can be fast to
  washing and fugitive to daylight; fast to light and rub off onto a
  car seat; fast to everything and destroyed by sweat. Each is a
  separate test against a separate enemy, and a dye can pass any of
  them while failing the others completely.
ROOT: evidence / a claim of durability is meaningless until the
  attack it survived is named.
THREAD: computing (secure against one attack and open to another),
  building (a coating rated for water and not for sunlight), medicine
  (immunity to one strain and none to the next).
ASKED-AS: colour fading curtains sunlight washing rubbing off jeans blue transfer fastness testing dye

ESSENCE: dyeing colours the fibre through its thickness; printing
  lays colour on one face, held there by a binder. So a printed
  cloth has a right side and a wrong side, a stiffness where the
  print sits, and a life set by the glue rather than by the dye — a
  dye fades, but a print cracks, peels and washes away in flakes.
ROOT: craft / the failure plane sits at the weakest layer in the
  stack, and a print adds a layer that was not there before.
THREAD: painting (a stained timber against a painted one), printing
  (ink on paper against paper dyed in the pulp), teaching (learned
  through against memorised on the surface).
ASKED-AS: printed tshirt cracking peeling washing inside out fading pattern dyed through back

ESSENCE: the later in the process colour is added, the cheaper and
  the more responsive the business — and the shallower the result.
  Fibre dyed before spinning gives the deepest, most even colour and
  commits months ahead. Yarn dyed before weaving is the only way to
  get a stripe or a check at all. Piece dyed after weaving is quick,
  cheap, and can only ever be one colour.
ROOT: money / an option kept open has a price, and here the price is
  paid in depth of colour and in what patterns are possible at all.
THREAD: cooking (seasoning early against at the table), manufacture
  (a part painted before or after assembly), management (a choice
  locked at design against one deferred to delivery).
ASKED-AS: striped shirt woven check colour dyed yarn plain fabric fashion season ordering deep

ESSENCE: most of what your hand feels in a shop is finish, not
  cloth. Softeners, resins, coatings, brushing and calendering are
  applied at the end, and a good share of them wash out. So the new
  hand is a sample of the finish, and the fifth wash is the first
  honest sample of the fabric underneath.
ROOT: evidence / a sample must be taken at the state you care about,
  and here the state on the shelf is not the state you will live
  with.
THREAD: cars (a showroom polish over the paint underneath), food (a
  glazed apple), people (an interview performance against six months
  in the job).
ASKED-AS: fabric soft new towels stiff after washing feels different finish coating shop hand

ESSENCE: shrinkage is three unrelated things wearing one word.
  Relaxation: yarns pulled straight by machinery going back to their
  natural crimp, once, cured by pre-shrinking. Swelling: fibres
  fattening when wet so the yarn's path gets shorter, which happens
  every wash and reverses on wearing. Felting: wool's ratchet, which
  is permanent. Each has a different cure and one has none.
ROOT: chemistry / a single visible symptom can have several
  mechanisms, and a remedy is only ever aimed at a mechanism.
THREAD: medicine (one symptom with several diseases behind it),
  building (a crack from settlement, from drying, or from movement),
  computing (one error message from three causes).
ASKED-AS: shrink wash tumble dryer smaller jeans preshrunk wool jumper cold water size

ESSENCE: a pill is a ball of broken fibre ends that rubbing has
  rolled up and that the fabric will not release. A weak fibre snaps
  and the pill falls off unnoticed; a strong one holds on and the
  ball stays. So the durable fabric is the one that looks shabby,
  and the pilling is not evidence of cheapness but of strength.
ROOT: engineering / a failure appears at whichever step in the chain
  gives way first, so making one step stronger relocates the visible
  damage rather than removing it.
THREAD: this file (a seam thread stronger than its cloth moving the
  tear into the cloth), building (a hard repair concentrating the
  movement into the soft brick beside it), dentistry (a filling
  harder than the tooth).
ASKED-AS: bobbles jumper pilling fleece shaver cashmere fuzz balls fabric wear cheap quality

ESSENCE: cloth almost never fails by being pulled apart. It fails at
  the elbow, the seat, the heel and the strap, where something rubs
  it thin — and resistance to rubbing is a different property from
  strength, coming from fibre toughness, from short floats, and from
  a smooth surface. A cloth with a fine tensile figure can wear
  through in a season.
ROOT: evidence / measure the thing you care about, and here the
  laboratory measures the pull while the world applies a rub.
THREAD: engineering (a bearing that fails by wear, not by load),
  roads (a surface rated for weight and destroyed by studs), teaching
  (a course examined on one skill and used for another).
ASKED-AS: wearing through elbows knees trousers seat holes durable fabric rubbing thin strong

ESSENCE: a seam fails in exactly three ways and they need three
  different cures: the thread breaks, the cloth tears along the row
  of needle holes, or the yarns slide sideways and the seam opens
  with every stitch still intact. The commonest mistake in the trade
  is answering the third with a stronger thread, when nothing was
  broken.
ROOT: evidence / a fix aimed at the wrong mechanism does nothing at
  all, however well it is executed.
THREAD: building (a repair to the wrong member), medicine (treating
  a symptom whose cause is elsewhere), computing (adding capacity to
  a system limited by something else).
ASKED-AS: seam split open stitching broke trousers stretched gaping fabric slipping sewing thread strong

ESSENCE: a lockstitch ties its two threads together inside the
  cloth, so cutting one stitch loses one stitch. A chainstitch loops
  through itself, so it is fast, stretchy and undone by pulling one
  end — the whole seam unzips in a single motion. That is why a
  flour sack opens with one tug and why a hem sewn that way
  sometimes vanishes overnight.
ROOT: engineering / a structure where each element depends on the
  next has a single point that releases everything.
THREAD: computing (a chained encryption where one lost block ruins
  the rest), knitting (a dropped stitch running), rope (a knot that
  capsizes and spills in one movement).
ASKED-AS: hem unravelled whole seam pulled thread sack opening chainstitch overlocker stitch type sewing

ESSENCE: a seam has to stretch as far as the cloth it joins, or the
  thread becomes the limit and snaps the first time the garment is
  put on. A straight lockstitch stretches almost none, so a knit
  needs a zigzag, an overlock or a stitch built with slack in it —
  and this is why home-sewn jersey pops at the shoulder.
ROOT: engineering / in any series of parts the least extensible one
  carries the strain and decides the failure.
THREAD: building (a rigid pipe run across a moving joint), the body
  (a scar that will not lengthen with the limb), agreements (a fixed
  clause inside a changing arrangement).
ASKED-AS: sewing jersey stitches popping stretch zigzag overlocker knit fabric machine shoulder seam broke

ESSENCE: some cloths look different from each end, not just along
  each thread — velvet, corduroy, brushed wool, many prints. Every
  piece of the garment has to be laid the same way up on the roll,
  because two panels cut head-to-toe against each other read as two
  different colours under the same light.
ROOT: light / a surface of leaning fibres reflects differently
  according to which way it leans, so shade is a direction and not a
  dye.
THREAD: farming (a mown lawn showing stripes from one mowing),
  photography (the same object lit from two sides), painting
  (brushing a nap all one way).
ASKED-AS: velvet corduroy panels different shade cutting nap direction pattern layout fabric one way

ESSENCE: cloth is bought as a length at a fixed width, so the way
  the pattern pieces are laid out on it is what the garment costs.
  Matching a check, allowing for a nap, or cutting anything on the
  bias all mean leaving cloth unused — which is why cheap clothes
  have small pieces, no matching and no nap, and why those three
  things are what a good garment quietly announces.
ROOT: money / a physical constraint becomes a price, and the price
  becomes a visible class of product.
THREAD: printing (an imposition deciding how many pages per sheet),
  building (standard timber lengths deciding room sizes), computing
  (packing data into fixed blocks).
ASKED-AS: fabric how much needed pattern layout matching checks stripes expensive dress bias cutting

ESSENCE: a garment has to be bigger than the body or it cannot be
  moved in, and the extra comes in two kinds that are often
  confused: the ease that lets you breathe, sit and lift an arm, and
  the ease that makes a shape. Most fit complaints are the wrong
  ease in the wrong place, not the wrong size.
ROOT: the body / a body changes dimension as it moves — a bent elbow
  needs more length across the outside and a sitting body needs
  several inches more around.
THREAD: engineering (clearance designed into a moving assembly),
  building (an expansion gap in a floor), agreements (slack written
  into a schedule on purpose).
ASKED-AS: tight shirt arms raising jacket riding up sitting trousers fit size bigger movement

ESSENCE: a size system is a fiction that everyone agrees to. Bodies
  differ in dozens of independent dimensions and a size number
  carries two or three, so a system fits the average of the
  population it was drawn from and misfits everyone else in all the
  dimensions it never mentioned.
ROOT: mathematics / reducing many dimensions to one number throws
  away everything except what was chosen, and what was thrown away
  does not go anywhere.
THREAD: medicine (a drug dose by weight alone), teaching (one class
  level for children who differ in five ways), computing (a single
  score standing in for a rich profile).
ASKED-AS: sizes different shops medium fits sometimes not vanity sizing measurements chart clothes online

ESSENCE: how a cloth falls is a fight between its bending stiffness
  and its own weight, and stiffness rises roughly with the cube of
  thickness — so a cloth half again as thick is more than three
  times as stiff. That is why drape belongs to fine yarns loosely
  held, and why a heavy fluid cloth and a light board-like one both
  exist.
ROOT: physics / resistance to bending grows very fast with
  thickness, so small changes in thickness are large changes in
  behaviour.
THREAD: engineering (a beam's depth mattering far more than its
  width), paper (card against tissue), hair (fine and heavy hanging
  differently from thick and light).
ASKED-AS: drape fabric hangs stiff flowing dress swatch shop heavy light bolt fluid

ESSENCE: a hide is a three-dimensional felt of collagen fibres that
  the animal wove itself, denser near the outside and looser toward
  the belly. Tanning does not build that structure — it only stops
  it rotting. So leather's strength, its stretch and its markings are
  the animal's history, and different parts of one hide are
  different materials.
ROOT: the living world / a structure grown for a purpose keeps the
  shape of that purpose, and skin was grown to be tough on the back
  and stretchy at the flank.
THREAD: wood (heartwood and sapwood in one plank), meat (the same
  animal giving cuts that behave differently), stone (a block with a
  bed and a grain).
ASKED-AS: leather stretched belt bag quality hide back belly marks scars grain thick

ESSENCE: there are two entirely different materials sold under the
  word leather. Vegetable tanning uses plant tannins, takes weeks,
  and gives a firm thick leather that can be tooled, wet-moulded and
  darkens with age. Chrome tanning takes a day, and gives a soft
  supple water-tolerant leather that never firms up and never tools.
  Same word, opposite behaviour.
ROOT: chemistry / two different agents stabilising the same protein
  leave it stabilised in two different states.
THREAD: metals (quenched and annealed states of one steel), cooking
  (one cut braised or seared into two different foods), building
  (lime and cement mortars behaving oppositely).
ASKED-AS: leather veg tan chrome tooling stamping stiff soft belt wallet moulding darkens

ESSENCE: the strong part of a hide is the thin outer layer where the
  fibres are tightest. A thick hide can be split into two, and only
  the top piece keeps that layer — the underneath piece is loose,
  fuzzy and weak, and when it is coated with plastic and embossed
  with a grain pattern it is still the weak half. The label names
  the layer, not the quality.
ROOT: engineering / strength lives where the structure is dense, so
  cutting a material in two does not give two half-strength pieces.
THREAD: wood (veneer over chipboard), metals (a plated finish over a
  poor base), food (a reformed meat product with a printed surface).
ASKED-AS: genuine leather top grain full split bonded cheap wallet peeling label quality edge

ESSENCE: leather is a protein, so heat is its enemy. In hot water
  collagen shrinks, hardens and turns toward glue; in dry heat the
  fats that keep the fibres sliding leave, and the fibres crack
  against each other. Everything in the care of leather is keeping
  it cool and keeping fat in it.
ROOT: chemistry / cooking a protein is one-way, and leather is a
  protein that has merely been prevented from rotting.
THREAD: cooking (a steak past its temperature), the body (a burn
  that does not reverse), wood (a beam kiln-dried too fast).
ASKED-AS: leather boots dried cracked stiff radiator fire conditioner oil wet drying shrunk

ESSENCE: laid rope is strands twisted one way, made of yarns twisted
  the other, and it holds itself together by exactly the balance
