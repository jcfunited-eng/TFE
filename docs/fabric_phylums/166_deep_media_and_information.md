# 166 DEEP MEDIA AND INFORMATION — how the story reaches people
Between a thing happening and a person knowing about it sit a great
many machines, and each one takes a cut. This file is about those
machines: what they must select, what they cannot carry, who pays
them, and why an honest reporter and a dishonest one both end up
describing a world that is not shaped like the real one.

ESSENCE: every message competes for the one thing that cannot be
  manufactured — a person's minutes. Everything else in this business
  can be increased. So the winner of any contest between stories is
  rarely the truest one; it is the one that got there first and
  refused to be scrolled past.
ROOT: economy / scarcity decides what is fought over, and here the
  scarce thing sits behind the reader's eyes.
THREAD: farming (a market where the scarce thing sets every price),
  medicine (a waiting list rationing a doctor's hours), roads (a lane
  that fills to capacity however many are built).
ASKED-AS: attention economy too much news overwhelmed scrolling competing content time scarce

ESSENCE: who pays decides what the thing is. A page funded by
  advertisers is built to deliver readers to advertisers; one funded
  by readers is built to keep readers paying; one funded by a
  government or an owner is built for whatever that owner wants. The
  content follows the money without anybody deciding to be corrupt.
ROOT: premise — behaviour follows incentives, and an organisation's
  incentives are written by whoever supplies its income.
THREAD: medicine (who funded a trial), law (who pays the expert
  witness), building (a survey commissioned by the seller).
ASKED-AS: who pays for news advertising subscription owner bias funding model influence content

ESSENCE: when advertisers pay, the reader is not the customer — the
  reader is the goods. What the page must produce is a person in a
  buying mood, delivered cheaply and often, which is a different
  product from an informed person and occasionally the opposite one.
ROOT: this file / an organisation serves whoever pays it, and here the
  reader is on the wrong side of that transaction.
THREAD: farming (a crop grown for the buyer's specification rather
  than the eater's), medicine (a service billed to somebody other than
  the patient), television (a programme built around the breaks).
ASKED-AS: advertising funded free news reader is product clickbait advertisers influence commercial pressure

ESSENCE: when readers pay, the outlet must keep them — which fixes
  some things and breaks others. It stops chasing strangers for clicks
  and starts flattering the people who already subscribed, because a
  story that offends the paying audience costs money in a way it never
  did before.
ROOT: this file / an organisation serves whoever pays it, and paying
  readers are a narrower and more particular master than a crowd.
THREAD: theatres (a season programmed for the season-ticket holders),
  churches (a message shaped by the congregation that funds it),
  restaurants (a menu that stops surprising its regulars).
ASKED-AS: paywall subscription news pay for journalism echo chamber readers cancel telling audience

ESSENCE: what a platform can measure is time, clicks and shares, so
  those become the target — and anger travels through all three better
  than anything else. Nobody chose outrage. It simply wins the
  competition that was set up, over and over, until the system looks
  designed for it.
ROOT: premise — behaviour follows incentives, and a measurable proxy
  becomes the incentive for everything that touches it.
THREAD: farming (a crop bred for what the buyer measures, which is
  size, and losing what is not measured, which is taste), schools (a
  syllabus bent toward the examinable), sport (rules that reward what
  the camera can see).
ASKED-AS: algorithm outrage engagement angry posts spread rage bait metrics clicks emotional

ESSENCE: something decides the order in which you see the world, and
  it is a set of rules written by a company to serve its own aims. It
  is an editor — it selects, it promotes, it buries — but it was never
  appointed, cannot be questioned, and has no obligation to explain a
  single decision.
ROOT: people and power / whoever chooses what is seen holds most of
  the power in a channel, whether the chooser is a person or a
  procedure.
THREAD: libraries (what is put on the front table), government (an
  agenda that decides which motions get heard), schools (a reading
  list that is an argument nobody made aloud).
ASKED-AS: algorithm decides what i see feed ranking unelected editor platform control transparency

ESSENCE: whatever a platform rewards, the people who make things
  become. Lengths, titles, thumbnails, the first eight seconds, the
  rhythm of a sentence — all of it is reshaped to fit a scoring rule,
  so a change to that rule remakes an entire craft in a fortnight.
ROOT: this file / the payer sets the shape, and here the payer is a
  formula that pays in visibility.
THREAD: farming (a variety grown because it travels, not because it
  tastes), architecture (buildings shaped by what the code permits),
  sport (technique reshaped by a scoring change).
ASKED-AS: creators chase algorithm thumbnails titles format changed rules livelihood platform dependent

ESSENCE: people are not sealed inside separate worlds. Most heavy news
  users meet more disagreement online than off, not less. But the
  effect is real at the edge — for the few who go deepest, the path
  down is smooth, well lit and never once suggests going back.
ROOT: evidence / an average across everybody can be small while the
  tail is severe, and the tail is where the harm lives.
THREAD: medicine (a drug safe for most and dangerous for a few),
  roads (a design fine for ordinary drivers and lethal at the extreme),
  schools (a class where the average conceals both ends).
ASKED-AS: filter bubble echo chamber radicalisation rabbit hole recommendations extreme content overstated

ESSENCE: the more common act is not seeking agreement but avoiding the
  whole thing. Faced with more news than anyone can carry, and most of
  it distressing and unactionable, a large share of people simply stop
  — and a public that has switched off cannot be reached by better
  reporting.
ROOT: the mind / attention is one beam and distress without a possible
  action is a cost with no return, so people stop paying it.
THREAD: medicine (a patient who stops opening letters from the
  hospital), charity (appeals so relentless that people stop reading
  them), households (a pile of unopened post).
ASKED-AS: stopped watching news avoid depressing switch off overwhelmed tune out doom fatigue

ESSENCE: a thing spreads when passing it on does something for the
  passer. It must be quick to grasp, must fit a feeling strong enough
  to move a thumb, and must make the sharer look informed, funny or
  righteous. Truth is not on the list, and neither is importance.
ROOT: people together / a message travels by being useful to the
  messenger, so the messenger's needs select what survives.
THREAD: language (a proverb survives by being repeatable), disease
  (spread depending on contact patterns rather than severity), music
  (a tune that can be hummed by somebody who heard it once).
ASKED-AS: viral why things spread shares emotional simple identity signal luck unpredictable

ESSENCE: the correction never travels as far as the error. The first
  story arrives when the subject is hot and everyone is looking; the
  correction arrives days later, smaller, to an audience that has
  moved on and already told other people the wrong thing.
ROOT: this file / attention is scarce and it has already been spent
  on the first version.
THREAD: medicine (a withdrawn study still cited for decades), law (an
  acquittal that never catches the arrest), families (an apology that
  reaches fewer people than the accusation).
ASKED-AS: correction retraction never seen apology page fourteen false story spread damage

ESSENCE: the first report of anything is wrong in some particular, and
  not because anyone is careless. In the hours when a thing is news,
  the facts are held by frightened, partial, uncertain witnesses — so
  speed and accuracy are being traded against each other, live, with
  the trade never stated.
ROOT: evidence / verification takes time, and being first has a value
  that is collected before verification is possible.
THREAD: medicine (a diagnosis on arrival against one after tests),
  courts (an arrest against a verdict), weather (a first radar echo
  against a confirmed track).
ASKED-AS: breaking news wrong first reports early details incorrect rushed live coverage speed

ESSENCE: a report is not finished when it is understood, it is
  finished when the time runs out. Everything published is a draft
  that a clock stopped — which is why the same journalist's account of
  the same events is better a year later and reaches almost nobody.
ROOT: this file / a schedule has to be met, so understanding is
  whatever could be assembled inside the interval.
THREAD: medicine (treating on what is known at three in the morning),
  building (a design frozen so the work can start), law (a verdict
  reached on a date rather than on completeness).
ASKED-AS: deadline rushed story incomplete published later better understanding time pressure journalism

ESSENCE: to be understood by a general reader, a complicated thing has
  to be cut down, and the cutting is done by somebody who is not an
  expert in it. Most errors in reporting are neither lies nor
  laziness — they are the places where something true would not fit.
ROOT: language / a summary is a lossy copy, and the losses are chosen
  by whoever cannot see what is being lost.
THREAD: teaching (a first explanation that is a useful lie), medicine
  (a consent conversation short enough to be heard), maps (a scale
  that forces every road to be drawn too wide).
ASKED-AS: reporting simplified wrong details experts annoyed complicated subject general audience explain

ESSENCE: news runs on a cycle, and the cycle is a property of the
  business rather than of events. There is a slot to fill every day
  whether or not anything happened, and a story lives roughly as long
  as the machine can find a new angle for it — then it ends, resolved
  or not.
ROOT: this file / a publication is a factory with a schedule, and a
  schedule must be filled regardless of supply.
THREAD: farming (a mill that must be fed whatever the harvest),
  television (a schedule that cannot go dark), schools (a lesson
  filling its hour whether or not it needed one).
ASKED-AS: news cycle story died down moved on daily slot filling nothing happened coverage

ESSENCE: news means the unusual, never the important. A hundred
  thousand people doing the ordinary thing safely is not reportable
  and one collapse is — so the picture assembled from reporting is a
  catalogue of exceptions presented as if it were a description.
ROOT: information / a message carries only what could have been
  otherwise, so the routine is unreportable by construction.
THREAD: medicine (a case report never describing the recovered
  majority), aviation (crashes reported, safe landings not), farming
  (the one failed harvest that is the only one anybody hears about).
ASKED-AS: news only bad things unusual rare events normal not reported picture distorted

ESSENCE: the slow catastrophes go uncovered. A thing that kills
  steadily for thirty years never has a day on which it happened, so
  it has no peg, no picture, and no moment — and it loses every
  contest to a sudden small event that does.
ROOT: this file / reporting attaches to events, and a gradual process
  contains no event to attach to.
THREAD: medicine (a chronic disease against an outbreak), the
  environment (a slow loss against an oil spill), farming (soil
  thinning over decades and a flood in an afternoon).
ASKED-AS: slow disaster no coverage chronic problem gradual crisis ignored no event peg

ESSENCE: which story gets covered is decided before any judgement of
  importance, by whether there is a peg to hang it on — a report
  published today, a figure released, an anniversary, somebody
  important saying something. This is why old facts become news the
  week a document with a date arrives.
ROOT: this file / an article must be about something that happened,
  because the form requires a today.
THREAD: courts (a case that becomes news on the day of a hearing),
  research (a finding known for years and reported on publication),
  government (an announcement timed to be the day's event).
ASKED-AS: news peg why now anniversary report released timing story hook old facts

ESSENCE: how dangerous something feels is decided by how easily an
  example comes to mind, and examples come to mind because they were
  vivid and repeated. So a public's fears track coverage rather than
  frequency, and the rarer a horror is the more coverage each instance
  gets.
ROOT: the mind / a judgement of how common a thing is comes from how
  quickly an instance can be recalled, which measures memorability
  instead.
THREAD: medicine (patients fearing a rare disease and ignoring the
  common one), transport (fear of flying and comfort in a car), crime
  (perceived rise during a measured fall).
ASKED-AS: people scared wrong things rare events feel common risk perception news coverage

ESSENCE: the strongest choice a report makes is not the wording, it is
  where the story starts and who is at its centre. The same events told
  from a week earlier, or through a different person, produce opposite
  conclusions without a single false statement anywhere in either
  version.
ROOT: story / a narrative needs a beginning and a protagonist, and
  both are chosen rather than found.
THREAD: law (a charge that decides what the trial is about), history (a
  war that begins at a different date in two countries), families (an
  argument whose story starts at whichever moment suits the teller).
ASKED-AS: framing same facts different conclusion story starts whose perspective slant both true

ESSENCE: for most readers the headline is the article. It is written
  by somebody other than the reporter, to be clicked rather than to be
  accurate, and it has to survive being read on its own by a person
  who will never see the qualification in paragraph nine.
ROOT: this file / attention is scarce, so the shortest part of a piece
  is the part that is actually consumed.
THREAD: medicine (a leaflet whose warnings nobody reaches), law (terms
  that are agreed unread), packaging (a front of a box against the
  panel on the back).
ASKED-AS: headline misleading only read title clickbait subeditor wrote article says different

ESSENCE: a number breaks free of the work that produced it. The
  careful study becomes a press release, the release becomes a
  headline, the headline becomes a fact everybody knows — and by the
  third step the conditions, the sample and the doubt have all been
  left behind and cannot be reattached.
ROOT: information / each retelling compresses, and what gets dropped
  first is whatever qualifies the claim.
THREAD: language (a saying that outlives its context), medicine (a
  dose remembered without the condition it applied to), maps (a scale
  bar lost in the copying).
ASKED-AS: statistic everyone quotes source study press release misrepresented number context lost

ESSENCE: a story needs somebody it is happening to. Where there is no
  protagonist — a slow systemic failure, a diffuse harm, an arithmetic
  problem — there is no story, so those things go unreported no matter
  how large they are.
ROOT: story / narrative requires an actor with a want, and processes
  have neither.
THREAD: charity (one named child raising more than a statistic about
  millions), law (a case that must be about parties), medicine (a
  disease covered through one patient).
ASKED-AS: systemic problem no story faceless statistics human interest one person example

ESSENCE: a reporter who covers something powerful depends on being let
  in. Access is granted by the people being covered and withdrawn from
  whoever writes the wrong thing — so the reporter closest to a story
  is the one least free to describe it, and both sides know that
  perfectly well.
ROOT: people and power / whoever controls a scarce input can price it,
  and here the input is being told anything at all.
THREAD: medicine (a specialist who depends on the department they
  might criticise), sport (a correspondent who travels with the team),
  government (a lobby that keeps its passes by not embarrassing
  anyone).
ASKED-AS: access journalism insider reporters soft questions sources cut off criticism embedded

ESSENCE: an unnamed source lets a true thing be told that could not be
  told otherwise, and it also lets somebody attack a rival at no cost
  to themselves. The same convention protects the whistleblower and
  the briefer, and from outside the two are indistinguishable.
ROOT: evidence / a claim's weight comes partly from who is staking
  their name on it, and anonymity removes that stake.
THREAD: law (a protected witness), medicine (a confidential incident
  report), workplaces (an unsigned complaint that may be brave or may
  be cowardly).
ASKED-AS: anonymous sources unnamed officials trust reporting leaks protected identity credibility

ESSENCE: most leaks are not rebellions, they are moves. Somebody
  chooses this document, this reporter and this week, and the choosing
  is the act — a leak reaches the public having already been aimed,
  and its selection carries an argument the document itself does not
  make.
ROOT: people and power / releasing information is an action with a
  purpose, and the purpose lives in the timing and the selection.
THREAD: law (evidence disclosed selectively), sport (a story placed to
  unsettle a rival), families (one letter produced from a long
  correspondence).
ASKED-AS: leak documents released timing motive who benefits selective strategic anonymous briefing

ESSENCE: a proposal is sometimes released in order to be shot at.
  Floated through an unnamed source, it is not an announcement but an
  experiment on the public — and if the reaction is bad it was never
  policy, merely speculation, and nobody has to withdraw anything.
ROOT: people and power / a position stated publicly must be defended,
  so a position stated deniably can be abandoned for free.
THREAD: negotiation (an offer made through an intermediary), sport (a
  transfer story that measures the reaction of the crowd), families (a
  suggestion made as a joke to see how it lands).
ASKED-AS: trial balloon floated idea unnamed official testing reaction deniable proposal leaked

ESSENCE: a great deal of what looks like reporting is a press release
  with the verbs changed. It arrives free, ready-written, already in
  the format the page needs, at an outlet with fewer staff than pages
  — and using it is not laziness so much as arithmetic.
ROOT: this file / a newsroom must fill a schedule with a fixed budget,
  and subsidised material is the cheapest possible input.
THREAD: farming (a subsidised import undercutting local production),
  schools (a lesson from a free industry pack), medicine (a briefing
  supplied by whoever makes the treatment).
ASKED-AS: press release journalism churnalism rewritten pr sources newsroom cuts free content

ESSENCE: an advertisement dressed as an article buys something an
  advertisement cannot: the trust the reader extends to the
  publication. The label exists, is small, and is missed by most
  people — which is not an accident but the mechanism being paid for.
ROOT: evidence / a claim's weight comes from its source, so disguising
  the source transfers weight that was never earned.
THREAD: shops (an own-brand packaged to look like the maker's),
  medicine (education funded by whoever sells the treatment), letters
  (a circular printed to look handwritten).
ASKED-AS: sponsored content native advertising looks like article label disclosure paid promotion

ESSENCE: what an owner or sponsor blocks matters less than what never
  gets started. Nobody has to censor anything: an editor with a
  mortgage learns which subjects consume a year and end badly, and
  those subjects quietly stop being proposed.
ROOT: people together / a rule is learned from what happens to the
  people who test it, and the lesson spreads without ever being
  stated.
THREAD: schools (a teacher who stops raising a subject), medicine (a
  researcher who does not apply for the awkward grant), workplaces (a
  complaint that everybody has learned not to file).
ASKED-AS: owner influence stories not written self censorship pressure sponsors avoided subjects

ESSENCE: when local reporting dies, nobody watches the council, the
  courts or the contracts — and the measurable results are not
  ignorance but expense. Borrowing costs rise, fewer people stand for
  office, turnout falls, and the same-party ticket gets voted down the
  line because nobody knows anything about anybody.
ROOT: people and power / unwatched power drifts toward its holder, and
  local reporting was the watching.
THREAD: farming (a hedge nobody maintains until the field is gone),
  building (an inspection regime that lapses), medicine (a screening
  programme quietly ended and the cases that follow).
ASKED-AS: local newspaper closed council unwatched corruption civic turnout costs news desert

ESSENCE: the case for a publicly funded broadcaster is that some
  reporting is worth more than anybody will pay for it — everyone
  benefits from a watched council whether or not they read about it,
  so it is systematically underfunded by every market that has ever
  been tried.
ROOT: people and power / a benefit nobody can be shut out of will not
  be funded voluntarily at scale.
THREAD: roads (built by everybody because nobody would build them
  alone), medicine (public health that pays for people who never
  attend), lighthouses (a light no ship can be charged for).
ASKED-AS: public broadcasting licence fee taxpayer funded independent state media argument justify

ESSENCE: propaganda rarely tries to make you believe a specific lie.
  It works by repetition until a claim feels familiar, by supplying
  the words an argument must be had in, and by making one side feel
  like the normal side. Belief follows familiarity more reliably than
  it follows evidence.
ROOT: the mind / a claim heard often feels true, because ease of
  recall is mistaken for having checked it.
THREAD: advertising (a name repeated until it feels chosen),
  education (a framing absorbed before any argument), language (a
  loaded word that settles the question before the sentence ends).
ASKED-AS: propaganda repetition believe lies familiar messaging state media convince population

ESSENCE: the modern way to suppress a fact is not to hide it. It is to
  surround it — with denials, alternatives, mockery, competing
  accusations and enough noise that an ordinary person concludes
  nobody can know. Doubt is cheaper to manufacture than belief and
  achieves the same end.
ROOT: evidence / a claim is judged against the field of claims around
  it, so adding claims degrades every one of them.
THREAD: law (a defence that offers six incompatible explanations),
  medicine (a settled question kept open by manufactured
  controversy), markets (a rumour that makes a real signal
  unreadable).
ASKED-AS: flood the zone noise doubt confusion too many claims nobody knows censorship

ESSENCE: two different things wear one word. Misinformation is wrong
  and passed on in good faith by somebody who believes it.
  Disinformation is wrong and released on purpose. They look identical
  in the wild, they spread through the same people, and they need
  different responses.
ROOT: evidence / the truth of a claim and the intent behind it are
  separate facts, and only one of them is visible in the claim.
THREAD: medicine (an error and a fraud producing the same wrong
  result), law (a mistake and a lie under oath), teaching (a pupil who
  is confused and one who is copying).
ASKED-AS: misinformation disinformation difference intent deliberate spreading false shared good faith

ESSENCE: checking a claim is slow, expensive and reaches a fraction of
  the people the claim reached. Worse, the audience most exposed to a
  falsehood is the audience least likely to seek out an organisation
  built to contradict it.
ROOT: this file / attention already spent cannot be recalled, and the
  check arrives after the spending.
THREAD: medicine (a rumour about a treatment outrunning every
  correction), law (a retraction that reaches nobody who read the
  charge), gossip (a denial that spreads to a fraction of the
  original).
ASKED-AS: fact checking works reach audience debunking false claims slow effort convinced

ESSENCE: warning people in advance works better than correcting them
  afterwards. Show somebody the shape of a manipulation before they
  meet it and they recognise it later; wait until they believe
  something and you are asking them to admit they were fooled, which
  is a far larger request.
ROOT: the mind / a belief once held defends itself, so the cheap
  moment is before it is held.
THREAD: medicine (a vaccine given before exposure), teaching (showing
  a trick before it is played), security (a drill run before the
  emergency).
ASKED-AS: prebunking inoculation warn advance manipulation techniques resistant correcting afterwards backfire

ESSENCE: media literacy is not suspicion. Suspicion applied evenly
  makes a person believe nothing and therefore anything. The real
  components are narrow and teachable: leave the page to find out who
  is speaking, look for the original, and check whether the claim is
  reported anywhere by people with something to lose.
ROOT: evidence / a claim's weight comes from its provenance, so the
  work is tracing the provenance rather than inspecting the claim.
THREAD: accounting (a figure traced back to its document), law (chain
  of custody), cooking (checking what is actually in a jar rather than
  what the label announces).
ASKED-AS: how to spot fake news check sources verify claim reliable website teaching literacy

ESSENCE: asking journalism to be neutral misunderstands what it does.
  Every act of reporting is a selection — what to cover, whom to ask,
  where to begin, what to leave out — and selection cannot be neutral
  because a selection is a judgement. Honest practice is not
  neutrality; it is showing your working.
ROOT: evidence / an instrument records what it was built to record,
  and here the instrument is a person choosing.
THREAD: mapping (a map that must leave things out to be readable),
  history (a period described by what its writer thought mattered),
  photography (a frame that excludes by existing).
ASKED-AS: media bias objective neutral reporting both sides selection unavoidable transparency honest

ESSENCE: giving two sides equal time is not fairness when one side is
  a settled body of evidence and the other is three people. Balance
  measures the number of positions, not their weight — so the format
  itself transmits a false impression of how much doubt exists.
ROOT: evidence / weight of evidence and count of positions are
  different quantities, and a debate format displays only the second.
THREAD: courts (an expert on each side implying an even split),
  medicine (a fringe view given a platform beside a consensus),
  schools (a debate format applied to a matter of fact).
ASKED-AS: both sides false balance debate equal time consensus fringe view platform

ESSENCE: partisans on both sides of a dispute watch the same coverage
  and each conclude it favours the other. It is not hypocrisy: a
  strongly held position makes even-handed treatment feel like an
  attack, because it declines to say the obvious thing.
ROOT: the mind / a belief supplies the standard by which its own
  evidence is judged, so neutrality is measured from where the viewer
  stands.
THREAD: sport (both sets of fans certain the referee was against
  them), families (a parent accused of favouritism by every child),
  law (a judge described as biased by whoever lost).
ASKED-AS: everyone thinks media biased against them hostile perception both sides complain fair

ESSENCE: coverage rarely changes what people think and reliably
  changes what they think about. The list of subjects a country
  believes are the important ones is assembled almost entirely by
  what has been covered lately — and that list decides elections
  without anybody's opinion having moved.
ROOT: the mind / attention is one beam, and whatever is not in it is
  not weighed at all.
THREAD: schools (a syllabus deciding what a generation considers
  knowledge), households (an argument about whatever was mentioned at
  breakfast), shops (a display that decides what gets compared).
ASKED-AS: agenda setting what people talk about issues important media influence opinion topics

ESSENCE: covering a contest is easier than covering a question. Who is
  ahead, who gained, who fell — it is fresh daily, requires no
  expertise, and cannot be wrong for long. Reporting on what would
  actually be done is difficult, dull, and dated the moment anything
  changes.
ROOT: this file / a newsroom must fill a schedule, and a race supplies
  an endless stream of new facts while a policy supplies almost none.
THREAD: sport (the table covered more closely than the play), schools
  (a league position discussed more than the teaching), markets (a
  price watched rather than a business).
ASKED-AS: horse race polls who is winning election coverage policy substance ignored campaign

ESSENCE: some events exist only because they will be covered. A
  ceremony with nothing behind it, an announcement of an intention to
  announce, a stunt arranged for the picture — the coverage is not
  reporting the event, the coverage is the event, and everyone
  involved knows it.
ROOT: this file / reporting requires something to have happened, so
  anybody who wants coverage can supply the happening.
THREAD: sport (a presentation staged for the cameras), politics (a
  visit arranged entirely for one photograph), business (an
  announcement of a plan to make an announcement).
ASKED-AS: photo opportunity staged event announcement stunt manufactured news pseudo event coverage

ESSENCE: the real damage from convincing fakes is not that people
  believe false things. It is that everybody now has a defence: any
  recording, however genuine, can be waved away as fabricated. The
  liar gains more from the possibility of fakes than the forger gains
  from any actual fake.
ROOT: evidence / a kind of proof is only worth what it would cost to
  counterfeit, and cheap counterfeiting destroys the whole class.
THREAD: money (a note that stops being accepted once forgery is easy),
  law (a signature that no longer proves anything), photography (a
  picture that stopped being an argument).
ASKED-AS: deepfakes video evidence denied fake claim real footage doubt proof worthless

ESSENCE: an interview is raw material. What is broadcast has been cut,
  ordered and placed, and a person who spoke for an hour is presented
  through ninety seconds chosen by somebody with a story already in
  mind. Every sentence can be theirs and the impression can be
  invented.
ROOT: story / a narrative is made by selection and order, and both
  belong to whoever edits rather than to whoever spoke.
THREAD: law (a transcript quoted in part), music (a performance
  assembled from takes), families (a remark repeated without the
  conversation around it).
ASKED-AS: quote out of context edited interview clip misrepresented selective editing hour recorded

ESSENCE: a crowd and a small determined group look identical through a
  channel that transmits volume rather than headcount. A hundred
  accounts can produce the appearance of a public, and every
  measurement available — replies, shares, trending — counts messages
  and not people.
ROOT: evidence / a measurement records what it can count, and here
  what it counts is cheap to multiply.
THREAD: theatre (a claque hired to applaud), politics (a postbag
  organised by one campaign), reviews (a rating built by people who
  never bought the thing).
ASKED-AS: bots trending outrage online everyone angry small group astroturf fake accounts

ESSENCE: the people who post are not the people who watch, and they
  are not typical of them. A comment section, a reply column and a
  trending list are all written by a small, unusual, highly motivated
  minority — and reading them as the public's view is a mistake that
  moves real decisions.
ROOT: evidence / a sample selected by willingness to participate
  describes the willing and nobody else.
THREAD: meetings (a room's view set by whoever speaks), surveys (a
  response rate that decides the answer), schools (a class judged by
  three loud pupils).
ASKED-AS: comments section vocal minority not representative online reaction typical readers silent

ESSENCE: something said to a room can now be heard by everybody, and
  it arrives without the room. A joke among colleagues, a shorthand
  among specialists, a sentence that meant one thing on a Tuesday
  among friends — each becomes evidence in front of an audience that
  was never there.
ROOT: language / meaning is completed by the situation, and a
  recording carries the words and leaves the situation behind.
THREAD: law (a remark quoted without the conversation around it),
  families (a private complaint repeated outside the house), theatre
  (a rehearsal watched by an audience).
ASKED-AS: taken out of context joke online everyone saw private remark public audience
