# 133 DEEP PUBLIC HEALTH — keeping populations well
Health practice holds what one trained pair of hands does for one
patient. This file holds the other trade: the one whose patient is
a whole population, whose instruments are water, law, tax, roads
and trust, and whose successes are invisible by construction.
Where the evidence is genuinely unsettled the entry says UNSURE.

ESSENCE: a doctor asks what is wrong with this person. A population
  worker asks what is different about this crowd, and the two questions
  have different answers about the same disease. Why one man has lung
  cancer and why this country has a hundred thousand cases are separate
  problems with separate solutions.

  for an individual; evidence + measurement (the unit of analysis
  decides what can be seen at all).
THREAD: evidence + measurement (a question that determines its own
  answer), money (a firm's accounts against an economy's), history (why
  a change in a whole society is never the sum of decisions).

ESSENCE: most cases of most common diseases come from the enormous
  middle of the population, not from its dangerous edge. A small extra
  risk carried by millions produces more illness than a large risk
  carried by thousands — so the measure that helps a whole country
  barely helps any single person in it, and feels pointless to each of
  them.
ROOT: mathematics / a small number multiplied by a very large one beats
  a large number multiplied by a small one; this file (rates belong to
  crowds and are invisible from inside).
THREAD: chance + risk (rare times many equals often), money (a penny on
  every transaction against a fortune from a few), power (a policy whose
  beneficiaries never know who they were).

ESSENCE: there are two ways to attack a disease and they are not
  alternatives. Find the highest-risk people and treat them intensively,
  or shift the whole population slightly. The first is efficient per
  person and misses most cases; the second catches most cases and treats
  mainly people who were never going to be ill.

THREAD: money (a few large accounts against a mass market), law (a rule
  aimed at offenders and one aimed at everybody), strategy + conflict.

ESSENCE: a risk factor is something that travels with a disease. A cause
  is something that, when moved, moves the disease. Most of what gets
  called a cause has only ever been shown to travel — and the way to
  find out is to move it and watch, which is expensive and often
  impossible.

THREAD: nutrition (a nutrient that marked a food and did nothing on its
  own), evidence + measurement (the untouched twin), money (a leading
  indicator mistaken for a lever).

ESSENCE: what is worth fixing in a population depends on how strong a
  factor is and on how many people carry it, multiplied together. A
  cause that triples the risk in one percent of people matters less than
  one that raises it a fifth in everybody. The question is never how
  dangerous, it is how much of this disease would disappear.

ESSENCE: the honest unit for a preventive measure is how many people

  be hundreds — hundreds of people taking a tablet daily for years so
  that one avoids an event.

ESSENCE: the people who do the healthy thing differ from everyone else
  in a hundred other ways — richer, better educated, more likely to
  attend appointments, less likely to smoke. Any measured benefit of the
  healthy thing carries all of that inside it, and no statistical
  adjustment removes what was never measured.

ESSENCE: when a trial is impossible, the next best thing is a change
  that arrived like a coin toss — a law passed in one region and not the
  next, a lottery for a programme, an inherited difference that shuffles
  at conception. Most of what is confidently known about causes in
  populations was learnt this way.

ESSENCE: a pattern true of countries need not be true of people. Nations
  that eat more fat have more heart disease, and within any nation the
  individuals who eat most fat may not. The two are different questions
  with different answers, and mixing them has produced some of the
  field's largest and longest mistakes.

ESSENCE: the considerations for judging whether an association is a
  cause — strength, consistency, dose-response, timing, plausible
  mechanism — are a way of organising an argument, not a test that can
  be passed. Their author said so explicitly and everyone has been
  treating them as a checklist ever since.

ESSENCE: survival after diagnosis is the wrong measure, and it is the
  one everybody quotes. Diagnose people earlier and they survive longer
  from diagnosis while dying on exactly the same day. The measure that
  cannot be fooled this way is deaths in the whole population, which is
  why it is the one that matters and the one nobody finds persuasive.

ESSENCE: screening catches slow disease preferentially, because slow
  disease sits around waiting to be found while fast disease appears and
  kills between appointments. So the cases a programme finds are, by
  construction, gentler than the cases it misses — and the programme is
  then credited with their gentleness.
ROOT: chance + risk / a sampling method that selects on duration
  over-represents the long-lasting; this file (a measure that creates
  the population it is measured on).
THREAD: health practice (a harvest of findings that would never have
  progressed), chance + risk (a sample selected by how long something
  lasts), evidence + measurement.

ESSENCE: before a programme is worth running, several things must all be
  true: the disease must be common enough and serious enough, there must
  be an early stage, treating that stage must work better than treating
  the late one, the test must be tolerable and reasonably accurate, and
  the system must be able to handle everyone it flags. Fail any one and
  the programme does harm.
ROOT: engineering / a chain delivers nothing if any link is missing, and
  a screening programme is a long chain ending in treatment; health
  practice (a positive result is a beginning, and everything after it is
  where the harm lives).
THREAD: engineering (a process chain failing at its weakest stage),
  health practice (each false alarm buying its own procedure), money (a
  programme costed at the test and not at the pathway).

ESSENCE: a screening test does not diagnose anybody. It sorts a
  population into a small group worth investigating and a large group
  that can be left alone, and everything that matters happens in the
  investigating. The public hears a verdict; the programme issued a
  sorting.
ROOT: evidence + measurement / a cheap test tuned to miss little must
  therefore flag many, so its output is a shortlist rather than an
  answer; health practice (a result updates a chance, it does not
  replace it).
THREAD: chance + risk (a threshold chosen between two ways of being
  wrong), health practice (reading a positive against the
  before-chance), evidence + measurement.

ESSENCE: the number that decides an outbreak's shape is how many people
  one case infects on average. Above one it grows, below one it dies
  out, and every control measure is an attempt to push it under. It is
  not a property of the germ alone — it depends on how people live,
  travel, crowd and behave, so it differs between cities and changes
  weekly.
ROOT: mathematics / a quantity multiplied by itself repeatedly either
  runs away or vanishes, and one is the dividing line; this file (a rate
  is a property of a population and its habits).
THREAD: chance + risk (multiplication below one and above it), computing
  (a network's spread threshold, identical arithmetic), money
  (compounding that runs away or dies).

ESSENCE: the coverage a population needs to stop a disease is set by how
  contagious it is, so it differs enormously by disease — measles needs
  almost everybody and several others need far less. And it is not a
  national average: a country at the threshold overall still burns
  wherever the unvaccinated cluster together.

  case reaches, which is a local quantity; health practice (protection
  that is shared cannot be opted out of privately).
THREAD: chance + risk (an average hiding a distribution), computing (a
  network whose connections are clustered rather than even), people

ESSENCE: most people infect nobody and a few infect very many. That
  lopsidedness is the rule rather than the exception, and it changes
  everything: an outbreak can die out repeatedly by chance before one
  gathering sets it running, and control that finds the events rather
  than the average does far more per unit of effort.
ROOT: chance + risk / an average says nothing about a spread, and where
  the spread is extreme the average describes almost nobody; this file
  (the same total transmission arranged two ways behaves differently).
THREAD: chance + risk (rare events dominating a total), money (a
  portfolio whose return comes from a handful of positions), people
  together (the gathering as the unit of transmission).
ASKED-AS: superspreader event most people infect nobody average variation gatherings indoor outbreak clusters control

ESSENCE: the gap between catching something and showing it decides what
  any control measure can do. A long gap gives tracers time to reach
  contacts before they infect anyone; a short one means the chain has
  already moved on. Quarantine lengths, tracing windows and travel rules
  are all this one number wearing different clothes.
ROOT: this file / control is a race between the spread and the response,

  strategy + conflict (an operation's feasibility set by the enemy's
  cycle time).
THREAD: strategy + conflict (getting inside an opponent's decision
  cycle), computing (patching before the worm arrives), health practice
  (a treatment window measured in hours).

ESSENCE: control built on symptoms fails against a disease that spreads
  before them or without them. Fever checks at borders, staying home
  when ill, isolating the visibly sick — all of it assumes the
  infectious and the unwell are the same people, and for several
  important diseases they are not.
ROOT: this file / a measure can only act on what it can detect, so an
  undetectable fraction sets a ceiling on what the measure can achieve;
  evidence + measurement (a test that misses a category entirely).
THREAD: health practice (a reserve masking a loss until it is gone),
  computing (an infection that spreads before any symptom appears in the
  logs), evidence + measurement.

ESSENCE: contact tracing works when four things hold at once: cases are
  found fast, contacts are reached faster, people can afford to stay
  home, and the number of cases is small enough for the staff available.
  Take any one away and it becomes an expensive way to produce
  statistics.
ROOT: engineering / a system with several serial requirements succeeds

  disease's own clock).
THREAD: money (an intervention whose binding constraint is a household's
  cash flow), engineering (a chain of serial dependencies), power (a
  measure that requires the public to cooperate and must therefore be
  worth cooperating with).

ESSENCE: quarantine is for the exposed and isolation is for the
  infected, and the difference is the whole logic. Quarantine restricts
  people who have done nothing and may well be perfectly healthy, which
  is why it is the harder thing to justify and the more powerful thing
  to do — it acts before the infectiousness starts.

ESSENCE: how deadly a disease is depends entirely on the denominator.
  Deaths divided by known cases is one number; deaths divided by
  everyone actually infected is another and always smaller, because the
  mild and the silent were never counted. Most public arguments about
  lethality are arguments about which denominator.

  as by what goes on top; evidence + measurement (the measured

  chance + risk (a rate quoted without its base), money (a return
  computed on the wrong capital).

ESSENCE: the numerator is usually easy and the denominator is usually
  the hard part. Counting the cases, the deaths, the events is laborious
  and doable; knowing how many people were exposed, in what age
  structure, over what period, is the work that decides whether the

ESSENCE: a case definition is a decision, and it moves the numbers.
  Widen it and the epidemic appears to grow; narrow it and it appears to
  shrink; change it mid-outbreak and the curve has a step in it that no
  germ caused. Every long time series of disease has these steps hidden
  in it.

ESSENCE: the count that survives every argument about definitions,
  testing and attribution is how many more people died than usually die.
  It needs no diagnosis, no test and no committee. It captures the
  deaths a crisis caused indirectly, and it is frequently much larger
  than the official toll.

ESSENCE: surveillance sees what it looks at. A rise in reported cases
  can be a rise in disease, in testing, in awareness, or in reporting
  rules — and the four look identical in the data. Whatever is not
  routinely counted is invisible to policy, which is why the diseases of
  poor and stigmatised people are systematically under-recorded.

ESSENCE: acting early against an epidemic always looks like an
  overreaction, because if it works nothing happens. The measures that
  would have been proportionate are only visible in the disaster they
  prevented, so the successful public health official is
  indistinguishable from the one who panicked over nothing.

ESSENCE: in an outbreak, speed usually beats precision. A measure taken
  a week early with imperfect information does more than a perfect one
  taken a week late, because the thing being fought multiplies while the
  analysis is done. The correct posture is to act on the plausible bad
  case and revise loudly.

ESSENCE: as countries get richer their diseases change hands. The
  infections that killed children retreat, life expectancy climbs, and
  the ground is taken by heart disease, cancer, diabetes and dementia —
  conditions of long life and long exposure. The institutions built for
  the first set are usually still there, aimed at the wrong target.
ROOT: history / a system built for one problem persists after the
  problem does; this file (a population's disease pattern is set by its
  age structure and its exposures).
THREAD: history (institutions outliving their purpose), health practice
  (prevention against cure at national scale), money (a cost structure
  that changes shape as a business matures).
ASKED-AS: diseases changed heart cancer diabetes infections used to kill children hospitals system transition

ESSENCE: the famous figure of a life expectancy in the thirties never

  down by enormous numbers of dead infants, and the adults who survived
  childhood often reached old age. Almost all of the historical rise
  came from stopping children dying.

  observation about how services distribute themselves, not an

ESSENCE: a new health measure is taken up first by the people who were

ESSENCE: crowding, cold, damp and poor ventilation are medical facts.
  Respiratory infections spread with shared indoor air, cold homes kill

  in children. Housing policy is respiratory medicine conducted with

ESSENCE: some exposures have no safe level. Lead damages developing
  brains at every concentration measured, fine particles from combustion
  shorten lives with no threshold anyone has found. These cannot be
  managed by advice, because nobody can smell them and no individual can
  avoid them — they are removed by law or not at all.

THREAD: power (regulation as the only available instrument), energy
