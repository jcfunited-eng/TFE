# 165 DEEP BUSINESS AND ORGANISATIONS — how enterprises actually work
A company is two machines bolted together: one that makes something
people want, and one that gets people to arrange themselves and do it.
Both have laws, and the laws mostly say no. Why firms with full order
books run out of money, why the right decision for today's customer
ends the company, and why the shape of a team ends up inside the
product.

ESSENCE: a business is not a good idea and it is not a sale. It is a
  way of making something people want that can be done again next
  week, joined to a way of keeping some of what it is worth. Do one
  without the other and you have a hobby or a hold-up.
ROOT: money / value needs wanting and rarity — the making supplies
  the wanting, the keeping needs the rarity.
THREAD: farming (a harvest against a farm), craft (a commission
  against a workshop), medicine (a cure against a practice).
ASKED-AS: what makes a business repeatable customers value capture profit hobby one off sale

ESSENCE: three different questions wear the same word, money. Revenue
  is what you were promised, profit is what is left after the cost of
  promising it, and cash is what is in the account this morning. A
  firm can be excellent at one and dying of another.
ROOT: accounting / earning and paying are recorded when they happen,
  and the money itself moves on a separate timetable.
THREAD: farming (a sold harvest, a season's costs, and a cheque that
  comes in March), households (a salary, a budget, and the day before
  payday), building (a signed contract and an empty bank account).
ASKED-AS: revenue profit cash flow difference sales figures bank balance company money confusing

ESSENCE: firms rarely die of being unprofitable. They die on a Tuesday
  when the wages are due and the customer who owes them pays in forty
  days. Profit is an opinion about a period; running out of money is
  an event with a date on it.
ROOT: this file / earnings are measured across a stretch of time while
  obligations fall due at single points inside it.
THREAD: households (a good salary and an overdrawn account), farming
  (rich in standing crop and unable to buy diesel), plumbing (a full
  tank behind a frozen pipe).
ASKED-AS: ran out of cash profitable but broke payroll customers pay late insolvent timing

ESSENCE: growing costs money before it makes money. You buy the stock,
  hire the people and do the work before anybody pays, so every extra
  order digs the hole deeper and the faster you grow the deeper it
  goes. A firm can go under from too many customers.
ROOT: this file / cash leaves on the cost's timetable and arrives on
  the customer's, and growth widens the gap between the two.
THREAD: farming (an extra field planted with seed bought first),
  building (a bigger job needing materials before the first stage
  payment), households (a better job that starts a month before it
  pays).
ASKED-AS: growing fast out of money orders stock upfront customers pay later funding growth

ESSENCE: before anything else, ask whether one of them pays. One
  customer, one delivery, one job — does the money it brings exceed
  what it cost to win and to serve? If not, volume is not a strategy.
  Volume is the same injury applied repeatedly.
ROOT: mathematics / a total is a single case multiplied, and a
  negative case multiplied stays negative however large the total.
THREAD: cooking (a dish sold under the price of its ingredients),
  transport (a route that loses on every run), farming (a crop whose
  inputs cost more than the buyer pays).
ASKED-AS: losing money every sale scale up volume unit economics per customer costs

ESSENCE: what is left after the direct cost of the thing you sold is
  the entire budget for everything else — the building, the selling,
  the inventing, the mistakes. A business keeping eight pence in the
  pound cannot afford the habits of one keeping eighty.
ROOT: this file / capture is the size of the slice, and the slice is
  the only source that every other activity is paid out of.
THREAD: shops (a grocer's pennies against a jeweller's pounds),
  farming (bulk grain against a rare herb), restaurants (drinks paying
  for the room the food sits in).
ASKED-AS: gross margin thin markup afford salespeople overheads pricing pennies pound business type

ESSENCE: costs that arrive whether or not you sell anything and costs
  that arrive only with a sale are two different animals, and which
  one you keep decides how you must behave. Heavy fixed costs mean you
  must fill the capacity. Heavy variable costs mean you must protect
  the price.
ROOT: mathematics / a cost that does not move with volume is divided
  by volume, and a cost that moves with volume never is.
THREAD: aviation (a seat that expires at the gate), farming (a
  combine paid for whatever the yield), building (a hired crane
  costing the same on a wet day).
ASKED-AS: fixed costs variable overheads discount empty seats fill capacity pricing cost structure

ESSENCE: where most costs are fixed, profit swings much harder than
  sales. A tenth more revenue can double the profit and a tenth less
  can erase it, because everything above the fixed pile is yours and
  everything below it is a hole. The same firm looks brilliant and
  reckless in consecutive years.
ROOT: this file / a fixed pile subtracted from a moving line leaves a
  remainder that moves by a multiple of the line.
THREAD: theatres (a full house and a half-empty one, same cast),
  farming (a fixed rent against a variable yield), sailing (a hull
  that goes faster and turns worse for the same reason).
ASKED-AS: profits swung wildly small drop in sales fixed costs amplified good year risky

ESSENCE: there is a volume at which you stop losing and start
  earning, and it is the single most useful number a small firm can
  carry in its head. Below it every day subtracts; above it the
  arithmetic changes character, because the fixed pile is already
  paid for.
ROOT: this file / fixed costs are covered by what each sale
  contributes above its own variable cost, so the count is the pile
  divided by the contribution.
THREAD: farming (acres that must be planted before a season pays),
  events (tickets that must go before the hall is covered), households
  (the earnings needed before a month is clear).
ASKED-AS: break even point how many sales cover costs monthly overheads minimum volume

ESSENCE: a price is not a sum you calculate, it is a sentence you say
  about what this is and who it is for. Adding a fixed markup to your
  costs prices the thing by an accident of your own efficiency and
  tells the buyer nothing except that you did not think about them.
ROOT: money / a price is where two valuations meet, and your costs
  are not standing on the buyer's side of that meeting.
THREAD: art (a canvas priced by the name on it), restaurants (a menu
  arranged so the prices explain each other), clothing (an identical
  garment at two prices sold to two different people).
ASKED-AS: how to price product markup cost plus too cheap what customers pay positioning

ESSENCE: the same thing sold at different prices to different people
  is not a swindle. It is how a seller reaches buyers who could never
  pay one middle price. It needs a fence — a time, a place, a card, a
  smaller size, an inconvenience — and the fence is what people are
  really paying for.
ROOT: this file / a single price refuses everybody below it and gives
  away value to everybody above it.
THREAD: transport (one seat sold six ways), medicine (one drug priced
  by country), cinemas (an afternoon showing).
ASKED-AS: different prices same product discounts students early booking coupons unfair segments

ESSENCE: a price cut teaches. Whoever buys at the lower price learns
  what the thing is worth and learns that waiting works — so the
  discount does not end when the promotion does. You have moved your
  price and called it a sale.
ROOT: the mind / a number once seen becomes the reference that every
  later number is judged against.
THREAD: shops (a chain that can never stop discounting), publishing (a
  cheap edition that caps the price of the next book), restaurants (a
  voucher crowd that vanishes at full price).
ASKED-AS: sale discount promotion customers wait cheaper devalue full price never again

ESSENCE: a lower price is the one move every rival can copy by
  lunchtime, and copying it costs them only money they were losing
  anyway. Anything that took you two years to build cannot be answered
  that afternoon — which is why price wars have no winners and slow
  advantages do.
ROOT: this file / an advantage lasts exactly as long as reproducing it
  takes, so the reproduction time is the advantage.
THREAD: sport (a tactic copied in a season against a decade's
  academy), farming (anyone can undercut, nobody can hurry a
  vineyard), retail (a war that leaves both shops poorer).
ASKED-AS: competitor undercut price war lower prices copy match advantage rival cheaper

ESSENCE: two numbers decide whether a business can grow — what it
  costs to win a customer, and what that customer is worth before they
  leave. The relation between them is the whole engine, and the second
  number is not known for years, so most firms are spending against a
  guess.
ROOT: this file / a customer is an investment paid for now and repaid
  in instalments, so the question is a return measured over a period.
THREAD: farming (an orchard paid for years before it fruits),
  education (training that repays across a career), fishing (fuel
  burned to reach a ground before anything is landed).
ASKED-AS: cost to acquire customer lifetime value marketing spend payback growth advertising worth

ESSENCE: losing a small share of customers every month is not a small
  problem. Five in a hundred a month is nearly half the book in a
  year, and it sets a ceiling — at that rate growth stops the moment
  arrivals equal departures, however good the selling is.
ROOT: mathematics / a constant fraction removed repeatedly is decay,
  and decay meeting a fixed inflow settles at a level rather than
  rising without limit.
THREAD: plumbing (a tank with a hole finding its own level), teaching
  (a class re-recruited every term), farming (a herd whose losses
  decide the size of the herd).
ASKED-AS: churn customers leaving cancel subscriptions retention growth ceiling leaky bucket monthly

ESSENCE: selling is a series of narrowing gates, and the end is the
  fractions multiplied, not averaged. Five stages that each pass half
  deliver three in a hundred. That is why a small improvement at a
  wide early gate beats a heroic effort at the last one.
ROOT: mathematics / independent fractions multiply, so a chain of
  gates falls away far faster than any single gate suggests.
THREAD: manufacturing (yield through many steps), education (a cohort
  narrowing at each stage), medicine (a screening chain that loses
  people at every step).
ASKED-AS: sales funnel conversion rates leads prospects closing pipeline stages percentage drop off

ESSENCE: every other way of getting customers is a tap you pay to keep
  open. Customers who bring customers are the only source that grows
  on its own — and it cannot be bought, only earned by something good
  enough that recommending it makes the recommender look good.
ROOT: mathematics / a source proportional to the base grows by
  multiplication, while a purchased source only ever adds.
THREAD: farming (seed saved from the crop against seed bought each
  year), disease (spread by contact against spread from one source),
  music (a song passed between friends).
ASKED-AS: word of mouth referrals recommend friends organic growth paid advertising stops compounding

ESSENCE: you can feel it in which direction the effort runs. Without
  it you push — every sale is a persuasion and the thing stops being
  used the day you stop calling. With it you are pulled: people arrive
  before you are ready and complain when it breaks, which is a form of
  love.
ROOT: money / value needs wanting, and real wanting shows up as
  behaviour rather than as agreement.
THREAD: teaching (a class that asks questions against one waiting for
  the bell), medicine (a treatment patients ask for by name), markets
  (a stall people queue at before it opens).
ASKED-AS: product market fit customers actually want pushing hard traction demand pull signal

ESSENCE: agreement is not demand. A trial, a pilot, a letter of
  interest and a warm meeting are all things a person can hand over
  without giving anything up, so they measure their own politeness and
  nothing else. The only evidence is somebody spending something they
  would rather keep.
ROOT: evidence / a claim is worth what it cost to make, and an
  enthusiastic yes costs nothing whatever.
THREAD: charity (people who say they will give and do not), research
  (a survey about a future purchase), friendship (a plan agreed and
  never arranged).
ASKED-AS: pilot free trial customer said yes never bought interest commitment paying proof

ESSENCE: a firm where one customer is a third of the revenue does not
  have a customer, it has an owner. Every negotiation is one-sided,
  every price rise is a risk, and the day that customer leaves is not
  a bad quarter but an ending.
ROOT: people and power / power in a dealing is the quality of each
  side's alternative, and a firm with one large buyer has none.
THREAD: farming (a grower supplying one buyer), employment (a
  contractor with one client, which is a job wearing an invoice),
  towns (one employer and everything that follows from it).
ASKED-AS: one big customer most of revenue dependent losing them terms dictated concentration

ESSENCE: revenue per customer is easy to see and tells you the wrong
  thing. The large account that demands meetings, special terms and
  three redeliveries can be the one you are subsidising, while the
  quiet small ones pay for the building.
ROOT: accounting / costs land on activities rather than on the size of
  the invoice, so serving is a separate figure from selling.
THREAD: medicine (a patient whose care costs many times the tariff),
  transport (a delivery address that eats an afternoon), farming (a
  field whose access track costs more than its yield).
ASKED-AS: biggest customer least profitable cost to serve support demands discounts unprofitable accounts

ESSENCE: the better thing does not win. The thing that reaches people
  wins, and reaching people is a machine that has to be built like any
  other. Most firms that died of a better product died of having no
  road to the buyer.
ROOT: this file / making value and keeping value are separate
  problems, and distribution is the bridge between them.
THREAD: farming (a crop with no route to market rotting in the field),
  publishing (a good book with no shelf), medicine (a treatment that
  exists and never reaches a clinic).
ASKED-AS: better product failed distribution channels reach customers marketing sales route market

ESSENCE: whoever stands between you and the buyer owns the
  relationship and will price accordingly. They can drop you, put
  their own version on the shelf beside yours, or simply raise the
  fee — and every year they learn more about your customers than you
  do.
ROOT: people and power / whoever holds the scarce link in a chain
  collects the value of the link.
THREAD: farming (a grower and the buyer who takes the whole crop),
  music (a performer and the venue), publishing (a writer and the
  shop).
ASKED-AS: retailer takes cut platform fees own the customer direct selling channel dependent

ESSENCE: a customer stays not because they are pleased but because
  leaving costs them — the records to move, the habits to relearn, the
  contracts to redo. That cost is not paid by you and it protects you
  anyway, which makes it the most reliable and least admirable
  advantage there is.
ROOT: this file / a purchase compares the alternative, and the
  alternative includes the price of getting there.
THREAD: banking (an account nobody ever moves), typing (a keyboard
  layout that outlived its reason), building (a heating system that
  decides the next twenty years).
ASKED-AS: switching costs locked in customers leave change supplier migration contract stuck

ESSENCE: a brand is not a logo, it is a promise about what will
  happen — and its economic work is saving the buyer the trouble of
  checking. That saving is worth real money, which is why a name can
  charge more than the identical unnamed thing standing beside it.
ROOT: economy / where quality cannot be verified before purchase the
  buyer needs something else to go on, and this is that something.
THREAD: medicine (a licence standing in for a judgement nobody can
  make), food (a familiar name trusted in a foreign town), craft (a
  maker's mark).
ASKED-AS: brand value logo trust name premium price promise customers pay more recognisable

ESSENCE: an advantage is a moat only if it gets harder to cross the
  longer you hold it. Being cheaper today, being first, being clever —
  none of those deepen on their own. Habits, standards, accumulated
  learning and things worth more as more people use them do.
ROOT: economy / profits persist only behind a barrier, so the real
  question is whether the barrier repairs itself.
THREAD: farming (soil built over decades against one good year),
  language (a standard nobody can leave alone), sport (a youth system
  against a signing).
ASKED-AS: competitive advantage moat lasts copied rivals barriers defensible first mover technology

ESSENCE: every distinguishing feature becomes an expectation. What was
  a reason to choose you turns into a reason to reject anyone who
  lacks it, and stops earning anything — so the ground under a product
  is always sliding toward sameness, and standing still is falling.
ROOT: this file / an advantage lasts as long as reproducing it takes,
  and a feature is the fastest thing there is to reproduce.
THREAD: technology (a capability that was a luxury and is now
  assumed), hotels (amenities that became requirements), farming (a
  variety that everybody plants).
ASKED-AS: features copied competitors commodity price pressure differentiate standard expectation premium gone

ESSENCE: the way a good firm dies is by doing everything right. Its
  best customers ask for improvements to what already exists, its
  money comes from the old thing, and the new thing is small, worse
  and unprofitable — so every honest analysis says ignore it, and the
  analysis is correct right up until it is fatal.
ROOT: this file / decisions get judged against today's customers and
  today's margins, and a new market has neither.
THREAD: farming (a machine that first did the job badly and then did
  all of it), medicine (a crude cheap test that spread past the
  accurate slow one), music (a worse-sounding format everybody chose).
ASKED-AS: big company disrupted ignored new technology customers wanted improvements startup cheaper dilemma

ESSENCE: the dangerous newcomer does not arrive better. It arrives
  worse and cheaper, serving the people you were glad to lose — and
  then it improves faster than its customers' needs do, which is the
  part nobody is watching.
ROOT: this file / what a product can do rises along a curve while what
  people need rises far more slowly, so the two lines cross.
THREAD: transport (a rough vehicle doing an unglamorous job),
  photography (a poor picture that was instant), education (a course
  worse than a lecture and available at midnight).
ASKED-AS: cheap inferior competitor low end entered improved fast overtook incumbents ignored segment

ESSENCE: if your new product takes sales from your old one, that is
  not a failure, it is the only way those sales stay yours. Somebody
  will build the replacement; the only question is whether you are in
  the room when they do. Firms that protect the old line reliably lose
  both lines.
ROOT: this file / formats and features are reproducible, so the choice
  is never whether the change happens but who owns it.
THREAD: publishing (a paper edition defended into oblivion),
  photography (a profitable film business and a camera it would not
  sell), farming (a new variety that ends the old one's acreage).
ASKED-AS: new product eats old sales cannibalise protect existing line internal resistance replace

ESSENCE: everything that grows fast slows down, and the moment to
  start the next thing is while the current one still looks wonderful
  — when there is money to fund it and nobody can see why you would.
  Start once the decline is visible and you are building from
  weakness.
ROOT: this file / both capability and demand saturate, so every
  product has a ceiling it reaches before anyone believes it exists.
THREAD: farming (an orchard planted while the old one still crops),
  sport (rebuilding a winning team), the body (training built in
  health rather than after the injury).
ASKED-AS: growth slowing next product invest while successful decline too late reinvent timing

ESSENCE: an unsold hour is gone. A firm selling hands and hours holds
  an inventory that expires continuously, which is why such businesses
  live or die by how full the diary is, and why they will take work at
  a bad price rather than have an empty Tuesday.
ROOT: this file / capacity that cannot be stored has no second chance
  to be sold, and its cost was already paid.
THREAD: transport (a seat that departs whether or not it is sold),
  hotels (a room worth nothing at midnight), medicine (a clinic list
  with three gaps in it).
ASKED-AS: service business hours utilisation empty slots billable time idle staff scheduling

ESSENCE: making things gets cheaper as you grow and running the place
  gets dearer. Every person added brings a decision to be routed, an
  agreement to be reached, a chance to be misunderstood — so the
  savings won in the workshop are eventually eaten in the corridor.
ROOT: this file / production cost per unit falls with volume while
  coordination cost rises with head count, and the two curves cross
  somewhere.
THREAD: kitchens (a brigade that stops working past a size), building
  (a site with more supervisors than trades), armies (a staff growing
  faster than the fighting strength).
ASKED-AS: company bigger slower bureaucracy scale savings coordination overhead management layers diseconomies

ESSENCE: whatever you buy, you never learn. Make-or-buy is not only a
  question about this year's cost — it decides which skills will be
  inside the building in ten years and which will belong to somebody
  who can raise their price.
ROOT: this file / a capability is built by doing, so the boundary of a
  firm decides what the firm becomes able to do.
THREAD: cooking (a kitchen that buys its stock and forgets how),
  farming (contractors who own all the machinery and all the skill),
  medicine (a hospital that outsourced a laboratory and lost its
  judgement).
ASKED-AS: make or buy outsource in house build ourselves supplier capability lose skills

ESSENCE: owning your supplier buys control and destroys a price. The
  outside quote used to tell you what the work was worth; once it is
  internal the figure is a negotiation between colleagues, the
  customer cannot leave, and nobody has to win the work.
ROOT: money / a price carries news, and an internal transfer is a
  price with nobody on the other side of it.
THREAD: farming (a mill owned by the growers), building (an in-house
  joinery that is never the cheapest quote), families (a relative
  doing the work and nobody able to complain).
ASKED-AS: buy our supplier vertical integration control costs internal transfer price captive competition

ESSENCE: the wage bill you removed comes back as specification,
  chasing, inspection and rework. Sending work outside converts a
  problem you could walk downstairs and look at into a problem that
  has to be written down, transmitted, misunderstood and repaired by
  message.
ROOT: language / an instruction is a compressed copy of an intention,
  and distance removes every chance to correct the copy in passing.
THREAD: building (a subcontracted trade that needs a full-time
  supervisor), publishing (a translation checked line by line),
  cooking (a recipe sent to somebody who has never tasted the dish).
ASKED-AS: outsourcing cheaper overseas hidden costs managing supplier quality rework specification distance

ESSENCE: a thing built by four teams comes out in four pieces, joined
  where the teams talk. The shape of the product ends up matching the
  shape of the people who made it, because the seams appear wherever
  agreement was expensive.
ROOT: people together / coordination has a price, so work gets divided
  along the lines where talking costs the most.
THREAD: building (a house designed by three trades that meet at one
  awkward corner), music (an album stitched from two studios),
  government (a service split across departments and felt as a queue).
ASKED-AS: teams product structure mirrors organisation seams handoffs integration departments design

ESSENCE: how many people report to one person decides the number of
  layers, and layers are where information goes to be summarised. Wide
  spans force people to be autonomous because nobody can be watched;
  narrow spans buy supervision and pay for it in distance between the
  top and the ground.
ROOT: this file / a manager's attention is fixed, so it gets divided,
  and the divisor sets the shape of the whole building.
THREAD: schools (a class size that decides what teaching is possible),
  armies (a section led directly and a division led by message),
  farming (a stockman and the number of animals he can actually see).
ASKED-AS: how many direct reports manager layers hierarchy flat supervision team size structure

ESSENCE: two bosses means two priorities and no way to settle them
  below the level where both bosses meet — so the conflict the
  structure was built to hold is carried by whoever is at the bottom
  of it, who has no authority to resolve anything.
ROOT: people and power / an unsettled question travels downward until
  it reaches somebody who cannot pass it on.
THREAD: families (a child with two adults who contradict each other),
  building (a site where client and contractor both give orders),
  medicine (a patient under two consultants).
ASKED-AS: matrix two managers reporting conflicting priorities dotted line project functional confusion

ESSENCE: most slowness in an organisation is not disagreement, it is
  nobody knowing who is allowed to decide. An unclear owner costs more
  than a wrong one, because a wrong decision can be corrected and an
  undecided question simply sits there eating meetings.
ROOT: people together / a group cannot act until somebody is entitled
  to end the discussion.
THREAD: ships (one captain, and a very good reason for it), sport (a
  penalty taker chosen before the moment arrives), households (a
  kitchen where nobody is cooking and everybody is helping).
ASKED-AS: who decides unclear ownership slow decisions consensus meetings stuck approval authority

ESSENCE: the pay plan is the real strategy document. Whatever is
  painted on the wall, people do what they are paid for, and they do
  the literal words of it rather than the intention behind them. Read
  the bonus scheme and you can predict the year.
ROOT: premise — behaviour follows incentives, and an incentive is read
  as text rather than as purpose.
THREAD: sport (a striker paid per goal who stops tracking back),
  medicine (a clinic paid by the procedure), schools (a teacher judged
  on one subject and the timetable that follows).
ASKED-AS: bonus scheme incentives behaviour targets commission strategy ignored pay plan drives

ESSENCE: culture is not the values on the wall. It is the worst
  behaviour the place has decided to live with, because that is what
  everyone has watched being allowed. One tolerated bully or one
  unpunished shortcut teaches more than any number of statements.
ROOT: people together / a rule is learned from what happens when it is
  broken, never from what is written about it.
THREAD: schools (a class that learns the real rules in a week), sport
  (a team where one player is exempt), families (a house rule that
  applies to some people).
ASKED-AS: company culture values wall behaviour tolerated bad manager allowed change real rules

ESSENCE: interviews barely predict performance. A conversation
  measures how somebody converses, and confidence, resemblance to the
  interviewer and a well-told story all score highly and do no work
  afterwards. Most hiring rests on a signal nobody has ever checked.
ROOT: evidence / a test predicts only what it actually samples, and a
  chat samples chatting.
THREAD: sport (a trial match against a season), medicine (a symptom
  that impresses and predicts nothing), education (an entrance exam
  that ranks preparation).
ASKED-AS: hiring interviews predict performance bad hire gut feeling work sample structured references

ESSENCE: keeping somebody who is failing is not the kind option. The
  cost is paid by their colleagues in covered work, by the customers,
  and by them — spending years failing where they might have been
  suited elsewhere. Delay does not avoid the bill, it lets it run.
ROOT: this file / a cost that accrues continuously is paid whether or
  not anybody writes it down.
THREAD: medicine (a treatment continued because stopping is awkward),
  sport (a place kept by seniority), farming (a machine nursed three
  seasons past its end).
ASKED-AS: firing someone underperforming keep waiting team morale cost delay difficult conversation

ESSENCE: a new person is a cost for months before they are a
  contribution, because they consume the time of the people who
  already know things and most of what they must learn was never
  written down. Hiring to relieve an overload makes the overload worse
  first.
ROOT: language / a workplace's real knowledge is unwritten, so it can
  only move by somebody stopping to explain it.
THREAD: kitchens (a new pair of hands slowing service for a
  fortnight), farming (a hand who must learn the ground), medicine (a
  rotation that costs the ward before it helps).
ASKED-AS: new hire slow start onboarding training productive months team capacity worse first

ESSENCE: organisations reward good work by handing over a different
  job. The best engineer becomes a manager and the best nurse becomes
  an administrator — and the skills that earned the promotion are not
  the skills of the new post, so people rise until they are no longer
  good at what they do.
ROOT: this file / a promotion is a change of occupation disguised as a
  prize for the previous one.
THREAD: sport (a great player who makes a poor coach), schools (a
  teacher promoted out of the classroom), armies (a soldier who was
  better before the commission).
ASKED-AS: promoted good at job bad manager career path technical track demotion reward

ESSENCE: when people leave, the written work stays and the knowledge
  goes — who to ask, why that rule is there, which customer must be
  handled carefully. A team that turns over quickly is permanently
  young: it repeats old mistakes because nobody present remembers
  making them.
ROOT: people together / an institution is re-taught into each arrival,
  and teaching runs slower than replacing.
THREAD: hospitals (a ward with a new team every rotation), sport (a
  squad rebuilt every season), farming (a holding sold before anybody
  learns its fields).
ASKED-AS: staff turnover knowledge leaves quitting retention documentation team memory repeated mistakes

ESSENCE: the founder is not a job title, it is a pile of judgements
  nobody ever wrote down. Replacing them looks like replacing a chief
  executive and is really replacing a hundred small decisions a day
  that never appeared in anyone's description.
ROOT: this file / a firm's early years run on one person's taste, and
  taste is the hardest thing there is to hand over.
THREAD: kitchens (a restaurant after the chef leaves), farming (a
  holding passed to a child with different instincts), music (a band
  after the songwriter).
ASKED-AS: founder stepping back new chief executive transition company changed handover startup grown

ESSENCE: there is a size at which a firm must stop being a group of
  people who know each other and start being a set of arrangements
  that work between strangers. It always feels like a betrayal of what
  made the place good, and it is also the only way the place survives.
ROOT: people and power / below the size where everyone knows everyone,
  judgement is cheaper than rules; above it, it is not.
THREAD: sport (a club that must finally appoint a secretary), families
  (a business among relatives that needs written terms), villages (a
  settlement that outgrows knowing everybody).
ASKED-AS: startup growing process bureaucracy formal systems informal chaos scaling structure needed

ESSENCE: firms break at predictable sizes, and always at the joint
  that was carrying more than it was built for. First the founder's
  own hours, then the informal way decisions were made, then the way
