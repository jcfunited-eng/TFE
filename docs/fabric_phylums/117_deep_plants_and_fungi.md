# 117 DEEP PLANTS AND FUNGI — the quiet kingdoms
Two kingdoms that cannot move. One earns by standing in the light
and paying in water; the other earns by growing through its own
dinner. Almost everything else follows from those two facts.

ESSENCE: a plant is buying carbon with water. It opens holes in
  its leaves to let the air's carbon in, and water pours out of
  those same holes — hundreds of molecules lost for every one
  gained. Its shape, its roots, its schedule and the map of where
  it can live all come out of that exchange rate.
ROOT: chemistry / the same pore that admits one gas releases the
  other, so gas exchange and water loss are one event.
CANNOT: no taking carbon in without giving water up, in any
  species or any weather — the trade is forced by the geometry,
  and only the rate can be managed. And no saving sunlight for
  later: light is used the instant it lands or it is gone, so the
  conversion has to happen in real time.
THREAD: money (a purchase whose price is set by the mechanism),
  cooking (an exchange you can slow down and never avoid).
ASKED-AS: photosynthesis plants sunlight water carbon dioxide leaves sugar oxygen trade losing thirsty

ESSENCE: a leaf is flat because light must be caught over an area
  while carbon only has to travel a very short way to reach the
  cells using it. Those two demands give the same answer
  everywhere — thin, wide, held out into the light — and every
  weakness a leaf has follows from that same shape.
ROOT: physics / capture scales with area while gas movement works
  only across tiny distances, so the organ must be broad and thin.
CANNOT: no thick leaf that also works well — cells buried deep get
  neither light nor air and are pure freight. And no leaf that is
  both large and cheap to protect: a broad surface is exactly what
  is easiest to eat, so a leaf is either well defended or treated
  as expendable and dropped when it stops paying.
THREAD: engineering (a radiator's form decided by its duty),
  design (a shape dictated by two demands rather than chosen).
ASKED-AS: leaves flat thin shape why broad wide surface light catching veins delicate tearing

ESSENCE: a big leaf catches more light and struggles to shed heat
  — it sits inside a thicker layer of still air that it warms, and
  it cooks. A small leaf sheds heat easily and gathers less. Which
  is why shaded wet forests grow enormous leaves and deserts and
  mountaintops grow tiny ones, in unrelated plants, everywhere.
ROOT: physics / a large flat surface holds a thicker blanket of
  motionless air around it, and motionless air is an insulator.
CANNOT: no big leaf in full sun without water to spend on cooling
  — the cooling IS evaporation, so leaf size is capped by water
  and not by light. And no small leaf competing in deep shade:
  where light is scarce area is everything, and a small leaf
  simply cannot collect enough of it to pay for itself.
THREAD: engineering (a heat sink's fins sized by the airflow),
  clothing (covering area traded against ventilation).
ASKED-AS: leaf size big small tropical desert plants needles cactus shade sun heat cooling

ESSENCE: the pores in a leaf are gates with muscles, and a plant
  spends its day deciding how far to open them. Wide means fast
  growth and fast water loss; shut means safety and no income at
  all. Afternoon wilting is usually not thirst but a decision —
  the plant closing the shop rather than running itself dry.
ROOT: chemistry / the exchange is forced, so the only control
  available is how wide the gate stands and for how long.
CANNOT: no growth with the pores shut, so a plant under water
  stress stops earning long before anything looks wrong. And no
  opening them at night for most plants: the carbon could come in
  but the light is not there to use it, so an open pore in
  darkness is loss with no matching gain.
THREAD: money (a shop trading at a loss or shutting and earning
  nothing), engineering (one valve controlling two flows at once).
ASKED-AS: stomata pores leaves open close wilting afternoon water loss hot day growth stops

ESSENCE: two ways round that bargain have been invented many times
  over. One concentrates carbon inside the leaf so the pores can
  be nearly shut and the machinery still fed — maize, sugarcane,
  most tropical grasses. The other opens only at night, stores the
  carbon as acid, and works on it by day sealed shut: cactus and
  pineapple.
ROOT: chemistry / if carbon can be gathered and held internally,
  the gate can be closed while the work carries on inside.
CANNOT: no free lunch in either — both need extra machinery, and
  both lose to ordinary plants in cool wet dim conditions, which
  is why the world is not made of them. And no fast growth from
  the night-opening trick: the store is limited by how much acid
  the tissue can hold, so cacti are slow whatever they are fed.
THREAD: engineering (a buffer tank separating supply from demand),
  money (borrowing overnight in order to trade by day).
ASKED-AS: cactus pineapple maize sugarcane hot dry plants water saving night carbon tropical grasses

ESSENCE: plants are green because green is the part of sunlight
  they do NOT use — they take the red and the blue and throw the
  middle back at you. So the light beneath a leaf is the light the
  leaf refused, and everything growing in that shade is trying to
  make a living on somebody else's rejects.
ROOT: chemistry / a pigment absorbs certain wavelengths and
  reflects the rest, so the colour seen is by definition the part
  that was not captured.
CANNOT: no plant under a canopy living on the same light as one
  above it — the quality has changed and not merely the amount, so
  shade plants need different pigments or a different plan
  entirely. And no black plant, though it would catch more: why
  not is genuinely unsettled, and worth holding as an open question.
THREAD: light (a colour is always what was turned away), farming
  (why an understorey crop behaves unlike a full-sun one).
ASKED-AS: plants green colour leaves why chlorophyll light absorbs red blue shade under canopy

ESSENCE: most plants do not gather their own minerals. Their roots
  are sheathed in or entered by fungal threads far finer than any
  root can be, reaching further and into smaller spaces, and the
  plant pays in sugar for the phosphorus and the reach. The
  arrangement is ancient, nearly universal, and entirely unseen.
ROOT: mathematics / uptake depends on surface in contact with
  soil, and a thread a hundredth of a root's width has vastly more
  of it for the same material.
CANNOT: no root system at any density matching a fungal net's
  contact with the ground — the geometry forbids it, so growing
  more root is not a substitute. And no thriving on poor soil
  without the partner: sterile ground is why so many transplanted
  trees fail slowly with nothing visibly wrong.
THREAD: money (outsourcing a job to somebody with better reach),
  engineering (surface area bought entirely with thinness).
ASKED-AS: roots fungi mycorrhiza soil nutrients phosphorus trees partnership threads underground trading sugar

ESSENCE: the air is four-fifths nitrogen and almost no plant can
  touch it, because the molecule is welded shut. A few species
  rent bacteria that can break it and pay heavily — in sugar, and
  in building the housing — which is why the arrangement is common
  on poor ground and quietly shut down where the soil is rich.
ROOT: chemistry / breaking that bond takes a great deal of energy,
  so the partnership only pays where nitrogen is the shortest
  supply in the field.
CANNOT: no free nitrogen from the air even for the plants that can
  reach it — it is bought with sugar that would otherwise have
  been growth. And no fixing amid plenty: the nodules close down
  when soil nitrogen is high, so feeding a legume stops the very
  process it was planted to perform.
THREAD: money (an in-house capability worth keeping only while
  buying is dear), farming (a rotation built on this exact switch).
ASKED-AS: legumes beans clover nitrogen air bacteria nodules roots soil poor fertiliser cost energy

ESSENCE: a plant runs two pipes in opposite directions. One
  carries water and minerals up from the roots, is built of dead
  empty cells, and is pulled from above by evaporation. The other
  carries sugar down and sideways from wherever it was made to
  wherever it is wanted, is alive, and is pushed by pressure.
ROOT: physics / water can be pulled by evaporation and a sugar
  solution cannot, so the two flows need different mechanisms and
  therefore different plumbing.
CANNOT: no sugar travelling in the water pipe — they are separate
  systems, which is how a plant can be full of water and starving
  in one branch. And no upward pull without leaves at the top: the
  evaporation IS the pump, so a tree stripped of foliage stops
  drinking until it grows some back.
THREAD: engineering (two circuits with different pumps inside one
  machine), the body (arteries and veins doing unrelated work).
ASKED-AS: sap tree water rising sugar moving trunk pipes xylem phloem transport up down

ESSENCE: the living transport layer is a thin cylinder just under
  the bark, and the wood inside it is dead plumbing. Cut a ring
  right round a trunk and the leaves keep drinking for weeks —
  that pipe is untouched — while the roots receive nothing and
  starve, so the tree dies from the bottom upward, months later.
ROOT: physics / the two systems lie at different depths, so a
  shallow injury removes one and leaves the other working.
CANNOT: no early sign of the injury, because the part anyone looks
  at is on the side that still has its supply. And no repair
  across a complete ring: the living layer cannot bridge a gap, so
  a partial cut heals over and a complete one is fatal however
  narrow it is.
THREAD: medicine (a blockage whose symptoms appear far
  downstream), engineering (a severed return line, not a supply).
ASKED-AS: tree ring barking damage deer rabbits strimmer bark stripped dies later roots girdling

ESSENCE: nothing pumps water to the top of a tree. It is pulled —
  evaporation at the leaves tugs on a column of water that holds
  together because water molecules cling to each other, so the
  whole thread is in tension from the leaf to the root, like a
  rope being hauled up a well.
ROOT: physics / water molecules attract one another strongly
  enough that a narrow column can be pulled without coming apart.
CANNOT: no pushing water up there from below — air pressure alone
  gives out around ten metres, so a pump in the roots could never
  do the job whatever its power. And no unlimited height: the
  tension climbs with length until the column breaks into vapour,
  which is precisely where the tallest trees stop.
THREAD: engineering (a chain in tension against a column in
  compression), physics (a ceiling set by a material property).
ASKED-AS: tall trees water top redwood pump height limit pulling tension evaporation column suction

ESSENCE: a tree does not grow the way an animal does. It lays a
  new layer of plumbing outside last year's and leaves the old one
  in place as scaffolding — so the inside of a trunk is a
  graveyard of used pipework, and that graveyard is exactly what
  holds the new growth up.
ROOT: engineering / a structure that keeps its spent parts as
  support pays nothing for demolition and stiffens every year.
CANNOT: no hollow tree dying of being hollow — the heartwood was
  already dead, so it loses stiffness and not one living cell. And
  no expansion from the middle: growth happens only at the outer
  skin, which is why a nail driven into a trunk stays at the same
  height for the whole life of the tree.
THREAD: building (a scaffold that becomes the wall), keeping
  knowledge (an archive that ends up structuring its institution).
ASKED-AS: tree trunk growing wider hollow trees still alive nail height heartwood dead rings

ESSENCE: where there is a season, each year's growth leaves a wide
  pale band and a dense dark one, so a stump is a bar chart of a
  life — wide for a good year, pinched for a drought, scarred for
  a fire. And since every tree in a district shares one weather,
  the patterns match and chain back thousands of years.
ROOT: evidence / a record made continuously by an indifferent
  process, with an independent copy in every neighbour, is very
  hard to fake and very hard to misread.
CANNOT: no rings from a tree with no seasons — many tropical trees
  have none at all, so the whole method has a geography. And no
  reading a single trunk: one tree's oddities are its own, and the
  signal only appears when many are matched against each other.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (independent copies making a record
  trustworthy), measurement (an instrument nobody had to install).
ASKED-AS: tree rings count age wide narrow drought dating wood climate record matching stumps

ESSENCE: bark is a dead outer skin that a tree makes from
  underneath and sheds from outside, because a trunk that keeps
  widening must either split its covering or continuously replace
  it. Its job is sealing rather than strength — against drying,
  insects, fungus and fire — and fire is what thick bark is for.
ROOT: physics / a rigid covering on an expanding body has to
  crack, so it must be renewed rather than maintained.
CANNOT: no bark that both stretches and seals, so every species
  has chosen a way of failing — which is why bark patterns differ
  so sharply and can be used to name a tree in winter. And no
  regrowing it across bare wood: a tree walls a wound off instead
  of covering it, and grows around the gap forever.
THREAD: building (cladding renewed rather than repaired), the body
  (skin as a barrier and not a structural member).
ASKED-AS: bark tree peeling cracked thick fire birch cork identify species damage wound sealing

ESSENCE: a tree must not wake during a mild week in January, so it
  does not answer warmth at all until it has counted enough cold.
  Only when a required number of chilled hours is banked does
  warmth mean anything to it — a lock with two keys that have to
  be turned in the right order.
ROOT: the living world / a cue must be impossible for the wrong
  conditions to fake, and warmth by itself is faked every winter.
CANNOT: no forcing a dormant bud early with heat — before the
  chilling is complete, warmth does nothing whatever. And no fruit
  tree in a climate that never gets cold enough: the requirement
  is a hard number for each variety, and a winter short of it
  gives a tree that leafs out raggedly or not at all.
THREAD: engineering (a two-stage interlock preventing an
  accidental start), farming (varieties matched to a region's winter).
ASKED-AS: buds spring warm spell winter chill hours fruit trees blossom early cold requirement

ESSENCE: a seed is three things inside one coat — a paused embryo,
  a packed lunch, and a lock. The lunch is what lets a seedling
  exist before it can feed itself. The lock is the clever part:
  without it every seed would start in the first warm rain of
  autumn and die in the first frost.
ROOT: the living world / a start that cannot be undone must not be
  made on ambiguous evidence, so the trigger has to be hard to
  counterfeit.
CANNOT: no germination without spending the store — a seedling
  runs on a fixed sum with no income, so it must reach the light
  before the lunch is gone, which is what sets how deep a seed can
  be buried. And no pausing once begun: a started seed cannot
  return to waiting.
THREAD: money (a one-way spend against a deadline), engineering (a
  safety catch being the important part of the mechanism).
ASKED-AS: seeds sprouting stored food inside embryo dormant waiting starting wrong time deep planting

ESSENCE: different seeds demand different proofs before they will
  start. Some want a winter's cold, so they cannot begin in
  autumn. Some want fire, or smoke. Some want to have passed
  through an animal. Some want light itself, which tells a tiny
  seed it is at the surface rather than buried past its own
  supplies.
ROOT: evidence / each requirement tests for a condition the
  seedling will actually need, and the test must be one that the
  wrong situation cannot pass.
CANNOT: no seed with a requirement germinating on warmth and water
  alone, which is why so much wild seed refuses to behave like a
  garden packet. And no single treatment across species: the
  requirement names a habitat, so the key for a fire plant is
  nothing like the key for a woodland one.
THREAD: security (a check built around what an impostor cannot
  supply), teaching (a prerequisite that genuinely is one).
ASKED-AS: seeds wont grow soaking scratching cold treatment fire smoke light surface wild packet

ESSENCE: the ground under any field holds thousands of live seeds
  in every square metre, waiting years or decades for their
  signal. That is why disturbing bare soil produces a crop of
  weeds nobody sowed, and why clearing a wood can bring up plants
  that have not been seen on the site in living memory.
ROOT: the living world / a seed with a strict lock can wait, and
  waiting spreads a lineage across time instead of across space.
CANNOT: no clean ground after removing the visible plants — the
  standing weeds are a small fraction of the population and the
  bank is untouched. And no emptying that bank quickly: it is
  drawn down only as seeds germinate or die, so a few tidy years
  barely dent a century of deposits.
THREAD: money (a dormant liability on nobody's books), keeping
  knowledge (an archive outliving everyone who could read it).
ASKED-AS: weeds appear digging bare soil seeds waiting years buried germinate disturbance clearing ground

ESSENCE: every way of moving a seed buys a different landing. Wind
  is cheap, travels far and arrives at random. An animal's gut
  travels far and arrives where the animal chose to go, which is
  usually somewhere good. Fur delivers to a path. Ants carry a
  short way and file the seed underground where fire cannot reach.
ROOT: chance / a method's worth is distance multiplied by the odds
  of the landing site being survivable, and those two trade off.
CANNOT: no aiming a wind-blown seed, so a plant using wind accepts
  the odds and pays for them in numbers. And no long journey for a
  heavy seed: weight and reach are opposed, which is why
  big-seeded trees advance slowly and lose ground fastest when
  their carriers disappear.
THREAD: money (a cheap broad campaign against a targeted one),
  post (an addressed parcel costing more than a leaflet drop).
ASKED-AS: seeds spread wind animals birds burrs sticky ants dandelion coconut floating distance carried

ESSENCE: a fruit is a bribe with terms attached. The flesh is the
  payment, the seed is the cargo, and the timing is the contract:
  while the seed is unfinished the fruit is hard, green, bitter
  and often mildly poisonous, and the moment the cargo is ready it
  turns sweet, soft and loud.
ROOT: the living world / paying before the goods are ready is a
  straight loss, so the reward is withheld until the cargo is
  worth shipping.
CANNOT: no fruit that is both a good bribe and a safe container —
  sweet flesh attracts everyone, so the seed must be armoured,
  bitter or slippery enough to survive the trip. And no reward for
  an animal that chews: a seed-crusher is a thief rather than a
  carrier, and plants defend against those separately.
THREAD: money (payment on delivery instead of in advance), trade
  (advertising that starts only when there is stock).
ASKED-AS: fruit ripe green bitter sweet birds eating seeds passing through poison unripe animals

ESSENCE: a plant that flowers alone has wasted its flowers, so the
  timing has to be shared across a whole population — and the only
  signal reliable enough to synchronise on is day length, which no
  warm spell can fake. Temperature adjusts the schedule; the
  calendar sets it.
ROOT: astronomy / the planet's tilt makes day length exactly
  repeatable while weather is not, so reliability beats relevance.
CANNOT: no synchrony off a warmth cue — a mild March would scatter
  the population and each plant would flower into an empty field.
  And no moving the setting: a variety carried north or south
  flowers at the wrong time of year and often fails to set at all,
  which is why crop varieties travel east and west far better.
THREAD: farming (a variety tied to a latitude more tightly than to
  a climate), people together (a meeting time nobody can dispute).
ASKED-AS: flowering time daylight length spring plants same week variety moved latitude fails soybean

ESSENCE: a flower is an advertisement addressed to one reader, and
  the address is written in its shape, colour, scent and opening
  hours. Red and tubular with no smell means a bird. White,
  heavily scented, open at night means a moth or a bat. Dull,
  warm and stinking of meat means a fly that breeds in carrion.
ROOT: the living world / a flower is charged for every visit it
  attracts, so excluding whoever cannot do the job is worth money.
CANNOT: no flower open to everybody prospering — a generalist pays
  in nectar for visitors who carry its pollen to the wrong doors.
  And no reading the intended visitor from beauty: the flowers
  people find loveliest are addressed to bees, which see
  ultraviolet and read markings that are invisible to us.
THREAD: image (a design aimed at one audience and not another),
  money (a shop front that filters its own customers).
ASKED-AS: flowers colours shapes smell night white red tubular birds moths bats flies pollinators

ESSENCE: giving up on animals means giving up petals, scent and
  nectar and paying instead in sheer bulk of pollen — grams of it
  off one tree, of which a few grains land usefully. It also means
  flowering before the leaves open, because a canopy in leaf is a
  filter, which is why hay fever starts before the woods go green.
ROOT: chance / with no aiming possible the only strategy left is
  enough attempts, so the cost moves out of advertising and into
  raw material.
CANNOT: no wind pollination for a plant living scattered among
  others — the odds collapse with distance, so it works in
  forests, grasslands and crops standing in stands and almost
  nowhere else. And no colour or scent on such a flower: paying
  for an advertisement nobody reads is pure loss.
THREAD: money (a mass mailing against a sales call), farming (why
  wind-pollinated crops are planted dense and in blocks).
ASKED-AS: hay fever pollen wind grass trees catkins spring before leaves flowers plain green

ESSENCE: a plant that pollinates itself gets a guaranteed seed and
  guaranteed sameness. One that insists on a partner gets variety
  and risks getting nothing. Most species hedge — machinery that
  discourages their own pollen, plus a quiet fallback that permits
  it when the season is closing and nobody has come.
ROOT: mathematics / a certain small return against an uncertain
  larger one, with the answer depending on how likely the partner is.
CANNOT: no getting variety and certainty out of one flowering. And
  no self-pollinating lineage prospering indefinitely: faults
  accumulate with nothing to mask them, so those lines tend to be
  short-lived on the long view even when they win the season.
THREAD: money (a safe small return against a risky larger one),
  heredity (variety as insurance bought at a real premium).
ASKED-AS: self pollination flowers own pollen fruit set alone variety inbreeding partner insects failed

ESSENCE: oaks and beeches across a whole region produce almost
  nothing for several years and then, all together, an enormous
  crop. The lean years cut back the squirrels and weevils that eat
  the seed, and the flood year swamps whatever survived — so far
  more seed gets through than a steady output ever would.
ROOT: mathematics / an eater's numbers track its food, so a
  variable supply keeps it permanently behind while a steady one
  lets it catch up and stay level.
CANNOT: no benefit at all unless the trees are synchronised — one
  tree fruiting heavily on its own simply feeds everybody, so the
  strategy requires a whole region acting as one. And no eater
  adapting to it: nothing can build its numbers on a food that
  disappears for three years at a time.
THREAD: money (irregular supply defeating anyone planning on it),
  war (feint and mass instead of steady pressure).

ESSENCE: an annual spends everything on seed and dies — a single
  all-in bet that pays on disturbed ground and in short seasons. A
  perennial holds most of its gains back as root, wood or bulb and
  lives to try again, which wins wherever conditions last long
  enough for holding back to earn out.
ROOT: money / spending the whole capital maximises this year's
  return and forfeits every later one, so the right answer depends
  on how long the ground stays available.
CANNOT: no annual competing in a settled meadow — it starts from
  nothing each spring while its neighbours start from a full root,
  so it loses the race for light before it begins. And no
  perennial dominating fresh bare ground: the reserve that buys
  survival is exactly what it did not spend on arriving first.
THREAD: money (dividend against reinvestment), farming (why the
  weeds of ploughed ground are annuals almost without exception).
ASKED-AS: annual perennial plants die after seeding weeds bulbs roots return every year garden

ESSENCE: competing for light is not like competing for anything
  else, because the plant above takes it first and the one below
  gets what is left. There is no sharing and no reciprocity —
  which is why plants race upward at the expense of everything
  else, and why height in a crowd is worth more than health.
ROOT: physics / light is intercepted in order of height, so the
  resource is taken in sequence rather than drawn from a pool.
CANNOT: no negotiated coexistence over light of the kind that soil
  nutrients allow, since those come from a shared store and this
  does not. And no value in height by itself: the cost is only
  worth paying because everyone else is paying it, which is why an
  open-grown tree is short and wide and a forest tree is a pole.
THREAD: strategy (a contest where being ahead is the whole prize),
  money (an arms race everybody would rather leave).
ASKED-AS: trees tall forest crowded straight trunks open grown spreading light shade competing height

ESSENCE: a plant can be built to grow fast in the open or to
  survive in deep shade, and not both. The quick one has thin
  cheap leaves and dies when the light fails; the tolerant one has
  thick expensive long-lived leaves and is left behind in a
  clearing. A wood is running two unrelated careers at once.
ROOT: the living world / cheap fast tissue and durable efficient
  tissue come out of the same budget and pull opposite ways.
CANNOT: no species holding both strategies, so every forest gap is
  taken by one kind and later taken from it by the other. And no
  hurrying a shade-tolerant seedling with light and feed: the
  slowness is structural, and extra resources do not convert it
  into a pioneer.
THREAD: money (a slow high-margin business against a fast thin
  one), work (a sprint career against one measured in decades).
ASKED-AS: seedlings shade tolerant fast growing gap clearing forest slow understorey saplings waiting light

ESSENCE: some plants poison the ground around themselves. Walnut,
  eucalyptus and a good many weeds release chemicals from roots
  and fallen leaves that stop other seeds starting nearby — buying
  space in a way no amount of growing could. It is warfare over
  standing room.
ROOT: the living world / a competitor can be beaten by suppression
  as effectively as by outgrowing, and chemistry is cheaper than
  wood.
CANNOT: no allelopathy that works on everything — the compounds
  are specific and some neighbours are simply indifferent, which
  is why the effect looks patchy and was argued over for decades.
  And no clean site once the plant is removed: the chemicals
  persist in the soil, so the ground stays hostile for seasons.
THREAD: war (denying ground rather than occupying it), money
  (competing by raising a rival's costs instead of cutting yours).
ASKED-AS: nothing grows under walnut tree pine needles eucalyptus soil chemicals weeds suppress germination

ESSENCE: a plant cannot run, so it has to be unpleasant instead.
  Thorns and hairs raise the price of the first bite, silica and
  tough fibre wear teeth down, and the chemistry does the rest —
  bitterness as a warning, toxins as a punishment, and compounds
  that block digestion so an animal that eats its fill still
  starves.
ROOT: the living world / something that cannot escape must make
  itself expensive to eat, and the expense can be charged at the
  mouth, at the tooth or in the gut.
CANNOT: no defence that is free — every thorn and every toxin is
  growth not made, so the best-defended plants are the slowest and
  the fast ones are the tender ones. And no defence against
  everybody: a specialist that has solved one plant's poison eats
  it exclusively, and often stores the poison for its own use.
THREAD: money (security spending as a permanent drag on output),
  war (imposing cost rather than winning outright).
ASKED-AS: thorns spines bitter taste poisonous plants nettles stinging toxins eating animals defence tough

ESSENCE: much of a plant's chemistry is summoned rather than
  standing. Bitten leaves raise their toxin levels within hours,
  and the untouched leaves on the same plant do it too — and in
  several species so do the neighbours, which pick up compounds
  drifting in the air and prepare before anything has reached them.
ROOT: money / a defence held ready costs continuously, so it pays
  to keep the capacity and spend it only when attacked.
CANNOT: no protection for the leaf bitten first, since the
  response takes hours and the first damage is always paid in
  full. And no reading a plant's real defences off an undamaged
  sample: a laboratory test on a calm leaf understates what a
  browsing animal actually meets an hour later.
THREAD: the body (immunity ramping up after exposure rather than
  before), money (holding capacity instead of holding stock).
ASKED-AS: plants respond eaten insects chemicals increase neighbours warning released air defence hours grazing

ESSENCE: the chemicals that discourage insects are our spices, our
  medicines and our poisons, and nothing separates the three but
  quantity and target. Caffeine is an insecticide, willow bark is
  aspirin, foxglove steadies a heart or stops it. None of it was
  made for us and none of it is safe by virtue of being natural.
ROOT: chemistry / a molecule's effect depends on the dose and on
  the machinery it interferes with, and that machinery differs
  between a beetle and a person.
CANNOT: no safe category called natural — a plant is a weapons
  cabinet, and several of the most poisonous substances known are
  ordinary plant and fungal products. And no reading safety off
  familiarity: rhubarb leaves, apple pips and green potatoes are
  all everyday items with the defence still fitted.
THREAD: medicine (a window between useless and lethal), cooking
  (traditional preparation as a detoxification step).
ASKED-AS: herbs spices medicine poison natural safe plants drugs caffeine aspirin foxglove dose

ESSENCE: a gall is an insect making a plant build it a house. The
  larva releases chemicals that hijack the plant's own growth
  instructions, and the plant obligingly constructs a specific
  structure — a set shape, a set lining, feeding tissue included.
  A dozen different insects on one oak produce a dozen different
  buildings.
ROOT: the living world / a body that grows to instructions can be
  redirected by anything able to write instructions it will obey.
CANNOT: no part of a gall built by the insect — every scrap is
  plant tissue, which is exactly why the shapes are so precise and
  so reliably particular to one species. And no defending against
  it by being tough: the attack is on the signalling and not on
  the surface.
