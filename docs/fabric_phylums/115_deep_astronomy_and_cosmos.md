# 115 DEEP ASTRONOMY AND COSMOS — what is out there and how it is known
Nobody has been anywhere. Every fact in this file was got out of
arriving light, arriving particles, and arithmetic — which makes
how it is known as much of the subject as what is known.

ESSENCE: nothing far away has ever had its distance measured
  directly. Every figure beyond a few thousand light years rests
  on a method calibrated against a nearer method, and that on one
  nearer still — a ladder whose bottom rung is plain geometry and
  whose top rung is the age of the universe.
ROOT: measurement / a technique that works over one range is used
  to calibrate one that reaches further, then handed the load.
CANNOT: no repairing a fault in a low rung by improving a high
  one, because an error near the bottom multiplies all the way up
  — which is why the size of the universe has been revised by a
  factor of two more than once. And no self-check inside a rung:
  only a different method covering the same range can test it.
THREAD: measurement (a chain of calibrations is worth its worst
  link), money (a price pinned to a comparable pinned to another).
ASKED-AS: how far stars galaxies distance measured light years know accurate ladder steps calibration

ESSENCE: hold a finger up and blink each eye in turn — it jumps
  against the background, and the nearer it is the more it jumps.
  The earth's orbit gives astronomy two eyes three hundred million
  kilometres apart, and a nearby star's tiny annual jump is the
  one distance that assumes nothing whatever about the star.
ROOT: mathematics / a triangle with a known base and a measured
  angle is fully decided, with no physics required at all.
CANNOT: no parallax for the far stars — the angle shrinks with
  range until it drowns in the instrument's own wobble. And no
  sound ladder without it: every rung above is eventually
  calibrated against these, so how far parallax reaches sets the
  accuracy of everything built on top of it.
THREAD: navigation (a position fixed by angles to known points),
  seeing (two eyes judging near distance and giving up on far).
ASKED-AS: parallax nearest star distance measured angle earth orbit months shift telescope geometry triangle

ESSENCE: if you know how bright a thing truly is and you measure
  how bright it looks, the gap between them is the distance,
  because light thins by the square as it spreads. The whole
  difficulty is knowing the true brightness in advance, and only a
  few objects announce it — certain stars by the rhythm of their
  pulsing, and one kind of exploding star by having a fixed fuse.
ROOT: physics / brightness falls with the square of distance, so
  apparent brightness plus real brightness solves for range.
CANNOT: no distance from a brightness alone — a faint glow is
  either a candle far off or a spark nearby, and nothing in the
  glow separates them. And no candle without local calibration:
  the method stands entirely on finding a few near enough to have
  their distance measured some other way first.
THREAD: sound (a shout's loudness gives range only if you know the
  shouter), money (a valuation by comparison needs one real sale).
ASKED-AS: how far galaxy brightness dim faint distance candle cepheid supernova compare known measure

ESSENCE: the brightest stars in the night sky are not the greatest
  stars. They are a mixture of close ordinary ones and distant
  furnaces, and the eye cannot tell which is which. The nearest
  star to the sun cannot be seen without a telescope, and some of
  the most powerful in the galaxy have no names at all.
ROOT: physics / what arrives is real output divided by distance
  squared, so two unknowns reach you welded into one number.
CANNOT: no ranking real power by apparent brightness, in any sky.
  And no honest constellation: the stars in a pattern usually have
  nothing to do with each other, sitting at wildly different
  ranges and lining up only from this one address, so the shapes
  would be unrecognisable from anywhere else.
THREAD: the mind (a near voice sounding like an important one),
  evidence (one number holding two unknowns needs a second reading).
ASKED-AS: brightest star sirius polaris constellations shapes distance far apart pattern real brightness nearest

ESSENCE: a spectrum is not one reading but a stack of them taken
  off the same lines. Which lines appear names the elements, how
  deep they cut gives the amounts, their position gives motion
  toward or away, their width gives pressure and spin, and their
  splitting gives magnetic field — all from a smear of light too
  faint to see.
ROOT: physics / an atom absorbs and emits at exact wavelengths,
  and each physical condition nudges those wavelengths its own way.
CANNOT: no chemistry of a star from an image, however sharp — a
  photograph records totals and hides every one of these. And no
  spectrum from a source too faint to divide: the light must be
  spread out before it can be interrogated, which is why splitting
  light always needs far more of it than merely photographing it.
THREAD: chemistry (a fingerprint that identifies without touching),
  medicine (one blood sample answering a dozen separate questions).

ESSENCE: the same gas shows dark lines or bright ones depending
  purely on where you stand. Look through a cool cloud at a hot
  source behind it and the cloud steals its own colours out,
  leaving gaps. Look at that same cloud from the side with nothing
  bright behind, and those exact colours are the whole picture.
ROOT: physics / a gas absorbs and re-emits the same wavelengths,
  so what you see depends on whether the re-emission is set
  against a bright background or is all there is.
CANNOT: no gas identified differently in the two cases — the
  wavelengths are identical either way, and that is what makes the
  identification safe. And no dark line without something brighter
  behind it: a lone cloud in empty sky can only glow or stay black.
THREAD: seeing (a stained window from inside and from outside),
  evidence (one fact reported positively or as an absence).
ASKED-AS: dark lines spectrum sun absorption emission nebula glowing gas cloud behind bright background

ESSENCE: a hot body radiates across a whole range with a peak that
  slides shorter as it heats — so a cold cloud peaks in infrared,
  a star in visible light, and gas falling onto a black hole in
  X-rays. The consequence is blunt: most of what the universe
  emits arrives at wavelengths no eye can register.
ROOT: physics / hotter matter radiates more and radiates it bluer,
  with a peak that tracks temperature exactly.
CANNOT: no seeing the cold universe with a visible-light telescope
  — newborn stars inside their clouds, cool dust and most early
  galaxies do not shine where eyes work. And no such thing as the
  true picture of anything: every image is a picture at a chosen
  wavelength, and the choice is half the result.
THREAD: heat (a warm room glowing to the right camera in the
  dark), photography (a filter deciding what the picture contains).
ASKED-AS: infrared telescope different colours heat temperature stars red blue invisible light wavelength image

ESSENCE: stretched light says a source is receding and by how
  much, but only because the true home wavelengths of those lines
  are known from a bench in a laboratory. Without that fixed
  reference the stretch cannot be measured at all — so the entire
  picture of an expanding universe rests on a hydrogen atom out
  there being a hydrogen atom here.
ROOT: physics / a shift is a difference from a known value, so
  measuring it requires possessing the known value.
CANNOT: no redshift from a smooth featureless glow — a source with
  no lines carries no ruler and can only be guessed at. And no
  separating a galaxy's own motion from the expansion nearby:
  close galaxies move both ways, and only far out does the
  stretching swamp everything else.
THREAD: sound (a siren's drop giving speed only if you knew the
  note), measurement (every measurement is difference from a mark).
ASKED-AS: red shift galaxies moving away speed measured lines stretched doppler laboratory wavelength reference

ESSENCE: a star begins as the coldest densest thing for light
  years around — a cloud so cold that gravity finally beats the
  pressure holding it open. Falling in heats it, and the heat
  fights back, so the collapse is slow and ignition comes only
  when the middle is hot enough to fuse. Cold is the requirement,
  never warmth.
ROOT: physics / gravity beats pressure only below a certain
  temperature for a given mass, so collapse is a cold-side event.
CANNOT: no star forming out of warm gas, since heat is the very
  thing preventing it — which is why star formation happens in
  dark cold clouds and nowhere else at all. And no star forming
  alone: a cloud heavy enough to collapse breaks into pieces as it
  falls, so stars arrive in batches of hundreds.
THREAD: weather (condensation needing cooling and not heating),
  cooking (a mixture that sets only once it drops below a point).
ASKED-AS: star born forming cloud collapse gas dust nursery cold nebula ignite protostar batch

ESSENCE: the first big stars to light up in a cloud immediately
  start wrecking it — their radiation, their winds and finally
  their explosions blow the remaining gas apart. So only a small
  fraction of any cloud ever becomes stars, and a galaxy's rate of
  making them is set less by how much gas it holds than by how
  hard the new arrivals push back.
ROOT: engineering / feedback — a process that manufactures its own
  suppressor settles at a low rate instead of running away.
CANNOT: no cloud converting fully into stars, and no galaxy
  spending all its gas — without this brake the universe would
  have burned its fuel long ago and gone dark. And no predicting a
  cloud's yield from its mass alone: the yield depends on what
  ignited first and where it sat.
THREAD: fire (a blaze that blows its own fuel away), money (a boom
  whose own consequences choke it off).
ASKED-AS: star formation rate gas cloud left over efficiency winds blow away stops galaxy nursery

ESSENCE: plot stars by colour against true brightness and they do
  not scatter — nine in ten land on one narrow diagonal. What sets
  a star's place along it is its mass and essentially nothing
  else, and the band is not a road a star travels but a queue it
  joins at birth and keeps for most of its existence.
ROOT: physics / a ball of gas in balance has one stable
  arrangement for a given mass and composition, so mass fixes all
  the rest of it.
CANNOT: no star sitting off the band while still burning hydrogen
  in its core — being elsewhere on the chart means the core has
  changed, and that is the whole diagnostic power of the picture.
  And no age from one star's position: the band is where a star
  sits for ninety per cent of its life, so it dates nothing.
THREAD: engineering (a family of machines whose every rating
  follows from one number), the body (size setting an animal's rates).
ASKED-AS: main sequence diagram stars colour brightness mass hot blue cool red band chart

ESSENCE: a star ten times the sun's mass does not burn ten times
  faster, it burns thousands of times faster, because the crush at
  its centre is that much fiercer. So it holds ten times the fuel
  and spends it in a thousandth of the time: the heaviest stars
  last a few million years and the lightest will outlive the
  present universe many times over.
ROOT: physics / the rate of fusion climbs very steeply with
  temperature, and central temperature climbs with mass.
CANNOT: no old heavy star anywhere — every blue giant in the sky
  was made recently, so their presence dates a region as young
  without any other evidence. And no watching a small star die:
  none has finished yet, and none will for far longer than
  everything so far has lasted.
THREAD: money (a large income spent at a far larger rate), engines
  (power output rising much faster than tank capacity).
ASKED-AS: big stars die young live short sun lifetime billions massive burn fast dwarf

ESSENCE: a star holds steady for billions of years because its
  balance corrects itself. Squeeze it and it heats; hotter gas
  pushes back harder, so it swells and cools again. That single
  loop is why the sun has not flickered, flared out or gone dim
  across the whole history of life on this planet.
ROOT: engineering / a correction that grows with the error is a
  governor, and a star's own gas is one for free.
CANNOT: no runaway in a star that is able to expand, because the
  expansion IS the safety valve. Which is exactly why the
  dangerous cases are the ones that cannot expand — a core held up
  without heat cannot cool itself by swelling, so a flash there
  runs away instead of settling.
THREAD: engineering (the flyball governor, this idea in brass),
  the body (a fever held inside a narrow band by one loop).
ASKED-AS: sun steady stable not exploding balance gravity pressure fusion controlled thermostat billions years

ESSENCE: the sun's core is not hot enough to fuse hydrogen — not
  by any straightforward arithmetic — and it manages anyway, very
  rarely, through a quantum loophole. The first step of the chain
  is so improbable that a given particle waits billions of years
  for its turn, and that improbability is precisely why the sun
  lasts as long as it does.
ROOT: physics / like charges repel fiercely close up, so fusion
  proceeds by a rare leak through the barrier rather than over it.
CANNOT: no fast burning at solar temperatures, so no star of this
  mass with a short life. And no copying the sun's own reaction as
  a power source down here: reactors must use easier fuels at
  higher temperatures, because the reaction the sun runs on is far
  too slow to be useful anywhere except inside a star.
THREAD: chance (an event so rare that it functions as a clock),
  chemistry (a whole process paced by one difficult step).
ASKED-AS: why sun burns slowly rate reaction core not hot enough fusion reactor earth copy

ESSENCE: mass decides everything about a star and cannot be read
  off an image or a spectrum. It is obtained by watching two stars
  orbit each other and timing them — so nearly everything known
  about stellar masses comes from the roughly half of stars that
  happen to have a partner, and is then applied to the rest.
ROOT: physics / gravity's effect on an orbit depends on mass, so
  an orbit is the only weighing scale that works at that range.
CANNOT: no weighing a lone star directly; its mass is inferred
  from its place on the band, and that band was itself calibrated
  on pairs. And no orbit without patience: some pairs take
  centuries to go round once, so the catalogue is limited by how
  long people have been watching rather than by instruments.
THREAD: measurement (weighing a thing by its pull on another),
  navigation (a moving reference showing what a still one cannot).
ASKED-AS: star mass weigh how known double binary orbit period two stars together measure

ESSENCE: a cluster's stars were all born together, so they are one
  age at many masses — and the heavy ones leave the band first.
  Find the point where the stars are just beginning to peel off
  and you have read the cluster's age directly, because that is
  the mass whose lifetime has this moment run out.
ROOT: physics / lifetime depends on mass, so a population born at
  once burns down from the top and the burn line is a clock hand.
CANNOT: no age from a mixed-age population — the method needs
  everything to have started together, which is why ages come from
  clusters and not from ordinary sky. And no cluster older than
  the universe: this measurement set one of the first hard floors
  under cosmic age, and any conflict had to be resolved.
THREAD: farming (a planted stand ageing together and thinning from
  the top), evidence (a natural experiment beating a designed one).
ASKED-AS: star cluster age how old globular turnoff diagram same time born oldest measure

ESSENCE: when a star's core runs out of hydrogen the core
  contracts and heats while the outside does the opposite — the
  shell around the dead middle burns furiously and the envelope
  swells enormously and cools. A red giant is a tiny fierce core
  inside a vast thin skin, and the sun will eventually reach out
  past where this planet now orbits.
ROOT: physics / a shrinking core gives up gravitational energy as
  heat, and more heat poured into an envelope inflates it.
CANNOT: no giant that is simply a scaled-up ordinary star — the
  size is all envelope, and that envelope is so thin it would pass
  for a vacuum in a laboratory. And no return: once the core is
  inert ash the structure cannot go back to the band, so the
  swelling is one-way.
THREAD: cooking (a crust setting while the inside changes wholly),
  the body (a system compensating opposite to its own fault).
ASKED-AS: red giant sun future swell earth swallowed betelgeuse size huge core helium burning

ESSENCE: there is a second way to hold a star up that has nothing
  to do with heat. Squeeze matter far enough and its electrons
  refuse to be packed any closer, and that refusal pushes back
  hard and forever without needing to be warm — so a white dwarf
  can sit cooling for the rest of time and never collapse.
ROOT: physics / identical particles cannot occupy the same state,
  so crowding them costs energy and yields a pressure that does
  not depend on temperature.
CANNOT: no thermostat in a star held up this way — the support no
  longer cares about heat, so a burst of fusion cannot cool itself
  by expanding and runs away instead. And no white dwarf above a
  certain mass: this support has a ceiling, and past it nothing
  stands at all.
THREAD: engineering (a mechanical stop that holds with no power),
  crowds (a packed room resisting entry regardless of enthusiasm).
ASKED-AS: white dwarf held up crushed matter dense teaspoon tons cooling forever limit collapse

ESSENCE: a star of the sun's size never gets hot enough to burn
  past carbon, so it ends gently — pushing its outer layers off
  over a few thousand years and leaving a glowing shell lit by the
  bare core it has exposed. The shell drifts away within a few
  tens of thousands of years and the core cools for eternity.
ROOT: physics / fusion stops when a core cannot reach the next
  fuel's ignition temperature, and mass decides whether it can.
CANNOT: no supernova from a small star acting alone — that drama
  needs mass this one has not got. And no permanence in the
  beautiful shell: those glowing rings are the briefest thing in
  the whole catalogue, which is why so few are visible at any
  moment although every small star makes one.
THREAD: fire (embers left when the flame has gone), keeping
  knowledge (the visible phase being the shortest part of a life).
ASKED-AS: planetary nebula ring shell dying star sun end white dwarf glowing gas ejected

ESSENCE: a heavy star builds shells of steadily heavier ash until
  it makes iron, and iron is the end of the road — fusing it costs
  energy rather than giving it. Support vanishes at that instant,
  the core falls in over a fraction of a second, and the rebound
  and the flood of escaping particles tear the rest of the star
  apart.
ROOT: physics / a star stands on the energy that fusion releases,
  and iron is exactly where fusion stops paying its way.
CANNOT: no gradual ending for a big star — the failure is a sudden
  loss of support and not a fading, which is why the collapse
  takes about a second after ten million years of steadiness. And
  no iron core in a small star: it never reaches the temperatures,
  so this route belongs to the heavy ones alone.
THREAD: engineering (a structure failing the instant its prop goes
  rather than slowly), money (a run arriving in a day after years).
ASKED-AS: supernova explosion star death iron core collapse neutron blast bright sudden seconds

ESSENCE: the other kind of exploding star is a white dwarf that
  has been fed past the mass its support can bear — and because
  that ceiling is one particular number, these explosions all come
  out about equally bright. That accident is what turned them into
  the measuring rod used to find the distances of far galaxies.
ROOT: physics / a support with a fixed ceiling fails at a fixed
  size, so the event's brightness is set by physics rather than by
  circumstance.
CANNOT: no such uniformity in the collapse kind — those vary
  enormously with whatever star made them and are useless as
  rulers. And no assuming the fuse never changed: whether the far
  old ones match the near young ones is a live question, and the
  whole distance scale rides on the answer.
THREAD: manufacturing (a fuse rated to blow at one current),
  measurement (a standard that must be shown not to have drifted).
ASKED-AS: supernova type white dwarf explode same brightness standard measuring distance galaxies limit mass

ESSENCE: press a collapsing core past the electrons' limit and the
  particles merge — a thing heavier than the sun ends up the size
  of a city, spinning many times a second, beaming radio along a
  tilted magnetic axis. From here that beam sweeps past as a pulse
  regular enough to rival an atomic clock.
ROOT: physics / a shrinking body spins faster and concentrates its
  magnetic field, and a tilted beam on a spinning object is a
  lighthouse.
CANNOT: no seeing one whose beam misses us, so most are invisible
  and the catalogue is a small biased slice of what is out there.
  And no perfectly constant rate: they slow measurably as they
  radiate, and that slowing is how their ages and their field
  strengths are worked out.
THREAD: navigation (a lighthouse identified by its flash interval),
  measurement (a precision clock found rather than manufactured).

ESSENCE: nothing about a black hole can be seen, so every known
  one was found by what it does to something else — a star
  swinging round an invisible partner, gas heated to X-ray
  temperatures as it queues to fall in, spacetime ringing when two
  merge, or a shadow cut out of the glow behind it.
ROOT: evidence / an object that emits nothing can still be located
  precisely by its effects, provided the effects are unmistakable.
CANNOT: no photograph of one, ever — the famous image shows the
  glowing material outside, and the dark middle is an absence
  rather than a thing. And no confusing one with a dim star at
  these masses: nothing else can be that heavy and that small,
  which is what makes the identification safe.
THREAD: evidence (a hole in a pattern being as telling as a mark),
  medicine (a blockage diagnosed by what fails downstream of it).

ESSENCE: at the middle of essentially every large galaxy sits a
  black hole of millions to billions of suns, and its mass tracks
  the properties of the galaxy around it. That is strange, because
  its direct gravitational reach covers a minute fraction of what
  it correlates with — so something in the growing tied them.
ROOT: evidence / a tight relation between two quantities that
  cannot directly influence each other points to a shared history
  rather than to a shared force.
CANNOT: no explaining that link by the hole's pull, which at
  galactic distances is nothing — the connection has to be the
  energy it threw out while feeding. And no quiet centre in a
  galaxy whose hole is being fed: a feeding core outshines its
  entire galaxy from a region no bigger than a solar system.
THREAD: money (a small central actor scaling a whole economy),
  engineering (two parts coupled by exhaust rather than contact).

ESSENCE: when two heavy compact objects spiral together they shake
  the geometry of space itself, and the ripple that spreads out
  stretches and squeezes everything it crosses. Catching it meant
  building an instrument able to measure a change thousands of
  times smaller than a proton across four kilometres of vacuum.
ROOT: physics / mass curves space, so mass in violent motion sends
  that curvature outward as waves.
CANNOT: no blocking or absorbing them — they pass through a planet
  unhindered, which is why they bring news out of places light can
  never leave. And no direction from a single detector: it takes
  three or more, timed against each other, to say where in the sky
  the event happened.
THREAD: sound (hearing an event round a corner that cannot be
  seen), measurement (a whole science waiting on one instrument).

ESSENCE: light made in the sun's middle is delayed for ages by the
  crowd it has to push through, so sunshine is old news off the
  surface. Neutrinos leave the core directly and arrive in eight
  minutes, passing through the entire sun and the entire earth
  without noticing — the only direct report of what is happening
  inside a star right now.
ROOT: physics / a particle that interacts almost never travels
  almost unhindered, so it carries information out of places
  nothing else escapes.
CANNOT: no catching them easily — the same reluctance means a
  detector must be enormous, buried deep, and content with a
  handful a day. And no picture from them: the arrival direction
  is crude, so they answer what is happening and almost never
  exactly where it is happening.
THREAD: medicine (a blood test reporting on an unreachable organ),
  evidence (the one messenger that ignores every wall).

ESSENCE: different elements came from different factories and the
  split is clean. Hydrogen and most helium were made in the first
  minutes of the universe. Everything from carbon up to iron was
  cooked inside stars. Gold, uranium and their neighbours need
  conditions no ordinary star provides, and are made in explosions
  and in the collision of two neutron stars.
ROOT: physics / building a nucleus needs particular temperatures,
  densities and neutron supplies, and no one place offers them all.
CANNOT: no gold out of ordinary stellar burning at any temperature
  a normal star reaches, so every gold atom here arrived from a
  catastrophe. And no undiscovered fourth source for the light
  elements: the measured amounts of helium and lithium match the
  first-minutes prediction too closely to leave room for one.
THREAD: cooking (dishes that need different ovens), manufacturing
  (a supply chain of processes that cannot share a building).
ASKED-AS: where elements come from gold made stars supernova iron carbon collision neutron origin

ESSENCE: fusing light nuclei together releases energy and
  splitting heavy ones releases energy, and iron sits at the
  bottom of the valley where neither pays. That one fact sets the
  fuel of every star, the manner of every heavy star's death, and
  why both a fusion bomb and a fission reactor work while nothing
  at all works on iron.
ROOT: physics / binding energy per particle peaks close to iron,
  so reactions run downhill toward it from both directions.
CANNOT: no energy from fusing iron and none from splitting it — it
  is the ash of both processes and the universe's final rubbish.
  And no star surviving its own iron: making the stuff is not
  merely unprofitable, it actively drains the core that made it.
THREAD: chemistry (a reaction running toward the lowest state),
  money (an asset at the bottom of everybody's ladder).

ESSENCE: nebula was a word for anything fuzzy and it still covers
  four unrelated things: clouds glowing because a hot star excites
  them, clouds merely reflecting a neighbour's light, clouds
  visible only as black gaps because they block what is behind,
  and the shed skins of dying stars. Three of the four make no
  light of their own.
ROOT: light + seeing / a faint patch of sky can be a source, a
  mirror or a hole, and at low resolution all three look alike.
CANNOT: no telling which kind by looking — it takes a spectrum,
  since a glowing cloud shows its own bright lines while a
  reflecting one shows a copy of the star's. And no colour in any
  of them by eye: the famous colours are long exposures, and at
  the eyepiece almost everything is grey.
THREAD: evidence (three mechanisms wearing one appearance),
  photography (the picture and the sight being different things).
ASKED-AS: nebula orion pictures colours real eye telescope dark dust glowing reflecting types grey

ESSENCE: the space between stars holds a thin smoke of grains that
  scatters blue more than red, so distant stars look both dimmer
  and redder than they truly are — the same reason a sunset is
  red. Every distance and every colour measured through the
  galaxy's plane has to be corrected for it, and the correction
  itself is uncertain.
ROOT: physics / small particles scatter short wavelengths far more
  strongly, so passage through dust removes light and shifts its
  balance at once.
CANNOT: no seeing the galaxy's own centre in ordinary light — the
  dust between is thick enough to hide it completely, which is why
  the middle of our own galaxy was mapped in infrared and radio.
  And no separating a reddened star from a genuinely cool one
  without some further measurement to break the tie.
THREAD: weather (haze making a hill look further off), evidence (a
  systematic bias that must be modelled, never averaged away).

ESSENCE: a cluster hands astronomy the thing it normally cannot
  get: many stars at one distance, of one age, out of one batch of
  material, differing only in mass. Nearly everything known about
  how mass governs a star's life was read off these, because in a
  cluster the other variables are already held still.
ROOT: evidence / an experiment is a comparison with all but one
  thing held constant, and nature occasionally supplies one free.
CANNOT: no such comparison among scattered field stars — differing
  distances alone ruin it, since brightness cannot be untangled
  from range. And no cluster lasting forever: the loose ones are
  pulled apart by the galaxy within a few hundred million years,
  so the ancient survivors are all of the tightly bound kind.
THREAD: medicine (a study of siblings controlling what a broad
  survey cannot), teaching (a clean comparison beating a big messy).

ESSENCE: galaxies come in two conditions more than two shapes.
  Some are flat, still holding gas, still making blue new stars
  along their arms. Others are shapeless piles of old red stars
  with almost no gas and nothing new for billions of years. The
  type is a report of whether the gas ran out or was taken away.
ROOT: the living world / a system's present state records which of
  its supplies was exhausted, and roughly when.
CANNOT: no spiral without gas — the arms are visible precisely
  because bright young stars trace them, so an old galaxy cannot
  display them whatever its shape. And no going back up the
  sequence: mergers and stripping push galaxies one direction, and
  nothing common pushes them the other way.
THREAD: farming (a field's state naming what it was given),
  history (an institution's shape recording its interruptions).
ASKED-AS: galaxy types spiral elliptical shape arms blue old red gas stars forming andromeda

ESSENCE: galaxies collide often and their stars essentially never
  do — the spacing is so vast that two galaxies pass clean through
  each other with no stellar impacts at all. What does collide is
  the gas, and that is what lights the burst of new stars, while
  gravity draws the stars out into long tails.
ROOT: measured premise — stars sit millions of times their own
  size apart, while gas fills the space between them continuously.
CANNOT: no wreckage of smashed stars in a merger, so for anything
  solid the event is silent. And no watching it happen: the whole
  encounter runs across a billion years, so every photograph ever
  taken of one is a single frozen frame from a process nobody will
  ever see move.
