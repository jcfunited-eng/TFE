# 115 DEEP ASTRONOMY AND COSMOS — what is out there and how it is known
Nobody has been anywhere. Every fact in this file was got out of
arriving light, arriving particles, and arithmetic — which makes
how it is known as much of the subject as what is known.

ESSENCE: nothing far away has ever had its distance measured
  directly. Every figure beyond a few thousand light years rests
  on a method calibrated against a nearer method, and that on one
  nearer still — a ladder whose bottom rung is plain geometry and
  whose top rung is the age of the universe.
ROOT: measurement / a technique that works over one range is used
  to calibrate one that reaches further, then handed the load.
THREAD: measurement (a chain of calibrations is worth its worst
  link), money (a price pinned to a comparable pinned to another).

ESSENCE: hold a finger up and blink each eye in turn — it jumps
  against the background, and the nearer it is the more it jumps.
  The earth's orbit gives astronomy two eyes three hundred million
  kilometres apart, and a nearby star's tiny annual jump is the
  one distance that assumes nothing whatever about the star.
ROOT: mathematics / a triangle with a known base and a measured
  angle is fully decided, with no physics required at all.
THREAD: navigation (a position fixed by angles to known points),
  seeing (two eyes judging near distance and giving up on far).
ASKED-AS: parallax nearest star distance measured angle earth orbit months shift telescope geometry triangle

ESSENCE: if you know how bright a thing truly is and you measure
  how bright it looks, the gap between them is the distance,
  because light thins by the square as it spreads. The whole
  difficulty is knowing the true brightness in advance, and only a
  few objects announce it — certain stars by the rhythm of their
  pulsing, and one kind of exploding star by having a fixed fuse.
ROOT: physics / brightness falls with the square of distance, so
  apparent brightness plus real brightness solves for range.
THREAD: sound (a shout's loudness gives range only if you know the
  shouter), money (a valuation by comparison needs one real sale).

ESSENCE: the brightest stars in the night sky are not the greatest
  stars. They are a mixture of close ordinary ones and distant
  furnaces, and the eye cannot tell which is which. The nearest
  star to the sun cannot be seen without a telescope, and some of
  the most powerful in the galaxy have no names at all.
ROOT: physics / what arrives is real output divided by distance
  squared, so two unknowns reach you welded into one number.
THREAD: the mind (a near voice sounding like an important one),
  evidence (one number holding two unknowns needs a second reading).

ESSENCE: a spectrum is not one reading but a stack of them taken
  off the same lines. Which lines appear names the elements, how
  deep they cut gives the amounts, their position gives motion
  toward or away, their width gives pressure and spin, and their
  splitting gives magnetic field — all from a smear of light too
  faint to see.
ROOT: physics / an atom absorbs and emits at exact wavelengths,
  and each physical condition nudges those wavelengths its own way.
THREAD: chemistry (a fingerprint that identifies without touching),
  medicine (one blood sample answering a dozen separate questions).

ESSENCE: the same gas shows dark lines or bright ones depending
  purely on where you stand. Look through a cool cloud at a hot
  source behind it and the cloud steals its own colours out,
  leaving gaps. Look at that same cloud from the side with nothing
  bright behind, and those exact colours are the whole picture.
ROOT: physics / a gas absorbs and re-emits the same wavelengths,
  so what you see depends on whether the re-emission is set
  against a bright background or is all there is.
THREAD: seeing (a stained window from inside and from outside),
  evidence (one fact reported positively or as an absence).
ASKED-AS: dark lines spectrum sun absorption emission nebula glowing gas cloud behind bright background

ESSENCE: a hot body radiates across a whole range with a peak that
  slides shorter as it heats — so a cold cloud peaks in infrared,
  a star in visible light, and gas falling onto a black hole in
  X-rays. The consequence is blunt: most of what the universe
  emits arrives at wavelengths no eye can register.
ROOT: physics / hotter matter radiates more and radiates it bluer,
  with a peak that tracks temperature exactly.
THREAD: heat (a warm room glowing to the right camera in the
  dark), photography (a filter deciding what the picture contains).

ESSENCE: stretched light says a source is receding and by how
  much, but only because the true home wavelengths of those lines
  are known from a bench in a laboratory. Without that fixed
  reference the stretch cannot be measured at all — so the entire
  picture of an expanding universe rests on a hydrogen atom out
  there being a hydrogen atom here.
ROOT: physics / a shift is a difference from a known value, so
  measuring it requires possessing the known value.
THREAD: sound (a siren's drop giving speed only if you knew the
  note), measurement (every measurement is difference from a mark).

ESSENCE: a star begins as the coldest densest thing for light
  years around — a cloud so cold that gravity finally beats the
  pressure holding it open. Falling in heats it, and the heat
  fights back, so the collapse is slow and ignition comes only
  when the middle is hot enough to fuse. Cold is the requirement,
  never warmth.
ROOT: physics / gravity beats pressure only below a certain
  temperature for a given mass, so collapse is a cold-side event.
THREAD: weather (condensation needing cooling and not heating),
  cooking (a mixture that sets only once it drops below a point).

ESSENCE: the first big stars to light up in a cloud immediately
  start wrecking it — their radiation, their winds and finally
  their explosions blow the remaining gas apart. So only a small
  fraction of any cloud ever becomes stars, and a galaxy's rate of
  making them is set less by how much gas it holds than by how
  hard the new arrivals push back.
ROOT: engineering / feedback — a process that manufactures its own
  suppressor settles at a low rate instead of running away.
THREAD: fire (a blaze that blows its own fuel away), money (a boom
  whose own consequences choke it off).

ESSENCE: plot stars by colour against true brightness and they do
  not scatter — nine in ten land on one narrow diagonal. What sets
  a star's place along it is its mass and essentially nothing
  else, and the band is not a road a star travels but a queue it
  joins at birth and keeps for most of its existence.
ROOT: physics / a ball of gas in balance has one stable
  arrangement for a given mass and composition, so mass fixes all
  the rest of it.
THREAD: engineering (a family of machines whose every rating
  follows from one number), the body (size setting an animal's rates).

ESSENCE: a star ten times the sun's mass does not burn ten times
  faster, it burns thousands of times faster, because the crush at
  its centre is that much fiercer. So it holds ten times the fuel
  and spends it in a thousandth of the time: the heaviest stars
  last a few million years and the lightest will outlive the
  present universe many times over.
ROOT: physics / the rate of fusion climbs very steeply with
  temperature, and central temperature climbs with mass.
THREAD: money (a large income spent at a far larger rate), engines
  (power output rising much faster than tank capacity).

ESSENCE: a star holds steady for billions of years because its
  balance corrects itself. Squeeze it and it heats; hotter gas
  pushes back harder, so it swells and cools again. That single
  loop is why the sun has not flickered, flared out or gone dim
  across the whole history of life on this planet.
ROOT: engineering / a correction that grows with the error is a
  governor, and a star's own gas is one for free.
THREAD: engineering (the flyball governor, this idea in brass),
  the body (a fever held inside a narrow band by one loop).

ESSENCE: the sun's core is not hot enough to fuse hydrogen — not
  by any straightforward arithmetic — and it manages anyway, very
  rarely, through a quantum loophole. The first step of the chain
  is so improbable that a given particle waits billions of years
  for its turn, and that improbability is precisely why the sun
  lasts as long as it does.
ROOT: physics / like charges repel fiercely close up, so fusion
  proceeds by a rare leak through the barrier rather than over it.
THREAD: chance (an event so rare that it functions as a clock),
  chemistry (a whole process paced by one difficult step).

ESSENCE: mass decides everything about a star and cannot be read
  off an image or a spectrum. It is obtained by watching two stars
  orbit each other and timing them — so nearly everything known
  about stellar masses comes from the roughly half of stars that
  happen to have a partner, and is then applied to the rest.
ROOT: physics / gravity's effect on an orbit depends on mass, so
  an orbit is the only weighing scale that works at that range.
THREAD: measurement (weighing a thing by its pull on another),
  navigation (a moving reference showing what a still one cannot).

ESSENCE: a cluster's stars were all born together, so they are one
  age at many masses — and the heavy ones leave the band first.
  Find the point where the stars are just beginning to peel off
  and you have read the cluster's age directly, because that is
  the mass whose lifetime has this moment run out.
ROOT: physics / lifetime depends on mass, so a population born at
  once burns down from the top and the burn line is a clock hand.
THREAD: farming (a planted stand ageing together and thinning from
  the top), evidence (a natural experiment beating a designed one).

ESSENCE: when a star's core runs out of hydrogen the core
  contracts and heats while the outside does the opposite — the
  shell around the dead middle burns furiously and the envelope
  swells enormously and cools. A red giant is a tiny fierce core
  inside a vast thin skin, and the sun will eventually reach out
  past where this planet now orbits.
ROOT: physics / a shrinking core gives up gravitational energy as
  heat, and more heat poured into an envelope inflates it.
THREAD: cooking (a crust setting while the inside changes wholly),
  the body (a system compensating opposite to its own fault).

ESSENCE: there is a second way to hold a star up that has nothing
  to do with heat. Squeeze matter far enough and its electrons
  refuse to be packed any closer, and that refusal pushes back
  hard and forever without needing to be warm — so a white dwarf
  can sit cooling for the rest of time and never collapse.
ROOT: physics / identical particles cannot occupy the same state,
  so crowding them costs energy and yields a pressure that does
  not depend on temperature.
THREAD: engineering (a mechanical stop that holds with no power),
  crowds (a packed room resisting entry regardless of enthusiasm).

ESSENCE: a star of the sun's size never gets hot enough to burn
  past carbon, so it ends gently — pushing its outer layers off
  over a few thousand years and leaving a glowing shell lit by the
  bare core it has exposed. The shell drifts away within a few
  tens of thousands of years and the core cools for eternity.
ROOT: physics / fusion stops when a core cannot reach the next
  fuel's ignition temperature, and mass decides whether it can.
THREAD: fire (embers left when the flame has gone), keeping
  knowledge (the visible phase being the shortest part of a life).

ESSENCE: a heavy star builds shells of steadily heavier ash until
  it makes iron, and iron is the end of the road — fusing it costs
  energy rather than giving it. Support vanishes at that instant,
  the core falls in over a fraction of a second, and the rebound
  and the flood of escaping particles tear the rest of the star
  apart.
ROOT: physics / a star stands on the energy that fusion releases,
  and iron is exactly where fusion stops paying its way.
THREAD: engineering (a structure failing the instant its prop goes

ESSENCE: the other kind of exploding star is a white dwarf that

ESSENCE: nothing about a black hole can be seen, so every known

ESSENCE: different elements came from different factories and the

ESSENCE: fusing light nuclei together releases energy and

ESSENCE: the space between stars holds a thin smoke of grains that

  them — while collecting only as much light as their own two
  surfaces. So radio astronomy makes images sharper than any
  optical telescope using equipment spread across continents.
ROOT: physics / resolution depends on the width of the
  arrangement, light collected depends on the area of the
  collectors, and those are separate properties of one array.
THREAD: hearing (two ears set apart placing a sound precisely),
  engineering (buying one property without buying the other).

ESSENCE: the space between stars holds about one atom in a
  thimble, far emptier than the best vacuum ever pumped in a
  laboratory, and the space between galaxies is emptier still by
  an enormous factor. That emptiness is not a curiosity — it is
  why light crosses billions of years unscattered, and therefore
  why any of this can be known at all.
ROOT: measured premise — matter is gathered into a tiny fraction
  of the volume and the rest is nearly nothing.
THREAD: weather (fog ending sight within metres), evidence (a
  field of study made possible by a property of its subject).

ESSENCE: a comet leaves grit strung all the way along its orbit,
  and the earth crosses certain of those orbits on the same dates
  every year — so a meteor shower is an entry in the calendar and
  not an event. The streaks appear to fan out from one point
  simply because we are flying into them.
ROOT: physics / parallel paths seen from inside appear to radiate
  from the direction of travel, the way snow does at a windscreen.
THREAD: driving (rain seeming to come from straight ahead),
  calendars (an annual date with a physical rather than social cause).

ESSENCE: close to a planet, the difference in pull across a moon's
  own width beats the moon's grip on itself — so nothing large
  survives inside that radius and the material there stays as
  rings. Every ring system in this solar system lies inside that
  line and every substantial moon lies outside it.
ROOT: physics / gravity weakens with distance, so a big body close
  in is pulled apart by the difference in pull across its diameter.
THREAD: tides (the same difference-in-pull force at ocean scale),
  engineering (a structure failing under differential load).

ESSENCE: when one orbit's period is a simple fraction of another's,
  the same nudge repeats at the same place over and over and small
  pushes add up. That single fact empties gaps in the asteroid
  belt, locks Neptune and Pluto into a pattern that keeps them
  apart forever, and holds moons in chains around Jupiter.
ROOT: physics / repeated small forces applied in step accumulate,
  while the same forces applied at random cancel out.
THREAD: music (a note driving a string tuned to answer it),
  engineering (a bridge shaken by feet that happen to keep time).

ESSENCE: the planets sometimes appear to halt and travel backwards
  for weeks, and every earth-centred model had to bolt an extra
  circle onto each planet to reproduce it. The sun-centred picture
  gets the same motion for nothing: the backward loop is what
  overtaking looks like from a moving vehicle.
ROOT: evidence / a model that gets a fact as a consequence beats
  one that must add machinery to accommodate it, even when the two
  fit the observations equally well.
THREAD: evidence (fitting old data is cheap and predicting new

ESSENCE: the sun's magnetic field tangles because its equator
  turns faster than its poles, and about every eleven years the
  tangle breaks down and the whole field turns over — so spots,
  flares and eruptions rise and fall on that beat. Spot counts
  have been kept for four centuries, and they include a stretch of
  seventy years with almost none at all.
ROOT: physics / a magnetic field frozen into a sheared fluid gets
  wound up, and a wound-up field eventually lets go.
THREAD: engineering (a wound spring that must eventually unload),
  history (a record kept long enough to catch a rare absence).
