# 128 DEEP LAW AND JUSTICE — rules with teeth
File 17 says what law is. This one opens the machine: the engines
that make rules, the tests that decide cases, the way harm is priced,
and the compromises every legal system makes because it cannot try
every case, know every fact, or satisfy all four of its own aims.

ESSENCE: law's distinctive act is announcing, in advance, what will
  be done to you and for what. Not that rules exist — every group has
  those — but that they are published before the act, so a person can
  read them and steer.
ROOT: law / a rule must be findable to be owed; the mind / people can
  only aim at a target they can see.
THREAD: games (rules fixed before the deal), teaching (a mark scheme
  handed out with the assignment), employment (a policy that cannot
  discipline yesterday's conduct).
ASKED-AS: made it illegal afterwards retroactive new law punished vague rule fair notice

ESSENCE: law and morality overlap and neither contains the other.
  Much that is plainly wrong is perfectly legal, and some legal
  duties nobody thinks are moral. Law is the smaller, cruder set
  because it must be enforceable, provable, and stated in advance for
  strangers.
ROOT: right + wrong / a moral claim needs no procedure; law / a legal
  claim needs one, and the procedure is what shrinks it.
THREAD: medicine (what is licensed against what is wise), business
  (lawful and shameful trading), families (a promise that no court
  will ever hear).
ASKED-AS: legal but wrong immoral not illegal should there be a law morality difference

ESSENCE: two engines make law. One writes a general rule in advance
  and applies it forward to whatever arrives. The other decides one
  real case and lets the rule emerge behind it. The first is blind to
  the case; the second is exact and accumulates slowly.
ROOT: law / like cases must fall alike — and the likeness can be
  declared first or discovered afterwards.
THREAD: language (a grammar written down against usage hardening into
  rule), medicine (a protocol issued against practice settling from
  cases), building (a code against a trade custom).
ASKED-AS: statute case law legislation judges make law precedent codes which system

ESSENCE: a constitution is a law that ordinary law may not
  contradict, which only means something if somebody may strike down
  a validly passed act. So every such system contains a body that can
  overrule the elected — and that is the design, not a fault in it.
ROOT: people and power / the deepest rule is the one saying how rules
  may be changed, and this is that rule armed.
THREAD: sport (a governing body that voids a result the crowd wanted),
  clubs (a founding charter needing a two-thirds vote), families (a
  will that binds the heirs who would rather it did not).
ASKED-AS: constitution unconstitutional struck down supreme court unelected judges overrule parliament basic law

ESSENCE: splitting the power to write rules, to apply them, and to
  judge them is distrust turned into architecture. The point is not
  efficiency — it is that harming a citizen should require several
  bodies, with different interests and different timetables, to agree.
ROOT: power / power drifts toward concentration, so dispersal must be
  engineered against the current.
THREAD: banking (two signatures on a payment), aviation (two pilots
  who must both agree), publishing (a writer, an editor and a
  fact-checker who do not report to each other).
ASKED-AS: separation of powers checks balances branches government judiciary executive independent slow

ESSENCE: how an answer was reached is part of the answer. The same
  verdict is a different thing depending on whether the person was
  told the case, allowed to reply, and judged by somebody with no
  stake — because those three are what make the verdict believable to
  everyone who was not there.
ROOT: power / procedure removes persons from decisions, and belief in
  the outcome is what procedure buys.
THREAD: sport (a referee who is not a supporter), schools (a
  disciplinary hearing with the pupil present), medicine (a second
  opinion from outside the team).
ASKED-AS: due process fair hearing told the charge chance to reply impartial judge biased

ESSENCE: how sure a court must be is not a fixed thing — it is a
  deliberate choice about which mistake to make more often. Beyond
  reasonable doubt frees some guilty to protect the innocent. The
  balance of probabilities splits errors evenly, because money is
  recoverable and years are not.
ROOT: law / the two errors are unequal, so the threshold is set by
  what is at stake rather than by how much can be known.
THREAD: medicine (a screening threshold trading missed cases against
  false alarms), engineering (a safety factor chosen from the cost of
  failure), hiring (a bar that rejects good people to avoid bad ones).
ASKED-AS: beyond reasonable doubt balance of probabilities how sure standard proof civil criminal

ESSENCE: one act can raise two separate questions. Did you wrong the
  public, so that the state should punish you? And did you wrong this
  person, so that you should repay them? Different accusers,
  different thresholds, different outcomes — and one can be lost while
  the other is won.
ROOT: law / punishment and compensation answer different needs and
  therefore cannot share a proceeding.
THREAD: sport (a red card and a separate assault charge), employment
  (dismissal and a tribunal claim as distinct events), insurance (a
  payout unaffected by whether anyone was prosecuted).
ASKED-AS: criminal civil sued and charged both acquitted still liable damages punishment difference

ESSENCE: a serious offence needs two things: the forbidden act, and a
  state of mind. That state comes in a ladder — meant it, knew it
  would happen, saw the risk and went on anyway, or simply fell below
  the standard — and where you stand on that ladder is most of what
  separates murder from an accident.
ROOT: right + wrong / blame follows choice, and the ladder is the
  law's attempt to grade how much choice there was.
THREAD: medicine (a bad outcome from a good decision), driving (an
  accident, carelessness, and racing through a crowd), teaching (a
  wrong answer against copied work).
ASKED-AS: intent recklessness negligence meant to murder manslaughter accident state of mind

ESSENCE: a contract is two people writing private law for
  themselves, which courts will then enforce as if the state had
  passed it. Most of it is invisible: the law supplies default terms
  for everything the parties forgot, and those defaults govern until
  somebody writes over them.
ROOT: law / obligation flows from agreement; and a legal system
  cannot function if every deal must be written from nothing.
THREAD: building (a standard form with a schedule of amendments),
  language (grammar supplied so speakers need not agree it), games
  (house rules layered on a printed rulebook).
ASKED-AS: contract default terms implied not written in law fills gaps standard form

ESSENCE: consent is the source of a contract's force, so where
  consent was not real the contract is not either. Signed under
  threat, signed by a child, signed on terms nobody could have read —
  each is a case where the law refuses to treat a signature as a
  choice.
ROOT: right + wrong / agreement means less as the power gap widens,
  and the law must draw a line somewhere in that gradient.
THREAD: medicine (consent forms that must be explained, not just
  signed), employment (terms a tribunal will strike out), lending
  (rates capped whatever the borrower agreed).
ASKED-AS: signed under pressure unfair terms void small print unconscionable duress capacity minor

ESSENCE: the law usually does not force you to keep your promise. It
  makes you pay what breaking it cost the other side. So a contract
  is, quietly, an option: perform, or compensate — and sometimes
  breaking and paying is the efficient thing for everyone.
ROOT: law / a court can move money reliably and can supervise
  performance only clumsily, so it does what it does well.
THREAD: employment (notice paid in lieu of worked), building (a
  contractor who pays rather than finishes), tickets (a refund rather
  than a seat).
ASKED-AS: broke contract damages sue force them to do it specific performance compensation walk away

ESSENCE: damages aim to put the wronged party where performance
  would have left them — not where they started. And the claimant
  must limit their own loss: sitting still while the harm grows is
  not a bigger claim, it is an uncollectable one.
ROOT: law / the remedy must match the wrong, and the wrong here is
  the missing performance rather than the broken promise.
THREAD: insurance (a duty to prevent further damage after a leak),
  farming (a crop that could have been sold elsewhere), medicine (a
  patient who refuses the treatment that would have limited the
  injury).
ASKED-AS: how much can i claim damages mitigate loss expectation put back position

ESSENCE: tort is the law for harm between strangers who agreed
  nothing. It does not ask who is a bad person. It asks who bears a
  loss that has already happened — because the loss will not vanish,
  and leaving it where it fell is itself a decision.
ROOT: premise — every harm lands on somebody, so the only question a
  legal system can actually answer is where it should rest.
THREAD: insurance (the whole trade is a market in who bears losses),
  neighbours (a fallen tree and whose tree it was), medicine (an
  injury that must be lived with whoever pays).
ASKED-AS: sue for injury accident who pays compensation negligence claim damages personal

ESSENCE: negligence has four parts: you owed care to this person,
  you fell below the standard, that caused the harm, and the harm is
  real. The whole weight sits on "the standard" — what a reasonable
  person would have done, decided afterwards by people who now know
  what happened.
ROOT: law / a rule must be general, so the standard is a fiction of
  an ordinary careful person rather than a checklist.
THREAD: medicine (a protocol followed against a bad outcome), driving
  (a speed lawful and yet too fast for the conditions), employment (a
  risk assessment done after the fall).
ASKED-AS: negligence duty of care reasonable person standard breach caused harm hindsight

ESSENCE: two questions hide inside "caused". Would the harm have
  happened anyway without this act? And even if not, is this
  consequence close enough to count? The second exists because every
  effect traces back forever, and the law must cut the chain
  somewhere.
ROOT: causation / a cause is one link in an endless chain, and any
  stopping point is a judgement rather than a discovery.
THREAD: medicine (a treatment that made no difference to the
  outcome), history (a war traced back to a wrong turning), insurance
  (a policy naming which links it covers).
ASKED-AS: caused it but for remote consequence chain too far foreseeable proximate blame

ESSENCE: for some activities the law stops asking about fault. Keep
  a dangerous thing, sell a defective product, run a hazardous
  process — and if it harms someone you pay, careful or not. The
  incentive lands on whoever chose the activity, because they alone
  can choose not to.
ROOT: law / where proving fault is impossible for the victim and
  prevention is cheapest for the actor, liability follows the actor.
THREAD: farming (an escaped bull), manufacture (a product recall
  regardless of how careful the plant was), transport (a carrier
  liable for goods however the damage occurred).
ASKED-AS: strict liability no fault dangerous product defect regardless of care responsible anyway

ESSENCE: property is not a thing, it is a bundle of separate rights
  over a thing — to use it, to exclude others, to sell it, to lend
  it, to alter it, to leave it to someone. Those sticks can be held
  by different people at once, which is why one field can have four
  owners of different kinds.
ROOT: law / a title is an agreement about behaviour toward an object,
  and agreements can be divided as finely as anyone likes.
THREAD: music (writing, recording and performing rights held apart),
  land (mineral rights sold from beneath a farm), families (a house
  occupied for life and owned by the children).
ASKED-AS: own the land mineral rights lease easement mortgage who really owns bundle

ESSENCE: the law protects whoever actually holds a thing against
  everybody except the true owner. That looks like rewarding the
  wrong person and is not — because otherwise every dispute would
  begin with someone taking it back by force and arguing afterwards.
ROOT: power / the state exists to remove private remedy, so it must
  defend the present arrangement while the claim is heard.
THREAD: rentals (an occupier who must be evicted through a process),
  lending (a borrowed tool that must be sued for, not seized),
  squatting (occupation ripening into a right).
ASKED-AS: possession nine tenths take it back myself squatters eviction repossess owner title

ESSENCE: a right good against the whole world has to be findable by
  the whole world. That is what a register is for — not to record
  what people agreed, but to let a stranger buying tomorrow discover
  what he is about to be bound by.
ROOT: law / a rule must be findable to be owed, applied to rights
  over things rather than to conduct.
THREAD: computing (a directory turning a name into an address),
  shipping (a register of vessels and their charges), marriage (a
  public record so nobody marries a spouse who is already bound).
ASKED-AS: land registry title deeds search hidden charge buyer discover public record notice

ESSENCE: an idea copies for nothing, so nobody can be excluded from
  it naturally. Intellectual property manufactures that exclusion on
  purpose, to pay for the making — and then ends it, because the
  whole bargain was temporary monopoly in exchange for eventual
  public ownership.
ROOT: money / value needs rarity, and here the rarity is
  deliberately created by law rather than found in the world.
THREAD: farming (a plant variety protected for a period), medicine (a
  drug patent and the generic that follows), publishing (works falling
  into the public domain).
ASKED-AS: copyright patent how long expires public domain idea protect steal my idea

ESSENCE: three different tools get muddled into one phrase. A patent
  buys a short monopoly for publishing how a thing is done. Copyright
  protects a particular expression for a long time and no idea inside
  it. A trademark protects a name so buyers know who they are dealing
  with, and lasts as long as it is used.
ROOT: this file / artificial scarcity must be shaped to the job it is
  paying for, and the three jobs are different.
THREAD: cooking (a recipe unprotectable, a cookbook protected, a
  brand protected differently), engineering (a published method
  against a guarded process), language (a brand name swallowed into
  common speech).
ASKED-AS: patent copyright trademark difference protect invention name logo recipe secret

ESSENCE: when a loss must fall somewhere, the useful question is who
  could have prevented it most cheaply. Placing it there makes the
  rule do work in advance: the party best placed to avoid the harm
  now has a reason to.
ROOT: premise — behaviour follows incentives, so a liability rule is
  a price signal aimed at whoever can respond to it.
THREAD: shipping (loss allocated to whoever controls the cargo at
  each stage), banking (fraud losses placed to make the bank build
  better checks), building (the party who chose the material bearing
  its failure).
ASKED-AS: who should pay cheapest to prevent liability rule incentive safer allocate loss

ESSENCE: a company can own, sue, owe and be punished as if it were a
  person — and its owners cannot be made to pay its debts. That
  shield is a deliberate subsidy to risk-taking. What it hides is
  that the unpaid losses do not vanish; they land on creditors,
  employees and the public.
ROOT: law / a legal person is a fiction created because it is useful,
  and this one is useful precisely because it caps somebody's
  downside.
THREAD: money (investment that would not happen without a cap on
  loss), medicine (a dissolved firm and the patients still injured),
  families (a household that cannot ring-fence its debts the same
  way).
ASKED-AS: limited company liability shareholders not personally liable corporation fined bankrupt creditors

ESSENCE: there are two ways to stop harm. Write a rule that forbids
  the risky act in advance, or wait and make the harmer pay. Rules
  are certain, cheap to obey and clumsy about the odd case. Liability
  is exactly tailored, and it arrives after somebody has already been
  hurt.
ROOT: law / prevention and compensation are different instruments,
  and every system runs both because neither alone is enough.
THREAD: driving (a speed limit and a claim for careless driving),
  food (inspection and a claim for poisoning), building (a code and a
  suit against the builder).
ASKED-AS: regulation versus lawsuit prevent harm rules before or pay after inspection liability

ESSENCE: winning is three separate questions. Will this court hear
  it? Whose law does it apply? And can the judgment actually be
  collected where the loser's money is? A victory on the first two
  and a failure on the third is a piece of paper.
ROOT: power / a court's writ ends where the state's agents end, and
  states end at borders.
THREAD: shipping (a vessel arrested in a friendly port because it
  cannot be arrested at sea), money (assets moved to a place with no
  treaty), families (a custody order unenforceable across a frontier).
ASKED-AS: which country court jurisdiction enforce judgment abroad collect foreign law clause

ESSENCE: no system can try every case, so the discount for pleading
  must be big enough that most people plead. That means the penalty
  for insisting on a trial is the real pressure — and a large enough
  gap will make an innocent person with a weak case take the deal.
ROOT: premise — behaviour follows incentives, and here the incentive
  is a certainty against a gamble with a much larger loss.
THREAD: insurance (a settlement offered against the cost of fighting),
  employment (a payoff signed rather than a tribunal), games (a
  forfeit accepted because the appeal costs more).
ASKED-AS: plea bargain pleaded guilty innocent deal trial penalty pressure prosecutor discount

ESSENCE: a sentence is picked from a range, and the range is written
  by lawmakers reacting to the worst imaginable version of the
  offence. So the ordinary offender is sentenced against a monster —
  and the range only ever moves upward, because raising it is cheap
  politics and lowering it is dangerous politics.
ROOT: power / a decision with a named frightening case and a diffuse
  cost passes easily, and the cost of severity falls on people with
  no constituency.
THREAD: schools (a discipline policy written from the worst incident),
  insurance (premiums set by the catastrophic claim), aviation (rules
  written from the crash and never repealed).
ASKED-AS: sentencing too harsh guidelines maximum minimum mandatory judge discretion tariff ratchet

ESSENCE: an appeal is a second look at the law, rarely at the facts.
  The first court watched the witnesses, heard the hesitations and
  saw the room; the appeal court has a transcript. So it corrects
  reasoning and almost never re-decides who was telling the truth.
ROOT: evidence / a record preserves words and loses everything else,
  so the second reader knows less about the people and more about the
  argument.
THREAD: sport (a review that can check an offside line and not a
  referee's feel for a game), medicine (a case reviewed from notes),
  examinations (a remark of the script, not a re-sit).
ASKED-AS: appeal overturn verdict wrong facts new evidence higher court grounds retrial

ESSENCE: at some point an answer must stop being questionable, even
  if it is wrong. Without finality nothing is ever settled, nobody
  can build on a judgment, and the strongest party simply keeps
  litigating until the other runs out of money.
ROOT: law / predictability is the product, and a decision that can be
  reopened forever predicts nothing.
THREAD: accounting (books closed at year end though errors remain),
  building (a completion certificate), sport (a result that stands
  once the season has moved on).
ASKED-AS: case closed cannot reopen double jeopardy final judgment settled new evidence years

ESSENCE: a right you cannot afford to assert is a right in name.
  Costs, delay and the risk of paying the other side's bill mean that
  the party with deeper pockets can win by continuing — and both
  sides know it before anything is filed.
ROOT: money / a contest whose price is time and cash is won by
  whoever can pay longest, whatever the merits.
THREAD: medicine (a treatment that exists and is unaffordable),
  education (a right to appeal that requires a specialist), housing
  (a tenant with a good claim and no way to run it).
ASKED-AS: cannot afford lawyer legal aid costs delay big company outspend access justice

ESSENCE: claims expire. Not because the wrong stops being a wrong,
  but because evidence rots, witnesses die and memories rearrange
  themselves — so after some years a court can no longer tell a true
  claim from a false one, and trying does more harm than refusing.
ROOT: evidence / testimony decays with time, so a late trial is not a
  slow trial, it is a less reliable one.
THREAD: accounting (records destroyed after a statutory period),
  medicine (a history nobody can now reconstruct), archaeology (a
  site whose context was lost, leaving objects that prove little).
ASKED-AS: time limit statute limitations too late claim expired years after childhood abuse

ESSENCE: the rule of law is a claim about officials, not about
  citizens. It means the police, the minister and the tax office are
  themselves bound by rules they cannot change to suit a case — and a
  place where everyone obeys the government is not the rule of law,
  it is only obedience.
ROOT: power / a state's agents can always break the rule they are
  enforcing, so the doctrine is the discipline aimed at them.
THREAD: sport (a governing body sanctioning its own favourite club),
  business (a firm's complaints process judged by the firm), families
  (a parent who applies a rule to themselves).
ASKED-AS: rule of law government above the law officials bound sue the state powerful

ESSENCE: people arrange their whole lives around the law as they
  believe it to be — where they build, whom they hire, what they
  buy. Changing it with retroactive effect, or interpreting it in an
  unforeseeable way, destroys those arrangements even when the new
  rule is better than the old one.
ROOT: law / predictability is the product, and every unpredictable
  correction spends some of it.
THREAD: money (a tax rule changed after the year it applies to),
  building (a permission withdrawn after the foundations), sport (a
  rule change applied to completed seasons).
ASKED-AS: changed the rules afterwards relied on it grandfathered transition notice fair reliance

ESSENCE: rules produce injustice in odd cases, so systems grow a
  second body that can bend them for conscience. Then that body's
  decisions become precedents, its precedents become rules, and
  within a few generations there are two rulebooks needing a third
  thing to soften them.
ROOT: law / like cases must fall alike, which turns every merciful
  exception into a general rule within a generation.
THREAD: teaching (an exceptions policy that becomes the policy),
  offices (a favour granted twice becoming an entitlement), religion
  (dispensations hardening into canon).
ASKED-AS: equity fairness bend the rule exception became rule discretion consistency two systems

ESSENCE: laypeople decide the facts because facts are about ordinary
  life — whether that sounded like a threat, whether that was a
  reasonable thing to do. And because a body the state cannot select
  is a check on the state, a jury may refuse to convict, and nobody
  may ask it why.
ROOT: power / a decision-maker who cannot be appointed, promoted or
  dismissed by a party is the only one structurally free of that
  party.
THREAD: teaching (peer marking on presentation, not on chemistry),
  sport (a crowd's sense of a foul), medicine (a lay panel judging
  whether a patient was treated with respect).
ASKED-AS: jury trial twelve people decide verdict no reasons acquitted technical case ordinary

ESSENCE: some true things may not be told to a court. Not because
  they are false, but because their power to persuade exceeds their
  power to inform — a past conviction makes this charge feel likely
  when it changes nothing about whether this act happened.
ROOT: the mind / a vivid fact captures judgement out of proportion to
  what it actually implies.
THREAD: medicine (a diagnosis anchored by an earlier one), hiring (a
  gap in a CV weighed far past its meaning), sport (a player's
  reputation shaping the next decision).
ASKED-AS: inadmissible evidence prior convictions kept out jury not told prejudicial character

ESSENCE: a confession feels like the end of the argument and is the
  least reliable common evidence there is. Long questioning,
  isolation, fear and a promised way out will produce an admission
  from an innocent person — and once given, everyone stops looking.
ROOT: the mind / a person under enough pressure will choose the
  immediate relief over a distant consequence they cannot picture.
THREAD: medicine (a patient agreeing to a symptom that was suggested),
  history (confessions under torture proving whatever was wanted),
  teaching (a child admitting to whatever ends the questioning).
ASKED-AS: confessed false confession interrogation pressure recanted admitted it innocent hours questioning

ESSENCE: every right is an instruction to somebody else. A right to
  a trial is a duty on the state to fund courts; a right to be left
  alone is a duty on neighbours to keep off. So the real question is
  never whether a right sounds good but who is being obliged and
  whether they can bear it.
ROOT: law / an entitlement with no corresponding duty is a wish, not
  a right.
THREAD: medicine (a right to care requires trained people who exist),
  housing (a right to shelter requires somebody to build), schools (a
  right to education requires teachers and buildings).
ASKED-AS: right to housing healthcare who provides it duty obligation costs enforce declare

ESSENCE: the protections that make a trial fair — silence, disclosure,
  exclusion of tainted evidence — shield the guilty most often,
  because the guilty are most of the people charged. That is not a
  malfunction. There is no way to build a shield that only protects
  the innocent, since innocence is the thing in dispute.
ROOT: logic / a rule must be applied before the answer is known, so
  it cannot be conditioned on the answer.
THREAD: medicine (a screening rule that must apply before the
  diagnosis), security (a search rule applied to everyone), banking
  (fraud checks that inconvenience honest customers).
ASKED-AS: technicality guilty went free rights protect criminals unfair loophole procedure innocent

ESSENCE: every legal system keeps one door outside its own machinery
  — a pardon, a clemency, a prosecutor's decision not to charge. It
  exists because rules applied faithfully will sometimes produce a
  result nobody can defend, and no rule can be written to catch that.
ROOT: law / a general rule cannot anticipate every case, so
  somewhere a person must be able to say no.
THREAD: teaching (a head who overrides an exclusion), immigration (a
  minister's exceptional grant), employment (a manager waiving a
  policy for one person).
ASKED-AS: pardon clemency mercy prosecutor declined exception rules unjust result discretion favour

ESSENCE: only someone actually harmed may bring a case. That keeps
  courts from becoming a debating society — and it means harms spread
  thinly over millions have no plaintiff, because nobody's individual
  loss is worth the cost of suing.
ROOT: law / a court decides disputes between parties, so it needs a
  party with a real stake in the answer.
THREAD: power (a diffuse cost against a concentrated benefit),
  environment (pollution harming a region and no single victim),
  consumers (a penny overcharged on a million bills).
ASKED-AS: standing to sue not affected personally public interest case diffuse harm nobody

ESSENCE: the answer to the unsuable small harm is to bundle a
  million of them into one action. That turns a penny each into a
  real case — and hands enormous power to whoever assembles it, since
  they now speak for people who have never heard of them.
ROOT: people and power / organised numbers beat scattered ones, and
  this is that organisation performed by a law firm.
THREAD: unions (bargaining for members who did not vote for it),
  insurance (a pool acting for many policyholders), politics (a
  representative voting for people who disagree).
ASKED-AS: class action group claim opt out settlement lawyers fees millions small amounts

ESSENCE: nearly every case ends without a verdict. Both sides
  estimate what a court would probably do, discount it for cost and
  risk, and meet somewhere between. So the law most people
  experience is not what courts decide — it is the shadow that
  decisions cast over bargaining.
ROOT: law / predictability is the product, and a predictable outcome
  can be traded rather than fought for.
THREAD: money (a price found by two valuations meeting), employment
  (a settlement with a confidentiality clause), medicine (repeated
  claims settled without any of them being tried).
ASKED-AS: settled out of court no admission confidential shadow bargaining trial rare nda

ESSENCE: most of what proves a case is held by the party who did the
  wrong. So systems build a compelled exchange of documents — and it
  becomes the most expensive part of litigation, and the main weapon
  for exhausting an opponent who cannot afford to read a million
  pages.
ROOT: evidence / a claim is only as good as access to what happened,
  and access is usually unequal.
THREAD: employment (a worker who cannot see the emails about them),
  medicine (records held by the hospital being sued), regulation (an
  inspector's power to compel documents).
ASKED-AS: disclosure discovery documents hand over hiding evidence expensive burying paperwork request

ESSENCE: a lawyer's real work is translation. A client arrives with
  a life — a betrayal, a wreck, a broken business — and it must be
  turned into a form the law recognises, because the law will not
  hear a grievance that does not fit one of its shapes.
ROOT: language / a system can only process what its own vocabulary
  can express, and law's vocabulary is a fixed list of causes of
  action.
THREAD: medicine (symptoms translated into a diagnosis before
  treatment exists), forms (a complaint that must be fitted to the
  boxes), language (a feeling with no word in this tongue).
ASKED-AS: cause of action does not fit legal wrong no remedy lawyer translate claim

ESSENCE: law uses open pretences to get work done — a company treated
  as a person, a found item treated as never lost, a rule deemed to
  have been known. Everyone knows they are false. They survive
  because the alternative is rewriting a whole structure that
  otherwise works.
ROOT: language / a name is a tool, and a tool may be knowingly
  misapplied if it does the job and everyone knows it is being
  misapplied.
THREAD: mathematics (imaginary numbers, useful long before they were
  respectable), accounting (a notional charge for an internal
  transfer), games (a rule that treats a piece as being somewhere it
  is not).
ASKED-AS: legal fiction deemed treated as if pretend company person constructive notice presumed

ESSENCE: between states there is law and no sheriff. It works well
  for the routine — post, flights, shipping, trade — because
  reciprocity and reputation are enough there. It fails exactly where
  it matters most, because a state powerful enough to break it is
  powerful enough to bear the reputational cost.
ROOT: law / enforcement is what separates law from advice, and above
  states there is no body holding force.
THREAD: trade (contracts between firms in different countries),
  sport (international bodies whose sanction is exclusion), families
  (a household rule between adults with no authority over each other).
ASKED-AS: international law enforce treaty broken no world police sanctions reputation war crimes

ESSENCE: a code is written to be read by a stranger: general
  statements, numbered, arranged by subject. A body of decided cases
  is written to be read by a specialist: exact, tethered to facts,
  and impossible to summarise without losing what it said.
ROOT: writing / how knowledge is stored decides who can retrieve it,
  and these two make opposite choices about that.
THREAD: cooking (a written recipe against an apprenticeship),
  medicine (a formulary against clinical experience), craft (a
  standard against a shop's accumulated practice).
