ESSENCE: a polynomial has a polynomial square root ONLY when it
  is a perfect square — its shape
  matching a squared sum; x²+2x+1 has root x+1; x²+2x+3 has no
  polynomial root, dead, not unlikely.
ROOT: this file / the square of a sum — that shape, walked
  backward.
CANNOT: no polynomial root for an imperfect square — not hidden,
  absent; the leftover at the walk's end is the proof.
THREAD: this file (dividing polynomials — the sibling walk),
  mathematics (long division of numbers).
RULE: to take the square root of a polynomial — arrange by
  falling powers; the root of the leading term is the answer's
  first term; square what you have so far and take it away from
  the polynomial; double what you have so far, and ask what new
  term times that double (plus the new term times itself) matches
  what remains; that new term is the answer's next term; repeat
  until nothing remains — then the answer is exact — or until
  what remains cannot match — then no polynomial root exists.
