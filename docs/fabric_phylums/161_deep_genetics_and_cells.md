# 161 DEEP GENETICS AND CELLS — the instructions and their reading
File 32 holds heredity's plain laws and file 86 the cell's daily work.
This one holds the instructions themselves: how they are written, how
they are read, what happens when the reading goes wrong, and what can
and cannot be predicted from them. Every entry carries what it forbids.

ESSENCE: a gene does not correspond to a body part. It names one
  ingredient — usually a single protein — and says nothing at all
  about where that ingredient goes or what it will end up building.
  There is no gene for a hand any more than there is a line in a
  cake recipe for the left-hand corner of the cake.
ROOT: heredity / a recipe lists ingredients and steps, and the shape
  of the result is what happens when they are followed — it is
  written nowhere in the recipe.
CANNOT: no reading a body off its instructions — the only way to
  find out what a set of genes builds is to run them and look. And
  no gene for a feature: a feature is what thousands of ingredients
  did, in an order, in a particular place, over months.
THREAD: cooking (nothing in a recipe describes the finished loaf),
  computing (a short program whose output cannot be known without
  running it), craft.
ASKED-AS: gene for blue eyes height dna instructions recipe blueprint build body plan protein

ESSENCE: the instructions are written twice, on two strands that fit
  each other the way a word fits its hole. That is the whole reason
  copying is possible: pull them apart and each side carries enough
  to rebuild the other. It is also why damage to one strand is
  repairable and damage to both at the same spot usually is not.
ROOT: chemistry / four letters that pair only in fixed couples, so
  one strand specifies the other completely and neither is original.
CANNOT: no copying anything that does not carry its own template, so
  no life built on a one-sided instruction. And no repair of a break
  through both strands from the strand itself — the cell must find
  another copy or guess, and the guessing is where damage becomes
  permanent.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (a ledger kept in duplicate), craft (a
  mould and the thing cast in it), computing.
ASKED-AS: dna double helix strands copy letters pairs template broken repair damage two sides

ESSENCE: the master copy never leaves the safe. A working copy is
  made of the one stretch that is wanted, carried out, read into a
  chain of bricks, and then destroyed. Two separate steps, and the
  gap between them is where nearly all of the control lives.
ROOT: keeping knowledge / an original is protected by never being
  handled, so all the work is done from copies that are expendable.
CANNOT: no making a protein straight off the master — every product
  passes through a throwaway middle copy, which is what lets a cell
  silence a gene by shredding the copies without touching the gene.
  And no lasting harm from a bad working copy: it is discarded
  either way.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (an archive read only through
  photocopies), building (working drawings issued from a master
  set), computing.
ASKED-AS: dna rna protein made copy nucleus messenger read gene switched off working destroyed

ESSENCE: sixty-four possible triplets spell only twenty bricks, so
  most bricks have several spellings and the third letter of a
  triplet often does not matter at all. The code was built with
  slack in it, and the slack is what absorbs most copying errors
  before they can mean anything.
ROOT: engineering / a code with more symbols than meanings can spend
  the surplus on tolerating faults.
CANNOT: no code that is both maximally compact and error-tolerant —
  every spare spelling is capacity given up on purpose. And no equal
  weight to every letter: a change in one position of a triplet is
  invisible and in another is fatal, so counting changes tells you
  nothing about consequences.
THREAD: computing (spare bits spent on error correction), language
  (a spelling that survives a typo), music.
ASKED-AS: genetic code letters triplet amino acid same spelling third position typo harmless slack

ESSENCE: a changed letter usually swaps one brick for another, or
  for the same one. A letter added or removed is far worse:
  everything after it is read in the wrong grouping and the rest of
  the protein is gibberish. A whole doubled or missing stretch is
  different again. The kind of change decides the damage, not the
  size of it.
ROOT: language / meaning is carried by where the gaps fall, so a
  shift in the grouping destroys everything downstream of it.
CANNOT: no reading a change's seriousness from how many letters
  moved — one inserted letter beats a hundred swapped ones. And no
  recovery downstream of a shift: the reading frame is never
  re-found, so the wreckage runs to the end of the gene.
THREAD: language (a sentence with one letter inserted and the spaces
  kept where they were), computing (an off-by-one that corrupts
  everything after it), music (a bar line moved).
ASKED-AS: mutation dna change letter deleted inserted frameshift protein nonsense small big difference

ESSENCE: the overwhelming majority of changes do nothing whatever.
  Most of the instructions are not genes, most changes inside genes
  are silent, and most of the rest are covered by the spare copy on
  the other chromosome. Everyone walking about is carrying dozens of
  thoroughly broken genes and is perfectly well.
ROOT: chance + risk / a rare event landing on a mostly empty target
  hits nothing, most of the time.
CANNOT: no finding a disease by finding a variant — any two people
  differ in millions of places, so the background rate of harmless
  difference swamps everything. And no clean line between a variant
  and a mutation: the word records only whether anybody has yet
  shown it matters.
THREAD: chance + risk, keeping knowledge (a manuscript full of
  scribal slips that changed no reading), evidence + measurement.
ASKED-AS: mutation harmful most harmless variants everyone carries broken genes normal difference dna test

ESSENCE: the interesting part of the instructions is not the genes
  but the switches sitting in front of them. Stretches from which no
  protein is ever made decide which genes are read, in which cell,
  how strongly, at which hour. Two creatures with nearly identical
  parts lists differ mostly in their switching.
ROOT: this file / a cell holds the whole library and must choose a
  shelf, so the choosing machinery is where the real information is.
CANNOT: no explaining the difference between two creatures from
  their gene lists — a human and a mouse share nearly all of theirs.
  And no gene that is simply on: every one is on somewhere, at some
  time, and off everywhere else.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (a catalogue is more of a library than its
  books), computing (configuration doing more work than code),
  teaching.
ASKED-AS: same genes humans mice difference switches control which cells turn on off regulation

ESSENCE: a nerve, a liver cell and a white cell carry identical
  instructions, down to the letter. Nothing was thrown away and
  nothing was added. The whole difference between them is a
  difference in what is being read — and the reading pattern is
  handed on to the daughters when the cell divides.
ROOT: this file / one library, many readers, each holding a
  different shelf open and the rest shut.
CANNOT: no telling a cell's type from its sequence, so no reading a
  tissue's identity out of its instructions. And no cell that can
  escape carrying the rest: every one holds the instructions for
  every other, which is the only reason reprogramming is possible at
  all.
THREAD: teaching (one syllabus, different lessons taught from it),
  computing (one image booting into different roles), music (one
  score, different parts).
ASKED-AS: every cell same dna liver brain skin different why identical instructions read shelf

ESSENCE: a cell writes on top of its instructions without changing a
  letter — small chemical flags on the letters themselves and on the
  spools the strand is wound around, marking whole regions as open
  for reading or wound away and shut. The marks are copied at
  division, so a cell's choices outlive the cell that made them.
ROOT: this file / a choice that must persist through a division has
  to be written somewhere the copying machinery also copies.
CANNOT: no stable identity for a cell type without a mark that
  survives copying — memory in a dividing lineage has to be
  physical. And no reading the marks as a diary of a life: most are
  laid down by the cell's own development and have nothing to do
  with anything that happened to the person.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (a margin note that is photocopied with
  the page), building (a room's use fixed by what was walled up),
  teaching.
ASKED-AS: epigenetics marks methylation switched off inherited cells remember experience passed down tags

ESSENCE: a body is built by a cascade. One first difference — which
  end is which — turns on a small set of genes, whose products turn
  on the next set, which divides the block again, and again. Each
  step narrows what a region can still become, and no step can be
  taken out of order.
ROOT: this file / a switch's job is to set the conditions for the
  next switch, so the sequence carries a plan that no single cell
  holds.
CANNOT: no jumping a stage — a signal given at the wrong time does
  nothing at all, or something monstrous, because the cells that
  would have answered are not yet listening. And no repeating the
  early steps in a grown body: the switches that would have heard
  them have been shut for decades.
THREAD: cooking (steps that must go in order or the dish is
  something else), building (foundations before frame), teaching.
ASKED-AS: embryo development stages order limbs formed timing wrong signal birth defects weeks

ESSENCE: potency is how many things a cell can still become, and it
  only ever goes down. The very first cells can make an entire
  animal. A little later they can make any tissue but not a
  placenta. Later still, a marrow cell can make any blood cell and
  nothing else. Every step trades options for competence.
ROOT: the living world / doing one job superbly requires equipment
  that forecloses the others, so ability and possibility run in
  opposite directions.
CANNOT: no cell that is both fully capable and fully specialised, so
  no tissue whose repair cells are also its best working cells. And
  no unlimited supply: a tissue's reserve cells are few and slow,
  and using them uses them up.
THREAD: teaching (a general education against a trade), money (cash
  against a committed investment), craft.
ASKED-AS: stem cells embryonic adult bone marrow turn into any tissue repair limited reserve

ESSENCE: a specialised cell almost never goes back. But it is not
  locked — flood a skin cell with the handful of switch proteins the
  earliest cells use and it climbs back up the ladder and can make
  anything again. The door is one-way in practice and not in
  principle.
ROOT: this file / the state is held by marks and switches, and
  anything held by a switch can in principle be switched.
CANNOT: no spontaneous return — the marks are stable, which is
  exactly why a liver stays a liver for eighty years. And no safe
  reversal: a cell handed back full potency has also been handed
  back the ability to divide without limit, which is most of what a
  tumour is.
THREAD: materials (a metal annealed back to soft, at a cost), craft,
  computing.
ASKED-AS: skin cell turned back stem reprogramming grow organs lab one way permanent cancer

ESSENCE: a cell does not divide when it feels like it. It passes
  through gates: is there room, is there food, has the copying
  finished, is the copy undamaged, are the halves lined up. At each
  gate it either waits or is told to die. The gates are the brakes,
  and breaking them is most of what turns a cell into a tumour.
ROOT: engineering / a step that cannot be undone is preceded by a
  check, and the check has to be able to stop the line.
CANNOT: no dividing without passing every gate, so no fast growth
  that is not either checked or dangerous. And no gate that can be
  removed harmlessly on its own: each is a separate guard, which is
  why several must fail before anything runs away.
THREAD: engineering (an interlock before an irreversible step),
  computing (a build that will not ship until the tests pass), law.
ASKED-AS: cells divide when checkpoints damage stop growth control cancer brakes copying lined up

ESSENCE: a cell can die in two quite different ways, and the
  difference is felt by everything around it. The tidy exit packages
  itself and is eaten by a neighbour, and nobody notices. A burst
  cell spills its chemistry into the neighbourhood, and that spill
  is the alarm that starts inflammation. Which death happened
  decides whether there is a scar.
ROOT: engineering / a controlled shutdown and a rupture leave
  different messes, and the mess is what everything else reacts to.
CANNOT: no silent death for a cell killed by injury — the contents
  are the signal, so trauma always inflames and ordinary turnover
  never does. And no losing a great many cells quickly without
  inflammation, whatever the cause.
THREAD: cleaning (a bag tied against a bin knocked over), building
  (demolition behind hoardings against a collapse), war.
ASKED-AS: cell death apoptosis necrosis injury inflammation scar quiet burst spill neighbours notice

ESSENCE: the copying machinery cannot finish the very end of a
  strand, so every division shortens the tips. The tips are made of
  meaningless repeats for exactly that reason — a buffer built to be
  eaten. When it runs out the cell stops dividing for good, and that
  stop is a cancer brake as much as it is a clock.
ROOT: this file / a copier that must grab hold of what it copies
  always loses the last stretch, so the ends have to be expendable.
CANNOT: no unlimited division without rebuilding the tips, and no
  rebuilding them without handing a cell the endlessness a tumour
  needs — one enzyme does both jobs. And no reading a person's age
  off their tip length: the spread between individuals drowns the
  trend.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (a copy of a copy losing its margins),
  engineering (a sacrificial part that wears so the real one does
  not), money.
ASKED-AS: telomeres ends shorten aging cells divide limit cancer immortal enzyme test length

ESSENCE: damage is constant — tens of thousands of hits a day in
  every cell, from sunlight, from chemistry, from the cell's own
  burning. There is not one repair crew but several, each for a kind
  of damage, each with its own tools. Inherit a fault in one crew
  and you get a particular cancer, at a particular age, in a
  particular tissue.
ROOT: engineering / different failure modes need different repairs,
  so a single general-purpose fixer cannot exist.
CANNOT: no crew covering another's damage, so no compensating for a
  missing pathway with a working one. And no repair without a
  reference: a break through both strands has nothing to copy from,
  and the emergency mend that glues the ends together loses letters
  every single time.
THREAD: cleaning + maintenance (different tools for rust, rot and
  cracks), building, engineering.
ASKED-AS: dna damage repair sunlight smoking cancer inherited risk skin young double break

ESSENCE: when a parent's cells make eggs or sperm, the matching
  chromosomes lie alongside each other and swap sections. So the
  chromosome a child receives is neither grandparent's — it is a
  patchwork of both, stitched at a handful of joins. That is why
  siblings differ, and why they differ in patches rather than evenly.
ROOT: heredity / a shuffle needs cutting as well as dealing, or the
  same hands come round for ever.
CANNOT: no new combinations without physically breaking the
  chromosome and rejoining it, so the shuffle carries a real risk of
  joining the wrong ends. And no independence between neighbours:
  with only a few joins per chromosome, most of it travels in large
  blocks.
THREAD: card games (a cut as well as a deal), craft (patchwork cut
  from two cloths), chance + risk.
ASKED-AS: siblings different brothers sisters dna mixed grandparents chromosomes swap shuffle blocks inherited

ESSENCE: two genes sitting close together on one chromosome are
  nearly always inherited together, because a join rarely falls
  between them. How often they do separate measures how far apart
  they are — which is how the first maps of the instructions were
  drawn, decades before anyone could read a single letter.
ROOT: this file / the shuffle cuts at random, so the chance of a cut
  landing between two points is proportional to the distance between
  them.
CANNOT: no independent inheritance for neighbours, so no clean
  assortment of traits that happen to share a chromosome. And no
  telling which gene in a block is the one that matters — the whole
  block travels together, so a study finds a neighbourhood and never
  an address.
THREAD: mathematics (a distance measured by how often something
  falls between), maps, movement of goods.
ASKED-AS: genes linked together inherited same chromosome map distance study region which one matters

ESSENCE: dominant and recessive are not properties of genes. They
  are consequences of what the broken copy does. If a gene makes a
  worker molecule, one good copy usually makes enough and the broken
  one hides. If the product is a part of a structure, the broken
  piece jams the assembly, and one bad copy is enough to cause
  trouble.
ROOT: engineering / a shortfall in a catalyst is absorbed by spare
  capacity, and a bad part in an assembly is not.
CANNOT: no assigning dominance before the mechanism is known, so no
  rule that predicts it from a family tree alone. And no clean
  either-or: carriers of many recessive conditions are measurably
  different, only not ill, so dominance is partly a statement about
  how closely anybody looked.
THREAD: engineering (a redundant pump against a single bolt), money
  (a reserve absorbing a small loss), building.
ASKED-AS: dominant recessive genes carrier one copy enough parents eyes disease why hidden

ESSENCE: carrying the change, showing the condition, and how badly
  are three separate questions. Some faults show in everyone who
  carries them, some in half, some in one in twenty. And among those
  who show it, the identical fault can be a nuisance in one person
  and disabling in their brother.
ROOT: this file / a gene acts inside a whole body full of other
  genes and a whole life full of exposures, and the outcome is the
  sum of all of it.
CANNOT: no predicting an individual from a partly-penetrant fault —
  and partly is the normal case, not the exception. And no reading
  severity from a relative: the same change in the same family
  produces quite different lives.
THREAD: chance + risk, farming (identical seed in different fields),
  evidence + measurement.
ASKED-AS: gene test positive will i get it family member mild severe same fault different

ESSENCE: most of what people care about — height, blood pressure,
  how early the hair goes — is the summed effect of thousands of
  changes, each worth a fraction of a millimetre. Add thousands of
  small independent contributions and you get a hump with thin
  tails, which is why these traits are smooth and why extreme
  parents have less extreme children.
ROOT: mathematics / many small independent additions pile into a
  bell shape whatever the individual pieces look like.
CANNOT: no single gene to find for a smooth trait, so no test that
  gives an answer rather than a nudge to a probability. And no
  breeding true from extremes: two very tall parents average shorter
  children, because part of the extreme was luck and luck does not
  inherit.
THREAD: mathematics, chance + risk, farming (selection that works
  and always slides back).
ASKED-AS: height genes many small effects tall parents children average bell curve risk score

ESSENCE: heritability is a number about a crowd, never about a
  person. It says what share of the differences between people in
  this group, in this place, at this time, tracked their genes. Make
  everybody's food and schooling identical and heritability rises,
  because the only differences left to explain are inherited ones.
ROOT: evidence + measurement / a proportion of variation is a fact
  about the variation, and it changes when the variation does.
CANNOT: no reading a heritability as how much of a person is
  genetic — that question has no answer, in the way that asking
  which half of a field's yield was the seed has no answer. And no
  comparing the figure between groups: different environments
  produce different numbers from identical biology.
THREAD: evidence + measurement, farming (the same seed lot in a good
  field and a poor one), chance + risk.
ASKED-AS: heritability eighty percent genetic intelligence height how much genes environment groups compare

ESSENCE: the twin trick is simple. Identical twins share all their
  instructions, non-identical ones half, and both kinds usually
  share a home — so if the identical pairs are more alike, the extra
  likeness is credited to genes. The entire argument rests on both
  kinds being treated equally alike, and that is precisely the part
  nobody can measure.
ROOT: evidence + measurement / a natural experiment is only as good
  as what it holds constant, and this one holds it constant by
  assumption.
CANNOT: no separating genes from environment when the genes shape
  the environment — a child treated differently for being a
  striking twin, or who picks different friends because of a trait,
  breaks the split. And no simple generalising from twins: they
  share a womb, a birthday and a very unusual life.
THREAD: evidence + measurement, chance + risk, people together.
ASKED-AS: twins identical studies raised apart genes environment nature nurture assumption treated same

ESSENCE: reading a person's instructions tells you a great deal
  about what they carry and remarkably little about what will happen
  to them. For the handful of single-gene conditions the answer is
  near certain. For everything common, the test moves a lifetime
  risk by a few points — less than their weight and their address
  had already told you.
ROOT: this file / most traits are thousands of small effects meeting
  a whole life, so most of the outcome is simply not in the sequence.
CANNOT: no prediction better than the biology allows, however good
  the reading machine gets — the limit sits in the causation, not in
  the instrument. And no unknowing a result: a finding about one
  person is simultaneously a finding about their parents, siblings
  and children, who were not asked.
THREAD: chance + risk, law (information that is not only yours),
  evidence + measurement.
ASKED-AS: dna test results risk disease predict future ancestry kit accurate family finds out

ESSENCE: a person with one broken copy of a recessive gene is
  usually perfectly well and usually has no idea. Everybody carries
  several. They matter only when two carriers of the same one have a
  child — which is why these conditions cluster in small
  communities, in isolated valleys and among cousins, and not
  because anything is wrong with any of them.
ROOT: chance + risk / a rare event needing two independent draws
  gets its rate from how far apart the parents' ancestries are.
CANNOT: no recessive disease from one carrier parent, so no blame to
  hand out in a family — it takes two, and neither knew. And no
  screening that finds them all: there are thousands of these genes
  and a panel looks only for the ones somebody chose to include.
THREAD: chance + risk, people together (a small pool drawing on
  itself), farming.
ASKED-AS: carrier gene healthy parents child disease cousins small community screening test both

ESSENCE: the little power plants inside every cell carry their own
  small set of instructions, and they come only from the mother —
  the sperm's are discarded at fertilisation. So a fault in them
  passes from a woman to every one of her children, and from her
  sons to nobody. And a cell holds hundreds of the plants, so a
  person can be part faulty, which is why these diseases vary so
  wildly.
ROOT: heredity / the egg supplies the whole cell and the sperm
  supplies little more than a half-library, so anything outside the
  library travels down the female line alone.
CANNOT: no father passing these on, ever, so no family tree here
  that reads like ordinary inheritance. And no clean prediction of
  severity: how many faulty plants an egg happened to contain is a
  lottery, so siblings can differ enormously.
THREAD: heredity, engineering (a bought-in subsystem with its own
  manual), the living world.

ESSENCE: there is no single rule in nature for making a male or a
  female. Mammals use a chromosome carrying a switch. Birds use the
  opposite arrangement, with the mother deciding. Many reptiles use
  the temperature of the nest. Some fish change over in adult life.
  The trait is ancient and the mechanism is not.
ROOT: the living world / what matters is that two kinds are made in
  roughly equal numbers — how it is arranged is free to vary.
CANNOT: no reading one species' system onto another, so no argument
  from nature that settles anything about ours. And no simple switch
  even in mammals: the chromosome starts a cascade of dozens of
  genes and signals, and a break anywhere along it produces a body
  that does not match its chromosomes.
THREAD: the living world, engineering (many mechanisms reaching one
  outcome), law.
ASKED-AS: boy girl chromosomes temperature eggs turtles fish change sex determined nature switch

ESSENCE: there are only three things to do about a broken gene: add
  a working copy somewhere and let it work alongside, silence the
  bad one so it stops making trouble, or go in and correct the
  letters. The idea was never the hard part. Delivery is — getting
  the change into enough of the right cells and into nothing else.
ROOT: engineering / in most repairs the intervention is easy and the
  access is the entire problem.
CANNOT: no adding a copy to a whole body's worth of cells, so the
  approach works only where a small number of cells decides the
  outcome — an eye, or marrow taken out and put back. And no adding
  a copy against a dominant fault: the broken one is still there,
  still jamming the assembly.
THREAD: engineering (access is the cost in every repair), building,
  medicine.
ASKED-AS: gene therapy cure inherited disease add copy delivery eye blood one treatment cells

ESSENCE: the modern editing tools work by carrying a short guide
  that matches the address you want and cutting there. The trouble
  is that a short guide matches other places too, and a cut in the
  wrong place is a mutation that you made. The tool is exact about
  what it was told and not at all about what you meant.
ROOT: computing / a search string matches everything that looks like
  it, and across a genome this size near-matches are guaranteed.
CANNOT: no guide long enough to be unique and short enough to work,
  so no editing without off-target risk — only measurement of how
  much. And no recall: a cell that took the edit will not give it
  back, and every daughter carries it.
THREAD: computing (find-and-replace across an enormous document),
  craft, law.
ASKED-AS: crispr gene editing cut wrong place off target safe precise mistake permanent daughters

ESSENCE: change a gene in a person's blood or liver and the change
  dies with them. Change it in an egg, a sperm or an early embryo
  and it is in every cell of that person and in every one of their
  descendants, for ever. Same tool, same edit; they are not the same
  act.
ROOT: heredity / the body and the seed-line are separate lineages,
  and only one of them has a future beyond the person.
CANNOT: no consent from the people most affected, because they do
  not exist yet — that is the whole of the argument and no
  engineering removes it. And no trial run: an error in a germline
  edit is discovered in a person, and cannot be withdrawn from a
  population once it has bred.
THREAD: heredity, right + wrong, law.
ASKED-AS: gene editing babies embryo inherited future generations consent body line permanent change

ESSENCE: across a whole population what matters is not who carries
  what, but how common each version is. If nothing is pushing on
  them the proportions sit still, generation after generation — and
  that stillness is the ruler. Everything interesting in the field
  is measured as a departure from it.
ROOT: mathematics / a null that says nothing is happening is what
  makes it possible to detect something happening.
CANNOT: no detecting selection, mixing or inbreeding without a model
  of what no-cause looks like. And no stillness in any real
  population — the ruler holds only under conditions nothing ever
  meets, which is why it is a measuring stick and not a fact about
  the world.
THREAD: mathematics, chance + risk, evidence + measurement.
ASKED-AS: population genes frequency stays same generations selection baseline measure change detect null

ESSENCE: in a small population, chance beats fitness. A version can
  vanish or take over entirely because of who happened to have
  children, with no advantage involved at all — and the smaller the
  group the faster and more brutally it happens. Most differences
  between isolated populations are this, not adaptation.
ROOT: chance + risk / small samples swing wildly, and a generation
  is a sample.
CANNOT: no assuming a common trait is a useful one — the default
  explanation for a difference between two isolated groups is luck,
  and the burden is on anyone claiming otherwise. And no recovery of
  variation lost this way: a version gone from a small population
  comes back only from outside it.
THREAD: chance + risk (a short run of coin tosses), money (a small
  fund ruined by variance), the living world.
ASKED-AS: small population island isolated rare gene common chance luck not advantage lost variety

ESSENCE: start a population from twenty people and it carries only
  the versions those twenty happened to have. Whatever was rare in
  them is now common, and whatever they lacked is simply gone. Every
  island group, every herd rebuilt from a few survivors and every
  animal breed carries the accident of its founders for ever.
ROOT: this file / drift at its most extreme is the single moment the
  sample was taken.
CANNOT: no restoring variation from within — a bottleneck's losses
  are permanent unless outsiders are brought in, which is the whole
  of conservation breeding. And no clean founding stock: the
  founders' private faults become the population's characteristic
  disease.
THREAD: farming (a breed's inherited weakness), the living world,
  the human journey.
ASKED-AS: island founders few settlers rare disease common breed dogs bottleneck survivors variety lost

ESSENCE: some faults persist because carrying one copy is an
  advantage. The change that causes a serious blood disease in
  double dose gives partial protection against malaria in single
  dose, so in malarial country the broken version is maintained by
  the very deaths it causes. A harmful gene can be common because it
  is useful in halves.
ROOT: the living world / selection counts descendants, so a version
  can win on average while ruining a predictable minority.
CANNOT: no eliminating such a version while its pressure holds, so
  no explaining a common serious disease as one nobody has bred out
  yet. And no reading a gene's value without naming the place: the
  same copy is protective here and pure cost a thousand miles away.
THREAD: the living world, money (an insurance that pays on average
  and ruins some holders), strategy + conflict.
ASKED-AS: sickle cell malaria carrier protection common harmful gene why survives one copy africa

ESSENCE: new changes arrive mostly from fathers, and more of them
  from older ones. A woman's eggs are made once, early. A man's
  sperm are made continuously, from cells that keep dividing, and
  every division is another chance to mis-copy — so the count of
  brand-new mutations in a child climbs with the father's age and
  barely at all with the mother's.
ROOT: this file / errors come from copying, so whoever copies more
  contributes more of them.
CANNOT: no avoiding new mutations — every child carries dozens
