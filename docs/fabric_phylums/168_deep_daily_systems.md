# 168 DEEP DAILY SYSTEMS — running a life
A household is a small operation with no staff, no handover and no
day off, and it fails in the same handful of ways every time. This
file is what those failures have in common: the resource that cannot
be replaced, the decisions that are being made again and again, and
why arrangements outlast intentions in every single case.

ESSENCE: money spent can be earned again, a thing broken can be
  replaced, a friendship can be repaired. An hour is the one thing
  that leaves and does not come back — so every arrangement in a life
  is really an argument about what the hours are being turned into.
ROOT: physics / time moves one way, and no store of anything converts
  back into it.
THREAD: economy (a cost is what you gave up), farming (a planting
  window that closes), the human journey (a childhood that happens
  once).
ASKED-AS: not enough time wasted years priorities hours back save time spend

ESSENCE: hours are not interchangeable. There are two or three in a
  day when hard thinking is possible and a great many when it is not,
  and the amount of real work a person gets done depends almost
  entirely on what those few hours were spent on.
ROOT: the body / alertness runs on a daily rhythm, so capacity varies
  by several times across a single day.
THREAD: sport (training placed where the body can take it), farming (a
  task done at the hour the weather allows), cooking (a dish started
  when the kitchen is calm).
ASKED-AS: best hours morning person energy levels tired afternoon schedule demanding work timing

ESSENCE: urgent means it is making a noise now; important means it
  changes something later. Almost nothing is both, and the noisy thing
  wins by default — which is how a person can be busy every day for a
  year and arrive at the end with nothing they meant to do.
ROOT: the mind / attention goes to what is present and concrete, and
  a distant benefit is neither.
THREAD: medicine (a screening appointment postponed for a decade),
  building (maintenance always losing to today's job), farming (the
  fence that is mended after the animals are out).
ASKED-AS: urgent important busy all day nothing achieved firefighting priorities reactive matters

ESSENCE: like with like. Doing all the phone calls together, all the
  errands on one route, all the paperwork in one sitting costs far
  less than the same tasks scattered, because each kind of work has a
  setting-up cost that is paid once instead of six times.
ROOT: this file / starting a kind of work has a fixed cost, and a
  fixed cost divided across a batch is smaller per item.
THREAD: cooking (preparing everything before the pan goes on),
  workshops (one setup, many parts), shopping (one journey, eight
  errands).
ASKED-AS: batching errands together phone calls one go efficient switching tasks grouping

ESSENCE: stopping one thing to do another is not free and the cost is
  not the seconds. It is that the half-built picture in your head
  collapses and has to be rebuilt — which is why a five-minute
  interruption can cost half an hour and why a day full of small
  interruptions produces nothing at all.
ROOT: the mind / the working bench is small and holds a task's state
  only while attention is on it.
THREAD: cooking (a sauce abandoned and returned to), writing (a
  sentence lost to a doorbell), driving (a route thought about and
  then missed).
ASKED-AS: interruptions concentration lost focus multitasking switching cost distraction deep work

ESSENCE: if a thing takes two minutes, doing it now is cheaper than
  recording it, storing it, remembering it, finding it and doing it
  later. The rule is not about virtue — it is that the overhead of
  managing a small task exceeds the task.
ROOT: this file / handling has a fixed cost, so below some size the
  handling costs more than the doing.
THREAD: cleaning (a spill wiped now against a stain later), post (a
  letter opened once rather than moved four times), workshops (a tool
  put back rather than searched for).
ASKED-AS: two minute rule do it now small tasks pile up later quick jobs

ESSENCE: a head is a terrible place to keep a list. Anything unwritten
  keeps announcing itself in case it is forgotten, and the announcing
  is the cost — so the value of writing things down is not the record,
  it is the quiet.
ROOT: the mind / an unresolved intention keeps returning to attention
  until it is either done or trusted to somewhere else.
THREAD: navigation (a written course rather than a remembered one),
  cooking (a written order in a busy kitchen), farming (a diary of
  what was done to which field).
ASKED-AS: forgetting things head full write it down list mental load remember worrying

ESSENCE: a list you never reread is a place things go to be lost. What
  makes a capture system work is a fixed moment — weekly is the usual
  size — where everything is looked at again and each item is done,
  dropped, delegated or given a date.
ROOT: this file / trust in a store depends on it being checked, and an
  unchecked store cannot be trusted, so it stops relieving anything.
THREAD: accounting (a reconciliation that must actually happen),
  farming (a walk round the whole holding once a week), boats (a check
  of the whole vessel rather than the part in use).
ASKED-AS: weekly review list ignored never look again backlog clear out system trust

ESSENCE: work expands to fill the time it is given. A task with two
  weeks takes two weeks and a task with an afternoon usually takes an
  afternoon — not because people idle, but because more time permits
  more consideration, more polish and more waiting to start.
ROOT: the mind / effort is calibrated to the deadline rather than to
  the work, because the deadline is the visible constraint.
THREAD: study (an essay finished the night before whatever the notice),
  building (a job that fits its programme), cooking (a meal ready when
  the guests arrive and not before).
ASKED-AS: deadline last minute procrastination time given expands finish early pressure motivation

ESSENCE: a plan that assumes nothing goes wrong is not optimism, it is
  a specific arithmetic error. Every estimate is made by imagining the
  work going smoothly, because that is the only version anyone can
  picture — and the record of every similar past task says otherwise.
ROOT: the mind / a forecast is built by simulating the case, and
  imagination produces the clean run.
THREAD: building (a project that overruns by the usual proportion),
  cooking (a dinner that takes an hour longer than planned), travel (a
  journey timed without the parking).
ASKED-AS: took longer than expected estimate optimistic planning underestimate always overrun realistic

ESSENCE: a day packed edge to edge has no capacity to absorb anything
  going wrong, so one late thing pushes every later thing and the
  whole day fails. Slack is not waste; it is the thing that converts a
  problem into an inconvenience.
ROOT: mathematics / variation accumulates in a chain, and without gaps
  each delay is passed on in full to everything downstream.
THREAD: transport (a timetable with recovery time built in),
  engineering (a tolerance that lets parts fit), farming (a harvest
  window wider than the harvest).
ASKED-AS: back to back schedule running late whole day ruined buffer gaps overbooked

ESSENCE: overcommitting is not a character flaw, it is what happens by
  default. Each agreement is made separately, in a moment when it is a
  small favour to a person in front of you, and the collision happens
  later between things that were never compared to each other.
ROOT: the mind / a decision is judged against the moment it is made
  in, and future weeks feel emptier than present ones.
THREAD: money (spending decided purchase by purchase), farming (more
  planted than can be harvested), households (a calendar agreed one
  invitation at a time).
ASKED-AS: overcommitted too much on said yes calendar full future looks empty agreeing

ESSENCE: saying yes to something is spending, and the budget is
  already fully allocated. Every acceptance is a refusal of whatever
  would have occupied those hours — usually something with nobody
  present to argue for it, which is why it always loses.
ROOT: economy / every choice is a refusal, and here the refused thing
  is absent from the room where the choosing happens.
THREAD: money (a purchase that quietly cancels another), sport (a
  training week that cannot hold two priorities), farming (an acre
  under one crop).
ASKED-AS: saying no requests favours boundaries protect time yes means declining commitments

ESSENCE: handing a task over means handing over the standard, the
  deadline and the reasons — and most of that was never in words. So
  the first few times cost more than doing it yourself, and the whole
  case for delegating rests on the task repeating.
ROOT: language / an instruction is a compressed copy of an intention,
  so the missing part has to be supplied by watching or by correcting.
THREAD: cooking (teaching somebody a dish rather than cooking it),
  farming (showing a hand a job that will be done fifty times),
  teaching (an explanation that costs an hour and saves a year).
ASKED-AS: delegate faster to do myself teaching cost standard explain repeated tasks handover

ESSENCE: a checklist is for what you do rarely and cannot afford to
  get wrong — the trip abroad, the annual return, the boiler service,
  the house move. Its value is not the reminding, it is that the
  thinking was done once, calmly, by a person who had time.
ROOT: the mind / recall is unreliable and reconstructed, while a
  written list is complete on its worst day as on its best.
THREAD: aviation (a list read aloud before every flight), medicine (a
  count before closing), sailing (a departure check run in the same
  order every time).
ASKED-AS: checklist forgot something trip packing annual tasks rare procedure written once

ESSENCE: for what you do every day, the answer is not a list but a
  groove — the same order, the same place, the same time — because the
  purpose is to stop deciding. A routine is a decision made once and
  then collected repeatedly for nothing.
ROOT: the mind / a repeated sequence in a fixed context stops
  requiring deliberation, and deliberation is the expensive part.
THREAD: cooking (a kitchen where everything has one place), sport (a
  warm-up done identically), farming (a milking order that never
  changes).
ASKED-AS: routine habit same time every day deciding again automatic morning system consistency

ESSENCE: a house has a maintenance schedule whether or not anyone
  writes one. Filters, gutters, seals, batteries, servicing — each has
  an interval, and the interval is being kept by somebody or it is
  being ignored, and ignoring it is a decision to pay later at a much
  worse rate.
ROOT: physics / things wear continuously, so the only choice is
  whether the attention is scheduled or forced.
THREAD: aviation (a life measured in hours between inspections),
  farming (machinery serviced before the season, not during it), the
  body (a check-up on a date rather than on a symptom).
ASKED-AS: house maintenance calendar gutters filters servicing forgot annual tasks reminders upkeep

ESSENCE: some things should be replaced on a schedule and some should
  be run until they break, and the deciding question is what happens
  when they fail. If the failure is cheap, visible and convenient,
  wait. If it takes the rest with it, or fails at three in the
  morning, replace it early.
ROOT: chance / the cost of a failure is its likelihood multiplied by
  its consequence, and only the consequence distinguishes these two
  cases.
THREAD: engineering (a bearing changed on hours against a bulb changed
  when it goes), the body (a filling replaced before it cracks a
  tooth), motoring (a cambelt against a wiper blade).
ASKED-AS: replace before it breaks run until fails preventive maintenance cost consequence water damage

ESSENCE: one small failure that takes others with it is the whole
  reason for preventive attention. Water arriving where it should not
  ruins the floor, the ceiling below, the wiring and a month — and the
  original fault was a seal costing almost nothing.
ROOT: engineering / a failure that propagates has a cost set by what
  it reaches rather than by what it was.
THREAD: farming (a gate left open), sailing (one fitting that lets
  water in), computing (one failure that corrupts everything it
  touched).
ASKED-AS: small leak big damage cascading failure cheap part expensive consequences water ceiling

ESSENCE: a spare is only a spare if it exists before the failure. The
  bulb, the fuse, the key, the charger, the fitting — each is trivial
  to buy on an ordinary day and impossible to obtain at the moment it
  is needed, which is invariably a Sunday night.
ROOT: this file / a failure removes the capacity to respond to it, so
  the response has to be arranged in advance.
THREAD: sailing (a repair kit carried because there is no shop),
  medicine (a rescue dose kept at home), farming (a part held for the
  machine that will stop mid-harvest).
ASKED-AS: spare parts bulbs fuses keys none when needed sunday shops closed stock

ESSENCE: documents are lost in the same four places every time: a pile
  that was going to be sorted, a safe place nobody can remember, an
  email account, and somebody else's head. Findability is not tidiness
  — it is one known place per kind of thing, chosen once.
ROOT: the mind / recall of location is unreliable, so retrieval has to
  be answered by a rule rather than by remembering.
THREAD: libraries (a scheme that makes retrieval possible), workshops
  (a shadow board where a missing tool is visible), farming (records
  kept in one book rather than four).
ASKED-AS: cannot find document passport certificate filed somewhere safe place papers organised

ESSENCE: the household's information usually lives in one person's
  head — which accounts, which supplier, when the insurance renews,
  where the deeds are. That person is a single point of failure, and
  the failure arrives at the worst possible moment, when they are ill
  or gone.
ROOT: people together / knowledge that was never written cannot be
  transferred by an emergency.
THREAD: business (a firm that stops when the owner is ill), farming (a
  holding whose knowledge is one person's), medicine (a patient
  history nobody else can supply).
ASKED-AS: only i know accounts passwords bills husband wife ill emergency information household

ESSENCE: a backup that has never been restored is a belief, not a
  backup. The only evidence that a copy works is having retrieved
  something from it — and the commonest discovery, made at the worst
  time, is that the copying stopped silently months ago.
ROOT: evidence / an untested claim is a hope, and a mechanism that
  fails quietly gives no sign of having failed.
THREAD: sailing (a bilge pump nobody has switched on), medicine (an
  alarm never tested), building (a fire door that has never been
  opened).
ASKED-AS: backup photos never restored failed silently cloud copy lost files test

ESSENCE: paying for things automatically is not laziness, it is the
  removal of a recurring decision that will eventually be missed. A
  system that depends on remembering every month will fail in the
  month you are ill, travelling or distracted — and the penalty is
  charged then.
ROOT: this file / a decision repeated is a decision that will
  eventually be skipped, so the reliable version does not require one.
THREAD: farming (an irrigation timer against remembering), medicine (a
  weekly pill organiser), engineering (a process that fails safe
  rather than requiring a person).
ASKED-AS: direct debit automatic payments forgot bill late fee standing order remembering

ESSENCE: money behaves better in separate pots than in one. Bills from
  one account, spending from another, savings somewhere awkward to
  reach — the separation does the work that self-control was being
  asked to do, and self-control has a bad record.
ROOT: the mind / a single balance is judged as one number, and a
  number that contains commitments looks like money that is available.
THREAD: farming (seed kept apart from grain for eating), cooking
  (ingredients set aside before the dish begins), business (money held
  for tax that was never the firm's to spend).
ASKED-AS: separate accounts bills spending savings budgeting pots overspend balance looks available

ESSENCE: the emergency fund is not a round number, it is a number of
  months. What it must cover is the actual outgoings of a household
  during a period without income — and the two things that make that
  period long are also the ones that make it likely: illness and
  losing work.
ROOT: chance / a buffer is sized against the duration of the
  interruption, not against the size of any single shock.
THREAD: farming (grain held against a bad season rather than a bad
  day), sailing (fuel reserve measured against distance to shelter),
  business (a firm's runway counted in months).
ASKED-AS: emergency fund how much months expenses savings job loss buffer three six

ESSENCE: insure what would ruin you and pay for what would merely
  annoy you. A small loss you can absorb is cheaper carried than
  covered, because the cover has to pay for the administration and the
  profit as well as the loss.
ROOT: chance / a rare huge loss and a frequent small one can share an
  average and share nothing else, and only the first is unsurvivable.
THREAD: sailing (a lifejacket against a spare rope), farming (a barn
  insured and a fence not), medicine (a serious risk covered and a
  cold not).
ASKED-AS: insurance worth it extended warranty small claims deductible excess ruin cover afford

ESSENCE: paying a small charge repeatedly is invisible, and that is
  the mechanism. Each subscription was justified once, none is large
  enough to notice, and together they can be a significant part of a
  household's spending — going out for things nobody has used in a
  year.
ROOT: the mind / a recurring cost is felt at the moment of joining and
  never again, so it is never re-decided.
THREAD: business (a firm with software nobody remembers buying),
  farming (a standing charge on a meter for something disconnected),
  households (a gym membership as a monthly payment for an intention).
ASKED-AS: subscriptions adding up cancel unused monthly charges forgot signed up review

ESSENCE: the price you are paying quietly rises for staying. Renewals
  drift upward, the introductory rate ends, and the loyal customer is
  charged more than the newcomer — so doing nothing is a decision that
  costs money every year in several places at once.
ROOT: this file / a default decides for anybody without a reason to
  act, and the pricing was designed around that.
THREAD: money (a rate that changes without the borrower doing
  anything), farming (a contract that rolls over on worse terms),
  utilities (a tariff that ends without ceremony).
ASKED-AS: renewal price went up loyalty penalty switch provider insurance quote automatic increase

ESSENCE: whether to repair or replace is not about the price of the
  repair against the price of the new one. It is the repair's price
  against the new one's price divided across the years each will then
  last — and a cheap repair on a thing near its end is money spent to
  postpone the same decision.
ROOT: mathematics / a cost is compared over the period it buys, so two
  prices are only comparable per year of service.
THREAD: motoring (a repair bill against a car's remaining years),
  building (a patched roof against a new one), clothing (a resoling
  that is worth it once and not twice).
ASKED-AS: repair or replace worth fixing cost new one lifespan years old appliance

ESSENCE: the useful number for a purchase is what it costs each time
  it is used. A tool bought once and used weekly for a decade is
  almost free; the same money spent on something used twice is
  expensive, however good the thing was and however low the price.
ROOT: mathematics / a fixed cost divided by uses falls with the count,
  so frequency decides the real price and not the ticket.
THREAD: craft (a good tool that outlives its owner), building (a floor
  walked on every day), textiles (a garment worn weekly against one
  worn once).
ASKED-AS: cost per use expensive worth it used twice daily cheap version quality

ESSENCE: the cheap version of something you need often gets bought
  twice, and the second purchase is made after the inconvenience of
  the failure. There is a category where paying more is straightforward
  arithmetic — and a category where it is vanity, and the difference is
  how often the thing is touched.
ROOT: this file / cost is divided across use, so the frequently used
  thing repays quality and the rarely used one does not.
THREAD: farming (boots and a rarely used implement priced by opposite
  logic), cooking (a knife against a specialist mould), building (a
  hand tool against a hired machine).
ASKED-AS: buy cheap twice quality worth paying more daily use rarely occasional tool

ESSENCE: for the thing used once a year, borrowing or hiring is
  usually right, and the reason is not only the money. Ownership costs
  storage, maintenance and the decision of where to keep it, and those
  costs are paid every day whether or not the thing is used.
ROOT: this file / possession carries a continuous cost while use is
  occasional, and the two do not match.
THREAD: farming (contractors for machinery used two weeks a year),
  building (a hired scaffold against an owned ladder), sport
  (equipment for a sport tried once).
ASKED-AS: buy or hire tool used once storage borrow rent occasional own equipment

ESSENCE: everything that arrives has to land somewhere, and if there
  is no place for arrivals, the place becomes every horizontal
  surface. A household needs a spot where incoming things sit while
  they are dealt with, and a habit of emptying it — the pile is not a
  failure of tidiness, it is a missing station.
ROOT: this file / a system needs somewhere to put work in progress, or
  the work in progress occupies the workspace.
THREAD: kitchens (a place for dishes waiting to be washed), workshops
  (a bench that must be cleared before the next job), post (a tray
  emptied on a schedule).
ASKED-AS: pile of stuff kitchen table clutter landing place mail incoming clear surface

ESSENCE: a thing is only put away if putting it away is easier than
  putting it down. Storage that requires opening two doors, moving a
  box and finding a gap will not be used by anybody, and the object
  will live on the side — so the placement has to match the frequency.
ROOT: this file / effort decides behaviour more reliably than
  intention, so the storage's difficulty is what determines whether it
  is used.
THREAD: kitchens (utensils at the point of use), workshops (a tool
  hung where the hand reaches for it), farming (gates and equipment
  placed where the work is).
ASKED-AS: put away storage inconvenient cupboard boxes loft never used labelled hidden

ESSENCE: the constraint in clearing out is not space and it is not
  time. It is decisions — each object asks a question, the questions
  are tiring, and a person runs out of deciding long before they run
  out of afternoon. That is why the job stalls with the room half
  done.
ROOT: the mind / deliberate choices draw on a limited capacity, and
  the difficulty of each one is unrelated to the size of the object.
THREAD: writing (an edit that runs out of judgement), shopping (a
  choice that gets harder as the trip goes on), medicine (a clinic
  where the last decisions of the day are worse than the first).
ASKED-AS: decluttering stalled halfway exhausting decisions keep throw boxes moved tiring

ESSENCE: what you paid for a thing is gone and has nothing to say
  about whether to keep it. Yet an expensive mistake is kept for years
  precisely because it was expensive — the money is not being
  recovered, it is being paid again in space and in the small
  reproach of seeing it.
ROOT: economy / a cost already spent cannot be recovered by any future
  choice, so it is not a factor in any future choice.
THREAD: money (holding a bad investment to avoid taking the loss),
  farming (a machine kept because of what it cost), relationships (a
  course continued because of the years already in it).
ASKED-AS: cost so much cannot throw away expensive mistake guilt keeping unused wasted

ESSENCE: things that cannot be discarded because of who gave them are
  a different problem and need a different answer. The object is
  standing in for a relationship or a person, and getting rid of it
  feels like getting rid of them — which is why the usual reasoning
  does not touch it.
ROOT: meaning / an object can carry a meaning that has nothing to do
  with its use, and the meaning is the thing being kept.
THREAD: religion (a relic whose value is not material), families (a
  house kept for what happened in it), music (an instrument nobody
  plays and nobody will sell).
ASKED-AS: sentimental items cannot get rid gift inherited guilt memories keeping boxes

ESSENCE: one in, one out. Storage is fixed and acquisition is not, so
  without a rule the two diverge until the house is full — and then
  the household starts paying for space it does not have, in a loft,
  a garage or a unit rented for objects nobody will ever look at.
ROOT: physics / a container has a fixed volume, and an unbounded
  inflow into a bounded space overflows by arithmetic.
THREAD: farming (a shed that fills whatever its size), computing (a
  disk that fills to whatever capacity it has), roads (a lane that
  fills as soon as it opens).
ASKED-AS: house full storage unit garage more shelves fills up one in out

ESSENCE: a household runs on somebody noticing — that the milk is low,
  the form is due, the child needs new shoes, the appointment must be
  made. That noticing is the real work, it is invisible, it is rarely
  counted, and it does not transfer when a task is handed over.
ROOT: this file / the expensive part of a task is deciding it needs
  doing, and only the doing is visible to anybody else.
THREAD: business (a manager whose work is knowing what needs
  attention), medicine (a nurse noticing the change before the
  monitor), farming (a stockman who sees which animal is off).
ASKED-AS: mental load noticing household chores just ask me remembering everything invisible work

ESSENCE: two people cannot coordinate from two private calendars. A
  household needs one place where commitments live, visible to
  everybody who might make one — otherwise every arrangement is made
  from an incomplete picture and the collisions are discovered on the
  morning.
ROOT: people together / coordination requires shared information, and
  what is not shared cannot be coordinated around.
THREAD: business (a system of record that must be one place),
  navigation (a single agreed chart), sport (a fixture list everyone
  reads).
ASKED-AS: shared calendar double booked family clash both agreed forgot tell each other

ESSENCE: booking early is not organisation for its own sake — many
  things are rationed by queue rather than by price. The appointment,
  the school place, the trades person, the good slot: joining a list
  costs nothing today and cannot be bought later at any price.
ROOT: economy / where the price is not allowed to clear a shortage,
  the shortage is paid in waiting, and the waiting starts when you
  join.
THREAD: medicine (a waiting list joined at the referral), education (a
  place applied for a year ahead), travel (a booking that is cheaper
  and available only early).
ASKED-AS: book early waiting list appointment months ahead fully booked queue slots

ESSENCE: the standard slips one notch at a time and never announces
  itself. A thing left undone makes the next thing easier to leave, a
  small mess makes a larger one unremarkable — so the maintenance of a
  standard is done in the small cases, where it feels like it does not
  matter.
ROOT: people together / a rule is learned from what is tolerated, and
  a household teaches itself the same way a group does.
THREAD: cities (a broken window that changes what a street tolerates),
  sport (a training standard that slips in the unwatched sessions),
  farming (a fence line that goes gradually).
ASKED-AS: standards slipped gradually let things go mess normal small things matter

ESSENCE: a system is only real if it works in a bad week. Anything
  that requires you to be rested, motivated and undisturbed is not a
  system, it is a description of a good day — and the weeks when it
  fails are the weeks that decide how a life actually goes.
ROOT: engineering / a design is judged by its behaviour under load,
  and the load here is illness, travel and disorder.
THREAD: engineering (a structure designed for the storm), medicine (a
  regime a patient can keep on a bad day), farming (a routine that
  must hold through a wet fortnight).
ASKED-AS: system fell apart busy week ill stopped everything minimum version resilient bad

ESSENCE: the run of days is not the point, and treating it as one
  makes the first miss fatal. What matters is the rate over months, so
  the useful rule is not never miss but never miss twice — one gap is
  weather, two is a new pattern.
ROOT: the mind / an identity built on an unbroken record is destroyed
  by a single break, and destroyed identities do not restart.
THREAD: sport (training measured monthly rather than daily), money
  (saving judged by the year), teaching (a pupil's progress across a
  term).
ASKED-AS: broke my streak missed day gave up starting again consistency rate perfect

ESSENCE: a resolution is a plan to be a different person under
  identical conditions, and it competes against everything that made
  the old behaviour easy. Changing the conditions works because it
  does not require anybody to be different — it requires the biscuits
  to be somewhere else.
ROOT: this file / effort decides behaviour, so moving the effort is
  more reliable than increasing the resolve.
THREAD: farming (a fence that works better than watching), design (a
  default that decides for most people), households (a fruit bowl
  where the biscuit tin was).
ASKED-AS: new year resolution willpower failed change environment habits motivation arrangement discipline

ESSENCE: shopping is done inside a machine built to change your mind,
  so the decisions must be made outside it. A list written at home is
  not a memory aid, it is a commitment made by a calmer person, in a
  place with no smells, no music and no display at the end of the
  aisle.
ROOT: the mind / a choice is shaped by the situation it is made in, so
  the situation is chosen before the choice.
THREAD: money (a budget set before the purchase), medicine (a decision
  made before the symptom), sailing (a plan made ashore rather than in
  weather).
ASKED-AS: shopping list impulse buying extra items supermarket layout spend more planned

ESSENCE: deciding what to eat is a decision made three times a day
  forever, at the hour when deciding is hardest. A repeating plan is
  not a restriction — it removes the daily deliberation and, with it,
  most of the waste, most of the late shopping and most of the
  ordering in.
ROOT: the mind / a decision made repeatedly at low capacity is made
  badly, so removing the decision is worth more than improving it.
THREAD: farming (a feeding routine that does not depend on the day),
  hospitals (menus decided in advance for the same reason), sport (a
  fuelling plan set before an event).
ASKED-AS: meal planning what to eat decide every night takeaway waste food shopping

ESSENCE: errands have a shape, and the shape is geography and opening
  hours rather than the order they occurred to you. Two trips into
  town where one would do is not carelessness, it is the absence of
  the ten minutes that would have combined them.
ROOT: this file / the fixed cost of a journey is paid per journey, so
  the count of journeys is what determines the cost.
THREAD: logistics (a delivery round planned as one route), farming
  (one trip to town for everything), cooking (one heating of the oven
  for several things).
ASKED-AS: errands trips town route planning back again forgot combine journeys efficient

ESSENCE: undone small tasks do not stay small. Each one adds a
  reminder to carry, a decision to remake, and often a consequence —
  the unwashed pan makes the next meal harder, the unopened letter
  becomes a deadline, the unanswered message becomes an apology.
