# 173 WHEN WORDS MISLEAD — how matching by letters goes wrong
Letters are a cheap way to find candidates and a terrible way to
settle anything. Every entry here is one way a run of letters looks
like a meaning and is not, with the wall stated plainly enough to act
on and the remedy named. The rule under all of them is the same: one
shared word is a coincidence, and only agreement makes a match.

ESSENCE: the same letters live in unrelated areas — a bank of a river
  and a bank holding money, a crust of bread and the crust of the
  planet. The spelling is identical and the subjects never touch.
  The remedy is to look for a second word from the same area before
  accepting the first.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / two senses of one word can be
  strangers wearing the same coat.
CANNOT: no shared meaning from a shared spelling alone. A single
  word in common is the weakest evidence there is, and it is the
  evidence most often mistaken for a finding.
THREAD: computing (19: one filename in two folders opens two files),
  law (17: contracts define their terms to stop exactly this).
ASKED-AS: same word different areas bank crust wrong match unrelated spelling coincidence

ESSENCE: a word that appears nearly everywhere marks nothing. Words
  like system, thing, way, work, part and use turn up in every
  subject there is, so matching on one of them finds the whole
  library. The remedy is to weigh a word by how rare it is and to
  let the common ones cast no vote at all.
ROOT: language (09) / word frequency falls away steeply, so the
  commonest words carry almost no information.
CANNOT: no meaning from a word that appears in more than a tenth of
  everything. Such a word marks nothing, and counting it as a hit
  buries the words that do mark something.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (40: an index built on common words is
  noise), measurement (36: an instrument that always reads the same
  is not an instrument).
ASKED-AS: common word matches everything system thing way useless frequent no signal

ESSENCE: the rare word is the reliable one. A question that mentions
  soldering, or lambing, or a crankshaft, has told you its subject
  in one word, because nobody reaches for those words by accident.
  The remedy is to build the reading around the rarest word present
  and treat the rest as support.
ROOT: language (09) / an unusual word was chosen deliberately, and
  it was chosen for the thing being talked about.
CANNOT: no confident reading without a rare word in the overlap. If
  everything shared between question and knowledge is common, then
  nothing has actually been matched.
THREAD: computing (19: rarity is what makes a search term work),
  evidence (36: the discriminating test is the one worth running).
ASKED-AS: rare word signals subject unusual technical term reliable which word matters

ESSENCE: a word inside a fixed phrase is not doing its own work. In
  "by and large", "kick the bucket", "on the other hand" and "in the
  long run", the parts carry nothing. The remedy is to hold a list
  of such phrases and remove the whole phrase before reading what is
  left.
ROOT: language (09) / a set phrase is a single unit of meaning that
  happens to be spelled with spaces in it.
CANNOT: no subject in a word inside a fixed phrase. The phrase is
  one word wearing several, and its parts must not be read
  separately.
THREAD: words and meaning (66: dead metaphors forgot they moved),
  music (10: a lick is played as one gesture, not as notes).
ASKED-AS: fixed phrase parts carry nothing hand run bucket idiom whole unit

ESSENCE: an idiom means something its words do not. "Under the
  weather" is about illness and not about weather; "in hot water" is
  about trouble and not about heat. The remedy is to treat a known
  idiom as a single word with its own meaning and never to match its
  parts.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / meaning is agreement, and a community
  can agree on a phrase whose parts point elsewhere.
CANNOT: no literal reading in an idiom. Matching "weather" in "under
  the weather" lands in a forecast when the subject is a person who
  feels ill.
THREAD: humor (41: puns work by cashing the ignored sense), teaching
  (30: idioms are learned whole or not at all).
ASKED-AS: idiom under the weather hot water not literal meaning phrase whole

ESSENCE: a figure of speech taken literally produces confident
  nonsense. "Drowning in paperwork" has no water and "the market is
  hungry" has no stomach. The mark is a picture that cannot
  physically happen; the remedy is to read the impossible picture as
  a pointer to a subject somewhere else.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / metaphor moves a word's home and
  leaves the old sense behind, still spelled the same.
CANNOT: no literal subject in an impossible picture. Where the words
  describe something that could not happen, the real subject is not
  the one the words name.
THREAD: story (27: an image carries what a statement flattens),
  money (16: liquid, crash and flow are all borrowed water).
ASKED-AS: figure of speech literal drowning paperwork hungry market impossible picture metaphor

ESSENCE: a negated word looks exactly like an asserted one. "This
  does not use yeast" and "this uses yeast" share every content
  word, and a letter-counter reads them the same. The remedy is to
  find the negation words first and mark everything in their reach
  as denied rather than claimed.
ROOT: logic (11) / a negation reverses the claim while leaving its
  words untouched.
CANNOT: no claim from a word standing under a negation. Matching
  such a word reports the opposite of what was said, which is worse
  than reporting nothing.
THREAD: law (17: an exception is read with the clause it limits),
  medicine (06: a negative finding is a finding, not an absence).
ASKED-AS: negation not never without opposite meaning matched wrongly denied claim reversed

ESSENCE: a word in an example is not a word in a claim. "Unlike
  bread, this needs no proving" is not about bread, and a paragraph
  that mentions cars while explaining friction is not about cars.
  The remedy is to treat words after unlike, such as, for instance
  and say as illustration, not subject.
ROOT: teaching (30) / an example is a borrowed case used to point at
  a general thing.
CANNOT: no claim from a word inside an example. The example was
  chosen because it was familiar, not because it is the subject.
THREAD: mathematics (11: a worked case is not the theorem), story
  (27: an illustration is a servant of the point).
ASKED-AS: word in example not claim unlike such as instance illustration subject confusion

ESSENCE: the plural and the past hide a match. Bakes, baking, baked
  and baker are one word to a person and four to a letter-counter,
  so a real match is missed for the sake of a suffix. The remedy is
  to cut common endings before comparing and to accept the match
  when only the ending differed.
ROOT: linguistics (144) / word parts carry grammar, and the stem
  carries the subject.
CANNOT: no match refused for an ending alone. Two words that differ
  only in a plural, a tense or an agent suffix are one word for the
  purpose of finding a subject.
THREAD: computing (19: stemming exists because of this), language
  (09: inflection is grammar wearing letters).
ASKED-AS: plural past tense endings missed match baking baked stem suffix same

ESSENCE: two words that mean the same are missed entirely by
  letters. A question about a fridge and knowledge written about a
  refrigerator share nothing at all in spelling, and the match fails
  in silence. The remedy is a written list of near-words, so that
  meeting one is meeting all.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / one meaning has many handles, and
  which handle a speaker grips is an accident of habit.
CANNOT: no absence of a subject from the absence of one spelling. A
  failed match on letters proves nothing about meaning, and silence
  here is the most expensive failure of all.
THREAD: teaching (30: a learner who knows the other word looks
  ignorant), keeping knowledge (40: a catalogue needs see-also).
ASKED-AS: synonyms missed fridge refrigerator different spelling same meaning silent failure

ESSENCE: a technical sense and an everyday sense collide constantly.
  Work, force, power, current, charge, field, order and volume are
  all ordinary words and all precise ones, and the precise sense
  arrives with company. The remedy is to require a second technical
  word before reading a technical sense.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / a field mints few words and borrows
  many, because borrowing is cheaper than minting.
CANNOT: no technical reading from a technical word standing alone.
  Work in physics and work in employment are different quantities,
  and one word cannot tell you which.
THREAD: physics (01: work is force times distance and nothing else),
  law (17: consideration is a term of art, not thoughtfulness).
ASKED-AS: technical everyday collide work force power current charge field precise sense

ESSENCE: a word used as a name is not being used for its meaning. A
  person called Baker bakes nothing, a boat called Endeavour tries
  nothing, and a shop called The Old Forge forges nothing. The
  remedy is to spot the capital letter and the naming frame and let
  the word carry no ordinary sense.
ROOT: language (09) / naming detaches a word from its meaning and
  attaches it to a thing.
CANNOT: no ordinary meaning in a word used as a name. A name points
  at one thing and describes nothing, whatever its letters once
  meant.
THREAD: law (17: a trade name is protected as a mark), history (42:
  place names are fossils of forgotten senses).
ASKED-AS: word used as name baker endeavour proper noun capital letter meaningless

ESSENCE: a question's framing is not its subject. In "I read online
  that sourdough is hard, is that true", the subject is sourdough,
  not reading and not online. The remedy is to strip the frame — I
  heard, I read, someone told me, is it true that — and keep what it
  wraps.
ROOT: language (09) / a report of a claim is a container, and the
  claim is the content.
CANNOT: no subject in the framing of a question. The words that
  introduce a claim describe how it arrived, never what it is about.
THREAD: media (19: the source is not the story), evidence (36:
  provenance and content are judged separately).
ASKED-AS: framing not subject i read online heard someone said strip wrapper

ESSENCE: matching on the asker's own words finds the asking, not the
  subject. Why, how, can, should, please, quick question, any ideas
  — these appear in every question ever asked and point at none of
  them. The remedy is to separate the asking words from the content
  words before anything else happens.
ROOT: language (09) / a sentence carries its purpose and its topic
  on different words.
CANNOT: no subject in the words that do the asking. They say what
  kind of answer is wanted and nothing whatever about what it is
  wanted about.
THREAD: computing (19: a request has a method and a target, read
  apart), teaching (30: answer the question actually asked).
ASKED-AS: asking words not subject why how can should please separate content

ESSENCE: a long question drowns its own subject. Three paragraphs of
  background contain dozens of matchable words, and the one real
  question sits in a single line near the end. The remedy is to find
  the sentence that carries the asking and to weight it far above
  everything around it.
ROOT: language (09) / people supply context generously and state the
  ask once.
CANNOT: no subject from a count of matches in a long question. The
  most-mentioned thing is usually the background, and the least
  mentioned is usually the ask.
THREAD: medicine (06: the presenting complaint is buried in the
  story), law (17: the prayer for relief sits at the end).
ASKED-AS: long question background drowns subject many words one line ask

ESSENCE: a short question carries almost nothing. "Does it work",
  "why though", "the other one?" have no content words at all, and
  there is nothing in them to match. The remedy is to reach for the
  turn before, and if there is none, to say plainly that the subject
  is missing.
ROOT: language (09) / conversation drops whatever both sides already
  hold.
CANNOT: no subject in a question with no content word. Nothing can
  be recovered from it by reading it harder, and guessing produces
  an answer to an invented question.
THREAD: computing (19: a dangling reference is the same fault),
  people together (21: asking what was meant is not a failure).
ASKED-AS: short question nothing to match does it work why though empty

ESSENCE: one word in common is chance and two agreeing words are a
  signal. Any two long texts share some vocabulary; what matters is
  whether the shared words come from the same area of life. The
  remedy is to require agreement beyond the matched word itself.
ROOT: chance (35) / coincidences are certain in large collections,
  so a single hit means nothing on its own.
CANNOT: no match from one shared word without a second word from the
  same area. Two texts sharing exactly one word share a coincidence.
THREAD: evidence (36: one observation is an anecdote), measurement
  (36: a single reading has no error bar).
ASKED-AS: one shared word coincidence two agreeing signal company confirm second match

ESSENCE: a borrowed word read in its birthplace lands in the wrong
  field entirely. Bugs, viruses, streams, memory, cloud and windows
  all began somewhere else, and a question about a computer virus
  reaches medical knowledge every time. The remedy is to check which
  company the word is standing in, not where it was born.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / metaphor grows vocabulary without
  minting new sounds, and the old home keeps its claim on the word.
CANNOT: no reading of a borrowed word in the field it came from. A
  virus with a file beside it is software and a virus with a fever
  beside it is not.
THREAD: computing (19: the whole vocabulary is an office and a
  hospital), living world (31: a real virus is not a metaphor).
ASKED-AS: borrowed word wrong field virus bug memory cloud computer medical mixed

ESSENCE: words inside a quotation belong to whoever said them. "He
  told me the bread was terrible" is not a claim that the bread was
  terrible, and matching on the quoted words attributes them to the
  wrong speaker. The remedy is to keep the quotation attached to its
  source and to read the frame for the actual claim.
ROOT: language (09) / reported speech reports that something was
  said, not that it is so.
CANNOT: no claim from a word inside a quotation. The sentence
  asserts that it was said, and asserts nothing at all about
  whether it is true.
THREAD: law (17: hearsay is restricted for exactly this reason),
  media (19: reporting a claim is not endorsing it).
ASKED-AS: quotation words belong to speaker reported said not claim attribution wrong

ESSENCE: a word inside a longer name has no separate life. New in
  New York, spring in Palm Springs, and general in Attorney General
  are all fragments of names, not words doing work. The remedy is to
  match the longest name present first and to leave its parts alone.
ROOT: language (09) / a multi-word name is a single handle with
  spaces in it.
CANNOT: no reading of a word inside a longer name. Its letters were
  spent on the name and are not available for anything else.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (40: a catalogue matches longest first),
  computing (19: greedy matching exists for this reason).
ASKED-AS: word inside longer name new york palm springs fragment not separate

ESSENCE: a hedge changes what is being claimed. Might, usually,
  probably, in some cases, tends to — these turn a statement into a
  qualified one, and dropping the hedge reports something the writer
  did not say. The remedy is to keep the hedge attached to the claim
  it modifies.
ROOT: chance (35) / a probability is a stated amount of not knowing,
  and dropping it invents certainty.
CANNOT: no certainty from a hedged claim. A word inside usually or
  might carries a smaller claim than the same word standing plain,
  and reporting it plain is a misquotation.
THREAD: evidence (36: strength of evidence travels with the claim),
  law (17: a qualified opinion is a different opinion).
ASKED-AS: hedge might usually probably tends qualified claim dropped certainty overstated

ESSENCE: in a comparison, the thing compared against is not the
  subject. "Faster than the old kettle" is about the new one, and
  "unlike cats, dogs come when called" is about dogs. The remedy is
  to find the comparison word and to hand the subject to the near
  side of it.
ROOT: measurement (36) / a comparison names a standard and a thing
  measured, and only one of them is being discussed.
CANNOT: no subject in the thing being compared against. It is the
  ruler, not the object, and the object is on the other side.
THREAD: money (16: a benchmark is not the investment), sport (08: a
  record is what is being beaten, not who).
ASKED-AS: comparison subject near side than unlike compared against ruler not object

ESSENCE: a word appearing only in a heading is weak evidence. Titles
  are written to catch attention and often name a field rather than
  a claim, so a match in a heading and nowhere in the body is
  usually empty. The remedy is to require the word in the body
  before accepting it.
ROOT: writing (09) / a title advertises and a body argues.
CANNOT: no claim from a word that appears in a heading and nowhere
  else. The title says what the piece is filed under, not what it
  says.
THREAD: media (19: headlines are written by other people), keeping
  knowledge (40: a shelf label is not a summary).
ASKED-AS: heading title only match weak body claim advertise filed under evidence

ESSENCE: a forbidding sentence names a thing in order to rule it
  out. Knowledge that says "no browning in a wet pan" is about
  browning and about wet pans, but it asserts neither — it denies
  their combination. The remedy is to read a wall as a wall and
  never as a claim about what exists.
ROOT: logic (11) / a prohibition and an assertion have opposite
  force and can share every word.
CANNOT: no assertion from a forbidding sentence. Matching the words
  of a wall and reporting them as a fact turns a limit into a
  recommendation, which is the most dangerous error in the file.
THREAD: law (17: a prohibition names the act it bans), physics (01:
  a conservation law forbids rather than describes).
ASKED-AS: forbidding sentence names thing rules out wall not claim denied combination

ESSENCE: a word inside an analogy is wearing a costume. Knowledge
  that explains electricity by talking about water pipes will match
  a question about plumbing, and it is not about plumbing at all.
  The remedy is to weight the analogy's words below the subject's
  own words.
ROOT: teaching (30) / an analogy borrows a familiar structure to
  explain an unfamiliar one, and the borrowing is temporary.
CANNOT: no subject in an analogy's costume. The borrowed words were
  chosen for their familiarity, and the subject is the thing being
  explained.
THREAD: words and meaning (66: borrowed structure, caught moving),
  electricity (04: the water picture breaks at exactly one point).
ASKED-AS: analogy costume water pipes electricity explained borrowed familiar not subject

ESSENCE: one letter can hide a match. Colour and color, tyre and
  tire, organise and organize, and any ordinary typing slip, all
  fail a letter comparison while meaning the same thing. The remedy
  is to hold the known variant pairs and to allow a near miss on a
  long word.
ROOT: language (09) / spelling is a convention that varies by place
  and drifts by decade.
CANNOT: no absence of a match from one letter of difference in a
  long word. Two long words differing by one letter are the same
  word far more often than they are different ones.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (40: a catalogue must hold variants),
  computing (19: near matching is standard for exactly this).
ASKED-AS: spelling variants colour color tyre tire typo one letter missed

ESSENCE: short letter strings collide with real words. Any three
  letters are somebody's initials, a unit, or a word in some field,
  so a match on a short string carries almost no information. The
  remedy is to require more letters or a companion word before
  accepting a short match.
ROOT: language (09) / there are few short strings and many things to
  name, so collisions are certain.
CANNOT: no meaning from a match on fewer than three letters. Short
  strings collide with everything and distinguish nothing.
THREAD: measurement (36: a unit symbol needs its context), computing
  (19: short identifiers collide by design).
ASKED-AS: abbreviations initials short strings collide three letters unit acronym meaningless

ESSENCE: numbers and units look like words and are not. A question
  with 200C, 15 minutes or 3 amps in it has stated a constraint, and
  treating those as ordinary tokens loses the only precise thing in
  the sentence. The remedy is to read the number with its unit as
  one fact.
ROOT: measurement (36) / a number and its unit are a single
  quantity, and neither means anything alone.
CANNOT: no quantity from a number with no unit beside it. Two
  hundred is not a temperature, a weight or a price until the unit
  says which.
THREAD: cooking (02: a ratio is the recipe), engineering (04: unit
  confusion has destroyed real machines).
ASKED-AS: numbers units look like words constraint temperature minutes amps quantity precise

ESSENCE: an answer that repeats the question's words has not
  answered. "Why does the dough not rise" answered by "because the
  dough does not rise" scores perfectly on shared letters and says
  nothing. The remedy is to require the answer to add words the
  question did not have.
ROOT: evidence (36) / a claim that restates its premise has added no
  support.
CANNOT: no answer in a restatement of the question. If every word in
  the reply came from the asking, nothing new has been brought.
THREAD: mathematics (11: circular proof), teaching (30: a definition
  that uses the word being defined).
ASKED-AS: answer repeats question words circular restatement adds nothing new mirror

ESSENCE: some words take their sense from a word that is not there.
  "Two more, please" and "the small one" depend on something already
  named, and the sense cannot be read off the sentence at all. The
  remedy is to look backwards for the missing anchor and to say so
  if there is none.
ROOT: language (09) / speakers leave out what both sides already
  hold, and leave nothing in the words to recover it.
CANNOT: no sense in a phrase whose anchor is missing. Small, more,
  next and other are relative words with nothing to be relative to.
THREAD: story (27: a pronoun without an antecedent stops a reader),
  computing (19: state carried between messages).
ASKED-AS: missing anchor two more small one relative words backwards previous turn

ESSENCE: a counter-example mentions the thing being refuted. Text
  that says "people think yeast dies at room temperature, and it
  does not" is full of the wrong claim, and matching on it returns
  precisely the error. The remedy is to notice the refuting frame
  and to read the correction rather than what it corrects.
ROOT: teaching (30) / a misconception must be stated before it can
  be corrected.
CANNOT: no claim from a word inside a refutation. The refuted idea
  is present in the words and absent from the argument.
THREAD: persuasion (22: repeating a myth to deny it spreads it),
  evidence (36: a rejected hypothesis is still named in the report).
ASKED-AS: counter example mentions refuted claim myth stated then denied wrong match

ESSENCE: a word inside a definition of another word belongs to that
  other word. Knowledge defining "proving" as "the rest after
  shaping" contains rest and shaping, and it is not about resting or
  shaping. The remedy is to attach the words in a definition to the
  term being defined.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / a definition names a family and a
  difference, and both are borrowed for the occasion.
CANNOT: no subject in a word inside a definition. The definition is
  about its headword, and everything else in it is machinery.
THREAD: law (17: the definitions clause is not the obligations),
  keeping knowledge (40: a glossary entry is filed under its term).
ASKED-AS: word inside definition belongs headword machinery not subject glossary defining term

ESSENCE: asking about a word is not the same as using it. "What does
  proving mean" is a question about a word; "how long should I prove
  this" is a question about bread. The two need different knowledge,
  and their letters are nearly identical.
ROOT: language (09) / a word can be used to point at things or
  displayed as a thing itself.
CANNOT: no subject sense in a word being asked about. When the word
  is the topic, the answer is about language, and the field the word
  belongs to is background.
THREAD: teaching (30: vocabulary questions and practice questions),
  words and meaning (66: a dictionary reports use, it does not use).
ASKED-AS: asking about a word versus using it what does mean vocabulary topic

ESSENCE: a container word names where a thing is kept, not what it
  is. "A book about ovens", "a file for invoices", "a page on
  frost" — book, file and page are packaging, and the subject is
  what follows about, for or on. The remedy is to read past the
  container to its contents.
ROOT: keeping knowledge (40) / a record has a shell and a content,
  and only the content is the subject.
CANNOT: no subject in a container word. Book, file, page, video,
  guide and article describe the wrapping, and every one of them
  fits any subject at all.
THREAD: computing (19: a filename is not the file), media (19: the
  format is not the story).
ASKED-AS: container word book file page guide article packaging contents subject after

ESSENCE: a word can be common inside one area and rare outside it. In
  a file about the sea, water appears in every entry and separates
  none of them; in the whole library it is a strong signal. The
  remedy is to judge a word's usefulness inside the area it is being
  used to sort.
ROOT: measurement (36) / a discriminating measurement is one that
  varies across the things being told apart.
CANNOT: no separating power in a word every entry of an area holds.
  Inside that area it is a constant, and constants sort nothing.
THREAD: chance (35: a variable with no variance carries no
  information), evidence (36: a test everyone passes is not a test).
ASKED-AS: word common inside one area rare outside constant sorts nothing local

ESSENCE: opposites keep the same company. Hot and cold both travel
  with temperature, oven and degrees; rising and falling both travel
  with price and rate. Company places the subject and says nothing
  at all about direction, and a match on company alone can return
  the exact opposite of what was asked.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / a family of near-words shares a
  neighbourhood and disagrees inside it.
CANNOT: no direction from a shared company. The crowd around a word
  tells you the area and never which way along it, so the direction
  word itself must be read.
THREAD: measurement (36: a magnitude without a sign is half a
  reading), logic (11: a claim and its negation share their terms).
ASKED-AS: opposites same company hot cold rising falling direction lost wrong way

ESSENCE: a word inside a supposed world is not a fact about this
  one. "If the oven had been hotter, the crust would have set" tells
  you nothing about the oven that was used. The remedy is to find
  the if, the would and the suppose, and to mark everything in their
  reach as not asserted.
ROOT: logic (11) / an implication says nothing about whether its
  condition holds.
CANNOT: no fact from a word inside a supposition. It describes a
  world that was not the case, and reporting it as the case inverts
  the meaning.
THREAD: chance (35: a scenario is a possibility being examined),
  strategy (23: a war-game is deliberate false-world thinking).
ASKED-AS: hypothetical if would suppose imagine not a fact supposed world asserted

ESSENCE: one sentence can carry two senses of the same word, and
  usually on purpose. "I banked the plane and then banked the
  cheque" is a joke; "the current current" is a mistake; both defeat
  any single reading. The remedy is to allow a word two readings in
  one sentence rather than forcing one.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / ambiguity is normal and is settled by
  neighbours, not by the word.
CANNOT: no single sense forced on a word used twice differently. A
  sentence with two senses in it has two subjects, and picking one
  loses the other.
THREAD: humor (41: a pun is ambiguity cashed for a laugh), law (17:
  fortunes have turned on two readings of one clause).
ASKED-AS: two senses one sentence pun same word twice different reading forced

ESSENCE: a question built only of pronouns names nothing. It, that,
  this, one, them and there are pointers, and a pointer with nothing
  behind it points nowhere. The remedy is to take the subject from
  the turn before, and if there is no turn before, to ask.
ROOT: language (09) / a pronoun stands in for what was already
  established.
CANNOT: no subject in a question built only of pronouns. There is
  nothing in the sentence to find, and searching harder invents
  something that was never said.
THREAD: computing (19: a null reference), people together (21:
  asking for the missing word costs one line).
ASKED-AS: pronouns only question it that this one nothing to match ask

ESSENCE: when nothing matches, saying so is the answer. A reach for
  the nearest thing sharing two letters produces a confident reply
  about the wrong subject, which costs more than silence because it
  looks like knowledge. The remedy is to report what was missing and
  stop.
ROOT: evidence (36) / a report of nothing found is a finding, and a
  guess dressed as one is not.
CANNOT: no answer from a question with nothing found. An empty
  result must be reported empty, and the words that could not be
  placed are the most useful thing to hand back.
THREAD: measurement (36: a null result is a result), keeping
  knowledge (40: an empty record must say that it is empty).
ASKED-AS: nothing matched say so honest empty result guess worse than silence
