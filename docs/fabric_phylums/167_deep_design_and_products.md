# 167 DEEP DESIGN AND PRODUCTS — making things people can use
A made thing is a conversation between somebody who understands it
completely and somebody who has never seen it before, conducted
without either being in the room. Almost everything in this file
follows from that one asymmetry: what the maker knows cannot be
transmitted by wanting to, so it has to be built into the shape.

ESSENCE: nobody wants a product. They want their situation to be
  different afterwards — the hole in the wall, the calm about the
  bill, the child collected on time. The thing you built is a means
  they are willing to tolerate, and only for as long as nothing
  easier appears.
ROOT: the mind / attention and effort are spent toward an end, and the
  end is never the object in your hand.
CANNOT: no understanding a product's competition from its category —
  the alternative is often a pen and paper, a phone call, or simply
  not bothering, and none of those appear in any list of rivals. And
  no fixing a thing nobody wants by improving it: better means closer
  to a goal, and there isn't one.
THREAD: medicine (a patient who wants to stop worrying, not a test),
  building (a room wanted for what happens in it), farming (a tool
  bought for a harvest and not for itself).
ASKED-AS: what users actually want job to be done features product purpose goal underlying

ESSENCE: the feature and the goal are different objects, and users
  will describe the feature because it is the only vocabulary they
  have. Asking what they want gets you a faster horse; asking what
  they were doing when it went wrong gets you the thing itself.
ROOT: language / people can name what they have seen and cannot name
  what does not yet exist.
CANNOT: no design brief taken straight from a request — the request is
  a guess at a solution wearing the authority of a customer. And no
  ignoring the requests either: each one marks a real moment of pain,
  so the request is evidence of where to look and never of what to
  build.
THREAD: medicine (a patient asking for a specific pill), teaching (a
  pupil asking for the answer rather than the method), building (a
  client asking for a bigger window when the room is dark for another
  reason).
ASKED-AS: feature request customers asked for build what they said underlying problem symptom

ESSENCE: a thing tells you what can be done to it by how it is shaped.
  A handle asks to be pulled, a slot asks for a card, a flat plate
  asks to be pushed. Where the shape lies about what is possible,
  people fail at the door and think it is their fault.
ROOT: the mind / seeing is guessing, and the guess is built from the
  form in front of you before any label is read.
CANNOT: no instruction that beats a shape — a plate with PULL written
  on it is pushed all day, because the hand acts before the eye
  finishes reading. And no honest object with a misleading form: the
  form is the claim, and a label is only ever an apology for it.
THREAD: craft (a tool whose grip teaches the hold), building (a
  doorway that says which way it swings), farming (a gate a stranger
  can work out in the dark).
ASKED-AS: door push pull confusing handle shape signals how to use affordance obvious

ESSENCE: what a thing can do and what it shows you it can do are two
  separate facts, and only the second one gets used. A capability
  nobody can see does not exist for practical purposes, which is why
  so much of design is the work of making possibilities visible.
ROOT: information / an option that carries no signal cannot enter a
  decision, so it is the same as absent.
CANNOT: no discovery of a hidden capability by ordinary use — people
  find what is displayed and stop looking. And no rescuing it with
  documentation: whoever would read the manual to find a feature had
  already decided the thing could not do it.
THREAD: shops (goods behind a counter that nobody asks for), medicine
  (a service patients are entitled to and never told about), music
  (an instrument's technique that has to be shown).
ASKED-AS: hidden feature nobody found discoverable visible options users unaware capability shown

ESSENCE: a control should sit where its effect is and move the way the
  effect moves. When the arrangement of the knobs matches the
  arrangement of the things they change, nobody needs to learn
  anything; when it does not, everybody learns it wrong for years.
ROOT: the mind / a spatial layout is remembered far more cheaply than
  an arbitrary pairing, because it does not have to be memorised at
  all.
CANNOT: no learning away a bad mapping — under pressure the hand goes
  where the layout says, not where the label says, and pressure is
  exactly when it matters. And no fixing it with labels: a label is
  read once and a mapping is used forever.
THREAD: cooking (hob rings and the knobs that may or may not match),
  flying (a lever that moves the way the surface moves), music (a
  keyboard where higher is to the right).
ASKED-AS: controls confusing which knob wrong burner layout matches labels mapping intuitive

ESSENCE: an action with no answer is an action a person will do again.
  Every press, tap and pull needs an immediate sign that it landed —
  and if the system will take a while, saying so at once is part of
  the answer, not a substitute for it.
ROOT: the mind / a person predicts the result of their own action, and
  silence is read as failure rather than as delay.
CANNOT: no confident use without feedback — the person keeps pressing,
  and the duplicates are the system's fault. And no honest system that
  acknowledges before it has acted: a confirmation given before the
  thing is done is a lie that will be discovered later, at a worse
  moment.
THREAD: medicine (a machine that beeps to say it read the card),
  farming (a lever with a detent you can feel), telephones (a tone
  that says the number was taken).
ASKED-AS: clicked twice nothing happened feedback confirmation button response acknowledgement pressed again

ESSENCE: delay is not one thing. Under about a tenth of a second an
  action feels like your own; up to about a second the thread of
  thought holds; past ten and the person has left, mentally, and will
  come back needing to be told where they were.
ROOT: the mind / the sense of causing something depends on the result
  arriving inside the window where attention is still on the act.
CANNOT: no feeling of directness above the first threshold — beyond
  it, the machine is something you are commanding rather than
  something you are operating. And no attention held across the third:
  people will start another task, so the return has to re-establish
  the situation rather than assume it.
THREAD: music (a note that must sound when the finger falls), driving
  (steering that answers immediately), conversation (a pause that
  becomes a silence).
ASKED-AS: slow loading feels laggy response time delay seconds waiting sluggish interface speed

ESSENCE: while a thing is working, the honest signal is the one that
  says which part is happening and roughly how much is left. A bar
  that lies to look better ruins the only thing it was for, because a
  person who has been misled once reads every later bar as decoration.
ROOT: evidence / a signal is worth what its accuracy record is, so a
  flattering indicator destroys its own usefulness.
CANNOT: no patience without information — an unmarked wait is
  indistinguishable from a failure, so people abandon or restart, and
  restarting often makes it worse. And no comfort from a spinner that
  cannot end: motion without progress says the machine is alive and
  nothing about whether the work is.
THREAD: transport (a platform sign that says how many minutes), cooking
  (an oven that shows the temperature climbing), medicine (a waiting
  room that tells you where you are in the list).
ASKED-AS: loading spinner progress bar stuck how long waiting honest estimate cancel

ESSENCE: a thing that cannot be done wrongly needs no warning. A plug
  that only fits one way, a lid that will not close unless the part is
  seated, a form that cannot accept a date in the past — the shape
  does the work that a paragraph of instructions does badly.
ROOT: this file / a person acts from the form in front of them, so a
  form that excludes the error removes it without anyone attending.
CANNOT: no instruction as reliable as an impossibility — the notice is
  read once, by some people, on a calm day. And no constraint without
  a cost: every one of them forbids some legitimate rare case, so the
  design must decide whose unusual need is being sacrificed.
THREAD: engineering (a connector that only mates one way), medicine (a
  syringe that cannot fit the wrong port), cooking (a pan that will
  not sit on the wrong ring).
ASKED-AS: prevent mistakes impossible wrong way instructions ignored design constraint foolproof error

ESSENCE: the person knows what they want and the machine offers a set
  of moves, and the distance between the two is where all the
  suffering is. They must translate their intention into the system's
  vocabulary, and then translate what it shows back into whether they
  got what they meant.
ROOT: language / an intention has to be encoded into an available
  action, and every encoding loses something.
CANNOT: no closing the gap from the machine's side alone — it can only
  be narrowed by offering moves shaped like intentions rather than
  like the machinery underneath. And no user who thinks in your
  system's terms: they think in the terms of their own work, and they
  will not be retrained by a screen.
THREAD: language (asking for something in a foreign tongue), medicine
  (describing a pain in words a form will accept), building (a client
  and an architect meaning different things by the same word).
ASKED-AS: cannot work out how to do it interface terms system language intention translate

ESSENCE: everybody builds a story of how the machine works and then
  acts on it. The story is assembled from what they can see and is
  usually wrong — but it is what they will use, so a design's real job
  is to cause the right story rather than to be internally correct.
ROOT: the mind / a person predicts before acting, and prediction
  requires a model, so one gets invented whether or not it is
  supplied.
CANNOT: no user without a mental model — supply none and they build a
  false one, and the false one is now yours to live with. And no
  correcting it with an explanation: the story came from the
  behaviour, so only the behaviour can change it.
THREAD: medicine (a patient's theory of their illness driving whether
  they take the tablets), heating (a thermostat believed to work like
  a tap), cars (a driver's theory of the engine deciding how they
  drive).
ASKED-AS: users think it works differently mental model wrong assumption misunderstand behaviour explain

ESSENCE: if many people make the same error, it is not many people
  being careless. It is one design failing repeatedly and blaming a
  different person each time. The error rate is a property of the
  thing, and it can be measured before anyone is hurt.
ROOT: evidence / a pattern across many independent people is a fact
  about what they have in common, which is the object.
CANNOT: no reducing a recurring error by telling people to be careful
  — attention is not a resource anybody can add. And no learning from
  errors that are punished: blame drives them out of sight, so the
  system loses exactly the information it needed to improve.
THREAD: aviation (accidents traced to a cockpit layout rather than to
  a pilot), medicine (a drug error caused by two identical vials),
  roads (a junction where careful drivers keep crashing).
ASKED-AS: user error blamed people keep doing wrong design fault training careless mistakes

ESSENCE: two failures wear one word. A slip is doing the wrong thing
  while intending the right one — the hand went to the old place. A
  mistake is doing exactly what you intended, having intended
  something wrong. Better warnings fix neither, and each needs a
  different repair.
ROOT: the mind / an action has a plan and an execution, and either can
  fail on its own.
CANNOT: no preventing slips with more attention — they happen to
  experts precisely because the action is automatic. And no preventing
  mistakes with confirmations: the person will confirm, because they
  believe they are right, and the dialogue is asking the one question
  they cannot answer.
THREAD: music (a wrong note against a wrong key), driving (the wrong
  pedal against the wrong turning), cooking (salt for sugar against a
  recipe misread).
ASKED-AS: slip mistake different errors habit wrong intention confirm warning prevent types

ESSENCE: when the same control does different things depending on some
  invisible state, people will act on the state they last remember. A
  mode nobody can see is a trap with a delay on it, and the more
  practised the user, the faster they walk into it.
ROOT: this file / action follows the model in the head, and an
  invisible state cannot be in that model.
CANNOT: no safe mode that is not continuously visible — a state
  announced only when it changed is invisible five minutes later. And
  no expertise as protection: fluency means acting without checking,
  so skill increases exposure rather than reducing it.
THREAD: flying (an autopilot in a state the crew have lost track of),
  cameras (a setting from yesterday), typing (a caps key that changes
  what every following press means).
ASKED-AS: mode error caps lock wrong state invisible setting changed behaviour unexpected

ESSENCE: the ability to take it back is worth more than any amount of
  care beforehand. Undo makes a system safe to explore, and a system
  safe to explore is one people learn by using — which is the only way
  anybody ever actually learns anything.
ROOT: the mind / learning requires trying, and trying requires that
  being wrong is survivable.
CANNOT: no confident exploration where actions are permanent — people
  freeze, ask somebody, or avoid the feature entirely, and the
  avoidance never shows up in any report. And no substituting a
  confirmation for an undo: the question is asked before the person
  knows the answer, while undo is offered after they do.
THREAD: writing (a draft that can be revised against ink on stone),
  cooking (a dish you can still adjust), games (a save that makes a
  hard level worth attempting).
ASKED-AS: undo reverse mistake permanent delete confirm dialog safe explore recover accidental

ESSENCE: a dialogue that appears every time stops being read. People
  learn the position of the button and clear it without seeing the
  words, so the safeguard reliably fails on the one occasion it
  mattered — and it fails silently, having appeared to work.
ROOT: the mind / a repeated stimulus with no consequence stops being
  attended, and habit takes over the response.
CANNOT: no protection from a warning that fires on routine actions —
  frequency is what destroys it. And no fixing it by making the
  wording stronger: the words are not being read, so their content is
  not the variable.
THREAD: alarms (a hospital monitor that sounds so often nobody looks),
  driving (a warning light that has been on for months), law (terms
  agreed by everybody and read by nobody).
ASKED-AS: confirmation dialog clicked through warning ignored are you sure habit prompt

ESSENCE: whatever the default is, most people will keep it. Not from
  laziness — the default reads as a recommendation, changing it costs
  a decision, and people are busy. So the person who sets defaults is
  deciding the behaviour of the majority.
ROOT: the mind / a decision costs effort, so the option requiring none
  is chosen by anybody without a reason to spend.
CANNOT: no neutral default — every setting has to ship as something,
  and whatever it ships as is what most people will live with. And no
  defence in the choice being available: availability is not use, and
  the gap between them is where the design's real effect lives.
THREAD: medicine (a prescribed dose that becomes the standard), law
  (an opt-out that decides participation), farming (a seed rate
  printed on the drill and never changed).
ASKED-AS: default settings nobody changes preselected option opt out choice majority keeps

ESSENCE: making something take a moment longer is a design decision
  with a purpose — a pause before a permanent act, a step before a
  purchase. Making one direction easy and its opposite hard is the
  same tool aimed at the user rather than for them, and both are
  deliberate.
ROOT: this file / effort decides behaviour more reliably than
  intention does, so whoever allocates the effort allocates the
  outcome.
CANNOT: no design without friction somewhere — it is only ever moved,
  never removed. And no innocence in where it is placed: a
  subscription that takes one click to start and four calls to end is
  not an accident of engineering, and everybody involved knows it.
THREAD: shops (an exit routed past the tills), law (a right that
  exists behind an impossible form), households (a biscuit tin placed
  on a high shelf on purpose).
ASKED-AS: hard to cancel easy signup friction dark pattern deliberate steps unsubscribe difficult

ESSENCE: show the few things nearly everybody needs, and keep the rest
  one step away rather than absent. The beginner is not confused by
  power, they are confused by everything being present at once — and
  the expert is not served by simplicity, they are served by the
  extras being findable.
ROOT: the mind / the working bench is small, so the number of things
  visible at once decides whether anything can be chosen.
CANNOT: no serving beginners and experts on one flat surface — the
  amount that helps one buries the other. And no hiding by removing:
  a capability moved out of sight must leave a visible door, or it has
  been deleted for everybody who does not already know.
THREAD: cooking (a kitchen with the everyday tools out and the rest in
  a drawer), music (an instrument simple to sound and deep to master),
  shops (a counter display and a stockroom).
ASKED-AS: too many options overwhelming advanced settings hidden simple interface beginners experts

ESSENCE: people are far better at recognising than at remembering.
  Show the options and they choose correctly; ask them to recall a
  name, a code or where they put something and they fail — so every
  place a design asks for recall is a place it has moved its own work
  onto the user.
ROOT: the mind / recognition compares against something present while
  recall must reconstruct from nothing, and the two are not equally
  expensive.
CANNOT: no expecting recall across a gap — whatever is not on the
  screen has to be held in a head that is also doing the task. And no
  system that makes people remember its vocabulary: they will guess,
  guess wrongly, and conclude the thing does not do what they wanted.
THREAD: exams (multiple choice against an empty page), shops (a menu
  against being asked what you would like), music (reading against
  playing from memory).
ASKED-AS: remember command name menu shows options recall search browse forgot how

ESSENCE: doing the same thing the same way everywhere is worth more
  than doing it the best way somewhere. Consistency means a person
  learns your system once instead of once per screen, and that saving
  is larger than almost any local improvement.
ROOT: the mind / a pattern learned once transfers, so every exception
  is a fresh thing to be learned and remembered.
CANNOT: no local improvement that is free — it costs the transfer from
  everywhere else. And no consistency without a decision about which
  system you are consistent with: your own product, the platform, or
  the rest of the world, and those three will disagree.
THREAD: language (a spelling rule worth keeping even where it is
  ugly), driving (controls in the same places in every car), building
  (a stair riser that must not change halfway).
ASKED-AS: consistency same everywhere pattern learn once exception different screen standard interface

ESSENCE: consistency also preserves mistakes. Once a wrong thing is
  everywhere, correcting it in one place makes the product feel
  broken, so the error hardens into a convention and gets copied into
  the next thing — and the cost of that is invisible because nobody
  ever sees the version without it.
ROOT: this file / a learned pattern transfers, and it transfers
  whether or not it was any good.
CANNOT: no fixing a widespread mistake gradually — a half-changed
  system is worse than either version, so the choice is a costly
  sweep or living with it. And no measuring what it costs: the price
  is paid by users who never knew there was an alternative.
THREAD: language (a spelling nobody defends and everybody keeps),
  engineering (a thread size kept for compatibility), keyboards (a
  layout arranged for a machine that no longer exists).
ASKED-AS: legacy design bad pattern kept consistency stuck old convention change breaks

ESSENCE: the platform's way of doing something beats your better way,
  because the person arrives already knowing the platform and spending
  only minutes with you. Your improvement is charged to them as
  relearning, and they were not asking for a lesson.
ROOT: people and power / a coordination rule's entire value is that
  everybody follows the same one.
CANNOT: no adoption of a better convention by one participant alone —
  being right alone is being different, and different is the cost. And
  no reading approval from your own team's preference: they use the
  thing all day, which is the one situation your users are never in.
THREAD: driving (a country's side of the road), measurement (a unit
  kept because everybody uses it), music (a tuning nobody can improve
  unilaterally).
ASKED-AS: platform conventions standard behaviour custom widget familiar users expect different better

ESSENCE: designing for disability is designing for everybody, because
  everybody is disabled sometimes — one arm holding a child, glare on
  a screen, a noisy room, a broken wrist, sixty years old. The
  adjustments made for the permanent case are used most by people in
  the temporary one.
ROOT: this file / a design meets a person in a situation, and the
  range of situations is wider than the range of people.
CANNOT: no product used only in the conditions it was designed in —
  the tested case is a quiet room with good light and two free hands,
  and almost no real use is that. And no accessibility added
  afterwards: the structure it needs is the structure, so retrofitting
  means rebuilding.
THREAD: building (a ramp used by prams, trolleys and suitcases),
  television (subtitles watched in silence by choice), tools (a grip
  designed for weak hands and preferred by strong ones).
ASKED-AS: accessibility disabled users everyone benefits situational temporary curb cut design afterthought

ESSENCE: colour cannot be the only thing that carries a meaning. A
  large share of people cannot separate certain pairs, screens and
  printers shift them, and a photocopy removes them entirely — so
  anything said in colour alone is said to some of the audience and
  not the rest.
ROOT: information / a message needs a channel the receiver has, and
  colour perception is not a channel everybody has.
CANNOT: no meaning safely encoded in hue alone — add a shape, a
  position, a word, a pattern. And no testing it by looking: the
  designer sees their own colours correctly, so the failure is
  invisible from exactly the seat where the decision is made.
THREAD: maps (contours that also carry numbers), wiring (colours
  backed by printed codes), sport (kits that must differ in more than
  shade).
ASKED-AS: colour blind red green only indicator status meaning shape label alternative distinguish

ESSENCE: contrast and size are not taste, they are whether the thing
  can be read at all. Pale grey on white looks calm to a young
  designer on a good screen and disappears for an older reader in
  sunlight — and the reader does not complain, they simply fail and
  leave.
ROOT: the body / the eye's ability to separate near-identical
  brightnesses declines with age and falls apart in glare.
CANNOT: no legibility judged by the designer's own eyes on the
  designer's own screen — the sample is one young person in ideal
  light. And no beauty that survives being unreadable: an elegant page
  nobody can read has failed at the only thing a page does.
THREAD: signs (a road sign read at speed in rain), medicine (a label
  read by whoever must take the tablet), print (a book set for an
  ageing reader).
ASKED-AS: grey text hard to read contrast font size older eyes sunlight legibility

ESSENCE: a target for a finger has to be about the size of a
  fingertip, and it has to be far enough from anything dangerous that
  a near miss is not a disaster. A pointer is precise, a finger is a
  blunt object, and the same layout cannot serve both.
ROOT: the body / a fingertip covers a real area and its own contact
  hides the target from view.
CANNOT: no accurate tapping on targets smaller than the thing tapping
  them — the finger obscures the very thing being aimed at. And no
  placing a destructive control beside a common one at that size: the
  miss rate is a known quantity, so the damage is a decision, not an
  accident.
THREAD: engineering (a switch guard around an important control),
  cooking (handles spaced so a hand does not meet a hot pan), music (a
  fretboard sized to fingers).
ASKED-AS: touch target too small tapped wrong button finger phone spacing accidental hit

ESSENCE: a thing is faster to reach the bigger it is and the nearer it
  is, and that trade is arithmetic rather than opinion. So a control
  used constantly should be large and close, and the one you should
  rarely press should be small and far — which is the same sentence
  read backwards.
ROOT: the body / aiming is a series of corrections, and both distance
  and target size decide how many corrections are needed.
CANNOT: no equal-sized layout that respects how often things are used
  — treating every control alike makes the common ones slow and the
  rare ones dangerously easy. And no reaching a screen edge slowly:
  edges and corners are effectively infinite in size, which is why
  they are the best real estate on any display.
THREAD: cooking (the pan you use daily on the nearest hook), flying (a
  control's size matching its importance), workshops (a bench laid out
  by frequency of use).
ASKED-AS: button size placement reach often used far corner easy hit distance

ESSENCE: more choices take longer to choose from, and past a point
  they stop being chosen from at all. A list of six is scanned; a list
  of sixty is abandoned or answered by taking whatever is at the top —
  which means the ordering, not the range, is the real decision.
ROOT: the mind / a decision's cost grows with the options considered,
  so a wide field is answered by not considering.
CANNOT: no benefit from an option nobody reaches — beyond the point of
  scanning, adding choices removes them in practice. And no neutral
  ordering of a long list: whatever comes first is what most people
  take, so the order is a recommendation whether or not it was meant
  as one.
THREAD: shops (a shelf so wide that people buy the familiar), menus (a
  restaurant list too long to read), pensions (a scheme with so many
  options that most people take none).
ASKED-AS: too many choices paralysis long list menu options fewer defaults order first

ESSENCE: the word on the button is the interface. People do not read
  the documentation and they do not learn your model — they read the
  label and act on what they think it means, so a badly named thing is
  a badly designed thing regardless of how it works.
ROOT: language / a name carries a promise, and the promise is what the
  user will act on.
CANNOT: no rescuing a confusing name with an explanation nearby — the
  name is read and the explanation is not. And no internal vocabulary
  on an external surface: the word your team uses came from how the
  thing is built, and the user cares about what it does.
THREAD: shops (a product name that says what it is for), medicine (a
  drug name confusable with another and the harm that follows), law (a
  clause heading that decides what people think they agreed).
ASKED-AS: naming button label confusing wording jargon internal terms users misunderstand words

ESSENCE: an icon without a word is a guess. A very small number are
  genuinely universal and the rest are learned from having been shown
  once — so a screen of unlabelled symbols is a memory test dressed up
  as a clean design.
ROOT: language / a symbol means what a community has agreed it means,
  and outside that community it means nothing.
CANNOT: no self-explanatory icon for an abstract action — a picture
  can show a thing and cannot show a concept. And no measuring this
  from inside the team: everybody there was told what the symbols mean
  on their first day, and cannot unknow it.
THREAD: roads (signs that must be taught before they are read), maps
  (a legend that has to exist), religion (a symbol dense with meaning
  to insiders and blank to everyone else).
ASKED-AS: icons unclear no labels guess meaning symbols universal hamburger recognise text

ESSENCE: how things are grouped decides whether they can be found, and
  the grouping that matters is the user's, not the organisation's.
  Splitting a service across departments makes it unfindable, however
  correctly it reflects who does the work.
ROOT: the mind / people look where a thing belongs in their own
  picture of the world, and search stops when that place is empty.
CANNOT: no findability from a structure that mirrors the builder —
  the org chart is not a map of anybody's task. And no single correct
  arrangement either: people group differently, which is why any deep
  structure needs a search that forgives the wrong guess.
THREAD: shops (goods arranged by how people shop rather than by
  supplier), libraries (a scheme that decides what is discoverable),
  government (a form split across offices and lost between them).
ASKED-AS: cannot find it navigation menu structure categories grouping where would look organisation

ESSENCE: search is what people use when the structure failed them, so
  the search results are an inventory of the design's mistakes. It
  also has to forgive: wrong spelling, the wrong word for the thing,
  the name they used at their last job.
ROOT: language / people name things in their own vocabulary, and no
  designer's vocabulary matches everyone's.
CANNOT: no search that only accepts the official term — the user does
  not know it, which is why they are searching. And no ignoring the
  queries that returned nothing: those are direct evidence of what
  people wanted and could not find, and they are usually the cheapest
  research anybody has.
THREAD: libraries (a catalogue that finds a book from a half-
  remembered title), shops (an assistant who understands what you
  meant), medicine (a coding system that must accept how patients
  describe things).
ASKED-AS: search no results wrong spelling synonyms terms users type failed queries

ESSENCE: every field on a form costs you some of the people filling
  it. They are not paying for the field, they are paying with the
  effort of finding the number, deciding whether to trust you, and
  remembering what they were doing — so an optional question is never
  free.
ROOT: this file / effort decides behaviour, and each additional
  demand is another chance for a person to stop.
CANNOT: no adding a question at no cost — the price is paid in
  completed forms, invisibly, by people who left. And no asking for
  what you already hold: repeating a question you know the answer to
  tells the person the system does not remember them, which is its own
  quiet lesson.
THREAD: shops (a checkout with too many steps), medicine (a form so
  long that people arrive without it), government (a claim abandoned
  halfway).
ASKED-AS: form too long fields abandoned signup required optional asking again completion

ESSENCE: an error message has one job: to say what to do next. Naming
  the fault, blaming the person, or reciting a code satisfies the
  system and abandons the user at exactly the moment they are least
  able to think.
ROOT: language / a message is worth what the receiver can act on, and
  a diagnosis without a next step is unactionable.
CANNOT: no useful error message that does not name a recovery — the
  person is stuck, and describing the stuckness does not move them.
  And no message written by the part of the system that failed: it
  knows the internal cause and not the human situation, which is why
