# 135 DEEP ARITHMETIC AND NUMBER — the behaviour of quantity
Quantity has manners of its own. These are the ones that decide whether
an answer is right before anybody checks it, the handful of gaps no
cleverness has ever closed, and the places where a correct calculation
still answers the wrong question.

ESSENCE: division asks two different questions with one symbol. Twelve
  divided by four is either "twelve shared among four, how many each" or
  "how many fours fit inside twelve" — the same answer every time, and
  never the same question.
ROOT: premise — multiplying joins a count of groups to a size of group, so
  undoing it can hand back either one of them.
THREAD: cooking (splitting a batch against counting portions), money (cost
  each against how many I can afford), building (cuts per plank against
  planks per job).
ASKED-AS: divide sharing how many each fit into groups per split between meaning

ESSENCE: long division is repeated taking-away done in bundles — strip out
  the biggest easy multiple you can see, write down how many you took, and
  carry on with what is left. The school algorithm only makes the bundles
  march place by place.
ROOT: this file / division as measuring — how many fit is answered by
  taking them out until no more will go.
THREAD: money (making change by handing the largest coins first), packing
  (filling boxes until what remains will not fill one), time (turning
  minutes into hours and minutes).
RULE: to divide a long number by a short one — work the places left to
  right; at each place ask how many whole divisors fit in what stands
  there, write that count above the place, take away what it accounts for,
  and bring what remains down into the next place; when the places run
  out, what remains is the remainder.
ASKED-AS: long division how to steps by hand bring down remainder digits school method

ESSENCE: the remainder is an answer, not a scrap. A hundred people and
  thirty to a bus is three and a third buses, which means four buses; a
  hundred pounds and thirty a ticket means three tickets. Same number, and
  it refuses to say which way to round.
ROOT: this file / the two divisions — sharing tolerates parts, measuring
  usually does not.
THREAD: shipping (part loads still need a whole lorry), staffing (there is
  no third of a nurse), cooking (a part egg, which is why small recipes
  are awkward).
ASKED-AS: remainder left over round up down buses tickets whole how many extra

ESSENCE: a quick test for a divisor exists only where ten behaves simply
  on that divisor's wheel. Ten is two fives, so twos and fives read off
  the last digit; ten leaves one on three and nine, so their test is the
  digit sum. Seven gets nothing, and that is why.
ROOT: mathematics / written numbers are bundles — a test asks what a
  single bundle of ten is worth on the divisor's own wheel.
THREAD: computing (a machine bundles by two, so its easy tests are for
  two, four and eight), calendars (sevens are the awkward ones there too),
  music (why some rhythms never subdivide).
RULE: to test a number for a divisor — for two, five or ten look only at
  the last digit or two; for four or eight look at the last two or three;
  for three or nine add all the digits and test that sum the same way,
  repeating until one digit remains; for eleven add the digits alternately
  forwards and backwards and test what is left; for anything else, divide
  and see.
ASKED-AS: divisible by test trick check three nine eleven last digit sum rule

ESSENCE: factors come in couples — each one has a partner it multiplies
  with to make the number, and the two sit on opposite sides of the square
  root. So a search only ever needs to climb as far as the root;
  everything above has already been written down as somebody's partner.
ROOT: mathematics / multiplying is counting by copies — a small count
  forces a large size, and the two are found together.
THREAD: computing (why testing a number for primeness is far cheaper than
  it looks), building (rectangles of a fixed area: long and thin, or near
  square), packing (box shapes for a fixed count).
RULE: to list every factor of a number — try each whole number from one
  upward; whenever it divides exactly, write it and its partner down as a
  pair; stop as soon as the trial number passes the square root, because
  every factor left is already on the list as a partner.
ASKED-AS: factors of a number list all pairs find divisors square root stop testing

ESSENCE: the prime pile of a number is a full description, not a
  curiosity. Read straight off it: every divisor it has, how many divisors
  there are, whether it is a perfect square — and laid beside another
  number's pile, everything the two share and everything they do not.
ROOT: mathematics / primes are the atoms of number, one pile per number
  and only one pile ever.
THREAD: chemistry (a formula settles what a compound can react with),
  music (which rhythms will eventually line up), computing (the difficulty
  of un-piling is the lock on the codes).
ASKED-AS: prime factors what use divisors how many perfect square compare two numbers

ESSENCE: the greatest common divisor is the largest piece two numbers can
  both be built from — the coarsest ruler that measures both with nothing
  left over. It is also the smallest amount you can make by adding and
  taking away copies of them, which is the same fact seen from underneath.
ROOT: this file / the prime pile — the shared part of two piles, each
  prime taken at the smaller of its two counts.
THREAD: building (the largest tile that fits two rooms with no cutting),
  money (the smallest unit two currencies both express), gears (teeth
  counts sharing a factor repeat sooner).
RULE: to find the greatest common divisor — take the smaller number from
  the larger and keep the new pair; repeat, always taking the smaller from
  the larger; when the two become equal, that is the answer. Faster:
  replace the larger by its remainder on the smaller, and repeat until the
  remainder is nothing — the last number above nothing is the answer.
ASKED-AS: greatest common factor largest divides both simplify tiles fit reduce shared

ESSENCE: the least common multiple is where two cycles first meet again —
  a bus every twelve minutes and one every eighteen leave together every
  thirty-six. Multiply the two numbers and you get a meeting; divide out
  what they share and you get the first meeting.
ROOT: this file / the prime pile — the joined pile, each prime taken at
  the larger of its counts, so the shared part is not paid for twice.
THREAD: astronomy (planets returning to the same alignment), music (two
  rhythms crossing and re-crossing), rotas (two shift patterns falling on
  the same day).
ASKED-AS: lowest common multiple when meet again buses cycles line up together denominators

ESSENCE: two numbers sharing no prime are strangers, and strangers cover
  everything — a gear of thirteen teeth against one of twenty meets every
  pairing before repeating, while twelve against eighteen keeps
  introducing the same faces to each other.
ROOT: this file / the greatest common divisor — when it is one, nothing is
  shared that could shorten the cycle.
THREAD: engineering (hunting gears chosen coprime so wear spreads),
  farming (crop rotations that avoid re-meeting a pest's cycle), music
  (rhythms that take a long time to agree).
ASKED-AS: share no factors gears teeth wear pattern repeats every combination strangers cycle

ESSENCE: a negative is a direction, not only a debt. The line runs both
  ways from zero — adding steps one way, taking away steps the other — and
  once that is the picture, minus five stops being a broken number and
  becomes five paces the other side.
ROOT: mathematics / zero and the negatives were invented; this is the
  picture that makes the invention feel forced rather than clever.
THREAD: weather (below freezing), money (an overdraft), lifts (floors
  below the ground, numbered downward).
ASKED-AS: negative numbers line direction below zero colder which bigger minus nine two

ESSENCE: a minus sign is a turn-around. Multiplying by a negative faces
  the answer the other way; doing it twice faces it back. It is not a
  decree — it is the only rule that keeps the splitting law alive, and
  that law is worth more.
ROOT: mathematics / the rule-change law — widen the numbers, keep every
  old score; the sign rule is what the old scores demand.
THREAD: physics (a reversal reversed), language (two negatives in a
  sentence, where the same logic is optional and custom wins), electronics
  (a switch flipped twice).
ASKED-AS: two negatives make positive why minus times rule explain sense multiply signs

ESSENCE: taking away a negative adds. Cancel a debt and you are richer by
  exactly its size; walk backwards while facing backwards and you travel
  forwards. The double sign is the same turn-around wearing the
  taking-away costume.
ROOT: this file / the minus sign as a turn-around, applied to the
  counting-down walk instead of the copying one.
THREAD: money (a written-off loan showing up as income), temperature (the
  drop that did not happen), bookkeeping (a reversing entry undoing last
  month).
ASKED-AS: minus a negative double subtracting adds why gets bigger owe cancel debt

ESSENCE: times before plus is a reading convention, like reading left to
  right. It settles what a written line MEANS and settles nothing whatever
  about arithmetic. Brackets say the same thing out loud, which is why a
  line with enough brackets needs no convention at all.
ROOT: premise — writing is a code, and a code needs agreed parsing; the
  quantities underneath do not care how they were typed.
THREAD: language (punctuation deciding a sentence's meaning), computing
  (every language publishes its own precedence table and they differ), law
  (a misplaced comma changing a contract).
ASKED-AS: order of operations which first brackets viral maths problem answer argue rules

ESSENCE: adding and multiplying do not care about order or grouping;
  taking away and dividing care about both. The safe move is to turn every
  subtraction into adding a negative and every division into multiplying
  by an upside-down number.
ROOT: premise — the reordering laws belong to the two joining operations,
  and the other two are those same operations run backwards.
THREAD: cooking (steps that may be swapped and steps that may not),
  computing (why a compiler may reorder some arithmetic and must not
  reorder other), music (order of effects on a signal).
ASKED-AS: order does not matter swap rearrange subtraction division mistake left right chain

ESSENCE: the one law joining adding to multiplying is that a count of a
  sum equals the sum of the counts — seven of ten-and-three is seven tens
  and seven threes. Every mental arithmetic trick anybody has ever shown
  you is this law being cashed in.
ROOT: mathematics / multiplying is counting by copies — the copies may be
  laid down in pieces and the pieces counted separately.
THREAD: money (a discount taken on a whole basket rather than item by
  item), building (a floor's area split into strips), law (a rule applied
  to each member or to the group as one).
ASKED-AS: mental maths trick times nineteen split parts twenty minus distribute shortcut multiply

ESSENCE: a power counts copies being multiplied, and one law runs the
  entire subject: multiply two powers of the same thing and the counts
  add, because you have merely pushed two piles of copies together. Every
  other power rule is that one sentence wearing a costume.
ROOT: mathematics / multiplying is counting by copies — applied to itself,
  so the count becomes the thing counted.
THREAD: music (each octave doubles, so pitches add while frequencies
  multiply), computing (bits add while possibilities multiply), money
  (interest on interest).
ASKED-AS: powers exponents indices add rules multiply same base squared cubed why

ESSENCE: nothing chose that anything to the power nothing is one, or that
  a negative power flips the number over, or that a half power is a root.
  Once counts must add, all three follow: walk the powers of two down —
  eight, four, two, one, a half.
ROOT: this file / powers add their counts — the definitions were extended
  precisely so that the single law keeps working past where it was first
  written.
THREAD: music (a half step is a twelfth root of two, a fractional power
  you can hear), physics (half-lives), measurement (loudness and acidity
  are both written as powers).
ASKED-AS: power zero equals one negative exponent half root why rule proof makes sense

ESSENCE: a root asks the power question backwards — what, multiplied by
  itself so many times, gives this. Odd roots take any number happily,
  because an odd count of turn-arounds still faces backwards; even roots
  refuse negatives, because an even count cannot.
ROOT: this file / the minus sign as a turn-around — an even number of
  turns always ends up facing forward.
THREAD: geometry (the side of a cube read off its volume), money (the
  yearly rate hidden inside a multi-year gain), sound (root mean square,
  where the squaring is exactly what hides the sign and the rooting is
  what restores the size).
ASKED-AS: cube root negative allowed square not even odd calculator error nth power

ESSENCE: the square root of two cannot be any fraction. Suppose it were,
  in lowest terms; then its top would have to be even, and then so would
  its bottom, and lowest terms is contradicted. Yet it is the diagonal of
  a one-metre square.
ROOT: mathematics / parity — the even-odd heartbeat survives every
  multiplication, and it convicts the assumption.
THREAD: building (a diagonal no tape measure lands on exactly), music (why
  an evenly spaced scale cannot also keep pure whole-number ratios),
  geometry (pi is the same refusal, harder proved).
ASKED-AS: square root two irrational proof cannot be fraction decimal never ends diagonal

ESSENCE: each new kind of number was minted to make one operation always
  answerable. Counting numbers fail at taking away, so negatives; whole
  numbers fail at dividing, so fractions; fractions fail at roots; those
  fail at the root of a negative.
ROOT: mathematics / the rule-change law — each widening is legal because
  it keeps every score the old board had settled.
THREAD: language (loanwords admitted to say what could not be said), law
  (a right extended while the old cases still stand), music (notes added
  between notes, something lost each time).
ASKED-AS: types of numbers integers rational real complex why invented what each for

ESSENCE: scientific notation splits a number into what you know and how
  big it is — a few meaningful digits times a power of ten. The exponent
  does the heavy lifting and the digits stop pretending to a precision
  nobody measured.
ROOT: mathematics / written numbers are bundles — the bundling count
  pulled out and written on its own where it can be read.
THREAD: astronomy (distances that no ordinary writing survives), chemistry
  (a mole is a power of ten given a name), computing (this is precisely
  how a machine stores a fraction).
ASKED-AS: scientific notation times ten power big small numbers exponent write compare huge

ESSENCE: an order of magnitude is one step of ten, and thinking in steps
  is the fastest useful arithmetic there is. A thousand against a billion
  is not "both large" — it is six steps, and those six steps are the whole
  content of the argument.
ROOT: this file / scientific notation — the exponent is the step count, so
  exponents are what get compared first.
THREAD: money (a national budget set beside a household one), the living
  world (a bacterium beside a whale), computing (a billionth of a second
  beside a second).
ASKED-AS: million billion difference how much bigger orders magnitude scale ten times step

ESSENCE: always rounding a half upward tilts every long total upward,
  because the halves all lean the same way. Rounding halves to the nearest
  even number lets them lean both ways and the tilt disappears — which is
  why banks and machines do it that way and schools mostly do not.
ROOT: this file / rounding discards, and a discard rule that is not
  even-handed becomes an error that piles up instead of cancelling.
THREAD: money (a payroll rounding a penny a row, ten thousand rows),
  computing (error accumulating through a long sum), polling (percentages
  that add to a hundred and one).
ASKED-AS: rounding half up down banker even bias totals do not add drift

ESSENCE: round everything to one digit and a power of ten, then do the sum
  in your head. The answer will be wrong in its second digit and right
  about its size — and being right about the size is what catches the
  blunder a calculator will never notice.
ROOT: this file / orders of magnitude — the leading digit and the exponent
  carry almost all of the information in a number.
THREAD: engineering (a back-of-envelope check before any design proceeds),
  shopping (a basket estimated to the nearest pound), medicine (a dose a
  thousand times too large announcing itself to anyone who estimated
  first).
RULE: to estimate a calculation — replace every number by one digit times
  a power of ten, rounding whichever way is easier; do the arithmetic on
  the digits, and add or subtract the powers separately; then set that
  result beside the machine's answer, and if they differ by a whole step
  of ten, distrust what was typed in before anything else.
ASKED-AS: estimate roughly ballpark check answer sensible decimal point wrong sanity mental

ESSENCE: changes chain by multiplying, never by adding. A rise of ten
  percent is a multiply by one-point-one, a fall of ten percent is a
  multiply by nought-point-nine, and doing both leaves ninety-nine
  hundredths — down one percent whichever order you take them in.
ROOT: mathematics / no percent without its whole — each change takes its
  whole from the step before, so the steps compose like copies rather than
  like counts.
THREAD: money (compound interest, inflation stacked over years), shopping
  (discounts applied one after another), wages (a cut restored by an
  equal-sounding rise, which never restores it).
ASKED-AS: percent increase then decrease chain years compound total change stack discounts

ESSENCE: a rate moving from four percent to five percent has risen one
  percentage point and has also risen a quarter. Both sentences are true,
  they differ by twenty-five times, and which one gets quoted is usually a
  decision about who is being persuaded.
ROOT: mathematics / a percent needs its whole — and a change in a
  percentage has two candidate wholes, the scale it sits on and the old
  figure it started from.
THREAD: money (an interest rate rise reported both ways on the same day),
  medicine (a risk going from two in a thousand to three), politics (a
  poll movement inside its own noise).
ASKED-AS: percentage points versus percent rise interest rate risk doubled misleading news

ESSENCE: a proportion is a claim that two ratios are the same, and
  cross-multiplying is nothing clever — it is multiplying both sides by
  both bottoms, so the bars vanish and one plain equation is left
  standing.
ROOT: algebra / an equation is a balance — the same treatment given to
  both sides, chosen to clear away the division.
THREAD: cooking (scaling a recipe up or down), maps (a scale bar turned
  into ground distance), medicine (a dose worked out per kilogram of
  patient).
RULE: to solve a proportion — write it as one fraction equalling one
  fraction with the unknown standing alone in a single place; multiply the
  top of each side by the bottom of the other; that leaves one equation
  with no bars in it, and the unknown comes out by a single division.
ASKED-AS: proportion cross multiply solve for x scale recipe map dose ratio

ESSENCE: two quantities are directly proportional when their ratio stays
  put — double one and the other doubles — and inversely proportional when
  their product stays put: double the workers and halve the days.
ROOT: this file / proportion as a fixed ratio — the inverse case simply
  fixes the product instead of the ratio.
THREAD: physics (pressure against volume in a squeezed gas), work (people
  against days, until they start getting in each other's way), money (unit
  cost falling with quantity but never to nothing).
ASKED-AS: directly proportional inversely double halve workers days pressure volume straight curve

ESSENCE: a conversion factor is a fraction that equals one — sixty minutes
  over one hour, because top and bottom are one amount in two dresses.
  Multiplying by one cannot change an amount, so only the dress changes,
  and units cancel like numbers.
ROOT: mathematics / a rate's second partner is a unit — so units can be
  carried through the arithmetic as though they were quantities in their
  own right.
THREAD: engineering (a spacecraft lost to pounds against newtons), cooking
  (grams against cups, which needs a density as well), medicine
  (millilitres against milligrams, where the error kills the patient).
RULE: to convert units — write the amount as a fraction over one; multiply
  by conversion fractions chosen so each unwanted unit appears once on top
  and once underneath; keep going until only the wanted units survive; do
  the number arithmetic last, after the units have already agreed.
ASKED-AS: convert units miles kilometres cancel factor method hours seconds grams which way

ESSENCE: a unit with a "per" in it is a recipe for the arithmetic. Metres
  per second per second says a speed changing every second. Read the unit
  and you know which operation the quantity came from and which may
  lawfully be done to it next.
ROOT: this file / units carry through arithmetic — so the units are a
  running audit of the calculation, checkable at every line.
THREAD: physics (an equation whose units do not balance is wrong before it
  is even tested), money (dollars per share against dollars per year),
  farming (yield per acre per season).
ASKED-AS: units per second squared pressure meaning check equation add different what means

ESSENCE: when two things work at one job, add their rates and never their
  times. A tap filling a tub in three hours and one filling it in six do a
  third and a sixth an hour — a half together — so the tub fills in two.
ROOT: mathematics / a rate is an amount per unit — amounts add, so rates
  over the same unit add, and times simply do not.
THREAD: work (two crews on one wall), plumbing (a drain open while a tap
  runs, which is one rate minus the other), computing (two machines on one
  queue, until they start colliding).
RULE: to combine workers on one job — turn each time into a rate by
  writing one over it; add the rates, subtracting any that work against
  the job; then turn the total rate back into a time by writing one over
  it again.
ASKED-AS: two taps fill tub together how long work rate pipes add times

ESSENCE: average speed is total distance over total time, and that is
  almost never the average of the speeds. Drive out at thirty and back at
  sixty and the average is forty, not forty-five — because the slow leg
  took twice as long, so it gets twice the vote.
ROOT: this file / a rate is an amount per unit — averaging a rate means
  averaging over whatever sits in its bottom, which here is time and not
  trips.
THREAD: money (a true yearly rate over good years and bad), fuel (miles
  per gallon averaged across a mixed fleet), sport (a runner's pace across
  uneven splits).
ASKED-AS: average speed there and back round trip not forty five why time

ESSENCE: averaging averages is honest only when the groups are the same
  size. A class of ten scoring eighty and a class of forty scoring sixty
  do not average seventy — they average sixty-four, because the large
  class was always going to speak louder.
ROOT: mathematics / an average is the pile shared out even — and
  re-sharing already-shared piles throws away the count each pile was
  standing for.
THREAD: schools (a district figure built from school figures), money (a
  company margin assembled from its divisions), sport (an average across
  seasons of different lengths).
ASKED-AS: average of averages wrong classes sizes combine overall mean groups unequal

ESSENCE: a weighted average lets each item bring its own say. Give every
  value a weight, multiply each by its weight, add those products, and
  divide by the total weight. The plain average is simply the case where
  every weight happens to be one.
ROOT: this file / the average of averages — the repair is to carry the
  counts through the calculation instead of dropping them at the first
  step.
THREAD: school (a final grade from parts worth different shares), money (a
  portfolio's return, a price index), the living world (a measurement
  averaged over unequal populations).
RULE: to take a weighted average — multiply each value by its weight; add
  all those products together; add all the weights together; divide the
  first total by the second. If the weights already add to one, the second
  total is done for you.

ESSENCE: the base is only how big a bundle grows before it carries. Change
  it and the number does not change — nine sheep are nine sheep either
  way. What changes is which numbers look tidy: a third repeats in tens, a
  tenth repeats in twos.
ROOT: mathematics / written numbers are bundles — the bundle size was a
  choice and it could have been another.
THREAD: computing (why a tenth plus a fifth does not equal three-tenths in
  a machine), money (why financial systems store whole pennies), time
  (sixties, chosen because sixty has so many factors).
ASKED-AS: base ten binary sixty why not exact repeating decimal thirds bundles carry

ESSENCE: binary bundles by two, so every place holds nothing or one and
  the place values double. Machines use it because a wire can be reliably
  on or off and cannot be reliably one of ten voltages — the arithmetic
  followed the hardware.
ROOT: this file / the base is a choice of bundle — and two is the smallest
  bundle that still counts anything.
THREAD: computing (every stored thing is this), electronics (a switch),
  logic (true and false written as the same two marks).
RULE: to write a number in binary — halve it again and again, writing down
  the remainder each time as nothing or one; when nothing is left, read
  the remainders backwards. To read binary back, start at nothing and for
  each digit left to right double what you have and add that digit.
ASKED-AS: binary ones zeros convert number computer bits how read write doubling

ESSENCE: hexadecimal is a shorthand for binary, not a rival to it. Sixteen
  is two multiplied by itself four times, so every hex symbol stands for
  exactly four binary places with no ragged edge — which is the entire
  reason it exists.
ROOT: this file / the base is a choice of bundle — and a base that is a
  power of another translates place by place with no arithmetic at all.
THREAD: computing (colours written as six hex digits, two per channel),
  memory addresses, printing (any nested notation with a nested unit).
ASKED-AS: hexadecimal why sixteen colour codes memory address letters binary shorthand digits

ESSENCE: on a wheel of remainders, adding, taking away and multiplying all
  survive — do them before or after the wrap and the answers agree.
  Dividing does not. On the twelve-wheel, six times two and six times ten
  both land on nothing.
ROOT: mathematics / numbers wrap on a dial — the wrap respects every
  operation built out of adding, and division is not one of them.
THREAD: computing (hashing and checksums live on the wheel), music
  (transposing a tune is addition on the pitch wheel), calendars (what day
  of the week a date falls on).
ASKED-AS: clock arithmetic multiply divide remainder wheel modulo prime why no answer

ESSENCE: a check digit is a remainder deliberately bolted on so that a
  mistyped number lands in the wrong place on the wheel and gets refused.
  Bank accounts, book numbers and barcodes all carry one, which is why a
  single wrong keystroke usually bounces instead of paying a stranger.
ROOT: this file / the wheel keeps adding and multiplying — so a weighted
  sum of the digits has a predictable remainder that any change disturbs.
THREAD: computing (checksums and error-correcting codes are this grown
  large), money (card and account numbers), logistics (a barcode scanner
  refusing a smudge).
ASKED-AS: check digit barcode card number typo caught invalid account rejected weights

ESSENCE: reduce every number in a calculation to its digit sum, do the
  same arithmetic on those small numbers, and compare. A disagreement
  proves the working wrong. An agreement proves nothing — and that
  asymmetry is what a check is.
ROOT: mathematics / a number and its digit sum agree on the nine-wheel —
  the check is that agreement run across a whole calculation instead of a
  single number.
THREAD: computing (a passing test suite proves no correctness), science (a
  survived prediction is not a proof), medicine (a normal result that
  rules out far less than it appears to).
RULE: to check an arithmetic answer this way — add each number's digits,
  and keep adding until a single digit remains; do the same operation on
  those single digits and reduce again; do the same to your answer; if the
  two single digits differ the working is wrong; if they match, check
  again by another route.

ESSENCE: putting numbers on a line turns bigger into further right and

ROOT: premise — the numbers carry a complete order, so they can be laid in

THREAD: measuring (a ruler is a number line with a job), time (a

ASKED-AS: number line order bigger smaller left right negative stretch flip picture

ESSENCE: between any two fractions there is another — average them and you
  are already between. So the fractions leave no gap you can point at, and

ROOT: mathematics / some infinities are larger — the fractions list off
  one by one, the line's points refuse to.
THREAD: physics (whether space itself is grainy or smooth, still

ASKED-AS: between two fractions another next number smallest gap dense holes line points

ESSENCE: absolute value throws away the sign and keeps the distance from
  zero. Its real use is that the distance between any two numbers is the
  size of their difference — which is why it turns up wherever "how far

ROOT: this file / the number line — a distance is a length, and lengths do

THREAD: engineering (a tolerance stated as a distance from target), money

ASKED-AS: absolute value bars distance from zero negative sign removed how far off

ESSENCE: an inequality survives adding or taking away anything at all, and
  survives multiplying by a positive. Multiply or divide both sides by a

  whole line, and left and right change places along with it.
ROOT: this file / multiplying by a negative flips the line — the order is

THREAD: money (a debt comparison reversing when read as owed instead of
  held), engineering (a limit reversing when the quantity is redefined as
  a shortfall), law (a threshold written as at least, or as no more than).
ASKED-AS: inequality flip sign multiply negative divide reverse solve less greater rules

ESSENCE: a decimal that repeats forever is not an approximation — it is a
  fraction in disguise, and the fraction can be recovered exactly. This is
  why nought point nine recurring is not nearly one; it is one, the same
  number written the long way round.

  away from that, which cancels the endless tail exactly; what is left is
  a plain equation, and one division gives the fraction.
