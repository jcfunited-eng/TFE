# 87 DEEP EVOLUTION AND ECOLOGY — why living things are the shapes they are
Middle floors under the shapes: the arithmetic that makes bodies,
and the accounting that decides who lives where and with whom.
Every entry carries its cannot-twin.

ESSENCE: the engine has three parts and needs all three — things
  vary, offspring resemble their parents, and some leave more
  copies than others. Nothing about life is required for it: the
  same arithmetic runs in a kennel, a market, or a folder of copied
  files. Life is simply where it has run longest.
ROOT: mathematics / small differences that are inherited and
  counted over and over compound into large ones.
THREAD: money (products bred by which ones sold), language (words
  kept by use), computing (programs bred rather than written, on
  purpose, by this exact recipe).
ASKED-AS: evolution natural selection darwin variation inherited survive offspring breeding change generations works

ESSENCE: fitness is not strength, cleverness or complexity. It is a
  count of surviving offspring, here, now. A trait is fit the way a
  key is fit — to one lock. Change the lock and the champion turns
  liability: the drug-proof bacterium loses to its plain cousins
  the week the drug stops.
ROOT: the living world / the process counts results in one place at
  one time and has no other measure available to it.
THREAD: sports (a record is partly the field it was set against),
  money (a business model fitted to one decade), strategy (an army
  built for the last war).
ASKED-AS: survival fittest strongest better evolved advanced primitive adapted environment changed advantage useless

ESSENCE: the process never sees a design — only a body that lived
  or did not. It gets no report of why, so it cannot learn a
  lesson, only keep a result. A brilliant idea implemented badly
  dies; a poor idea that happens to work is kept and built on for a
  hundred million years.
ROOT: the living world / selection reads outcomes and never
  reasons.
THREAD: money (markets price outcomes, not reasoning), teaching
  (grading only the answer breeds the same wrong methods), evidence
  (why the record shows repetition, not a ladder).
ASKED-AS: evolution random purpose designed intelligent goal stupid bad design mistakes nature blind pointless

ESSENCE: nothing can be built for a use it does not yet have —
  every step must pay for itself the day it appears. So new
  equipment is almost always old equipment repurposed: jaw bones
  became ear bones, feathers kept warmth before they caught air, a
  lung began as a swim bladder. Half a wing is not half a flight;
  it is a whole something else.
ROOT: the living world / a step that is worse now for better later
  cannot be taken, because the worse-now version is the one that
  dies.
THREAD: engineering (a bridge rebuilt while the traffic runs),
  money (no firm survives a decade of losses for a later payoff),
  building (barns become houses; almost nobody demolishes).
ASKED-AS: half an eye wing useless intermediate transition fossil purpose future planned steps missing link

ESSENCE: one budget, spent once. Energy put into growing is not
  there for breeding, defense or repair, and the choice is forced
  every day of a life. Thousands of cheap young and no care, or two
  expensive ones and years of it — both work, and the middle
  usually does not.
ROOT: physics / energy is never created, so a living thing's income
  is finite and its accounts are joined.
THREAD: money (return against safety in one portfolio), farming (a
  cow bred for milk gives up meat and hardiness together), sports
  (training for one event costs you the other).
ASKED-AS: tradeoff perfect strong fast babies eggs turtle elephant cost energy limited why not

ESSENCE: keeping a trait costs, so anything that stops earning is
  actively removed, not merely forgotten. Cave fish lose their
  eyes, island birds lose flight, and our own ancestors lost the
  ability to make vitamin C while fruit was always in reach. Losing
  is a saving, and selection collects it.
ROOT: the living world / selection keeps whatever leaves more
  copies, and not paying for an idle organ leaves more copies.
THREAD: money (a division that stops earning is closed, not
  mothballed), language (sounds drop out once they stop
  distinguishing anything), craft (a technique unused is gone in
  one generation).
ASKED-AS: cave fish blind eyes lost flightless kiwi appendix vestigial useless organs tail leftover

ESSENCE: when two lineages improve against each other, both spend
  more and neither gains — the deer gets faster, the wolf gets
  faster, the chase stays even. The score in such a race is never
  absolute ability, only the gap. And the sides are unequal in
  motive: the rabbit runs for its life, the fox only for dinner.
ROOT: the living world / selection acts on whatever is currently
  costing offspring, and a rival who also inherits keeps moving the
  target.
THREAD: strategy (armour and weapons outbidding each other),
  computing (spam and filters, locks and picks), money (an edge
  everyone copies stops paying and still costs).
ASKED-AS: predator prey speed cheetah gazelle poison resistance venom shells escalation defenses race

ESSENCE: a mimic wears a reputation it never paid for — the
  harmless fly in wasp colours is charged nothing for the sting
  that teaches the lesson. Which is why it works only while fakes
  stay rare: let the copies outnumber the originals and the lesson
  stops being learned, and the disguise stops protecting anybody.
ROOT: the living world / a signal carries information only while it
  is mostly honest.
THREAD: money (counterfeits work while rare and kill the currency
  when common), persuasion (a trusted format borrowed by a scam),
  law (a forged seal needs real seals to mean something).
ASKED-AS: hoverfly wasp stripes harmless snake coral fake warning colors copy poisonous pretend

ESSENCE: camouflage does not copy a thing, it defeats a method.
  Eyes find edges and match shapes against memory, so the counters
  are: break the outline with patterns that cross it, cancel the
  shading that betrays roundness — dark back, pale belly, undoing
  the sun — and above all hold still. Motion beats every coat ever
  grown.
ROOT: light + seeing / an eye works by edges and shading, so hiding
  is an attack on those two and barely on colour at all.
THREAD: war (dazzle paint and disruptive patterns, borrowed
  directly), image + design (a logo is the opposite craft — an
  outline made unmissable), survival (stillness before cover).
ASKED-AS: camouflage blend stick insect chameleon countershading belly snow hare hiding spots stripes freeze

ESSENCE: a display anything could produce proves nothing, so the
  ones that carry information are the ones a weak animal cannot
  afford: the tail that spoils flight, the antlers regrown every
  year, the song that costs a night's feeding. The waste is not a
  side effect. The waste is the guarantee.
ROOT: money / a promise is worth exactly what it would cost to
  fake.
THREAD: money (an expensive shop front says solvent out loud),
  persuasion (the guarantee that would ruin a liar), people
  together (gifts that hurt to give are the ones read as sincere).
ASKED-AS: peacock tail antlers attract mates showing off birdsong colors males display expensive useless

ESSENCE: your instructions are not only in you — a brother carries
  about half of them, a cousin an eighth. So a body that spends
  itself on relatives can leave more copies than one that does not,
  and the sums come out: help when the gain to them, discounted by
  closeness, beats what it costs you. The sterile worker bee is no
  puzzle; her sisters are her copies.
ROOT: the living world / what gets counted is copies of the
  instruction, not lives of the individual carrying it.
THREAD: money (a family firm taking terms no stranger would), law
  (inheritance rules track this arithmetic closely), people
  together (the pull that thins with each step of kinship).
ASKED-AS: altruism family sacrifice bees ants sterile workers mothers relatives helping siblings selfish

ESSENCE: cooperation is stable exactly where defecting is made
  expensive, and there are only a few ways to make it so: be able
  to leave a bad partner, meet again so today's cheat is tomorrow's
  cost, be locked together so neither can walk away, or punish.
  Find a partnership that has lasted and one of the four is there.
ROOT: the living world / cheating pays in the short run, so
  anything long-lasting has something absorbing that pressure.
THREAD: law (a contract is enforcement imported from outside),
  money (deposits, escrow and ratings are all the same fix), people
  together (small towns run on meeting again).
ASKED-AS: cooperation trust cheating partners fig wasp cleaner fish reciprocity punishment reputation strangers repeat

ESSENCE: where cooperation pays, cheating pays more — take the
  benefit, skip the cost. So detection gets built, and detection
  costs too, and the arms race moves inside the relationship:
  better fakes, better checks. Signals drift toward being hard to
  fake rather than easy to read.
ROOT: mathematics / a strategy everyone adopts stops paying, so
  cheats and checkers settle into a mixture and never at zero.
THREAD: money (audits exist only where they pay for themselves),
  computing (spam scores, captchas, fraud flags), law (a penalty
  for perjury is a price on one lie).
ASKED-AS: cheating detect lying cuckoo eggs freeloading audit catch dishonest signals mixture equilibrium

ESSENCE: a parasite's problem is arithmetic, not cruelty — it must
  not wreck the host faster than it can reach the next one. So how
  nasty a thing becomes is set by how it travels: spread by
  handshakes between people who must stay on their feet, it stays
  mild; spread by water, insects or crowded wards, it can afford to
  flatten you.
ROOT: the living world / a trait's fate is set by copies passed on,
  and killing early cuts the passing on — unless the route does not
  need you walking.
THREAD: medicine (clean water made cholera unaffordable to itself),
  farming (crowding in a shed pays for savagery), computing (a worm
  that crashes hosts too fast dies out).
ASKED-AS: parasites tapeworm fleas malaria mosquito deadly mild cholera crowding host spread nasty

ESSENCE: a partnership that lasts long enough stops being two
  organisms. Each side drops what the other supplies — paying for
  what a partner provides is waste — and one day neither can live
  alone. Your cells' power plants were free bacteria once; the
  termite does not digest wood, its tenants do; a lichen is a
  fungus farming an alga.
ROOT: the living world / any ability that stops earning is dropped,
  and a reliable partner is what makes an ability stop earning.
THREAD: money (a supplier you can no longer replace is not really a
  supplier), engineering (systems grown around each other until
  neither runs alone), people together (long marriages divide the
  skills until neither half runs a household).
ASKED-AS: symbiosis lichen gut bacteria mitochondria termites coral algae partnership dependent together survive

ESSENCE: two species chasing the same living are decided by
  whichever single thing runs shortest, not by everything at once.
  Whoever can still make a living at the lowest level of that one
  thing wins — slowly and completely. The escape is to split it:
  one tree fed five warblers, each working a different band of
  branches.
ROOT: the living world / the losing line thins toward nothing
  wherever the overlap is exact.
THREAD: money (two shops on one street at one price), sports
  (roster spots at the one scarce position), people together (a
  newcomer takes an unheld role or contests a held one).
ASKED-AS: competition niche habitat overlap coexist exclusion warblers barnacles specialists divide resources scarce

ESSENCE: each eating step throws away about nine tenths of what it
  takes — not to waste but to heat, movement, and the work of
  staying alive long enough to eat again. That one number writes
  the shape of everything: chains four or five links long and never
  twenty, and top predators rare, far-ranging, and first to vanish.
ROOT: physics / disorder wins — every transfer of energy pays a tax
  in heat, and living transfers pay a heavy one.
THREAD: money (a chain of middlemen each taking a cut), farming
  (grain feeds far more people than the beef it could become),
  engineering (efficiency multiplies downward through stages).
ASKED-AS: food chain pyramid predators rare top energy lost eating meat trophic levels biomass

ESSENCE: two kinds of limit exist and only one can hold a number
  steady. Crowd-sensitive brakes — food, disease, territory — press
  harder as the crowd grows and settle a population near a level.
  Crowd-blind blows — a frost, a flood, a fire — knock it down
  regardless of how many there were, and can never regulate
  anything.
ROOT: engineering / feedback — a correction that scales with the
  error is a governor; one that ignores the error is only a shock.
THREAD: money (rates as a governor, a crash as a shock), farming
  (the pasture's honest number), people together (what actually
  caps a town is rarely the obvious thing).
ASKED-AS: population limit carrying capacity overpopulation deer starvation crash weather nesting water stable bottleneck

ESSENCE: cycles are the signature of a delay. Predators keep
  breeding for a season after the prey has crashed; the prey
  recovers before the predators have thinned. So the numbers swing
  past each other forever instead of settling. Nothing is broken —
  a lag with a strong response simply cannot come to rest.
ROOT: engineering / a correction that arrives late overshoots, and
  the harder the correction the wider the overshoot.
THREAD: money (the pig cycle and the property cycle, same shape
  from the same lag), the body (blood sugar hunting its target),
  driving (the phantom traffic jam on an empty road).
ASKED-AS: boom bust cycles lemmings hare lynx locust plague crash overshoot swings years repeat

ESSENCE: influence is not weight. Remove a species that is a
  hundredth of the living matter and the whole place reorganises;
  remove another that is a third and little changes. The heavy ones
  are often just heavy. The ones that matter sit on a control point
  — holding down a would-be dominator, building the structure
  others live in, or hauling nutrients across a border.
ROOT: engineering / in any connected system a few parts are
  load-bearing and most are not, and size is no guide to which.
THREAD: computing (one small library holding up an industry),
  people together (the quiet person whose leaving breaks a team),
  money (a small market whose failure freezes a large one).
ASKED-AS: keystone species otters urchins wolves yellowstone beavers remove collapse important ripple small

ESSENCE: after a fire or a flood, rebuilding runs in order, because
  each wave changes the ground for the next. Fast cheap colonists
  that grow anywhere make soil and shade — and that shade is
  exactly what lets slower, taller, longer-lived species take the
  site away from them. Every stage destroys its own advantage.
ROOT: the living world / an organism alters the place it lives in,
  and the altered place suits somebody else.
THREAD: building (clearance, then foundations, then finishing),
  people together (a rough district gentrified by the people the
  roughness drew in), money (wildcatters making a market safe for
  the firms that displace them).
ASKED-AS: forest regrow after fire weeds pioneer clearing old growth meadow logging recovery stages

ESSENCE: isolation takes away the neighbours that were holding a
  shape in place, and shapes drift strangely. No ground predators,
  so flight — expensive — is dropped and the birds walk. Few
  rivals, so one arriving finch's descendants split into every
  empty job. The size rules invert: big animals shrink on thin
  food, small ones swell with nothing hunting them.
ROOT: the living world / a trait is pinned in place by whatever
  would punish its loss, and an island removes the punishers.
THREAD: language (isolated dialects go strange fast), craft (a
  valley's technique with no outside check), computing (a codebase
  with no outside users grows unshareable).
ASKED-AS: islands dodo galapagos finches flightless giant tortoise dwarf elephants isolated unique tame

ESSENCE: ending is the ordinary fate. Nearly every species that has
  ever lived is gone, most after a few million years, in a steady
  drizzle broken by rare catastrophes. Species are mortal the way
  individuals are, and what the fossil record actually shows is
  turnover, not a climb.
ROOT: the living world / fitness is local, and every local
  eventually changes past what a lineage can track.
THREAD: money (firm lifespans draw the same curve), craft (trades
  die when their setting does), keeping knowledge (which texts
  survive is mostly luck too).
ASKED-AS: extinction normal background rate dinosaurs passenger pigeon endangered gone forever specialists rare

ESSENCE: the same problem in the same physics gets the same answer
  from strangers. Fish, ichthyosaur and dolphin arrived at one body
  from three directions; eyes have been invented many separate
  times; the crab shape keeps reappearing in unrelated lines.
  Workable solutions are few, so resemblance is weak evidence of
  kinship.
ROOT: physics / the world sets the problem, and a problem with few
  good answers gets those answers over and over.
THREAD: engineering (unrelated firms converge on one design when
  the physics is tight), language (unrelated tongues invent the
  same grammar tricks), craft (the same knot found on every coast).
ASKED-AS: dolphin shark similar wings bats birds insects eyes evolved separately crabs lookalike

ESSENCE: big animals live slow. Slower heart, less fuel per gram,
  first young later, litters smaller, life longer — it scales
  together closely enough that a mouse and an elephant spend a
  similar number of heartbeats. Size buys safety from teeth and
  sells it straight back against anything faster than a lifetime.
ROOT: mathematics / surface grows slower than volume, and a body's
  rates follow its surfaces.
THREAD: money (large firms move slowly for the same structural
  reason), engineering (big machines have long maintenance cycles),
  oceans (why fishing strips the big and slow first).
ASKED-AS: elephant mouse heartbeat lifespan big animals live longer slow breeding recovery whales metabolism

ESSENCE: migration exists because the best place moves. Two
  half-year homes plus the fare beats one year-round home, but only
  where the seasons differ enormously — and the route is a chain of
  particular stops. Drain one marsh in the middle and a bird
  protected at both ends still dies.
ROOT: astronomy / the tilted planet carries warmth and food around
  the year, and travelling is sometimes cheaper than staying.
THREAD: movement of goods (a supply chain fails at one port),
  farming (a calendar that will not be argued with), people
  together (seasonal work follows the same map).
ASKED-AS: migration birds geese monarch butterflies wildebeest salmon route stopover winter instinct far

ESSENCE: a living thing cannot wait for the weather. Buds take
  weeks, young take months, so preparing must start before the
  conditions arrive — which is why bodies read the one signal the
  planet cannot break: day length. It works until warmth and
  daylight stop moving together, and then a perfectly honest cue
  gives a wrong answer.
ROOT: astronomy / the tilt makes day length exactly repeatable
  while weather is not, so reliability beats relevance.
THREAD: farming (planting by the almanac, not the thermometer),
  money (an indicator that stops tracking what it once predicted),
  the body (a clock that runs on light, not on tiredness).
ASKED-AS: seasons timing early spring flowering blossom hibernation daylight cue mismatch warm winter

ESSENCE: what a breeder actually selects for is calm and tolerance
  of crowding — and the rest arrives uninvited. Foxes bred for
  nothing but tameness came out with floppy ears, patched coats,
  curled tails and year-round breeding within decades. Traits
  travel in bundles, and the bundle is not negotiable.
ROOT: heredity / one instruction touches many things at once, so
  choosing on one trait drags its whole package along.
THREAD: farming (every crop is a wild thing bent by chosen
  parenthood), teaching (grading one measure quietly breeds the
  rest), money (a metric optimised is a metric that drags others).
ASKED-AS: domestication dogs wolves foxes tame breeding corn maize floppy ears cattle package

ESSENCE: what a creature is depends on what was holding it down.
  Move it and its parasites, predators and rivals stay behind while
  its weapons come along — so it is not the same animal in the new
  place. And the locals have never met it, so no defense was ever
  built, because nothing there ever needed one.
ROOT: the living world / arms races are local, and a newcomer
  arrives mid-race against opponents who were never running.
THREAD: money (a strategy that worked in one market, unhedged in
  another), computing (a bug loose in a network with no immunity),
  law (a rule imported without the customs that restrained it).
ASKED-AS: invasive species kudzu rabbits australia cane toads mussels predators native wreck introduced

ESSENCE: variety steadies a system for two dull reasons: different
  species fail under different conditions, so somebody is still
  working in every bad year, and several doing one job means the
  job survives losing one of them. It is insurance, not virtue —
  and the premium is real, because a mixed field yields less in a
  good year than the best single crop would.
ROOT: mathematics / independent risks pooled swing less than any
  one of them alone.
THREAD: money (a portfolio, exactly), farming (mixed planting caps
  any loss at a slice), computing (one program everywhere catches
  one cold).
ASKED-AS: biodiversity why matters stability monoculture crops resilience insurance variety redundancy backup collapse

ESSENCE: the engine is slow and the road is long. A change far too
  small to see in a lifetime — a fraction of a percent in a century
  — is a total rebuild over a million years, and there have been
  thousands of millions of those. Nobody has ever watched the
  process work, because nobody has ever seen enough of it.
ROOT: mathematics / compounding — a rate that looks like nothing is
  decided entirely by how long it is allowed to run.
THREAD: money (a few percent compounded over centuries), earth
  (mountains built at the speed a fingernail grows), keeping
  knowledge (records were invented to see slow things at all).
ASKED-AS: millions years how long evolution slow time scale fossils old earth generations

ESSENCE: not everything is for something. In a small population a
  trait can spread or vanish on pure luck — the wrong ten died in a
  storm — and most changes in the code are neither useful nor
  harmful, just carried along. Asking what a feature is for
  sometimes has no answer, and the honest reply is nothing.
ROOT: chance + risk / with small numbers luck outweighs advantage;
  with large numbers it does not.
THREAD: money (a small fund's record is mostly luck, a large one's
  is not), story (we invent reasons for outcomes that had none),
  evidence (a plausible explanation is the cheapest thing there is).
ASKED-AS: genetic drift random chance small population bottleneck founder neutral useless traits purpose luck

ESSENCE: what holds a species together is breeding across it — each
  mating mixes the pot and undoes local difference. So making two
  species out of one is really about stopping the mixing: a
  mountain rises, a river shifts, a habit or a mating season
  diverges, and the halves drift apart until the mixing could not
  resume even if the barrier fell.
ROOT: the living world / inheritance blends lineages that meet, so
  difference accumulates only where the meeting stops.
THREAD: language (dialects become languages when the talking
  stops), people together (a group split by distance grows customs
  that will not merge back), computing (a fork past merging).
ASKED-AS: new species how form separate island river mountain interbreed mule hybrid barrier split

ESSENCE: the line around a species is real in the middle and
  invented at the edges. Ring a mountain range with populations and
  each breeds happily with its neighbour while the two ends,
  meeting at last, cannot. Hybrids happen constantly, and much of
  what people carry came from a lineage the textbooks file as a
  different species.
ROOT: the living world / splitting is gradual, so a category with a
  hard edge is being laid over a process that has none.
THREAD: language (where a dialect becomes a language is politics,
  not linguistics), law (every legal line runs through a gradient),
  mathematics (a threshold imposed on a continuum).
ASKED-AS: species definition hybrid mule liger neanderthal interbreed dog wolf same different ring

ESSENCE: what spreads is whatever copies itself best, and that need
  not be good for the creature carrying it. Long stretches of the
  code do nothing but reproduce themselves inside the library, and
  some elements cheat their way into more than half the offspring
  while damaging the body they ride in.
ROOT: the living world / the count is copies of the instruction,
  not the health of the carrier — the body is the vehicle, not the
  point of the exercise.
THREAD: computing (a chain letter needs no benefit to spread, only
  a copier), money (a business that grows while destroying value),
  people together (an idea that spreads because it spreads well).
ASKED-AS: selfish gene junk dna jumping genes viruses genome cheat spread harmful copies

ESSENCE: relatives share half, not all — so their interests overlap
  and never coincide, and the gap is exactly where the fighting
  happens. An unborn child pulls for more than the mother should
  give, a weaned infant screams for more than the parent will
  spend, and each parent would rather the other did the carrying.
ROOT: the living world / kin arithmetic — sharing half means
  valuing the other's welfare at half, which is a great deal and is
  not the same as fully.
THREAD: money (partners aligned at ninety percent still fight over
  the ten), law (family disputes are the commonest kind for exactly
  this reason), people together (the closest ties carry the
  sharpest quarrels).
ASKED-AS: pregnancy complications weaning tantrums sibling rivalry parents conflict family fighting nursing demanding

ESSENCE: a trait that costs the individual and helps the group
  loses — because it is being removed inside the group every
  generation while groups compete only rarely, and the faster
  process wins. So "for the good of the species" almost never
  explains anything: lemmings do not leap to spare the food, and
  predators do not spare prey out of prudence.
ROOT: mathematics / when two selections run at different speeds on
  the same trait, the quick one sets the outcome.
THREAD: money (the shared pasture everyone overgrazes), power (an
  agreement without enforcement decays at the speed of its fastest
  defector), farming (fishermen who all know the shoal is finishing).
ASKED-AS: good of the species lemmings nature balance restraint predators prey harmony myth altruism

ESSENCE: being unusual can itself be the advantage. A hunter builds
  a picture of the common prey and skips whatever does not match,
  so an odd colour or an odd shape slips past for that reason
  alone. The rare form spreads until it stops being rare — then
  loses, which is why so many populations hold several forms at
  once instead of settling on one.
ROOT: mathematics / an advantage that shrinks as a strategy spreads
  cannot take over, so it settles into a mixture.
THREAD: money (a trade everyone crowds into stops working), sports
  (a tactic beaten once it becomes the expected one), strategy
  (surprise is spent by being used).
ASKED-AS: rare colors morphs snails predators search image variety mixed population common advantage

ESSENCE: some species do not live in a habitat — they are one. A
  coral is a city its own bodies built, a beaver makes the pond, an
  earthworm makes the soil, a wood makes the shade and damp that
  everything beneath it needs. Remove the builder and the structure
  does not simply stand there: it decays, and takes its tenants
  down with it.
ROOT: the living world / an organism alters the place it lives in,
  and a few alter it so thoroughly that the alteration becomes the
  place.
THREAD: building (a city is maintained daily or it is ruins),
  people together (an institution is the people doing it, never the
  building), craft (a workshop dies with the last person keeping it).
ASKED-AS: coral reef beavers dams earthworms soil trees forest shade habitat builders bleaching

ESSENCE: two flows, two shapes. Energy makes one pass — in from the
  sun, down through the eaters, out as heat, gone. Atoms go round
  and round forever: the carbon in you has been in a dinosaur, a
  limestone cliff and a leaf. That is why a sealed jar of life
  still needs a window, and why nothing ever needs a delivery of
  atoms.
ROOT: physics / energy is never created and disorder wins, while
  matter is never used up, only rearranged — two laws, two shapes
  of flow.
THREAD: money (cash circulates while purchasing power is consumed),
  cleaning (dirt is relocated, never destroyed), farming (the
  return counter that closes the loop).
ASKED-AS: nutrient cycle carbon water recycling energy flow sunlight heat lost atoms reused

ESSENCE: pour fertiliser into a lake and it does not get richer, it
  gets simpler. A few fast species explode, shade out the rest, die
  all at once, and the rot eats the oxygen — leaving a dead-bottomed
  pond where a varied one stood. More of a good ingredient does not
  scale a system up; it changes which system you have.
ROOT: the living world / whatever grows fastest takes the surplus,
  and speed and variety are held by different species.
THREAD: money (cheap money inflating whichever asset moves
  quickest), farming (fertiliser runoff, the same event one field
  downstream), people together (a windfall that reorganises a town
  badly).
ASKED-AS: algae bloom lake dead zone fertilizer runoff pond oxygen fish kill green

ESSENCE: a species does not live in a place — it lives in a range
  of conditions that happens to be there this century. Warm the
  world and those conditions walk uphill and toward the poles, and
  everything must walk with them. Whatever cannot move as fast as
  its conditions move runs out of map.
ROOT: the living world / fitness is local, and the local is a set
  of numbers rather than a patch of ground.
THREAD: farming (crop belts sliding across a map), people together
  (settlements built for a climate that is leaving), money (an
  industry whose conditions moved away from under it).
ASKED-AS: climate change species moving north uphill range shift coral bleaching polar bears adapt

ESSENCE: cut a wood in half and you do not get two woods. A
  fragment is mostly edge, and edge is a different world — drier,
  brighter, windier, and walked by everything that hunts along
  boundaries. Deep-forest species need interior, and a small piece
  has almost none, however much area it still shows on the map.
ROOT: mathematics / cutting multiplies perimeter while dividing
  area — the same law that makes small things all-surface.
THREAD: building (many small rooms lose floor to walls), farming
  (hedgerows and field margins are this geometry at work),
  computing (work split across machines pays at every boundary).
ASKED-AS: habitat fragmentation forest patches roads edge effects corridors reserves size wildlife isolated

ESSENCE: the bill arrives long after the deed. Cut a habitat to a
  fragment and its species do not vanish that year — they linger
  for decades as ageing populations that no longer replace
  themselves, and then go, one after another, killed by something
  that finished happening a generation ago.
ROOT: the living world / populations change slowly relative to the
  damage, so the effect of a loss is spread across all the
  lifespans it interrupted.
