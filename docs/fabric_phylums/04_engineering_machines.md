# 04 ENGINEERING AND MACHINES — physics with a budget
No new laws here. Engineering is physics forced to choose —
strength or lightness, speed or accuracy, cost or lifetime — and
every machine is a set of trades signed in metal.

ESSENCE: a machine trades distance for force — the long end of the
  lever moves far and easy so the short end can push hard; the
  product never grows. (force × distance in = force × distance
  out, minus friction)
ROOT: physics / energy is never created.
CANNOT: no lever, gear, pulley, or hydraulic multiplies work. The
  jack that lifts a car with one hand makes that hand travel the
  car's rise a hundred times over.
THREAD: the body (forearms are speed levers — muscle moves little,
  hand moves fast), martial arts (joint locks are levers against
  anatomy), bicycles (gears are the trade made shiftable mid-ride).
ASKED-AS: crowbar wheelbarrow seesaw bottle opener wrench screwdriver ramp lifting easier pry handle

ESSENCE: every machine is a chain of energy changing costume —
  fuel to heat to push to spin to spark — and each link keeps only
  part; losses multiply and never refund.
ROOT: physics / disorder wins — every conversion sheds waste heat.
CANNOT: no chain beats its links compounded — five links at 90%
  keep 59%, and no arrangement of lossy links reaches 100. The
  shed heat cannot be un-shed.
THREAD: farming and the body (sun to grain to muscle loses most at
  every meal — why food chains are short), money (middlemen
  compound exactly like lossy links), why engines need radiators.
ASKED-AS: mileage wasted warm hood exhaust electric charging power plant lightbulb generator gasoline

ESSENCE: friction is a tax and a grip — it bleeds every motion
  into heat, and it is also the only reason wheels bite, knots
  hold, brakes brake, and shoes push streets.
ROOT: physics / disorder wins (the tax); surfaces cling and catch
  at every real contact (the grip).
CANNOT: no machine runs forever — friction taxes every cycle. And
  no walking, driving, or stopping without it: on truly
  frictionless ice the wheel spins and the car sits.
THREAD: music (a violin bow is managed friction — stick-slip made
  to sing), cooking (the sear that sticks, then releases), martial
  arts (footwork is friction management), knots and climbing.
ASKED-AS: tires slipping oil grease lubricant squeak skid rubbing sandpaper sliding traction rug

ESSENCE: the wheel converts dragging into rolling — sliding's
  heavy tax becomes rolling's light one — and a bearing is wheels
  inside the wheel, buying the same discount again at the axle.
ROOT: engineering / friction is a tax; rolling contact peels apart
  instead of scraping, so the tax collapses.
CANNOT: rolling cannot be free — the discount is steep, never
  total. And wheels cannot beat soft ground — they dig; the sled
  wins in snow, which is why snow gets runners.
THREAD: the body (joints are bearings — cartilage the race, its
  fluid the oil), skates and skis (the other discount), every
  train, turbine, and hard road ever built.
ASKED-AS: cart suitcase luggage skateboard casters dragging furniture moving dolly smoother squeaky hub

ESSENCE: every design buys a virtue with a virtue — stronger is
  heavier, lighter is weaker, faster wears sooner, cheaper dies
  younger; the engineer's real product is a chosen compromise.
ROOT: premise — materials and budgets are finite; no property is
  free.
CANNOT: no part is best at everything at once. The unbreakable,
  weightless, costless machine cannot exist — past material
  limits, every gain somewhere is a loss somewhere else.
THREAD: flight (a plane is this law made ruthless — every gram
  argued over), the body (bird bones are hollow: same
  ruthlessness), money (risk against return, always).
ASKED-AS: car phone bike laptop thinner tank armor race perfect price durable flimsy

ESSENCE: no part is made exact — every dimension is a target plus
  a permitted miss, and machines work because the misses are
  budgeted to still fit together. (tolerance)
ROOT: premise — all measurement and manufacture scatter; perfect
  is not a size, it is a direction.
CANNOT: no two parts are identical, only interchangeable within
  budget. A fit cannot be zero-gap AND moving — bearings need
  their clearance; demand perfection and parts seize or never mate.
THREAD: cooking ("about a cup" — pastry is tight-tolerance
  cooking), music (tuning tempers small misses so every key
  works), the body (enzymes are tolerant locks — sloppy fit is
  why life runs at all, UNSURE at depth).
ASKED-AS: loose tight wiggle spare replacement screw bolt thread factory precision snug rattle

ESSENCE: a controlled machine watches its own output and steers
  against the error — measure, compare, correct, repeat; the
  thermostat is the whole idea.
ROOT: premise — the world drifts, so commands go stale; only
  measurement ties action to what is actually happening.
CANNOT: no holding a target blind — without measurement, drift
  wins. And correction cannot be both hard and late: overdo it and
  the fix becomes the wobble (overshoot, then oscillation).
THREAD: the body (temperature, balance, blood sugar — you are a
  tower of thermostats), cooking (taste-and-adjust IS the loop),
  markets (prices correcting demand, overshooting into crashes),
  riding a bicycle (a thousand corrections a minute, unfelt).
ASKED-AS: cruise control autopilot furnace air conditioner steering wobble swinging oven shower faucet hunting

ESSENCE: metal dies by repetition — a load far too small to break
  a part once will break it in a million gentle cycles, as an
  invisible crack grows a hair per flex. (fatigue)
ROOT: building / cracks feed on corners — run a million times on a
  microscopic flaw.
CANNOT: a survived test cannot promise a survived lifetime. And
  aluminum never earns a safe threshold — every aluminum wing
  carries a countdown; steel alone, kept below its limit, flexes
  forever.
THREAD: the paperclip bent to death in your fingers, the body
  (runners' stress fractures — bone fatigues too, but heals),
  bridges and airliners retired by cycle count, not by age.
ASKED-AS: bending wire snap broke suddenly cracking metal shaking vibration rattling worn hinge

ESSENCE: engineers assume the worst twice — parts are built
  several times stronger than the biggest expected load (safety
  factor), and critical jobs get standing spares (redundancy).
ROOT: engineering / tolerance, applied to fate — loads, materials,
  and humans all scatter beyond prediction.
CANNOT: no margin covers an unimagined KIND of load — factors
  guard against size of surprise, not species. And spares sharing
  one weakness are one spare: two engines fed by one clogged tank
  drown together.
THREAD: the body (two kidneys, two lungs, a clotting cascade),
  money (reserves and insurance), farming (seed corn held back
  through the hungriest winter).
ASKED-AS: backup spare tire elevator cable overbuilt extra generator parachute belt suspenders overkill

ESSENCE: a system fails at its weakest point, and fails there
  totally while its strong parts stand idle — strength does not
  average; the chain IS its worst link.
ROOT: premise — load finds every path; one path's break dumps its
  share onto the next (the cascade).
CANNOT: strengthening the strong buys nothing. No system is safer
  than its worst single point unless the load is given other roads
  — which is redundancy's whole reason.
THREAD: the body (the ACL tears, never the femur), castles (walls
  fall at gates), computing (one bad line downs the program),
  money (the run starts at the weakest bank and spreads).
ASKED-AS: rope fuse bottleneck breaking snapped zipper seam achilles domino collapse spot failure
