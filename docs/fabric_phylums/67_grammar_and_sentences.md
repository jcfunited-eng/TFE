# 67 GRAMMAR AND SENTENCES — the rules that let words combine
Words alone are a list. Grammar is the joinery that turns a list
into a claim somebody can agree with or deny.

ESSENCE: a sentence needs a doer and a doing — "the kettle" is a
  nudge and "boils" is a nudge, but "the kettle boils" is a claim
  you can agree with or call a lie.
ROOT: this file / a sentence says something about something.
THREAD: mathematics (11: the equals sign is arithmetic's verb),
  law (17: a charge must state what the person actually did).

ESSENCE: in English, position says who did what to whom — "the dog
  bit the man" and "the man bit the dog" use identical words and
  report two very different days.
ROOT: language (09) / order carries meaning; with sounds arbitrary,
  position is the cheapest place to store roles.
THREAD: chemistry (03: same atoms, different arrangement, different
  substance), cooking (02: eggs before or after the heat).

ESSENCE: doer and doing must match in number and person — one bird
  sings, two birds sing; the verb quietly repeats what the doer has

ESSENCE: tense sets when — walked, walks, will walk are one action

ESSENCE: pronouns are pointers — he, she, it, this, that borrow all

ESSENCE: the same parts build three different machines — "you are

ESSENCE: register is the clothing on the same content — "we regret

ESSENCE: three rules carry most of clarity — one idea per sentence,
  the actor before the action, the known before the new; each hands
  the reader something to stand on before asking them to step.
ROOT: the mind (20) / working memory is small, and a sentence
  holding too much open at once is simply dropped.
THREAD: teaching (30: build on the known), development methods (60:
  order is part of the procedure, not decoration).
