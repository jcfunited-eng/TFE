# 171 WORDS THAT CARRY THE QUESTION — what a sentence is actually asking
Two things are happening in every question: something is being asked
about, and something is being asked for. The asking words say which
kind of answer will count — a cause, a method, a name, a time — and
they almost never name the subject. This file separates the two, so a
sentence can be read for what it wants as well as what it mentions.

ESSENCE: why asks for what produced the thing. The answer is not a
  description and not a method — it names something earlier that
  made this happen, and a reply that does not point backwards has
  not answered, however true it is.
ROOT: evidence (36) / a cause is the claim that changing one thing
  would have changed the other.
CANNOT: no answer to why without something earlier to point at — a
  why answered by restating the thing is a refusal in a reply's coat.
THREAD: physics (01: mechanism is the honest form of a cause),
  teaching (30: because it just is teaches nothing).
MEANS-SAME: why reason cause because behind purpose explain how come what for
ASKED-AS: why does this happen reason cause behind explain what for

ESSENCE: how asks for a method — the ordered steps that get from
  here to there. A true statement about the thing is not a method; a
  method has a first move, and if the answer cannot be started, it
  is not an answer to how.
ROOT: craft (29) / a procedure is knowledge in the order the hands
  need it.
CANNOT: no method without a first step that can be taken now — an
  answer to how that begins in the middle has skipped the part that
  was being asked for.
THREAD: teaching (30: showing beats telling because order is the
  content), computing (19: an algorithm is a how with no gaps).
MEANS-SAME: how method steps procedure process approach way to instructions manner
ASKED-AS: how do i steps method way to procedure process instructions approach

ESSENCE: what asks for a naming — put this thing in a family and say
  what marks it off from its neighbours. It is the only asking word
  whose answer may contain no verbs, no times and no people, only a
  place in the web of kinds.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / a definition is a family plus the
  difference.
CANNOT: no naming without a family to sit in — an answer to what
  that gives only examples has pointed instead of naming.
THREAD: living world (31: the tree of life is one long answer to
  what), law (17: the definitions clause decides the case).
MEANS-SAME: what define definition meaning is a kind of called named identify
ASKED-AS: what is a define meaning called kind of thing identify

ESSENCE: who asks for a person or a body of people, and the answer
  must be nameable — a role, a name, an office. An answer naming a
  cause or a process has changed the question, and usually to
  something easier.
ROOT: people together (21) / responsibility attaches to persons and
  offices, never to conditions.
CANNOT: no who answered by a process — "the system failed" names
  nobody, which is why it is the commonest way to dodge the word.
THREAD: law (17: liability needs a party), power (24: an office
  survives the person holding it).
MEANS-SAME: who whom whose person people responsible name owner author which one
ASKED-AS: who is responsible person name whose owner author which one blame

ESSENCE: when asks for a position in time, and it has two shapes —
  a point (the day, the hour) and a condition (as soon as the paint
  is dry). Both are answers; a duration is not, because how long is
  a different question.
ROOT: measurement (36) / time is read either off a clock or off an
  event, and the two are not interchangeable.
CANNOT: no when without a clock or an event to hang it on — soon
  answers nothing that a deadline would have answered.
THREAD: logistics (39: lead time and delivery date are different
  promises), farming (14: the season is a when read off the world).
MEANS-SAME: when time date day hour deadline schedule after before as soon
ASKED-AS: when will it date time deadline schedule how soon after before

ESSENCE: where asks for a place, and a place is only an answer if
  the asker can get to it — a location, a container, a page, a
  street. Naming a region so large it contains the asker is a
  non-answer with the shape of one.
ROOT: navigation (12) / a position is only useful against a frame
  the traveller shares.
CANNOT: no where without a frame the asker can stand in — a
  coordinate in an unshared frame directs nobody anywhere.
THREAD: logistics (39: an address is a route in disguise), computing
  (19: a path is a where).
MEANS-SAME: where place location address position situated found which room area
ASKED-AS: where is it located place address position which room find area

ESSENCE: which asks for a choice out of a set that already exists,
  named or implied. It is the only asking word that promises the
  answer is already on the table — so an answer that adds a new
  option has quietly refused the question.
ROOT: strategy (23) / choosing is picking from a made list, and
  making the list is a different act.
CANNOT: no which without a set to choose from — where nothing has
  been listed, which collapses into what.
THREAD: design (28: options are authored before they are chosen),
  chance (35: a choice needs the field of outcomes stated).
MEANS-SAME: which one choose choice option alternative between pick select prefer
ASKED-AS: which one should i choose option alternative between pick better select

ESSENCE: can and could ask whether a thing is possible — and
  possible has three separate meanings that the word does not
  distinguish: is it allowed, is it within someone's power, and does
  anything forbid it.
ROOT: right and wrong (25) / permission, ability and physical
  possibility are three different walls.
CANNOT: no possibility answered without saying which wall is meant —
  "you can't" from a rule and "you can't" from physics are different
  sentences and different fixes.
THREAD: law (17: may is permission and can is ability), physics
  (01: conservation laws are the only walls that never move).
MEANS-SAME: can could able possible allowed permitted capable feasible manage might
ASKED-AS: can i could it possible allowed permitted able capable feasible

ESSENCE: should and ought ask for a judgement, which means the
  answer needs a standard. Any reply is worthless unless it names
  what the answer is good for — cheapest, safest, quickest, kindest
  — because those four point four different ways.
ROOT: right and wrong (25) / a judgement is a comparison against a
  standard, and the standard is the argument.
CANNOT: no should without a standard behind it — an ought with the
  goal left out is a preference dressed as a conclusion.
THREAD: persuasion (22: hidden standards are how advice smuggles),
  design (28: fit for purpose is the only honest praise).
MEANS-SAME: should ought better best advisable recommend worth supposed to right thing
ASKED-AS: should i ought best better recommend advisable worth doing right thing

ESSENCE: how much and how many ask for a quantity, and the answer
  needs a unit or the number means nothing. Many splits from much on
  whether the thing can be counted, and that split alone tells you
  what kind of thing is being asked about.
ROOT: measurement (36) / a number without a unit is not a
  measurement, only a digit.
CANNOT: no quantity without a unit — twelve is not an answer to how
  heavy, how long, or how much it costs.
THREAD: money (16: a price is a number with a currency), cooking
  (02: a cup and a gram measure different properties).
MEANS-SAME: how much many quantity amount number count total cost weight size
ASKED-AS: how much many amount number quantity total cost weight size

ESSENCE: is, are, does and did ask for a verdict — yes or no. They
  carry almost no subject themselves, so the whole subject sits in
  what follows them, and a yes-no question answered with an essay
  has usually been dodged.
ROOT: logic (11) / a proposition is the kind of thing that is either
  true or not.
CANNOT: no verdict without a claim sharp enough to be wrong — a
  yes-no question whose terms are vague cannot be answered either
  way, and the honest reply says so.
THREAD: law (17: a witness answers the question asked), evidence
  (36: a testable claim is one that could fail).
MEANS-SAME: is are does did was will has yes or no true whether
ASKED-AS: is it does it did true whether yes no confirm right

ESSENCE: every question has two kinds of word in it — the ones that
  say what is being asked about, and the ones that only shape the
  asking. Why, how, is, should, can, please and thanks carry no
  subject whatever; they say what the answer must look like.
ROOT: language (09) / a sentence carries its topic and its purpose
  on different words.
CANNOT: no subject in an asking word — a question matched on why,
  how or can has been matched on its shape and not on its content.
THREAD: computing (19: a request has a method and a target, and
  they are read separately), teaching (30: answer the question
  actually asked).
MEANS-SAME: why how what when where which can should would please tell explain
ASKED-AS: which words carry subject asking shape question topic purpose separate them

ESSENCE: the rarest word in a question almost always names the
  subject. Ordinary sentences are built out of a small stock of
  common words, so anything unusual arrived because the asker needed
  it, and it needed it for the thing being asked about.
ROOT: language (09) / word frequency falls away steeply, so rarity
  is a strong signal of deliberate choice.
CANNOT: no subject in a word that appears everywhere — the words
  shared by all questions cannot tell one question from another.
THREAD: computing (19: rarity is what makes a search term useful),
  keeping knowledge (40: an index is built on the uncommon words).
MEANS-SAME: rare unusual uncommon specific technical distinctive particular narrow precise telling
ASKED-AS: rarest word names subject unusual specific term which word matters most

ESSENCE: the commonest words never name the subject. The, is, of,
  and, to, a, it, this — they hold the sentence together and appear
  in every sentence ever written, so their presence in a question
  says nothing at all about what it is about.
ROOT: language (09) / a small set of words does the joining work in
  every sentence, and joining is not naming.
CANNOT: no meaning from a word that appears in nearly every
  sentence. Its presence is grammar, not subject.
THREAD: computing (19: stop words are removed for exactly this
  reason), keeping knowledge (40: an index that keeps them is noise).
MEANS-SAME: the and of to is it this that with for common everywhere
ASKED-AS: common words meaningless the and of stop words carry nothing subject

ESSENCE: a question about doing wants a change in the world; a
  question about being wants a fact about how things stand. How do I
  and what is may name the identical subject and need entirely
  different answers, and giving the wrong one is the commonest
  failure there is.
ROOT: craft (29) / knowing what a thing is does not tell the hands
  what to do first.
CANNOT: no doing answered by a definition — a person asking how to
  unblock a drain is not helped by what a drain is.
THREAD: teaching (30: explanation and instruction are different
  arts), medicine (06: a diagnosis is not a treatment).
MEANS-SAME: how do fix make build get change doing | what is means definition called being
ASKED-AS: how do i versus what is doing being fix definition difference

ESSENCE: a request is not a question. It wants an act, not
  information, and the give-away is that a true statement never
  satisfies it. "Can you open the window" wants a window opened, and
  answering "yes" is a joke everyone has heard.
ROOT: people together (21) / speech does things as well as says
  them, and the doing kind outranks the saying kind.
CANNOT: no request satisfied by a fact — a request answered with
  information has been refused politely.
THREAD: law (17: a demand and an inquiry start different clocks),
  persuasion (22: the form of an ask sets the range of replies).
MEANS-SAME: please could you would you can you make write give send do show
ASKED-AS: request wants action not information please could you make write do

ESSENCE: a request is marked by a wanted act sitting where the
  subject should be — make, write, build, send, show, give, fix. The
  polite wrapper around it (could you, would you mind, please) is
  removable without changing what is wanted.
ROOT: language (09) / politeness is a layer wrapped round an
  imperative, and it strips off cleanly.
CANNOT: no request without an act named in it — strip the wrapper
  and a verb of doing is left standing, or it was a question.
THREAD: computing (19: a command and a query are routed
  differently), craft (29: an order names the deliverable).
MEANS-SAME: please make build write create give send show fix draft list produce
ASKED-AS: markers of a request please make build write create give send

ESSENCE: a complaint says something is wrong and has already
  happened. It carries a broken thing, a word of failure, and often
  a time — "still", "again", "keeps". It wants a cause and a fix,
  not a definition, and never a lecture.
ROOT: engineering (04) / a fault report is data, and the first
  answer it deserves is a cause.
CANNOT: no complaint answered by agreement alone — sympathy without
  a cause or a next step leaves the thing exactly as broken.
THREAD: medicine (06: the presenting complaint drives the workup),
  people together (21: being heard is half of what is wanted).
MEANS-SAME: broken not working failed wrong error keeps still again crashed stuck useless
ASKED-AS: markers of a complaint broken not working failed keeps again wrong

ESSENCE: a greeting carries no subject at all. Hello, morning, hi,
  how are you — the words are a signal that a channel is open, and
  hunting for the subject of a greeting finds whatever is nearest
  and is wrong every time.
ROOT: people together (21) / most talk begins by establishing
  contact, not by transferring anything.
CANNOT: no subject in a greeting — treat it as a question and any
  answer given is an answer to nothing that was asked. No greeting
  answered by explaining what a greeting is — that is handing over
  the instruction instead of following it.
RULE: to answer a greeting — carry back a ready-made chunk, name no
  subject, and hold the channel open for what comes next.
CHUNKS: hello | morning | hi | good to see you | I am here | go on
THREAD: language (09: phatic speech carries contact, not content),
  ritual (26: openings are the smallest ceremonies).
MEANS-SAME: hello hi hey morning afternoon evening greetings welcome there again you
ASKED-AS: markers of a greeting hello hi morning how are you nothing

ESSENCE: a test question is one the asker already knows the answer
  to. The marks are a stated answer shape (name three, list the
  steps), a closed correct answer, and often a scoring word. What is
  wanted is proof of knowing, not the information.
ROOT: teaching (30) / an examination measures the answerer, not the
  subject.
CANNOT: no test without a known answer behind it — where the asker
  does not know, the same words are a real question and need
  different care.
THREAD: evidence (36: a check needs a known value), games (08: a
  quiz is a contest with facts as the field).
MEANS-SAME: name list state three explain briefly true false define correctly quiz test
ASKED-AS: markers of a test name three list state define correctly quiz

ESSENCE: a follow-up does not carry its own subject. It leans on the
  turn before it — "and the other one?", "what about at night?",
  "why though?" — and read alone it is nearly empty, which is
  exactly what marks it.
ROOT: language (09) / conversation drops whatever both sides already
  hold.
CANNOT: no reading a follow-up without the turn before it — the
  missing subject is not in the sentence and cannot be recovered
  from it.
THREAD: story (27: a scene inherits what the last one established),
  computing (19: a session is state carried between messages).
MEANS-SAME: and but what about that then also instead other one why though
ASKED-AS: follow up question leans on previous turn missing subject empty alone

ESSENCE: follow-ups are marked by a small set of hinge words —
  and, but, so, then, also, what about, instead, that one, the other
  — sitting at the front with nothing substantial behind them. A
  short sentence starting with one of these is almost never new.
ROOT: language (09) / connectives point backwards, and that is their
  whole job.
CANNOT: no new subject after a hinge word with no noun — and, but
  and so at the front of a short line mean the subject stands
  behind, unrepeated.
THREAD: writing (09: a paragraph opening with and is continuing),
  people together (21: interruption and continuation sound different).
MEANS-SAME: and but so then also what about instead other same still again ok
ASKED-AS: words that mark a follow up and but what about then instead

ESSENCE: negation flips what is being asked without changing a
  single content word. "Why does this work" and "why does this not
  work" share every noun and want opposite answers, and a reader
  counting letters cannot tell them apart at all.
ROOT: logic (11) / a negation reverses the truth of the claim while
  leaving its subject untouched.
CANNOT: no meaning from a word standing under a negation — matching
  on "work" in "does not work" reports the opposite of what was
  asked.
THREAD: law (17: a clause and its exception must be read together),
  medicine (06: the absence of a sign is itself a finding).
MEANS-SAME: not no never cannot without fails lacks missing absent neither none unless
ASKED-AS: negation changes question not never cannot without missing opposite meaning

ESSENCE: a comparison question needs two things and a standard, and
  is unanswerable if any of the three is missing. Better, faster,
  cheaper and safer each point somewhere different, so the standard
  is the real question hiding behind the comparison.
ROOT: measurement (36) / a comparison is a difference measured
  against a stated property.
CANNOT: no comparison without two things and a property — better
  alone names neither the rival nor the test.
THREAD: design (28: trade-offs are what comparisons are made of),
  money (16: cheaper by purchase price is often dearer by year).
MEANS-SAME: better worse faster cheaper safer than versus compared instead more less
ASKED-AS: comparison question needs two things better than versus compared which standard

ESSENCE: a hypothetical asks about a world that is not this one, and
  it is marked — if, suppose, imagine, what if, would, were. The
  answer must stay inside the supposed world; dragging it back to
  the real one answers a question nobody asked.
ROOT: logic (11) / an implication says nothing about whether its
  condition holds.
CANNOT: no fact from a hypothetical — a word inside an if-clause is
  not being asserted, and matching it as a claim inverts the sense.
THREAD: chance (35: a scenario is a possibility being priced),
  strategy (23: war-gaming is thinking inside a false world).
MEANS-SAME: if suppose imagine assume what would were hypothetically pretend say scenario
ASKED-AS: hypothetical question marked if suppose imagine what would were pretend

ESSENCE: a figurative question is not about the thing it names.
  "How do I get my foot out of my mouth" has no foot in it. The mark
  is a body part, an animal or an object doing something impossible,
  sitting in a sentence about people.
ROOT: words and meaning (66) / metaphor moves a word's home and
  leaves the old sense behind, still spelled the same.
CANNOT: no literal subject in an impossible picture — where the
  words describe something that cannot physically happen, the
  subject is elsewhere.
THREAD: humor (41: a pun is this collision cashed for a laugh),
  story (27: an image carries what a statement would flatten).
MEANS-SAME: like as if figure speech saying expression idiom literally metaphor picture
ASKED-AS: figurative literal question idiom saying expression not really about foot

ESSENCE: a statement can be a question, and often is. "I can't get
  this to save" has no question word, no mark, and wants an answer
  as much as anything with a why in front of it. The want is carried
  by the trouble, not by the grammar.
ROOT: people together (21) / among people, saying the problem is
  asking for help.
CANNOT: no asking without a question mark or word in a great many
  real questions — the shape of a question is a convention, not the
  thing itself.
THREAD: medicine (06: the patient states, the clinician hears a
  question), persuasion (22: implied asks are harder to refuse).
MEANS-SAME: cant wont doesnt trying stuck need trouble problem help unable failing
ASKED-AS: statement is a question no question mark stuck trying trouble help

ESSENCE: an either-or question offers two answers and asks for one
  of them. It fails in two ways: the answer may be both, and the
  answer may be neither — and a reply of "yes" to an either-or is a
  refusal to choose.
ROOT: logic (11) / an exclusive choice claims the options do not
  overlap and cover the ground, and that claim is usually unstated.
CANNOT: no forced choice without the options being complete — a
  question offering two roads where three exist is an argument
  wearing a question.
THREAD: persuasion (22: the false choice is the oldest trap),
  strategy (23: naming the options is the real move).
MEANS-SAME: or either whether one two both neither rather versus choose alternative
ASKED-AS: either or question two options choose both neither false choice trap

ESSENCE: a loaded question carries a claim inside its framing, so
  that any direct answer accepts the claim. "Why is this so slow"
  has already settled that it is slow, and the honest first move is
  to check the framing rather than answer it.
ROOT: persuasion (22) / a premise smuggled into a question is
  granted by anyone who answers.
CANNOT: no answering a loaded question without granting its premise
  — the premise must be checked first or it has been agreed to.
THREAD: law (17: leading questions are restricted for this reason),
  evidence (36: the assumption inside the measurement).
MEANS-SAME: why is so still keeps always never obviously clearly of course everyone
ASKED-AS: loaded question hidden claim assumes premise why is so still

ESSENCE: a closed question can be answered in a word and an open one
  cannot. The difference decides the shape of the whole answer, and
  giving a paragraph to a closed question or a word to an open one
  are the two commonest mismatches.
ROOT: teaching (30) / the form of the question sets the form of the
  useful reply.
CANNOT: no open question answered in a word — and no closed question
  improved by a paragraph, which is padding whatever else it is.
THREAD: persuasion (22: open questions gather, closed ones confirm),
  medicine (06: history-taking opens then closes deliberately).
MEANS-SAME: is are did which yes no closed | what how why describe explain tell open
ASKED-AS: open closed question yes no one word describe explain long answer

ESSENCE: some questions name the shape of the answer they want — a
  list of three, a table, a number, a step by step, one sentence.
  That instruction is not the subject; it is the container, and it
  must be obeyed as well as the subject served.
ROOT: design (28) / a form serves a use, and the asker knows the use.
CANNOT: no answer counted as given if its shape was named and
  ignored — the right facts in the wrong container fail the ask.
THREAD: teaching (30: an exam rubric is a container), computing
  (19: a response format is part of the contract).
MEANS-SAME: list three steps table summary briefly sentence bullet points number format
ASKED-AS: question names answer shape list three table steps briefly one sentence

ESSENCE: an imperative asking — tell me, explain, show me, describe
  — is a question in a command's clothes. The verb chosen says which
  kind of answer is wanted, exactly as an asking word would: explain
  wants causes, describe wants features, show wants an example.
ROOT: language (09) / the verb of the command does the work the
  question word would have done.
CANNOT: no reading of an imperative asking without its verb — tell
  me, explain and show me are three different jobs on one subject.
THREAD: teaching (30: demonstrate, define and derive are separate
  tasks), craft (29: show me is the only honest test).
MEANS-SAME: tell explain show describe list compare summarise define outline walk through
ASKED-AS: tell me explain show describe summarise command shaped question which verb

ESSENCE: in most questions the subject sits after the asking words
  and before the tail of small words. Why is my bread not rising —
  strip why, is, my and not, and bread and rising are what is left
  standing, which is the whole question.
ROOT: language (09) / questions put the asking at the front and the
  matter in the middle.
CANNOT: no subject at the very front of a question that begins with
  an asking word — the first word says what kind of answer, never
  what about.
THREAD: computing (19: parsing separates the verb from its object),
  linguistics (144: word order carries grammatical role).
MEANS-SAME: subject middle after asking word strip small words remains standing left
ASKED-AS: where subject sits question after asking word strip small words remains

ESSENCE: a number in a question changes what a good answer is. It
  usually wants arithmetic, a count or a comparison against a
  threshold, and an answer that never touches the number has
  answered a different, vaguer question.
ROOT: mathematics (11) / a quantity in a problem is a constraint,
  not decoration.
CANNOT: no answer to a numbered question without using the number —
  if the figure could be changed and the reply stand unaltered, the
  reply was not about it.
THREAD: measurement (36: a threshold turns a fact into a decision),
  cooking (02: a ratio is the recipe, the rest is method).
MEANS-SAME: how many much total sum average per each times cost number figure
ASKED-AS: number in question arithmetic count total threshold answer must use figure

ESSENCE: how do I and what is share their subject and share nothing
  else. One wants an ordered procedure that ends with the thing
  done; the other wants a place in the web of kinds. The same file
  of knowledge answers both, and not with the same words.
ROOT: craft (29) / description and instruction are separate forms of
  the same knowledge.
CANNOT: no procedure inside a definition — a definition names what a
  thing is and nothing about the order to touch it in.
THREAD: teaching (30: a recipe and an encyclopedia entry on bread),
  computing (19: a reference and a tutorial are different documents).
MEANS-SAME: how do i steps procedure fix make | what is definition kind means called
ASKED-AS: how do i versus what is procedure definition same subject different answers

ESSENCE: a problem question — it broke, it stopped, it will not
  start — wants two things at once: what caused it and what to do
  now. Answering only the cause leaves the asker stuck; answering
  only the fix leaves it free to happen again.
ROOT: engineering (04) / a fault has a mechanism and a remedy, and
  they are different findings.
CANNOT: no fix without a cause identified — replacing the part that
  was not at fault leaves the fault in place with a new bill.
THREAD: medicine (06: treating the symptom and treating the disease),
  computing (19: a workaround is honest about being one).
MEANS-SAME: broke stopped failed wont start error crash stuck why fix what now
ASKED-AS: problem question wants cause and fix broke stopped failed error stuck

ESSENCE: which is better wants a judgement, and a judgement is only
  worth anything with its grounds attached. The useful answer names
  the standard, says which one wins on it, and says what is given up
  — because something always is.
ROOT: right and wrong (25) / a preference without grounds is a
  report about the speaker.
CANNOT: no judgement without a standard and a cost — a
  recommendation with nothing given up is either wrong or answering
  an easier question.
THREAD: design (28: every choice trades something), money (16: the
  cheapest option is priced in some other currency).
MEANS-SAME: better best worth recommend prefer instead versus tradeoff cost benefit choose
ASKED-AS: which is better recommend judgement grounds standard tradeoff what given up

ESSENCE: some questions are about the asker rather than the world —
  should I take it, am I being unreasonable, what would you do. The
  subject is a situation nobody else can see, so the honest answer
  asks before it answers.
ROOT: the mind (20) / a person asking about a choice is usually
  testing a decision already half made.
CANNOT: no advice without the asker's situation — the same question
  has opposite right answers for two different people, and nothing
  in the words says which one is asking.
THREAD: persuasion (22: advice is accepted for reasons of trust),
  medicine (06: a history is taken before a recommendation).
MEANS-SAME: should i my me am i being what would you my situation advice
ASKED-AS: advice question about the asker should i what would you situation

ESSENCE: some questions cannot be answered by any knowledge at all —
  what a named person is thinking, what a price will do next week,
  what happened where nobody was. The shape looks ordinary and the
  wall is total.
ROOT: evidence (36) / a claim needs something that could show it,
  and some claims have nothing.
CANNOT: no answer without something that could have shown it — a
  question about a private mind or an unrun future has no such
  thing, and saying so is the answer.
THREAD: chance (35: a forecast is a spread, never a value), the
  mind (20: reported reasons are reconstructions).
MEANS-SAME: will it what thinking feel next week future predict exactly guarantee certain
ASKED-AS: unanswerable question future private mind predict guarantee certain nobody could know

ESSENCE: a tag question — isn't it, right, don't you think — wants
  agreement rather than information. It is a check that both sides
  still hold the same thing, and answering it with a lecture misses
  what was being done.
ROOT: people together (21) / much of talk maintains agreement rather
  than transferring facts.
CANNOT: no information wanted in a tag — the asker has stated the
  answer inside the question and is asking whether it stands.
THREAD: persuasion (22: agreement is built in small steps), language
  (09: tags soften an assertion into an offer).
MEANS-SAME: isnt it right dont you think surely yes no wouldnt arent correct
ASKED-AS: tag question wants agreement isnt it right surely dont you think

ESSENCE: why splits in two and the word does not warn you. Why did
  the engine stop asks for a cause; why did you stop the engine asks
  for a purpose. One is answered by what pushed, the other by what
