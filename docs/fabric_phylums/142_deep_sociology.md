# 142 DEEP SOCIOLOGY — how societies hold together and come apart
File 91 holds the arithmetic of many people handling one thing. This
file holds the standing arrangements: the positions people step into,
the rules nobody wrote down, the lines drawn between groups, and why the
same pattern comes back after every one of its members has gone.

ESSENCE: a school, an army, a market keeps its shape while every person
  in it is replaced. The pattern is not in the people — it is in the
  positions, the expectations and the paths between them, which is why
  you can predict a great deal about a stranger's day from their job
  title and almost nothing from their character.
ROOT: people together / a group's arrangements have to outlast its
  members or nothing could be handed on, so they are built to be
  occupied rather than owned.
THREAD: engineering (a frame carrying loads whatever fills the rooms),
  the living world (a body replacing its cells and keeping its shape),
  law.
ASKED-AS: society structure system bigger than individuals positions roles same problems everywhere pattern persists

ESSENCE: a role is a bundle of expectations attached to a position, and
  it is held by everybody else, not by you. Which is why it can pull two
  ways at once — the nurse who is told to be efficient and kind, the
  manager who must be one of the team and rank them — and why the strain
  is not a personal weakness.
ROOT: this file / a position is defined by what others expect of it, and
  nothing prevents two sets of expectations from contradicting.
THREAD: engineering (a part loaded two ways at once), law (two duties
  that cannot both be discharged), family + lives.
ASKED-AS: role conflict job expectations torn between two demands boss family stress impossible position

ESSENCE: every job has a front and a back. In front of the customer,
  the patient, the class, a performance is put on and kept consistent;
  behind the door the same people mock it, rehearse it, and repair it —
  and both regions are necessary, because the performance cannot be
  maintained without somewhere to drop it.
ROOT: people together / an audience treats you as what it sees, so the
  seen version has to be managed, and managing it takes labour that must
  happen somewhere unseen.
THREAD: theatre, medicine (the corridor conversation and the bedside
  one), work.
ASKED-AS: staff room behind the scenes professional face customers different in private acting work

ESSENCE: most rules are enforced by nothing heavier than a look. A pause
  in the conversation, an eyebrow, a joke at your expense — cheap to
  give, administered by everybody at once, and effective in a way no
  official punishment can be, because it arrives immediately and comes
  from people whose regard you want.
ROOT: people together / behaviour is steered by what is felt at the
  moment, and the cheapest immediate consequence available is the
  reaction of whoever is present.
THREAD: law (the tiny fraction that reaches a court), teaching (a class
  held by attention rather than by sanctions), driving.
ASKED-AS: unwritten rules disapproval looks judged social pressure queue jumping embarrassing nobody says anything

ESSENCE: what counts as deviance is decided by the group, not by the
  act. The same killing is murder, war, or duty; the same drink is a
  crime here and a sacrament there; the same child is spirited in one
  classroom and disordered in the next. The act supplies the raw
  material and the group supplies the verdict.
ROOT: this file / a norm is a rule held by a group, so a breach exists
  only relative to that group's rule.
THREAD: law (crime as defined rather than found), medicine (where the
  line of illness is drawn), right + wrong.
ASKED-AS: deviant behaviour normal here crime elsewhere labelled wrong depends who decides acceptable rules

ESSENCE: being named is worse than being caught. Once a person is filed
  as a thief, an addict, a troublemaker, the label starts outranking
  everything else about them — doors close, watching increases, the old
  friends step back — and the life that remains is one in which the
  label is the sensible thing to be.
ROOT: the mind / a category, once applied, points every later reading of
  the person toward itself.
THREAD: law (a record as a life sentence in instalments), medicine, work
  (a reference that closes a trade).
ASKED-AS: labelled troublemaker record criminal reputation stuck expectations became living up to it

ESSENCE: nobody is taught the rules of their society as rules. They are
  taught as what food is, what a family is, what work is for — so by
  adulthood the arrangements have stopped feeling like arrangements and
  have become preferences. That is what makes them durable, and what
  makes them so hard to see.
ROOT: teaching / what is learned early and everywhere is stored as the
  world rather than as one option in it.
THREAD: teaching, language (a first tongue that feels like the plain way
  to speak), the mind.
ASKED-AS: brought up that way normal to me values taught childhood society expectations natural learned

ESSENCE: an institution is a habit that got out of its holders. Two
  people settle a way of doing something; they teach it to a third; and
  the third receives it as a fact of the world with no author. From then
  on it is obeyed by people who could not say who decided it or why.
ROOT: people together / a practice handed to somebody who did not
  invent it loses its history in the handing.
THREAD: history (frozen answers to vanished problems), computing (code
  everyone depends on and nobody owns), law.
ASKED-AS: how it is done always been that way who decided nobody knows institution habit

ESSENCE: the oldest line in any economy is between those who own the
  thing that makes the money and those who sell their hours to it. It is
  not about income — a well-paid employee is on one side and a
  struggling shop owner on the other — it is about where your living
  comes from when you stop turning up.
ROOT: money / a return flows either from a holding or from labour, and
  those two incomes behave in completely different ways.
THREAD: money, law (who bears the loss when a firm fails), power.
ASKED-AS: class working owner employee wages salary rich poor income where money comes from

ESSENCE: class also travels as equipment. How to speak to a doctor, when
  to complain, which fork, what to say in an interview, whether an
  institution feels like yours or theirs — all of it is learned at home,
  none of it is on any syllabus, and all of it is read as competence by
  the people holding the doors.
ROOT: teaching / the tacit rules are learned by living among them, so
  whoever grew up near an institution arrives already fluent in it.
THREAD: teaching, language (the variety that counts as correct), work
  (hiring for fit).
ASKED-AS: fitting in accent confidence interview posh manners knowing how to behave background class

ESSENCE: honour and money are two separate currencies and they can run
  in opposite directions. The village priest with nothing, the rich
  family nobody invites, the surgeon earning less than the man who fixes
  their roof — each is normal, because standing is granted by an
  audience and cannot simply be bought from it.
ROOT: people together / standing is other people's regard, so it obeys
  their rules and not the market's.
THREAD: money, meaning + ritual, sports (an amateur title worth more
  than a fee).
ASKED-AS: respect versus money status prestige nouveau riche looked down doctor teacher pay standing

ESSENCE: most measured movement between classes is not people swapping
  places — it is the ladder itself changing shape. When farms empty and
  offices fill, millions rise without anybody being displaced. Where the
  shape holds still, one person's rise has to be another's fall, and the
  rates go quiet.
ROOT: mathematics / positions can only be reallocated within their
  number, so movement beyond that requires the number of positions to
  change.
THREAD: money, history (the countryside emptying into cities), teaching.
ASKED-AS: social mobility rising above parents better life generation moved up stuck opportunity ladder

ESSENCE: the people closest to you carry you, and the people you barely
  know change your life. Close friends know what you know and move in
  your circles; the distant acquaintance stands in a different pool of
  news, which is why jobs, houses and opportunities arrive down the thin
  connections rather than the thick ones.
ROOT: this file / a tight group's members share their information, so
  inside it there is little that is new left to learn.
THREAD: information (a channel carries what the receiver lacked), work,
  the living world (a population needing outside blood).
ASKED-AS: got the job through someone friend of a friend networking acquaintances close friends contacts

ESSENCE: whoever stands between two groups that do not otherwise talk is
  in a position of quiet power — they hear both stories first, they
  choose what crosses, and the two sides cannot check them against each
  other. The advantage belongs to the gap, not to the person, and it
  disappears the moment somebody else bridges it.
ROOT: this file / information travels along ties, so a missing tie makes
  whoever supplies it indispensable.
THREAD: money (the middleman's real function), computing (a network's
  choke point), diplomacy.
ASKED-AS: go between only person who knows both sides gatekeeper connection power introduce networks bridge

ESSENCE: the ties a person can call on are an asset like any other,
  earning real returns in emergencies, in job hunts, in getting a form
  processed. And it comes in two kinds that pull against each other: the
  bonds that hold you up are also the bonds that hold you in.
ROOT: people together / obligations are built by repeated exchange, and

  you.
THREAD: money (savings held in people instead of a bank), family +

ASKED-AS: who you know favours community support obligations family help trapped expectations network belonging

ESSENCE: every society trusts somebody, and they differ in how far the
  circle reaches. Where it stops at the family, hiring a relative is
  simple prudence and a stranger's contract is a risk; where it reaches
  strangers, firms can grow past the cousins. Both are stable, and each
  makes the other look like corruption.
ROOT: this file / trust is built by repeated dealing, and who you deal
  with repeatedly is set by the arrangements you were born into.
THREAD: money (the cost of every guarantee that has to be bought), law,
  history.
ASKED-AS: trusting strangers family business nepotism hiring relatives corruption countries different trust circle

ESSENCE: a city is a place where you meet more people than you can
  know. So relationships become partial — the neighbour is a face, the
  colleague is a role — and the same arrangement that makes a city
  indifferent to you is what makes it free: nobody there has the
  standing to run your life.
ROOT: the mind / one person can hold only so many relationships, so past
  that number acquaintance has to be replaced with categories.
THREAD: people together, the mind, history (the countryside as the
  city's nursery).
ASKED-AS: city life anonymous neighbours dont know lonely freedom village everyone knows your business

ESSENCE: two kinds of group live inside every society. In one, the
  relationship is the point and the tasks are an excuse; in the other,
  the task is the point and the relationships are equipment. Most
  complaints about modern life are the discovery that a thing you
  treated as the first kind was running as the second.
ROOT: this file / a tie can be an end or a means, and which one it is
  decides what may be asked and what counts as betrayal.
THREAD: work (a team that calls itself a family), religion, sports
  (a club that is also a business).
ASKED-AS: community versus organisation belonging workplace family friends transactional real relationships club members

ESSENCE: everybody wants the strike to succeed and nobody wants to be
  the first one out of the door. The first mover pays the most and gains
  the least, so the action that would benefit thousands waits on a
  handful willing to act while it is still a bad idea for them
  personally.
ROOT: chance + risk / the cost of moving early is certain and the
  benefit is shared and uncertain, so the sum points the wrong way at
  the front of the queue.
THREAD: chance + risk, power, the living world (the first bird to break
  cover).
ASKED-AS: nobody wants to go first strike protest organise risk speaking up someone has to

ESSENCE: grievances are everywhere and movements are rare, so the
  grievance never explains the movement. What explains it is the boring
  supply: existing networks to spread the word, people with time and
  money, somewhere safe to meet, and a moment when the authorities are
  divided or distracted.
ROOT: this file / action travels along ties that already exist, so a
  movement is built out of whatever organisation the society already
  had.
THREAD: power, history (revolutions after reform, not at the bottom),
  churches and unions as the machinery movements are built from.
ASKED-AS: protest movement started why now organised churches unions leaders anger nothing happened before

ESSENCE: a hardship becomes a cause only when somebody supplies three
  things: a name for it that makes it an injustice rather than bad luck,
  a party responsible, and a demand that could actually be met. Most
  suffering never gets all three, and stays private.
ROOT: the mind / a state of affairs with no author is filed as
  misfortune, and misfortune generates grief instead of action.
THREAD: law (a wrong requires a wrongdoer), persuasion, story + drama.
ASKED-AS: framing cause injustice blame who is responsible demands campaign message bad luck unfair

ESSENCE: an identity has two halves that need not match: the one you
  claim and the one others assign. Where they agree, it is invisible;
  where they disagree — being read as foreign at home, as rich among the
  poor and poor among the rich — the mismatch itself becomes the
  experience.
ROOT: people together / a status is granted by others, so declaring one
  is a bid rather than a fact.
THREAD: the other mind, law (categories on official forms), work.
ASKED-AS: identity how i see myself how others see me labelled belonging accepted claimed assigned

ESSENCE: an ethnic group is held together by its boundary, not by its
  contents. Language, dress, food and religion inside it can change
  completely over three generations while the line between us and them
  stays exactly where it was — because what is being maintained is the
  difference, not the culture.
ROOT: the mind / a category is defined by what it excludes, so the edge
  does the work and the middle is free to drift.
THREAD: language (a dialect line drawn where a border was), the living

ASKED-AS: ethnic group identity differences culture changed still separate us them boundary markers heritage

ESSENCE: a nation is a group of people who will never meet feeling like
  a we — and the feeling is recent, however ancient it insists it is.
  Most national costumes, anthems and ancient traditions were assembled
  within the last two centuries, and the assembling has been forgotten
  on purpose.
ROOT: people together / belonging needs a picture of the group, and for
  millions of strangers that picture has to be manufactured and taught.
THREAD: history (print picking a dialect and schools enforcing it),

ASKED-AS: nation patriotism ancient traditions invented flag anthem national identity made up country belonging

ESSENCE: which work is men's and which is women's varies so wildly
  between societies that the arrangement cannot be reading off the
  bodies. Ploughing, weaving, trading, medicine and building have each
  been men's work somewhere and women's work somewhere else — and in
  every one of those places it felt like nature.
ROOT: this file / an arrangement learned in childhood is stored as the

ESSENCE: who lives with whom follows how the living is made. Where
  labour is the asset, households are large and hold three generations;
  where wages are paid to individuals and land does not need working,
  they shrink to a couple and their children; where work must be
  chased, they scatter and send money home.
ROOT: money / a household is an economic unit before it is anything
  else, so its shape tracks the way its income is earned.
THREAD: money, farming, history (children as labour and then as cost).

ESSENCE: what a congregation supplies, sociologically, is membership —
  a weekly appointment, people who will notice an absence, help in a
  crisis, and a calendar. Which is why decline shows up first as the
  emptying of the hall rather than as the loss of the creed, and why
  what replaces it has to replace all of that.
ROOT: people together / belonging is built by repeated shared time, and

ESSENCE: the press is weak at telling people what to think and enormously
  strong at telling them what to think about. It rarely moves an
  opinion; it decides which three subjects are in every mouth this week,
  and everything unmentioned quietly becomes a thing nobody has a view
  on at all.
ROOT: the mind / attention is one beam, so whatever fills it sets the

ESSENCE: now and then a small problem becomes a public terror. A villain
  is named, the coverage feeds on itself, everybody remembers a case,
  laws are passed at speed — and when the alarm fades the laws stay, out
  of proportion to a danger that was often shrinking the whole time.
ROOT: the mind / how easily an example comes to mind is used as a

ESSENCE: advantage is passed on by two machines working in series.

ESSENCE: alongside the subject, a school teaches punctuality, sitting

ESSENCE: a qualification is mostly a sorting device, not a training. It

ESSENCE: nobody has to want a separated city for one to appear. Give
  every household a mild preference — a third of my neighbours like me —
  and let people move when it is not met, and the map sorts itself into
  near-total separation. The outcome nobody chose is much more extreme
  than any preference in it.
ROOT: mathematics / a small individual rule applied over and over with

ESSENCE: people differ in how many others have to move before they will.
  A few go first at any time, most need a crowd, some need nearly
  everyone — so whether a room joins in or does nothing depends less on
  what they believe than on the order in which their thresholds happen
  to line up.
ROOT: this file / everybody is watching everybody, so each person's

ESSENCE: a social number always records a procedure as well as a world.
  The crime rate rises when reporting becomes easier; unemployment moves
  when the definition changes; a country's suicide figures track its
  coroners. Read one without asking how it was made and you are reading
  the office, not the country.

ESSENCE: people do not judge their lives against the world; they judge
  them against whoever they can see. Which is why a comfortable person
  among the rich feels poor, a soldier's grievance is with his own unit,
  and widening what everybody can see makes the same life feel worse
  without changing anything in it.
ROOT: the mind / a value is read against a nearby reference rather than

ESSENCE: people cluster with people like themselves without anyone
  excluding anybody. Similar people meet more, get on faster, and
  recommend each other — so a hiring process with no prejudice in it at
  all reproduces the room it started in, simply by running on the

ESSENCE: organisations in the same line of work end up looking alike —

ESSENCE: a profession is a trade that has won control of its own door.
  It sets the training, the examination and the right to practise —

ESSENCE: the ages of a life are administrative, not biological. Childhood
  ending at a school-leaving date, adulthood at a voting age, old age at
  a pension date — these are lines drawn by institutions, and they now

ESSENCE: a group with no offenders will find some. Clear out the serious

ESSENCE: a crowd can be confidently wrong with almost no information in

ESSENCE: no role exists alone. There is no boss without staff, no doctor

ESSENCE: in a disaster people mostly help. They organise, share, dig out
