# 94 DEEP HISTORY AND CHANGE — how things got the way they are
Nothing arrived because it was best and nothing arrived on time.
This file is about the order things had to come in, the accidents
that got locked in, and why the present always looks like an
arrival rather than a stretch of road.

ESSENCE: early accidents get locked in — a choice made once, for
  reasons long dead, becomes the thing everything else is built to
  fit, and after that the cost of changing it is everyone else's
  cost, not yours.
ROOT: whatever is adopted first gets adapted to, and each
  adaptation raises the price of switching.
THREAD: language (spelling frozen mid-change, silent letters and
  all), computing (formats and protocols nobody would choose
  today), the body (renovations stacked on a fish).
ASKED-AS: qwerty keyboard why designed weird standard stuck rail gauge legacy old system

ESSENCE: a technology arrives when its parts arrive, not when it
  is wanted — the idea is usually old and cheap, and the missing
  piece is some unglamorous prerequisite: the press needed paper,
  oil ink, and a metal alloy that cast type without shrinking, all
  of which had to exist first.
ROOT: an invention is an assembly, so it waits on its slowest
  ingredient.
THREAD: cooking (a dish that waits on a spice trade route),
  medicine (antibiotics needed industrial fermentation to matter),
  farming (the seed drill waited on precise metalwork).
ASKED-AS: invented same time two people who first discovery ready idea before its time

ESSENCE: invention is a moment and adoption is a generation — the
  working prototype is the start of the story, not the end, and
  most of the delay is not technical: it is habits, training,
  buildings, laws, and everyone else's equipment.
ROOT: a new thing must fit into a world built for the old one, and
  the world is heavy.
THREAD: teaching (a better method that no trained teacher can yet
  teach), money (productivity gains that show up decades after the
  invention), farming (a crop that needs a new mill).
ASKED-AS: new technology slow catch on adoption years later why took long change

ESSENCE: adoption follows cost, not merit — the version that wins
  is usually the cheap, good-enough one that fits existing habits,
  and the better system loses while being better.
ROOT: money / people buy what they can afford in the situation
  they are actually in, and merit is only one term in that sum.
THREAD: farming (high-yield varieties displacing tastier ones),
  music (the compressed file beating the better recording), the
  living world (good enough that out-bred its neighbours).
ASKED-AS: cheaper version won better product lost market format war quality price beat

ESSENCE: farming was a bad deal that could not be refused — the
  first farmers were shorter, sicker, worked longer, and ate a
  narrower diet than the foragers they replaced; what farming did
  was feed more people per acre, and more people is what wins.
ROOT: the living world / what spreads is what leaves more
  descendants, which is not the same as what makes a life better.
THREAD: money (a stored surplus is the first capital), power (the
  first states rose where grain could be counted and carted),
  medicine (settled crowds and their new diseases).
ASKED-AS: agriculture revolution hunter gatherers worse health why farming started settled villages

ESSENCE: domestication was a lottery with few winning tickets — of
  the world's large animals only about a dozen were ever tamed,
  because a candidate must breed in captivity, grow fast, eat
  cheaply, accept a herd rank, and not panic; a zebra fails on
  temperament alone.
ROOT: the living world / a species' behaviour is inherited, and
  the required combination is rare.
THREAD: chance (independent requirements multiply into rarity),
  farming (chosen parenthood — this is that law's raw material),
  power (armies with horses against armies without).
ASKED-AS: why some animals tamed zebra horse cattle domesticated pets wild never work

ESSENCE: continents have a grain — crops and herds spread easily
  east and west, where day length and season match, and slowly
  north and south, where every mile is a new climate; Eurasia's
  long east-west spread let one region's inventions become
  everyone's.
ROOT: astronomy / latitude sets day length and season, and a crop
  is tuned to both.
THREAD: farming (planting windows written by the tilt), trade
  (routes that follow climate bands), language (families that
  spread along the same corridors).
ASKED-AS: crops spread continents shape east west africa americas climate zones farming diffusion

ESSENCE: a city is division of labour made physical — put enough
  people close enough and a person can spend a whole life doing
  one narrow thing, because everything else is available within
  walking distance; the density IS the specialisation.
ROOT: money / trade needs a crossing, and specialisation needs a
  market big enough to buy the specialty.
THREAD: movement of goods (cities sit where paths cross), people
  together (past the village ceiling, rules replace relationships),
  the body (organs are cells that stopped doing everything).
ASKED-AS: cities why grew jobs specialised urban living population density crafts markets trade

ESSENCE: writing began as accounting, not literature — the oldest
  tablets are receipts, lists of grain and sheep and who owes
  what, and it took centuries before anyone wrote down a thought.
  The ledger invented the alphabet's ancestors.
ROOT: power / states run on extraction, and extraction needs
  records; what gets invented is what somebody needed counted.
THREAD: money (the ledger is trade's memory), keeping knowledge
  (archives are the descendants of those tablets), law (a contract
  is a receipt for a promise).
ASKED-AS: first writing invented cuneiform tablets records accounts why not stories clay ancient

ESSENCE: bronze needed a trade route and iron needed only a hot
  fire — tin is rare and usually far from copper, so bronze
  weapons belonged to whoever controlled long-distance exchange,
  while iron ore is nearly everywhere, and cheap iron put an edge
  in ordinary hands.
ROOT: geology deals the raw materials, and rarity decides who can
  hold a monopoly on the finished thing.
THREAD: power (dispersal of the means of force disperses power),
  money (a commodity's rarity decides who can corner it), craft
  (the smith becomes a village trade, not a court office).
ASKED-AS: bronze age iron why replaced tin trade metal weapons cheap spread collapse

ESSENCE: coined money is a state technology before it is a
  merchant's — a ruler who stamps metal can pay soldiers in it and
  then demand it back as tax, which forces everyone to obtain it,
  which creates a market that feeds the army without the state
  running one.
ROOT: power / extraction plus legitimacy; money — a token works
  where everyone must get hold of it.
THREAD: money (a token made valuable by agreement — here the
  agreement has a sword behind it), strategy (logistics as the
  real limit on force).
ASKED-AS: coins invented money taxes soldiers paid empire currency why accepted stamped metal

ESSENCE: the printing press did not make books better, it made
  them cheap — and cheapness changed what a book could do: an
  error could now be compared against another copy, a disagreement
  could outlive its author, and a wrong number could be corrected
  in the next edition rather than recopied forever.
ROOT: computing / copying costs decide what can circulate; when
  copying collapses in price, the constraint moves to attention.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (many copies beat one vault), evidence
  (replication needs a shared, fixed reference), power (censorship
  as a race the censor loses).
ASKED-AS: printing press gutenberg books cheap reformation spread ideas copies literacy change

ESSENCE: print froze speech — before it, a language was a
  continuum of local talk shading into the next valley's; a press
  needs one spelling and one grammar to sell to the widest market,
  so one dialect became the standard and the rest became mistakes.
ROOT: money / a printer prints what sells widest; language — a
  written standard slowly disciplines the spoken one.
THREAD: power (a shared language is a state's cheapest glue),
  teaching (schools enforce what the presses chose), keeping
  knowledge (formats and codes going extinct — the losing dialects
  are those).
ASKED-AS: standard english accent dialect correct spelling national language school proper regional

ESSENCE: where people could survive decided where people are —
  malaria kept farmers off good land, cattle disease kept horses
  and ploughs out of whole regions, and the map of settlement is
  as much a map of what could not kill you as of what could feed
  you.
ROOT: the living world / a parasite's range is set by its
  carriers' climate, and the range is largely fixed by geography.
THREAD: medicine (drainage and screens did more than any cure),
  power (empires stalled at disease lines, not at mountains),
  farming (livestock ranges shaped by parasites, not by grass).
ASKED-AS: malaria mosquitoes settlement empty land tropical disease colonists died swamps geography

ESSENCE: ocean sail joined the world's separate biologies, and the
  exchange ran both ways with wildly unequal force — potatoes and
  maize fed enormous population growth in the old world, while
  diseases the old world had lived with for millennia emptied the
  new one before most of its people ever saw a European.
ROOT: the living world / long-separated populations have no
  shared immunity, and crowd diseases require crowds and livestock
  to breed in.
THREAD: medicine (why a mild childhood illness can be a massacre
  elsewhere), farming (half the world's staple crops are
  immigrants), money (the trades that followed the seeds).
ASKED-AS: columbus exchange smallpox natives died potatoes tomatoes crops new world plague

ESSENCE: the plague raised wages — when a third of the working
  people die, the survivors' hands become scarce, and scarce hands
  can bargain; laws forbidding it were passed and failed, and old
  ties to the land loosened in a generation.
ROOT: money / a price is where scarcity meets need, and labour is
  priced like anything else however much anyone dislikes saying so.
THREAD: money (a decreed price relocates rather than dies), power
  (obligations hold only while alternatives are scarce), farming
  (land use shifts to whatever needs fewer hands).
ASKED-AS: black death wages peasants freedom labour shortage after plague serfdom ended

ESSENCE: cities were death traps and grew anyway — until clean
  water and sewers, urban deaths outran urban births, so every
  growing city was growing purely by people walking in from the
  countryside to replace the ones it killed.
ROOT: medicine / crowding plus contaminated water multiplies
  infection faster than any birth rate can answer.
THREAD: movement of people (migration as the normal condition, not
  the exception), medicine (the drain outperformed the doctor),
  farming (the countryside as the city's nursery as well as its
  larder).
ASKED-AS: cities unhealthy history growth countryside moved in deaths births sanitation slums

ESSENCE: a trade route carries more than goods — languages,
  religions, alphabets, recipes, and diseases all travel in the
  merchant's baggage, so the map of a faith's spread is usually a
  map of somebody's commerce a century earlier.
ROOT: people together / ideas move with the people who move, and
  traders are the people who move.
THREAD: language (borrowed words mark old routes like tide lines),
  money (the coin and the creed travelled in the same purse),
  keeping knowledge (scripts adopted wholesale by trading
  partners).
ASKED-AS: silk road religion spread islam buddhism trade language borrowed words routes

ESSENCE: empires end where their logistics do — an army marches
  only as far as its supply and its orders can follow, so before
  the railway and the telegraph every empire's size was roughly
  the distance a message could travel before its news went stale.
ROOT: power / rule needs agents, and agents at a distance must be
  supplied and instructed; movement of goods — the return leg is
  never free.
THREAD: strategy (supply as the real constraint on force), money
  (a business grown past its own control systems), computing (a
  network whose latency exceeds its coordination needs).
ASKED-AS: empire size limit rome collapse distance governing provinces supply lines communication

ESSENCE: gunpowder ended the castle and built the state — a high
  stone wall is protection against ladders and useless against
  cannon, and cannon are expensive, so defence passed from any
  local lord with a hill to whoever could afford artillery, which
  meant whoever could tax broadly.
ROOT: power / the means of force decide who holds it; money — a
  weapon that only large revenues can buy concentrates authority
  around large revenues.
THREAD: strategy (every weapon redistributes power, not only
  battles), power (dispersal or concentration follows the cost of
  the decisive tool).
ASKED-AS: castles obsolete cannon gunpowder kings power knights feudalism ended artillery walls

ESSENCE: coerced labour tracked the crop, not the climate — the
  crops that took hold with slavery were the ones needing gangs
  working in step on large blocks of land, sugar above all, while
  crops that a family could work never built the same system.
ROOT: money / an owner captures value only where the work can be
  supervised and its output measured; the crop decides both.
THREAD: farming (a monoculture demands one rhythm from everyone),
  power (extraction with an enforcement cost attached), law (what
  the statute permits, the ledger will attempt).
ASKED-AS: slavery plantations sugar cotton why south economics labour forced history crops

ESSENCE: the clock created paid time — before it, work was a task
  ("until the field is done"); after it, work is a duration, and a
  person can be bought by the hour. The bell in the tower had to
  come before the wage.
ROOT: measurement / what can be counted can be priced, and the
  clock made a working day countable.
THREAD: money (a price needs a countable unit), teaching (the
  school period is the factory hour, borrowed), the mind (the
  feeling that time is spent rather than lived).
ASKED-AS: clock work hours wage punch card factory time discipline bells shifts paid

ESSENCE: the steam engine's gift was portability of power — water
  and wind had always turned wheels, but only where the river ran;
  a fire can be lit anywhere, so the factory left the valley and
  went where the workers, the coal, and the customers were.
ROOT: physics / an engine runs on a difference, and a fuel is a
  difference you can carry; before that the difference had to be
  found in place.
THREAD: engineering (every engine is a heat difference put to
  work), movement of goods (the factory followed the transport it
  had just created).
ASKED-AS: steam engine industrial revolution factories rivers watermills coal power moved location

ESSENCE: each fuel in the ladder is denser than the last, and the
  density is what it buys — wood, then coal, then oil: each holds
  more energy per load, so each one carries further before its own
  transport eats the gain, and each unlocks a class of machine the
  previous could not.
ROOT: physics / energy is never created, so what improves is not
  the amount but the concentration and the ease of carriage.
THREAD: farming (sunlight as income against fossil inheritance),
  flight (every gram argued over), money (a store drawn down that
  looks like a flow).
ASKED-AS: wood coal oil energy density fuel why switched transition ships planes power

ESSENCE: electricity's real gift was that power could be sent —
  once energy travelled down a wire, the machine no longer had to
  stand next to the engine, so one great shaft driving a whole
  factory became a small motor in every tool, in every room, in
  every house.
ROOT: physics / charge pushed down a conductor carries energy
  without carrying matter, and the delivery is nearly free
  compared with belts and shafts.
THREAD: engineering (feedback control got its great use here),
  the body (nerves distribute signal the same way), computing
  (every machine needed this before it could exist).
ASKED-AS: electricity factories motors wiring homes appliances grid power distribution shaft belts

ESSENCE: the deep shift was skill moving out of hands and into
  tooling — once the jig, the gauge, and the machine hold the
  accuracy, the work can be done by someone who never learned the
  trade, and a factory can be set up where no trade exists.
ROOT: measurement / accuracy held by an instrument need not be
  held by a person; teaching — what a tool guarantees does not
  have to be taught.
THREAD: craft (mastery relocated rather than destroyed), money
  (wages follow scarcity, and the scarce skill moved), power
  (a country can now buy an industry it never grew).
ASKED-AS: factory unskilled workers craft skill machines jigs training deskilling toolmaker production

ESSENCE: the assembly line moved the work to the worker — instead
  of a man walking around a machine gathering tools, the machine
  walks past him and he does one thing all day; the saving is
  almost entirely in the walking and the switching.
ROOT: the mind + engineering / changing task costs time and error,
  and repetition moves work into the fast automatic channel.
THREAD: teaching (skill climbs by repetition — here weaponised),
  money (unit cost falling with volume), craft (what mastery
  becomes when the task is one motion).
ASKED-AS: assembly line ford cars cheaper mass production repetitive factory work efficiency boring

ESSENCE: standard time was invented by railways, not by
  astronomers — every town kept its own noon by the sun until
  trains made local noon lethal, because two clocks a few minutes
  apart put two trains on one track.
ROOT: measurement / a shared standard is a treaty, and treaties
  are signed when the cost of not having one becomes visible.
THREAD: navigation (longitude and the clock — the same problem in
  its earlier form), computing (machines must agree what time it
  is before agreeing on anything), law (statutory time zones).
ASKED-AS: time zones railways invented local noon standard schedules trains clocks towns agreed

ESSENCE: the shipping container moved the port out of the city and
  the factory out of the country — when loading a ship stopped
  costing weeks of gang labour, distance stopped mattering enough
  to keep making things near where they are used.
ROOT: money / when a cost falls far enough it stops being a
  consideration at all, and every decision that once respected it
  gets re-made.
THREAD: movement of goods (the standard box: this is what that
  standard did to geography), people together (a trade and its
  community ending together), power (unions built on a chokepoint
  that vanished).
ASKED-AS: shipping containers ports docks jobs lost globalisation manufacturing moved overseas cranes

ESSENCE: clean water beat the doctors — the great fall in death
  came from sewers, filtered supplies, milk inspection, and
  drainage, most of it built before anyone could say what a germ
  was, and all of it invisible in any history of medicine.
ROOT: medicine / infection compounds, so preventing exposure beats
  treating cases by an enormous margin.
THREAD: money (prevention is cheap and unglamorous, and therefore
  underfunded), keeping knowledge (the works are invisible until
  they fail), power (a public good no individual can buy alone).
ASKED-AS: sanitation sewers clean water cholera life expectancy improved doctors medicine plumbing

ESSENCE: a correct idea without a mechanism gets refused — hand
  washing was shown to cut deaths sharply decades before germs
  were known, and it was rejected, because a finding with no story
  behind it looks like a coincidence and an insult.
ROOT: teaching / new knowledge holds only where it hooks onto what
  is already believed, and a result with no explanation has
  nothing to hook to.
THREAD: evidence (a finding needs replication AND a mechanism to
  travel), the mind (beliefs defend themselves), power (the
  messenger's rung decides the hearing).
ASKED-AS: handwashing doctors refused germ theory ignored evidence rejected mechanism why proof

ESSENCE: vaccination was the first medicine that acted before the
  disease — everything earlier treated the sick, and this trained
  a healthy body in advance, which is why its benefits are
  invisible: nothing happens, to enormous numbers of people.
ROOT: medicine / the immune system's self-list can be taught by a
  harmless rehearsal, and rehearsal is cheaper than the event.
THREAD: engineering (maintenance is the same invisible good),
  evidence (measuring an absence is hard), money (insurance pays
  for what does not happen).
ASKED-AS: vaccines smallpox polio eradicated prevention invisible benefit side effects herd immunity

ESSENCE: anaesthesia made surgery a craft instead of a race —
  before it, the only virtue in an operation was speed, and the
  procedures that could be done were the ones a screaming person
  could be held down for; painlessness opened the whole interior
  of the body to careful work.
ROOT: the body / pain is an alarm that cannot be reasoned with,
  and removing it removes a hard limit on what can be attempted.
THREAD: teaching (removing the panic is what allows the learning),
  engineering (a constraint lifted reveals the next constraint).
ASKED-AS: anaesthesia surgery pain operations history speed ether chloroform infection before modern

ESSENCE: antibiotics changed what death means — infection went
  from the ordinary end of an ordinary life to an event with a
  treatment, so a cut, a birth, or a chest cold stopped being
  possible deaths and the whole shape of a family's expectations
  changed with them.
ROOT: medicine / infection compounds, and a drug that kills the
  compounding turns an unbounded process into a bounded one.
THREAD: farming (selection running against us — the same law),
  chance (a resource everyone draws on and nobody owns), the human
  journey (childhood deaths were the normal case within living
  memory).
ASKED-AS: antibiotics penicillin infection death changed resistance superbugs childbirth survival before modern

ESSENCE: half the nitrogen in your body came out of a factory —
  plants need nitrogen in a usable form, the air is full of the
  useless form, and a process that forces the two together is what
  the world's population growth since has actually been eating.
ROOT: chemistry / the air's nitrogen is bound so tightly that only
  extreme heat and pressure will break it; farming — land grows
  exactly what its nutrients allow.
THREAD: farming (the soil bank, rented rather than restored),
  money (a dependency that looks like an efficiency), energy (food
  as a fossil-fuel product).
ASKED-AS: fertiliser nitrogen population fed haber factory crops yields chemical farming dependency

ESSENCE: reliable contraception made family size a decision, and
  the decision everywhere has been fewer — once a child's survival
  was near-certain and children stopped being farm labour,
  households shrank, women's working lives changed, and populations
  that had grown for centuries turned over within two generations.
ROOT: people respond to the odds they actually face; when child
  mortality falls, the old strategy of many births is no longer
  needed and is expensive.
THREAD: money (children as cost rather than labour), the human
  journey (the shape of a life rearranged around fewer, later
  children), power (an ageing population is a different state).
ASKED-AS: birth rate falling family size contraception population decline children fewer women work

ESSENCE: the tall city waited on two unglamorous parts — a steel
  frame so the walls need not hold the building up, and an
  elevator with a brake so the top floors are worth renting; both
  are prerequisites, and neither is what anyone photographs.
ROOT: building / a wall that carries the load must thicken faster
  than the height; a frame moves the load into a skeleton and
  frees the skin.
THREAD: engineering (a load path is a proof in steel), money (land
  value made vertical), cities (density is what the frame bought).
ASKED-AS: skyscrapers tall buildings steel frame elevator invented penthouse top floor cities

ESSENCE: the car's big effects were the second-order ones — it did
  not simply replace the horse; it made land far from the centre
  usable, which built the suburb, which then required the car, so
  the tool and the layout locked each other in.
ROOT: path dependence / whatever is adopted first gets built
  around, and a settlement pattern is the most expensive thing to
  unbuild.
THREAD: computing (the office rearranged itself around the
  machine, not the reverse), movement of goods (congestion as
  everyone's sensible choice colliding), farming (mechanisation
  emptying the countryside).
ASKED-AS: cars suburbs sprawl highways changed cities driving everywhere dependent horses replaced

ESSENCE: the transistor is one fact repeated — a switch with no
  moving parts, so switching became almost free and could be made
  almost arbitrarily small; every computer, phone, and network is
  that single component, copied by the billion.
ROOT: physics / certain materials pass or block current on
  command, and nothing physical has to travel to make the choice.
THREAD: computing (the bit needs a body, and this is the body),
  engineering (metal dies by repetition — a part with no motion
  escapes that death), money (a cost that fell by a factor of a
  billion re-makes every decision it touched).
ASKED-AS: transistor chips computers smaller cheaper silicon valves switches electronics moore invention

ESSENCE: the telegraph was the first time a message outran the
  messenger — for all of history before, news travelled at the
  speed of a horse or a ship, and afterwards a price, an order, or
  a warning arrived before the person carrying it could have set
  out.
ROOT: computing / information is not matter, so it can be recoded
  into something faster than any vehicle.
THREAD: money (markets converging into single prices), power (an
  empire run in something like real time), movement of goods
  (distance died for words long before it eased for freight).
ASKED-AS: telegraph invented news travel speed horse messages wire first instant communication distant

ESSENCE: when information stops respecting distance, local
  monopolies die — a merchant, a newspaper, a teacher, or a
  performer once had a market protected by the difficulty of
  reaching it, and when reaching it becomes free the mediocre local
  competes against the best in the world.
ROOT: money / a price holds only while alternatives are costly to
  reach; removing the cost of reach removes the protection.
THREAD: money (winner-take-all in anything copyable), music (a
  town's own players against a recording), teaching (the best
  lecture available to everyone, and the local one unfunded).
ASKED-AS: internet local shops competing online global best winner takes all distance connected

ESSENCE: broadcast made a crowd out of strangers — one voice

ROOT: people together / a crowd forms where attention is shared,

THREAD: power (control of what is known), persuasion (the pitch

ESSENCE: the census is a state learning to see — a government

ESSENCE: the lasting company needed an invented moment — closing
  the books on a chosen date and declaring a profit. Before that a

ROOT: money / a result exists only once the reckoning is done, and

ESSENCE: the company with limited liability is a legal machine for

ROOT: law / a fiction with teeth — an entity that can own, owe,

THREAD: money (capital pooled far past any family's reach), law

ESSENCE: a patent trades secrecy for a monopoly — publish how it

ROOT: computing / information copies freely, so scarcity in it

ESSENCE: knowledge became cumulative when it became public and

ESSENCE: mass schooling was a state project before it was a

ESSENCE: war is the great adopter — it suspends the cost test, so
  things that were too expensive, too unproven, or too disruptive
  get built in a hurry and then never go away: radar, jet engines,
  mass antibiotic production, computing, containers.
ROOT: money / adoption follows cost, and wartime moves the cost
  test from profit to survival.
THREAD: engineering (a constraint lifted reveals the next),
  medicine (production scale as the real breakthrough), strategy
  (the second-order effects outlast the war).

ESSENCE: knowledge ratchets — each generation starts where the
  last one stopped, which is why change accelerates while
  individual people do not get cleverer; the gains are stored
  outside the head, in tools, texts, and trained hands.
ROOT: keeping knowledge / writing carries a mind's contents past
  its death; teaching — but only what is actually re-taught.
THREAD: teaching (the whole field is this mechanism), craft
  (apprenticeship carries what texts cannot), the living world
