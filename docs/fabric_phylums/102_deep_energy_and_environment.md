# 102 DEEP ENERGY AND ENVIRONMENT — power and its consequences
Where physics meets a bill and a sky. Energy obeys file 01's laws
without exception; what this floor adds is what those laws cost,
who pays, and when the payment falls due. Every entry carries its
cannot-twin.

ESSENCE: fuels rank by how much energy they pack per kilogram and
  per litre, and that ranking, not preference, decides what each
  can do: uranium is millions of times denser than coal, diesel
  some fifty times denser than the best battery.
ROOT: physics / energy is conserved and comes in stocks — a store
  has a size, and the size is a fact about the substance.
CANNOT: no long flight on a dilute store — a battery airliner is
  not merely undeveloped, it is forbidden by the ladder at any
  chemistry we have; the machine would be all tank and no cabin.
THREAD: engineering (every vehicle is a fight against its own
  weight), farming (grain and hay are dense and bulky food — the
  same ladder decides what an animal can carry).
ASKED-AS: petrol diesel battery range electric car plane fuel tank heavy energy pack

ESSENCE: a vehicle carries its own energy, so every kilogram of
  store is a kilogram of cargo forgone — and liquid fuel grows
  lighter as it burns while a battery weighs the same when empty as
  when full.
ROOT: energy density (this file) — the penalty is paid on every
  mile, not once at purchase.
CANNOT: no battery that lightens as it empties — the charge leaves
  and the mass stays, because electrons weigh essentially nothing.
  A plane that lands is a plane still carrying its whole store.
THREAD: flight (a jet is a third fuel at takeoff and lands light),
  logistics (the return leg of an empty truck), the body (a
  long-distance animal burns its own fat and gets lighter as it
  goes).
ASKED-AS: electric truck ship plane weight cargo empty battery full charge miles heavy

ESSENCE: electricity is not stored in the wires — what is made and
  what is used must match each other second by second across an
  entire continent, or the whole network's rhythm drifts and
  machines start dropping off to save themselves.
ROOT: physics / energy is conserved — a wire is a path, never a
  warehouse, so the books must balance continuously.
CANNOT: no shipping electricity to next Tuesday. And no grid
  running on a market that clears once a day: a system that must
  balance every second needs a controller acting every second.
THREAD: money (a bank that must settle continuously rather than
  nightly), the body (blood pressure held every heartbeat, with no
  reserve tank), traffic control.
ASKED-AS: power cut blackout grid frequency demand supply electricity outage lights surge balance

ESSENCE: storage is the hard problem because it must be sized for
  the worst gap, not the average one — hours are cheap, days are
  dear, and the rare two-week calm in midwinter is what sets the
  bill.
ROOT: the grid must balance instantly (this file) — a store is the
  only thing that breaks the instant-match requirement.
CANNOT: no store sized by the average — a system covering a typical
  week fails in the atypical one, and the atypical one is the whole
  reason the store exists. Averages do not fail; extremes do.
THREAD: farming (a granary is built for the bad year), money (an
  emergency fund is not sized by an ordinary month), engineering
  (design load is the worst case, never the usual one).
ASKED-AS: battery storage grid backup calm windless winter days weeks store power expensive

ESSENCE: a source running a quarter of the time is not a quarter of
  a plant — it is a whole plant plus something else to cover the
  other three quarters, so its honest price includes the cost of
  what stands behind it.
ROOT: the grid must balance instantly (this file) — a source that
  cannot be summoned is a source that must be accompanied.
CANNOT: no averaging away a common cause — a thousand panels under
  one cloud front are one panel, and a whole country's turbines can
  stand still together for days; scattering the machines does not
  scatter the weather.
THREAD: money (a portfolio of shares that all fall together is one
  share), aviation (spare systems that share a fuel feed), chance
  (independence is a claim about the world, not arithmetic).
ASKED-AS: solar cloudy night wind still calm backup gas plant intermittent unreliable weather covers

ESSENCE: the true scarce quality in a power system is not cheapness
  but nimbleness — how fast a source can change its output — and
  the old pairing of steady demand with unbudgeable plants was a
  marriage of two rigidities, not a virtue.
ROOT: the grid must balance instantly (this file) — matching a
  moving target is a question about speed of change.
CANNOT: no system built only of the inflexible — a nuclear plant
  cannot follow a cloud, and a grid of nothing but cheap unbudging
  power needs expensive nimble power beside it to be usable at all.
THREAD: business (fixed costs and the value of being able to turn
  down), the body (a resting heart rate is useless without the
  ability to climb), military logistics (reserves are speed).
ASKED-AS: base load nuclear coal steady turn down peak plant flexible ramp night demand

ESSENCE: one device decided the map of industry — because voltage
  can be traded up for the journey and back down at the door, the
  power station could be built at the coal mine and the light
  delivered to a town a hundred miles away.
ROOT: deep heat and electricity (85) / a wire's loss climbs with
  the square of the current — so a high push and a thin flow crosses
  distance that a low push cannot.
CANNOT: no long-distance power without a way to change voltage —
  sent out at household push, useful power dies within a mile or
  two, which is why the argument over which kind of current to use
  was settled by which one could be traded, and why places with
  generators but no such network still have no reach.
THREAD: shipping (containers made distance cheap and moved every
  factory), water (mains pressure deciding how far a town spreads),
  language (a compressed message crosses further for less).
ASKED-AS: power lines pylons high voltage transformer substation distance electricity wires far station

ESSENCE: every step from source to service keeps only part of what
  it was handed, and the fractions multiply — five steps at ninety
  percent leave fifty-nine, so a chain of good links is a poor
  system.
ROOT: engineering (04) / each link keeps only some — this floor
  runs the arithmetic to the end of the chain.
CANNOT: no chain better than the product of its links. And so the
  most valuable place to save is the last step, at the point of
  use: one unit not needed in a room is several units not dug out
  of the ground, because the whole upstream chain is spared.
THREAD: money (fees compounding through a chain of intermediaries),
  cooking (every transfer loses a little), teaching (a lesson
  passed through many retellings).
ASKED-AS: efficiency wasted energy chain power station wires charger bulb percent lost useful savings

ESSENCE: the energy books always close at a thermometer — whatever
  anyone does with energy, the trail ends as warmth in the
  surroundings, so a building's whole electricity bill leaves it as
  heat and a city's energy use and its heat output are one number.
ROOT: deep heat and electricity (85) / energy is never lost but
  always devalued — this floor uses that fact as an accountant does.
CANNOT: no energy use without a heat footprint, which makes waste
  visible and therefore auditable — a warm wall, a hot cable or a
  warm component is a bill being paid, and a thermal camera reads a
  building's failings faster than any survey. Nothing hides here.
THREAD: money (following payments to where they finally rest), the
  body (a person at rest is a hundred-watt heater, which is why a
  full room warms), computing (a data centre is a furnace).
ASKED-AS: warm room computer hot laptop server cooling bill heat camera leaking walls waste

ESSENCE: energy machines last decades, so today's sale fixes
  tomorrow's emissions — a boiler for fifteen years, a car for
  twenty, a power station for forty, a building for eighty — and
  the whole fleet can only change as fast as it wears out.
ROOT: engineering (04) / a wrong choice is cheap today and ruinous
  after everything is built on it — here the lock-in is physical.
CANNOT: no fast change of a slow stock — even if every new machine
  sold from tomorrow were clean, the existing ones keep running and
  emitting for their natural lives, so the only ways to go faster
  are paying to scrap things early or never building them wrong.
THREAD: cities (a street plan outliving every building on it),
  farming (an orchard planted for a climate thirty years out),
  policy (a standard's effect arriving a decade after the vote).
ASKED-AS: old boiler cars replace fleet years lifetime new build lasts decades slow change locked

ESSENCE: coal, oil and gas are ancient sunlight — a few hundred
  million summers of plant growth buried before it could rot and
  pressed into a store; we are spending in a century what took an
  age to accumulate.
ROOT: living world (31) / all energy is sunlight in some costume —
  these are simply its oldest, best-hoarded form.
CANNOT: no renewal on any human schedule — the store is not scarce,
  it is unrepayable, since nothing today buries carbon at the rate
  that buried theirs. And no clean exit by simply ceasing to burn:
  the same barrel is also our plastics, fertiliser and asphalt.
THREAD: farming (mining a soil's built-up fertility in a few
  seasons), money (spending a legacy and calling it income), deep
  earth (the geology that did the burying).
ASKED-AS: oil coal gas fossil formed dinosaurs millions years running out plastic fertiliser burn

ESSENCE: splitting a heavy nucleus releases millions of times what
  burning a carbon atom does, so the fuel is trivially small and so
  is the waste — but the waste is dangerous, and its danger and its
  lifetime pull in opposite directions.
ROOT: chemistry (101) / chemistry cannot reach the nucleus — this
  is the other floor, where the energy is a million times larger.
CANNOT: no neutralising the waste — nothing chemical touches it, so
  the answers are shielding, distance and time. And no waste both
  fierce and eternal: what is intensely radioactive is short-lived,
  and what lasts millennia is correspondingly faint.
THREAD: medicine (the same isotopes, used deliberately), law (a
  liability that outlives every company that made it), storage (a
  warning that must be readable in ten thousand years).
ASKED-AS: nuclear power waste uranium reactor radiation storage safe years dangerous fuel rods

ESSENCE: fusion's problem is not the fuel or the physics but the
  holding — the mixture must be hot enough, dense enough, and held
  long enough, all three at once, and every way of buying one of
  those spends the other two.
ROOT: physics / opposites pull and likes repel — two nuclei must be
  forced together against their own violent refusal.
CANNOT: no fusion in a container that touches it — nothing solid
  survives those temperatures, so the fuel must be held by magnetic
  fields or crushed faster than it can flee. The sun solves this
  with its own weight, and weight is the one tool we cannot build.
THREAD: farming (three conditions that must coincide for a crop),
  medicine (a treatment needing dose, duration and delivery all at
  once), cooking (heat, time and moisture traded against each other).
ASKED-AS: fusion reactor sun hydrogen hot plasma magnetic thirty years away energy future clean

ESSENCE: sunlight arrives already spread out — about a kilowatt on
  a square metre at noon and far less on the year's average — so
  every solar plan is a land plan; the fuel is free and the acreage
  and the material are not.
ROOT: physics / brightness thins with distance — the sun's output is
  vast and its delivery here is thin.
CANNOT: no panel producing more than the sunlight that lands on it,
  so no shrinking the farm by improving the cell past its own hard
  ceiling. Dilute sources trade a fuel bill for an area bill, and
  the area bill cannot be waived.
THREAD: farming (a field is a solar collector at about one percent
  efficiency, which is why food needs so much land), cities (roofs
  are the free acreage), building (shade is a real asset).
ASKED-AS: solar panels roof farm land space acres sunlight efficiency free power area needed

ESSENCE: a wind turbine's power rises with the cube of the wind
  speed — so a site with a quarter more wind gives about twice the
  power, and half the wind gives an eighth.
ROOT: physics / moving mass carries punch — both the amount of air
  arriving and the speed it carries scale together, three times over.
CANNOT: no useful power from a light breeze — not weak, effectively
  nothing; and no turbine taking all the wind either, since a blade
  that stopped the air completely would leave stopped air in the
  way of the next of it. The ceiling is about three fifths, proven,
  and no design passes it.
THREAD: driving (air drag rising the same steep way, which is why
  motorway speed costs fuel), swimming and rowing, money (any law
  where the average is a poor summary because the tail dominates).
ASKED-AS: wind turbine breeze speed power windy site offshore blades still day tall towers

ESSENCE: hydro is the one big cheap store we have — it generates
  and stores with the same equipment, and can be summoned in
  seconds — but it demands a gift of geography: height, flow, and a
  valley that may be flooded.
ROOT: physics / gravity is a bank — water raised is energy parked,
  and the parking is free once the wall stands.
CANNOT: no dam without a valley — this is the one source that
  cannot be manufactured, only found, and the best sites in most
  countries were taken a century ago. Nothing about it scales with
  cleverness.
THREAD: cities (settlements pinned to where the geography allowed),
  farming (irrigation and power competing for the same water),
  power (this is the nimble partner that the inflexible need).
ASKED-AS: dam hydro reservoir river valley flooded lake power water height turbine pumped

ESSENCE: a battery's ceiling is set by the chemistry inside it —
  the pull between its two materials and the weight of the atoms
  ferrying charge — and lithium sits near the top of the table
  because it is the lightest metal that will do the job.
ROOT: chemistry (101) / oxidation is a transaction — a battery is
  that transaction run slowly and made to pay out through a wire.
CANNOT: no battery beating the reaction it is built on, and no
  lighter metal to move to, so today's gains come from packing,
  cost and life rather than from a new element. And no charging
  faster than the ions can cross — hurry them and they plate up as
  metal spikes instead.
THREAD: the body (a muscle's own store, sized the same way),
  money (a technology near its physical ceiling competes on price),
  materials (the mining that a battery world implies).
ASKED-AS: battery lithium charge phone car capacity fast charging degrade cycles cheaper better limit

ESSENCE: hydrogen is the best fuel there is by weight and nearly
  the worst by volume — its virtue and its problem are the same
  fact of being the smallest molecule, which is why it must be
  crushed to hundreds of atmospheres or chilled near absolute cold
  simply to be carried.
ROOT: chemistry (101) / a gas fills its container and pushes back —
  and the smaller the molecule, the harder the container's job.
CANNOT: no cheap hydrogen tank — the containment costs more than
  the contents, the gas leaks through metals that hold everything
  else, and it makes the steel it passes through brittle. The
  difficulty is intrinsic, not a matter of engineering effort.
THREAD: shipping (a cargo whose packaging outweighs it), cooking
  (the same problem storing a gas as a liquid at home), chemistry
  (the largest current use is making fertiliser, not driving cars).
ASKED-AS: hydrogen fuel cell cars tank pressure leak store cold liquid green future clean

ESSENCE: the cheapest unit of energy is the one never needed —
  insulation and draught-sealing work every hour for decades with
  no fuel, no moving parts and no attention, and nothing else in
  this field can say that.
ROOT: physics / heat flows down a gap in proportion to the gap and
  the path — a wall is a resistance, and resistance is buyable.
CANNOT: no insulation stopping heat, only slowing it — every house
  reaches the outside temperature given enough time, and no
  thickness is infinity. What is bought is time, not immunity.
THREAD: politics (nobody cuts a ribbon on a wall cavity, which is
  why the cheapest option is the chronically unfunded one), the
  body (a coat is this, worn), money (a cost avoided beats income).
ASKED-AS: insulation loft draught windows double glazing heating bill warm house cheap savings wall

ESSENCE: making a service cheaper makes people buy more of it, so
  part of every efficiency saving is spent straight back — brighter
  and longer-lit rooms, bigger and heavier cars, more miles driven
  in the thriftier one.
ROOT: money / a price change moves behaviour — efficiency is a
  discount wearing a technical costume.
CANNOT: no saving guaranteed by efficiency alone — without a cap or
  a price, an efficiency gain reduces cost per use and not total
  use, and in the long history of lighting every gain in efficiency
  has been followed by more light bought, not less energy spent.
THREAD: roads (a new lane fills with new traffic), medicine (a safer
  procedure gets done more often), time (a faster tool inviting more
  work rather than more rest).
ASKED-AS: led bulbs cheaper bills bigger car more miles savings vanish efficient using more usage

ESSENCE: smoke is free to the one making it and costly to everyone
  downwind — and a price that leaves out a real cost is not a low
  price, it is a wrong one, so the market obediently makes the
  wrong choice with perfect efficiency.
ROOT: money / a price is a signal, and a signal missing a term
  points the wrong way with full confidence.
CANNOT: no right decision from a wrong price. And no volunteering
  out of it at scale: a producer who alone bears a cost nobody
  bills is simply undercut by one who does not, so goodwill loses
  to arithmetic unless the arithmetic is changed.
THREAD: law (the whole point of a rule here is to move a cost back
  onto its maker), accounting (a cost kept off the books is not a
  cost avoided), fishing and grazing.
ASKED-AS: pollution factory smoke cost cheap price downstream neighbours cleanup who pays fines

ESSENCE: a sink that is free and shared gets used until it is
  ruined, because each user takes the whole benefit of one more
  load and bears only a share of the harm — the arithmetic of the
  individual is sound, which is exactly what makes it a trap rather
  than a mistake.
ROOT: pollution is a cost pushed outward (this file) — an unowned
  sink is where all such costs collect.
CANNOT: no fixing it by persuasion — appeals change who feels
  guilty, never the arithmetic, so the cures are the ones that move
  numbers: a price, a quota, an owner, or an enforced limit. And no
  unowned resource stays plentiful merely because it is vast.
THREAD: fishing (the collapse of every open fishery), grazing,
  people together (the free rider), law (why some things are made
  property precisely to stop this).
ASKED-AS: overfishing shared air river dumping everyone free nobody owns ruined limits quota

ESSENCE: the air's carbon is a stock, not a flow — the warming
  answers to the total accumulated, so halving what we add each
  year still adds; the level falls only when the drain beats the
  tap, and this drain takes centuries.
ROOT: mathematics / a level is the sum of a flow — the bathtub is
  the whole of it, and it is arithmetic, not opinion.
CANNOT: no falling level while the tap runs above the drain — this
  is why cuts that slow the rise do not reverse it, and why "net
  zero" names the point where the level merely stops climbing, not
  where anything is undone.
THREAD: money (a debt with payments below the interest still grows),
  the body (weight as a running total of a small daily surplus),
  water (a reservoir judged by inflow against outflow).
ASKED-AS: carbon dioxide atmosphere reduce emissions net zero level rising cut half still

ESSENCE: methane is the exception that proves the stock law — a
  strong warmer that the air scrubs out within about a decade, so
  cutting it lowers the level rather than merely slowing its rise;
  it is a flow problem sitting inside a stock problem.
ROOT: carbon is a stock (this file) — the difference is entirely
  the length of the drain.
CANNOT: no permanent gain from methane cuts alone — the fast relief
  is real and it is one-off, since a gas that leaves quickly also
  stops helping quickly once the cuts stop. It buys time and buys
  nothing else.
THREAD: medicine (a fast-acting drug versus a slow cure), money (a
  short-term loan repaid quickly next to a long mortgage), farming
  (cattle, rice paddies and leaking wells are the sources).
ASKED-AS: methane cows gas leaks fracking short lived powerful warming quick fix decade slows

ESSENCE: the response to a push is not the push — some effects feed
  back and amplify, as melting ice bares dark water that absorbs
  more heat and melts more ice; others feed back and damp, as a
  warmer planet radiates harder to space.
ROOT: physics / a system with a loop from its output to its input
  moves by its own account after the shove.
CANNOT: no reading a system's future from the size of the push
  alone — the loops set the answer, and where the amplifying loops
  win, a small nudge produces a large and self-sustaining move.
THREAD: money (a bank run: fear causing the thing feared), the mind
  (a spiral of worry), engineering (a controller that steers
  against error, or a howling microphone that steers with it).
ASKED-AS: ice melting dark water absorbs more warming loop spiral amplify feedback runaway snowball

ESSENCE: the harm arrives long after the act — the ocean takes up
  heat for decades, so today's weather reports emissions long past
  and today's emissions will be felt by people not yet born; the
  system is committed before it is visible.
ROOT: physics / water resists changing temperature more than almost
  anything — the sea is the planet's flywheel and its delay line.
CANNOT: no learning by waiting for the evidence in a lagged system
  — by the time the signal is unmistakable the cause is decades
  old and unretractable, so steering must be done on the model, not
  on the thermometer.
THREAD: medicine (a disease with a long latency: the harm is done
  before the symptom), driving (a heavy vehicle steered by
  anticipation), money (a policy whose effects land after the term).
ASKED-AS: warming delay decades already committed pipeline ocean heat too late act now lag

ESSENCE: some systems hold two stable states with a threshold
  between, and pushed past it they move on their own and do not
  come back when the push stops — the route out is not the route in.
ROOT: feedbacks amplify or damp (this file) — a tipping point is
  the place where an amplifying loop takes over from a damping one.
CANNOT: no gentle reversal of a crossed threshold — an ice sheet
  does not regrow at the temperature that melted it, a collapsed
  fishery does not refill when the boats leave, and restoring the
  old conditions does not restore the old state. The asymmetry is
  the whole danger.
THREAD: health (the fall that starts a decline; the addiction),
  business (a reputation lost and not bought back at the old
  price), oceans (a circulation that could stall and stay stalled).
ASKED-AS: tipping point ice sheet collapse no going back permanent threshold sudden irreversible reverse

ESSENCE: mitigation is shared and adaptation is kept — build a sea
  wall and your town gets the whole benefit; cut your emissions and
  the benefit is spread thin across the world and the future.
ROOT: the unpriced sink (this file) — the same asymmetry that makes
  the sink a trap makes the two responses behave differently.
CANNOT: no adaptation to an unbounded change — a wall is built to a
  height, a crop bred for a climate, and both are bets on a target
  that stops moving; against a change that keeps going, adaptation
  buys years and never a settlement.
THREAD: war (defence is local, peace is shared), medicine
  (treatment versus prevention, and which one gets funded), money
  (private return versus public return).
ASKED-AS: sea wall flood defence adapt cope cut emissions prevent local global spending choose

ESSENCE: energy use and human welfare climb together until quite
  high up, so asking the poorest to use less is asking them to stay
  poor — and no argument outvotes tonight's cooking fire.
ROOT: living world (31) / living is paid-for order — a household's
  energy is food, warmth, light, and the ability to work.
CANNOT: no household choosing a distant climate over a present
  need. So there is no path that runs through restraint by the
  poor; the only durable route is making the clean option the
  cheapest one, because price is the only argument that reaches
  every kitchen without being made.
THREAD: history (every rich country got rich on the dirty path
  first), medicine (clean cooking is a health question before it is
  a climate one), fairness (who spent the stock, and who is asked).
ASKED-AS: poor countries development coal india africa fair cheap energy growth emissions blame right

ESSENCE: energy and power are two different quantities constantly
  confused — energy is how much is stored, power is how fast it can
  move; a battery has both numbers, they cost different money, and
  a system can be rich in one and starved of the other.
ROOT: physics / power is energy per unit of time — a rate and a
  quantity are never the same kind of thing.
CANNOT: no reading a store's usefulness from its size alone — a
  large slow store cannot cover a sudden surge and a small fast one
  cannot cover a long night, so the two must be bought separately
  and the wrong purchase fails in a way the badge never showed.
THREAD: money (wealth against income — the same confusion, equally
  expensive), water (a big tank with a thin pipe), the body
  (endurance and sprint are separate systems).
ASKED-AS: kilowatt hours watts battery size charging speed how much how fast confusing units bill

ESSENCE: a source must return more energy than it took to build,
  fuel and run — and the size of that surplus decides how much of
  society is left over for everything that is not energy.
ROOT: physics / energy bookkeeping — an energy source is itself an
  energy project, and its books must close.
CANNOT: no civilisation on a source that barely repays itself — at
  a return near one, the whole economy is employed making energy;
  the surplus, not the output, is what funds hospitals and schools.
  And no ignoring the build cost of anything fuel-free: its whole
  price is paid up front.
THREAD: farming (a crop must return more calories than the farming
  spent — the same test, and the reason draught animals ate the
  fields), money (a business whose costs eat its revenue).
ASKED-AS: energy return worth building payback cost make panel turbine mining net gain surplus

ESSENCE: a plant's badge is its best hour, not its year — the
  honest number is what fraction of the badge it actually delivers
  over time, and that fraction runs from about a fifth for a solar
  farm to nine tenths for a nuclear one.
ROOT: evidence (36) / a number's meaning is set by how it was
  measured — nameplate and delivered are two different measurements.
CANNOT: no comparing two sources by their badges — a megawatt of
  one is not a megawatt of another, and a headline counting
  installed capacity has not counted energy at all. The badge is a
  ceiling, and ceilings do not add up.
THREAD: business (headline capacity against actual sales), sport
  (top speed versus race average), medicine (best-case efficacy
  versus what happens in ordinary use).
ASKED-AS: megawatt capacity installed actual output average headline compare gigawatt real delivered badge

ESSENCE: a power system is built for the worst hour of the year and
  idles through the rest — so most of its cost is standing there
  ready, and moving demand off that hour is worth more than adding
  supply for it.
ROOT: the grid must balance instantly (this file) — the peak is
  where balance is hardest and therefore where the money goes.
CANNOT: no cheap peak — the plant that runs a hundred hours a year
  must earn its whole cost in those hours, so the last few percent
  of demand carries a price out of all proportion to its size.
THREAD: transport (a road built for rush hour, empty at noon),
  medicine (a hospital sized for its worst night), restaurants
  (staffed for the busiest service and idle at three o'clock).
ASKED-AS: peak demand evening everyone kettle rush hour tariff cheaper night shift usage expensive

ESSENCE: anything built once and run for forty years is priced by
  the cost of money, not by the cost of the machine — a change in
  interest rates moves the price of nuclear, wind, solar and hydro
  more than most engineering ever will.
ROOT: money / the future is discounted — a project whose whole cost
  is up front is a loan wearing a technical costume.
CANNOT: no cheap fuel-free power under expensive money — the very
  thing that makes these sources attractive, having no fuel bill,
  is what makes them hostages to the lending rate. And no comparing
  a fuel-burning plant to a fuel-free one without naming the rate.
THREAD: housing (a mortgage: the same house at two rates is two
  prices), farming (an orchard is a decade of cost before a crop),
  infrastructure generally.
ASKED-AS: interest rates cost build nuclear expensive loan finance upfront cheap fuel free years

ESSENCE: cost falls by a fairly steady percentage for every
  doubling of the total ever built — so deployment, not research
  alone, is what makes a technology cheap, and the early expensive
  units are the tuition.
ROOT: craft (29) / skill is built by repetition — an industry learns
  the way a hand does, by doing the thing many times.
CANNOT: no cheapness without volume — a technology that is never
  built at scale stays expensive whatever its promise, so waiting
  for the price to fall before buying is waiting for something that
  only buying causes.
THREAD: manufacturing (the whole story of cars, chips and panels),
  medicine (a surgeon's tenth operation and their thousandth),
  teaching (the cost of the first lesson and the hundredth).
ASKED-AS: solar got cheap panels price falling batteries costs learning scale mass production subsidy

ESSENCE: low-carbon does not mean low-material — a wind turbine is
  mostly steel and concrete, a solar farm is glass, aluminium and
  land, and a battery is mined metal; the fuel bill is replaced by
  a mining bill paid at the start.
ROOT: physics / a dilute source needs a large collector — thin
  energy must be gathered by a lot of matter.
CANNOT: no energy source without a footprint — every one takes
  land, materials and a mine somewhere, so the honest question is
  never whether there is harm but which harm, how much, and borne
  by whom.
THREAD: mining (the impact moves upstream and often abroad, which
  is how it becomes invisible), farming (the same trade: yield
  bought with inputs), fairness (who lives near the mine).
ASKED-AS: mining lithium cobalt steel concrete turbines clean energy dirty materials impact land footprint

ESSENCE: waste heat is usable only close to where it is made,
  because heat leaks out of any pipe over distance — so a power
  station beside a town can warm it and the same station in a field
  can only warm a river.
ROOT: heat is worth its gap (this file) — every metre of pipe
  spends some of the gap, and the gap is the whole value.
CANNOT: no exporting warmth far — heat cannot be shipped the way
  electricity or fuel can, which is why the decision that fixes
  whether this resource exists at all is where the plant is put,
  made once, decades before anyone wants the heat.
THREAD: cities (district heating in the countries that planned for
  it), industry (factories sited beside each other to trade steam),
  cooking (why a kitchen warms a house and not a street).
ASKED-AS: district heating hot water pipes waste heat factory power station town warm reuse nearby

ESSENCE: a thermal power station is a water user before it is a
  fuel user — coal, gas and nuclear all boil water and must then
  cool it, so a drought or a hot river is an electricity problem.
ROOT: heat needs somewhere colder to go (this file) — the cold end
  of every engine has to be a real place, and usually it is a river.
CANNOT: no thermal plant without a cooling sink — plants shut down
  in heatwaves precisely when demand for cooling peaks, which is
  the worst possible correlation and an entirely predictable one.
THREAD: farming (irrigation and power competing for one river),
  cities (water and energy planned as if separate), climate (the
  failure arrives on the hottest day, by design).
ASKED-AS: cooling water river drought heatwave power station shut nuclear hot summer supply demand

ESSENCE: energy transitions have historically been additions, not
  replacements — the world burns more wood now than in any previous
  century, and coal use grew through the whole age of oil; new
  sources have piled on top rather than displaced.
ROOT: history / demand grows with wealth — a new source meets new
  appetite before it meets old habit.
CANNOT: no automatic retirement of an old source by a new one —
  something must actively close the old plant, since nothing in the
  arrival of a cheaper option shuts a plant already built and paid
  for, whose fuel is its only remaining cost.
THREAD: technology (paper survived the computer; radio survived
  television), farming (the tractor did not end the horse for
  decades), policy (closure is a decision, never a consequence).
ASKED-AS: coal still growing wood burned replaced oil transition adding new energy old plants close

ESSENCE: the same fires do two harms on two clocks — soot and
  fumes that kill people locally within days, and carbon that warms
  the world for centuries; the local harm is nearer, better proven,
  and easier to act on.
ROOT: pollution is a cost pushed outward (this file) — two costs,
  two distances, one chimney.
CANNOT: no clean-up that helps only one — cutting the burning cuts
  both, so the health case can pay for the climate case, and a
  policy that argues only the distant harm is throwing away its
  stronger half.
THREAD: medicine (indoor smoke as a leading killer in poor
  households), cities (the smogs that forced the first clean air
  laws, decades before anyone spoke of climate), persuasion (a
  present, local benefit persuades where a distant one cannot).
ASKED-AS: air quality smog soot asthma lungs deaths pollution cities coal fires health climate

ESSENCE: the ozone hole is the case that got fixed, and it teaches
  what tractable looks like — a handful of manufacturers, a
  substitute available, a harm that was measured and named, and a
  treaty with a schedule.
ROOT: the unpriced sink (this file) — the same trap, with all four
  conditions for escape present at once.
CANNOT: no reading the same ease into the carbon problem — carbon
  has billions of sources, no drop-in substitute for most of them,
  and its harm arrives lagged and diffuse, so the comparison is a
  measure of the difference and not a promise.
THREAD: medicine (the diseases we eliminated share exactly these
  features), law (a rule works where the actors are few and
  identifiable), evidence (the harm had to be measured first).
ASKED-AS: ozone hole aerosols cfcs banned fixed treaty worked why carbon harder compare success

ESSENCE: emissions are counted where things are made, not where
  they are used — so a country that closes its steelworks and buys
  the steel abroad has exported its emissions and reported a cut.
ROOT: accounting (70) / a boundary decides what a number means —
  move the boundary and the same activity changes sides.
CANNOT: no global cut from a local reassignment — the air does not
  read national accounts, so an honest figure must count what a
  country consumes as well as what it produces, and the two
  numbers differ by a great deal for rich importers.
THREAD: money (transfer pricing: profit booked wherever it is
  cheapest), law (a rule at a border makes the border a business),
  measurement (choose the frame and you have chosen the answer).
ASKED-AS: emissions country made abroad imports china factories moved counted cuts offshore steel goods

ESSENCE: a payment counts as a reduction only if it changed what
  would otherwise have happened — and what would otherwise have
  happened is a claim about a world that never ran, so it cannot be
  measured, only argued.
ROOT: evidence (36) / a cause is shown by comparison against a
  control — and here there is no control to look at.
CANNOT: no verifying an offset by inspection — you can confirm the
  forest stands and still not know whether it was ever going to be
  cut, which is why paying for what was going to happen anyway is
  the standard failure and why it keeps being rediscovered.
THREAD: charity (the same question about any donation), medicine (a
  treatment credited with a recovery that would have come anyway),
  business (a marketing spend claiming sales it did not cause).
