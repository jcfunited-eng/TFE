# 84 DEEP MOTION AND FORCE — how things move and resist
The floors between physics' roots and the everyday world of levers,
spins, fluids, and breaking things. Essences only, never data. Every
entry carries its cannot-twin.

ESSENCE: friction has two settings — a stiff one that must be broken
  to start sliding, a weaker one that holds once moving. So motion
  begins with a lurch, and a slow pull stutters: grab, slip, grab.
ROOT: physics / motion persists — resting surfaces settle into each
  other's dents and must be lifted out first.
CANNOT: no smooth departure from rest, and no steady creep in the
  stuck regime — stick-slip is compulsory; chalk screeches on it.
THREAD: driving (a locked wheel slides on the weaker setting — the
  reason anti-lock brakes exist), music (a bow sings in that gap).
ASKED-AS: squeaky door stuck drawer dragging couch chalkboard brakes squeal jerky slipping

ESSENCE: mass is how much a thing refuses to be pushed; weight is
  only what a nearby planet charges for holding it. On the moon the
  crate weighs a sixth — and stopping it hurts exactly as much.
ROOT: physics / F = m·a for the refusal, gravity for the rent.
CANNOT: no losing mass by leaving a planet, and no gentle catch of a
  floating anvil — weightless is not massless; it breaks the hand.
THREAD: spaceflight (fuel budgeted by mass, never by weight), sport
  (a heavy bat is slow to swing anywhere in the universe).
ASKED-AS: kilograms pounds scale moon astronaut floating heavy weightless space bathroom shove

ESSENCE: every object acts as if all its weight hung at one point,
  and it topples the instant a straight drop from that point lands
  outside the ground it stands on. Not sooner, not later.
ROOT: physics / gravity, plus support that can only push up where
  the object actually touches.
CANNOT: no tipping while the point stays over the base — a leaning
  tower stands centuries on that. And no recovery once it steps out.
THREAD: martial arts (a throw walks the point off someone's feet),
  walking (you fall forward and catch it — a controlled topple).
ASKED-AS: tipping over falling chair leaning ladder truck rollover balance stacking blocks wobbly

ESSENCE: force alone cannot turn anything. Turning is force times how
  far out from the pivot it lands, so the same arm on a longer wrench
  is a stronger arm — and a push through the hinge does nothing.
ROOT: physics / F = m·a wrapped round a circle; reach is half the sum.
CANNOT: no turning from a force aimed through the pivot, at any
  strength. No freeing a seized bolt by gripping harder — only farther.
THREAD: doors (handles at the far edge, never near the hinge), the
  body (tendons attach close in, so muscles buy motion with force).
ASKED-AS: wrench bolt tight door handle hinge lug nuts cheater pipe leverage twist

ESSENCE: spin is stored as an amount AND a direction, and both are
  kept. Tilting a spinning wheel means rewriting a stored direction,
  so the faster it turns the stiffer it feels.
ROOT: physics / motion persists, applied to turning instead of travel.
CANNOT: no stiffness in a stopped wheel — nothing stored, nothing
  resists. And none for free: it was all paid for at spin-up.
THREAD: shooting (rifling buys it for a bullet), spacecraft (spun
  wheels are a ship's only handhold on nothing at all).
ASKED-AS: gyroscope spinning wheel bicycle stays upright toy stable frisbee steady wobble fast

ESSENCE: a spring pushes back in proportion to how far it is bent —
  twice the stretch, twice the pull. That plain proportion is why
  things oscillate: the overshoot is always the same fraction of home.
ROOT: chemistry / bonds are electric springs, true right up to yield.
CANNOT: no bouncing without a restoring force — something must want
  the old shape back. And no proportion past yield: both end there.
THREAD: clocks (a balance wheel is a spring keeping time), the body
  (tendons store a runner's step and hand most of it back).
ASKED-AS: spring mattress bouncy trampoline diving board scale stretch rubber band coil suspension

ESSENCE: pressure in a still fluid is set by depth and nothing else —
  not by how much fluid there is, nor by the shape of the container.
  A thimbleful in a tall pipe presses as hard as an ocean that deep.
ROOT: flight and fluids / pressure is the weight above, shared all ways.
CANNOT: no wide tank pressing harder than a narrow one at equal
  depth, and no squeezing a trapped fluid in one spot only — the push
  appears everywhere, which is the whole trick of hydraulics.
THREAD: dams (thickness set by depth, never by the lake's length),
  jacks and brakes (a small hand on a small piston lifts a car).
ASKED-AS: water tower dam deep hydraulic jack car lift brakes narrow tube pressure

ESSENCE: floating is not a force of its own. It is the leftover of a
  fluid pressing harder underneath than on top — pure arithmetic of
  depth. Remove the depth-difference and the lift dies with it.
ROOT: this file / pressure grows with depth, and only with depth.
CANNOT: no floating without gravity — in orbit a bubble does not
  rise, oil will not climb off water, a candle smothers in its fumes.
THREAD: spaceflight (fire behaves like nothing on Earth), geology
  (mountains have deep roots and ride like icebergs of rock).
ASKED-AS: floating sinking bubbles rising space station candle flame orbit oil separates layers

ESSENCE: a liquid's surface is a skin under tension. A molecule
  inside is pulled from all sides, one at the surface only inward —
  so a liquid shrinks its surface, then defends it against tearing.
ROOT: chemistry / molecules pull on neighbours; at the surface that
  pulling is one-sided.
CANNOT: no new surface without paying for it, so a free drop can be
  no shape but a sphere. And no wetting what a liquid likes less than
  itself — it beads instead of spreading.
THREAD: the living world (insects standing on ponds, sap climbing),
  soap (it works by weakening the skin so water can spread and lift).
ASKED-AS: droplets beading water strider soap bubbles waxed car raindrops round paper clip

ESSENCE: viscosity is friction inside a fluid. It resists the RATE of
  a shape change, never the change itself — honey and water both find
  level; honey just takes longer, and pays the delay out as heat.
ROOT: flight and fluids / a fluid cannot hold a shape, only stall.
CANNOT: no viscous fluid holding a shape against a standing force —
  given time it flows. And no free slowness: the delay always warms.
THREAD: engineering (oil and shock absorbers are hired viscosity),
  geology (a glacier is ice being viscous in public).
ASKED-AS: honey syrup molasses thick oil pouring slow shock absorber drips runny cold

ESSENCE: the difference between springing back and staying bent is
  where the energy went. Elastic stretch stores it and returns it, so
  elastic things bounce. Plastic bending spends it, so they protect.
ROOT: building / materials remember two ways, read as a ledger.
CANNOT: nothing bounces well and absorbs well — one event cannot both
  return the energy and swallow it. A crumple zone that sprang back
  would hand the whole crash straight to the passenger.
THREAD: helmets (the good ones die once, on purpose), sport (a lively
  ball against a dead one), packaging (foam chosen to be ruined).
ASKED-AS: crumple zone helmet foam bouncy ball dented fender padding airbag crash absorb

ESSENCE: force flows through a solid like water in a channel, and
  where the channel turns sharply the flow crowds. A sharp inside
  corner multiplies the push — and the multiplier is shape, not size.
ROOT: building / a crack is a force magnifier; this is why it starts.
CANNOT: no crowding in a piece of even section. And no escaping it by
  making the flaw small — a tiny sharp notch concentrates as fiercely
  as a large one, which is why a scratch decides where glass breaks.
THREAD: flight (square windows tore the first jetliners; all rounded
  since), packaging (tear-here notches are this law hired on purpose).
ASKED-AS: crack corner scratch glass notch tear perforated bag rip window rounded hole

ESSENCE: every object answers strongly to a short list of rhythms and
  shrugs off the rest. On the list, each push adds to what the last
  one left, so a whisper repeated in time grows into a shout.
ROOT: waves and motion / timed pushes add; the list is fixed by an
  object's size, stiffness, and weight.
CANNOT: no object defenceless at every rhythm and none safe at all of
  them — the list exists whether or not anyone finds it. And no
  endless growth: where leakage balances feeding is the ceiling.
THREAD: radio (tuning is choosing whose list to answer), medicine
  (ultrasound shattering a stone the surgeon never touches).
ASKED-AS: wine glass shatter opera singer bridge sway washing machine rattle buzz hum

ESSENCE: damping fights speed rather than position — the faster the
  motion, the harder it pushes back — so it drains a ringing thing to
  stillness. Too little rings for ages; too much crawls home.
ROOT: this file / viscosity and friction, put deliberately to work.
CANNOT: no damper killing the ring without slowing the useful
  response — a quick settle is bought with a lazy start. And no
  damping without heat: the ringing has to go somewhere.
THREAD: cars (shock absorbers; a bouncing car fails its test), doors
  (a closer that never slams), towers (tuned masses spoiling sway).
ASKED-AS: shock absorber bouncy car door closer slam wobble stops ringing settle springy

ESSENCE: in any collision the total punch is kept; the energy is not.
  Bouncy hits give the motion back, sticky ones bury it in dents and
  heat — and that share decides whether a crash is survivable.
ROOT: physics / momentum is conserved; energy carries no such promise.
CANNOT: no crash cancels its own momentum — safety can only stretch
  it over more time and more metal. And no perfectly bouncy real
  collision: every bounce returns lower than the last.
THREAD: sport (a live ball against a beanbag), pool (a near-perfect
  bounce, which is why the game can be calculated at all).
ASKED-AS: car crash bumper billiard balls clack superball bounce lower clay sticks tackle

ESSENCE: falling has a top speed. Air's push grows with speed while
  weight does not, so a falling thing speeds up only until the two
  match, then descends steadily however far is left to go.
ROOT: flight and fluids / drag grows with the square of speed.
CANNOT: no fall in air that keeps speeding up. And no single answer
  for the speed — it is weight against area, so a mouse and a horse
  dropped down one shaft live in different worlds.
THREAD: rain (why a drop falling two miles does not kill), parachutes
  (area bought suddenly), the living world (small bodies walk away).
ASKED-AS: skydiver parachute falling speed raindrop hail cat survives ant dropped shaft terminal

ESSENCE: a thrown thing lives two lives at once — sideways it simply
  keeps going, downward it accelerates — and neither knows about the
  other. Lay them together and the arc appears, undesigned.
ROOT: physics / motion persists sideways; physics / gravity downward.
CANNOT: no sideways speed delays a fall — a bullet fired dead level
  and one dropped from that hand hit the ground together. And no path
  that is not that same falling curve, merely stretched.
THREAD: sport (range is chosen by angle; about 45° carries farthest
  in still air), gunnery (sights are the aiming-up built in already).
ASKED-AS: throwing ball arc basketball shot cannon aim angle farthest bullet drop sprinkler

ESSENCE: going in a circle is not coasting — it is being yanked
  inward every instant, because straight is what the thing would
  rather do. The outward feeling is you going straight into the wall.
ROOT: physics / motion persists unless pushed — a curve is all push.
CANNOT: no circle without a constant inward pull — cut the string and
  the stone leaves along the tangent, never outward. And no outward
  force to find anywhere: the car door arrives at you.
THREAD: driving (a skid is the road failing to supply the pull),
  laundry (a spin dryer withholds the pull and lets water go straight).
ASKED-AS: merry go round spinning car turn thrown outward sling skid roundabout string

ESSENCE: an orbit's speed is set by its height alone, and lower means
  faster. So chasing something ahead by speeding up lifts you higher
  and leaves you further behind; to catch it you slow down and drop.
ROOT: physics / gravity weakens with distance — one speed per height.
CANNOT: no hovering in space without burning fuel — an orbit is not a
  place, it is a speed. And no direct chase up there: forward thrust
  cannot close a gap that lies ahead of you.
THREAD: spaceflight (every docking is this backwards dance), satellites
  (one height circles once a day — the only one that can hang still).
ASKED-AS: satellite orbit space station docking geostationary catch faster higher moon rendezvous

ESSENCE: a tide is not the moon's pull; it is the DIFFERENCE in that
  pull across the width of the Earth. Near water is tugged harder
  than the middle, far water less — so it bulges at both ends.
ROOT: physics / gravity fades with distance, so nothing large is
  pulled evenly across itself.
CANNOT: no single bulge while gravity fades with distance — the far
  side must lag as surely as the near side leads. And no tide from a
  pull that arrives evenly, however enormous.
THREAD: astronomy (moons torn into rings when the difference beats
  their own grip; the same stretch keeps Jupiter's moons molten).
ASKED-AS: tide high low ocean moon twice daily beach fishing pull sea level

ESSENCE: push a spinning thing and the answer comes out sideways —
  about a quarter turn round from where you pushed. So a leaning top
  does not fall; its fall becomes a slow circling of the axis.
ROOT: this file / spin is a stored direction, rewritten at right angles.
CANNOT: no direct answer from a spinning body — you cannot tilt a
  fast wheel where you pushed it. And no circling from a still one.
THREAD: cycling (leaning steers a bike more than the bars do),
  astronomy (Earth's axis circles in 26,000 years — pole stars retire).
ASKED-AS: top wobble spinning slows circle gyroscope steering lean bike pole star axis

ESSENCE: gears trade turns for twist at the ratio of their teeth — a
  small wheel turns many times to move a big one once, and pays for
  those turns in force. Chain them and the ratios multiply.
ROOT: engineering / distance traded for force, here cut into teeth.
CANNOT: no gear adds power — what a train gains in twist it loses in
  speed, plus friction at every stage. And no slipping: teeth keep
  count, which is why gears also keep time.
THREAD: cycling (a shiftable trade, chosen mid-ride), clocks (the
  whole mechanism is counted turns all the way down).
ASKED-AS: bicycle gears low high climbing hill drill torque transmission ratio winch teeth

ESSENCE: take a machine round a full cycle back to its exact starting
  arrangement and it has, by definition, done no net work on itself.
  A machine can only pass along what it is fed.
ROOT: physics / energy is never created; physics / disorder wins.
CANNOT: no closed cycle outputs energy — forbidden, not unlikely, and
  forbidden twice: by the books, and because even a fed machine leaks
  to friction. Every such drawing hides one skipped line.
THREAD: engineering (patent offices refuse the category unexamined),
  money (a scheme paying returns out of nothing is the same drawing).
ASKED-AS: perpetual motion free energy overunity magnet motor wheel forever invention scam patent

ESSENCE: work is the job — force times the distance it moves through.
  Power is how fast you do it. The same load up the same stairs is
  identical work at a stroll or a sprint; the sprint needs a bigger engine.
ROOT: physics / energy is never created — power is only work per second.
CANNOT: no change of pace alters the size of the job. And no small
  engine doing a big job quickly — time is its only currency.
THREAD: the body (strong and fit are two measurements), engineering
  (motors sized by power, batteries by energy — both bills arrive).
ASKED-AS: horsepower watts kilowatt hours engine size battery fast slow climbing stairs strong

ESSENCE: efficiency is not a fact about a machine — it is a ratio to
  what you wanted. Nothing is destroyed; "loss" only names the energy
  delivered to an address you had no use for.
ROOT: physics / energy is never created — so every loss is a delivery.
CANNOT: no efficiency figure without a stated purpose — a heater is
  perfect, since its waste heat IS the product. And no purpose escapes
  the tax: moving parts always post some to friction.
THREAD: lighting (a bulb judged by the share that leaves as light),
  cooking (an open pot heats the kitchen on this same accounting).
ASKED-AS: efficient wasteful mileage led bulb heat wasted furnace rating percent loss energy

ESSENCE: across a lever, the ratio of forces is exactly the inverse
  ratio of distances travelled. So levers run backwards too: effort
  close to the pivot buys reach and speed by SPENDING force.
ROOT: engineering / distance traded for force, read the unexpected way.
CANNOT: no lever hands out force and speed together — the trade IS
  the machine. And no arm built both ways: a biceps pulls many times
  the hand's load precisely in order to make the hand fast.
THREAD: the body (throwing, kicking, a jaw that crushes at the back
  and nips at the front), sport (a bat is a lever bought with grip).
ASKED-AS: lever crowbar seesaw pivot fulcrum arm muscle throwing bat swing speed tradeoff

ESSENCE: make a shape twice as big in every direction and its bones
  grow four times stronger while its body grows eight times heavier.
  Strength follows area, weight follows volume. Size is never scaled.
ROOT: geometry — area goes as the square of length, volume as the cube.
CANNOT: no giant insect — enlarge a beetle and its legs snap beneath
  it. And no elephant on slender legs: big animals are forced into
  thick pillars, and past a size no arrangement of limbs will do.
THREAD: building (a model bridge that holds proves nothing about the
  real one), the living world (the largest animals live in water).
ASKED-AS: giant insect movie monster ant strength elephant legs thick model scale bigger

ESSENCE: heat is made throughout a body but leaves only through its
  skin. Grow, and the inside outruns the surface — so big animals
  struggle to shed heat and small ones cannot hold it.
ROOT: this file / area against volume, applied to warmth not strength.
CANNOT: no large animal running a small one's furnace — an elephant
  burning fuel at a mouse's rate per pound would cook. And no small
  warm animal that can stop eating: a shrew starves in hours.
THREAD: the living world (elephant and fennec ears are radiators;
  arctic animals are round and short-eared), medicine (babies chill).
ASKED-AS: elephant ears big animals hot small shivering mouse eats constantly baby chills

ESSENCE: a solid has no single way of shaking — it has a ladder of
  them, each with its own pattern of moving and dead-still parts and
  its own rhythm. Any real wobble is a mixture drawn from that ladder.
ROOT: waves and motion / standing loops, carried from a rope to a body.
CANNOT: no object shaking in a shape off its ladder — strike it
  however you like and it answers only in its own patterns. And no
  dead spot that moves: the still places are as fixed as the rhythms.
THREAD: music (timbre is which rungs a body favours — one note on a
  violin and a flute differs only in the mixture), engineering.
ASKED-AS: ringing bell tuning fork struck table hum drum head sand patterns vibration

ESSENCE: what travels in a wave is a handshake between springiness
  and heaviness — each bit is pulled home and overshoots into its
  neighbour. Speed belongs to the material, never to the maker.
ROOT: this file / springs restore, plus physics / mass resists.
CANNOT: no shouting louder to make sound arrive sooner — loudness is
  height, not haste. And no wave entering a new material at its old
  speed: that forced change of pace is what bends and reflects it.
THREAD: sound (a train heard through the rail before the air), oceans
  (waves slowing in shallows, which turns surf to face the shore).
ASKED-AS: sound speed thunder delay steel rail echo underwater loud arrives bending waves

ESSENCE: the change in a thing's motion is force multiplied by how
  long the force lasts. Since the change is fixed by the crash, only
  the duration is negotiable — stretch the stop and the force falls
  in exact proportion. That is the whole art of landing softly.
ROOT: physics / momentum is conserved — the total change is set
  before the impact begins; only its schedule is free.
CANNOT: no gentle stop in no time. And no padding that reduces the
  momentum — an airbag, a bent knee, and a caught egg all buy the
  same thing, and buy it only in milliseconds.
THREAD: martial arts (rolling out of a fall is time-buying), sport
  (soft hands on a catch), packaging (crumple, foam, and air pockets
  are all duration for sale).
ASKED-AS: airbag catching egg bend knees rolling fall padding jump landing soft crumple

ESSENCE: height and speed are the same money in two pockets. Lift a
  thing and you have deposited energy in its position; let it go and
  the deposit turns into motion, coin for coin, and back again at the
  top of the next rise.
ROOT: physics / energy is never created — it is only ever moved
  between a position account and a motion account.
CANNOT: no swing, roller-coaster, or pendulum climbing higher than
  it started, ever. And no coasting uphill for free: every metre
  gained is bought with speed already owned.
THREAD: water (a dam is a bank account with a valve), the body (a
  runner's stride is a hundred small deposits and withdrawals),
  money (savings and spending are literally this arithmetic).
ASKED-AS: roller coaster hill swing height speed drop bike coasting dam water stored

ESSENCE: a rolling wheel's contact patch is standing still. Rolling
  is a wheel laying its rim onto the road and lifting it off again,
  never scraping — which is why the top of a rolling wheel travels
  twice as fast as its axle, and the bottom not at all.
ROOT: engineering / rolling replaces sliding — this is the geometry
  that makes the replacement possible.
CANNOT: no rolling without grip at the still point — on ice the
  wheel spins and the car sits. And no photographing a rolling
  spoke sharply at the top: it is the fastest thing in the picture.
THREAD: driving (a skid is the contact patch losing its stillness),
  trains (a steel wheel on steel rail has almost nothing to grip
  with, which is why trains cannot climb or stop quickly).
ASKED-AS: wheel rolling tire skid spinning ice traction spokes blurry train rails climb

ESSENCE: how hard a thing is to spin up depends not just on how much
  mass it has but on how far out that mass sits. Move weight to the
  rim and the same object becomes far harder to start and to stop.
ROOT: physics / F = m·a, applied circle by circle — each bit of mass
  counts by the square of its distance from the axis.
CANNOT: no hoop beating a solid disc down a ramp, whatever they
  weigh — the hoop must spend more of the hill on spinning and has
  less left for going. And no fast-spinning heavy rim for free.
THREAD: sport (a weighted racket head is harder to swing but harder
  to deflect), engineering (a flywheel is deliberate rim mass),
  clocks (a wide, slow balance wheel keeps steadier time).
ASKED-AS: flywheel hoop disc ramp race rolling down bat weight rim wheels heavy

ESSENCE: a resting thing is stable if nudging it lifts its weight,
  and unstable if nudging it lowers it. That is all stability is —
  a cone on its base returns because tipping raises it; the same cone
  on its point leaves because tipping is downhill.
ROOT: this file / height is stored energy — a valley holds, a hilltop
  releases, and the flat is indifferent.
CANNOT: no stability at the top of any hill — balance there is a
  performance, not a property, and needs continuous correction. And
  no toppling out of a valley without an outside push big enough.
THREAD: sailing (a keel puts the weight low so heeling lifts it),
  the body (standing is an unstable balance corrected all day long),
  markets and habits (some states resist a shove; some fall over).
ASKED-AS: balance wobble tipping cone bottom heavy keel boat stand upright steady rocking

ESSENCE: everything falls at the same rate, regardless of weight. A
  heavy thing is pulled harder — but it is exactly as much harder to
  get moving, so the two cancel perfectly and a hammer and a feather
  land together where there is no air to interfere.
ROOT: physics / gravity pulls by mass, and physics / mass resists by
  the same mass — the one quantity does both jobs.
CANNOT: no heavy object outracing a light one in a vacuum. And no
  test of this in air, ever — air resistance is what people are
  actually watching when they think they are watching weight.
THREAD: astronomy (the moon's dust makes the demonstration easy),
  medicine and sport (a big athlete falls no faster than a small),
  evidence (the wrong answer stood for two thousand years).
ASKED-AS: feather hammer drop heavy light same time falling vacuum moon paper crumpled

ESSENCE: you never feel gravity. What you feel is the floor stopping
  you from falling. Remove the floor and the sensation goes with it
  — which is why falling, orbiting, and floating in space are one
  experience, not three.
ROOT: physics / gravity acts on every part of you at once, so no
  part is pressed against another; only contact is felt.
CANNOT: no feeling weight in free fall, at any height and any
  strength of gravity. And no shielding from gravity by orbiting —
  the space station sits in nearly full Earth gravity and falls.
THREAD: amusement rides (the drop's thrill is the floor's absence),
  medicine (bones and muscle waste without the load they never
  actually felt), spaceflight (an orbit is a very long fall).
ASKED-AS: floating astronauts space station gravity zero stomach drop elevator falling roller ride

ESSENCE: steady motion cannot be detected from inside. No experiment
  performed in a smoothly moving cabin — no thrown ball, no poured
  drink, no swinging pendulum — reveals whether it is moving. Only
  changes of motion announce themselves.
ROOT: physics / motion is a state, not an activity, so a state
  shared by everything present is invisible to everything present.
CANNOT: no absolute speed exists to be measured — a speed is always
  relative to something chosen. And no smooth ride betraying itself:
  only the bumps, turns, and brakings do.
THREAD: flight (you pour coffee at 900 km/h without noticing),
  astronomy (the Earth's enormous motion is unfelt), the mind (a
  bias shared by everyone in the room cannot be seen from inside it).
ASKED-AS: airplane cruising smooth pour drink train moving still earth spinning feel speed

ESSENCE: friction depends on how hard two surfaces are pressed
  together and hardly at all on how much of them touches. Spread the
  same load over a wider patch and each part of the patch presses
  more softly — the two changes cancel almost exactly.
ROOT: physics / matter cannot pass through matter — the real contact
  is a few high spots, and their number grows with the pressing.
CANNOT: no extra grip from area alone under the same weight. And no
  simple appeal to this law for racing tyres: rubber cheats the rule
  by gripping partly through stickiness, not just pressing.
THREAD: driving (loading a truck's drive wheels buys traction that
  wide tyres alone would not), craft (a clamp works by pressing, not
  by covering), the body (a heavier grip beats a longer one).
ASKED-AS: tires wide grip traction skinny truck loaded weight braking distance clamp pressing

ESSENCE: bend a real material through a full cycle and it does not
  return quite the same way it went — the return path lags the going
  path, and the gap between them is energy lost as heat, every cycle,
  whether you do it fast or slow.
ROOT: building / materials remember two ways — a hint of the plastic
  memory contaminates every elastic one.
CANNOT: no perfectly springy real material — a bouncing ball must
  die. And no cold tyre on a long drive: rolling flexes the rubber
  thousands of times a minute and every flex is paid in warmth.
THREAD: cycling (soft tyres cost effort through this alone), the
  body (cartilage warming up before it works well), engineering
  (rubber mounts chosen for how much they lose, not how little).
ASKED-AS: tires warm rubber bouncing ball dies squishy flexing heat cycling soft rolling

ESSENCE: a long slender thing does not fail by being crushed — it
  fails by suddenly bowing sideways, at a load far below what the
  material could bear. The failure is a matter of shape and length,
  not of strength, and it arrives without warning.
ROOT: this file / stability — past a certain push, straight becomes
  the unstable state and bent becomes the downhill one.
CANNOT: no slender column carrying its material's crushing strength.
  And no fixing it with a stronger alloy — double the length and the
  safe load falls to a quarter, whatever the metal.
THREAD: building (why columns are fat, braced, or tubular), the body
  (a long bone braced by muscle; a tired leg folding sideways),
  packaging (an empty can holds a person until its wall dents).
ASKED-AS: ruler bends pushing column buckle can crush thin post snapped sideways ladder

ESSENCE: a ramp is a lever laid flat — it lets a load rise a little
  for every long push along the slope. Wrap that ramp around a
  cylinder and it becomes a screw, whose slope can be made so gentle
  that a hand at the rim moves tonnes at the tip.
ROOT: engineering / distance traded for force, now traded by slope
  instead of by pivot.
CANNOT: no ramp reduces the work of lifting — the load is only
  carried farther for less push at a time. And no undoing a shallow
  enough screw by pushing on its end: below a certain slope, friction
  locks it, which is why screws stay put and jacks do not drop cars.
THREAD: craft (a wedge is a moving ramp; an axe splits by slope),
  building (a wheelchair ramp is the same trade written in law),
  cooking (a knife's bevel is a wedge with a handle).
ASKED-AS: ramp slope wheelchair screw jack bolt wedge axe splitting log tighten stays

ESSENCE: hang a load on several strands of one rope and each strand
  carries its share, so the hand pulling one end feels the load
  divided — and pays by hauling that many times more rope.
ROOT: engineering / distance traded for force, here traded strand by
  strand instead of by slope or by pivot.
CANNOT: no pulley system lifts more work than the arm supplies. And
  no free ride from the rope count: four strands mean a quarter of
  the force and four times the pulling.
THREAD: sailing (blocks and tackle everywhere on a rig), climbing
  (a hauling system built from the rope already carried), the body
  (tendons wrapping bone act as small pulleys, redirecting force).
ASKED-AS: pulley block tackle rope hoist lifting engine sailboat winch strands easier haul

ESSENCE: a moving source crowds the waves it makes ahead of itself
  and stretches them behind. So the pitch of an approaching siren is
  raised and a departing one lowered — and the change is not in the
  siren, which never alters its note at all.

ESSENCE: for a thing to stay still, two separate books must balance:

  not enough — a load with balanced forces and unbalanced turns

ESSENCE: loose material will not stand at any slope you like. Pour
  sand, grain, or gravel and it settles at one particular angle and
  no steeper — steepen it and it fails in a sliding sheet, always at
  the same angle for that material.
ROOT: this file / friction between grains sets exactly how far a
  slope can lean before gravity's pull along it wins.
CANNOT: no dry pile steeper than its own angle, however carefully

  in drought.
THREAD: deep earth (scree slopes and landslides), farming and
  storage (silo and hopper walls cut steeper than the angle or the
  grain arches and hangs), snow (an avalanche is this law in white).
