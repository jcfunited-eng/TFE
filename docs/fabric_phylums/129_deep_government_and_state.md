# 129 DEEP GOVERNMENT AND STATE — how societies are steered
File 24 describes rule as it recurs. This one opens the machine of
the modern state: how it is joined, how it is chosen, what it can
actually do, what it cannot see, and why the difference between two
countries is more often capacity than ideology.

ESSENCE: a state claims every person and every acre inside a line,
  and membership is not offered, it is imposed. Nobody chooses the
  country they are born into, nobody may simply opt out of it, and
  leaving requires another state's permission to arrive.
ROOT: power / rule needs agents and revenue, and both require a
  defined ground and a defined list of people to draw them from.
CANNOT: no consent-based state in the ordinary sense — the founding
  generation may consent, and nobody after them is asked. No exit
  without an entry: the right to leave is worth exactly as much as
  somewhere else's willingness to admit you.
THREAD: families (nobody chooses their parents), language (the tongue
  you were raised in), clubs (the only association you join by being
  born inside its walls).
ASKED-AS: citizenship born here cannot opt out leave country passport stateless membership choose

ESSENCE: belief that a power is rightful comes from four different
  places — it has always been this way, it was arrived at properly,
  it delivers, or this particular person is extraordinary. Each is a
  different kind of credit, and each runs out in its own way.
ROOT: power / legitimacy is belief doing the daily work that force
  cannot afford to do.
CANNOT: no state resting on delivery alone that survives a bad
  decade — performance credit must be re-earned continuously. And no
  person-based legitimacy surviving the person; it cannot be handed
  to a successor, which is why such systems break at the funeral.
THREAD: business (a firm coasting on reputation, on process, on
  results, or on a founder), religion (tradition against a charismatic
  preacher), families (respect from age, from role, or from what you
  actually do).
ASKED-AS: why obey government legitimate tradition elections results strongman popular collapse trust

ESSENCE: sovereignty is the claim to have no superior — and it is
  made real from outside, not inside. A government controls its
  territory and is still not sovereign until other states treat it as
  such: recognise its passports, take its ambassadors, enforce its
  judgments.
ROOT: people and power / a claim becomes a fact when enough others
  act as though it were one.
CANNOT: no sovereignty by declaration alone; a place nobody
  recognises cannot bank, trade, fly, or sue, whatever it controls on
  the ground. And no full independence with it either — accepting
  recognition means accepting the rules of the club that gives it.
THREAD: money (a currency worth what strangers will take), marriage (a
  status made real by third parties acknowledging it), companies (a
  firm that exists once a registry says so).
ASKED-AS: recognised country independence declared breakaway region sovereignty other countries accept diplomatic

ESSENCE: order and liberty are bought from one purse. Every rule that
  makes strangers predictable to each other removes a choice from
  each of them, and every freedom returned puts some risk back into
  everyone's day. The argument is never whether to trade, only where.
ROOT: people together / rules are trust for strangers, and trust for
  strangers is paid for in each stranger's freedom.
CANNOT: no maximum of both at once — the extreme of either is worse
  than any middle, and both extremes end in the same place, since
  disorder produces a strongman and total order produces one too. No
  permanent settlement of the trade; the right point moves with the
  threat.
THREAD: schools (a strict class and a free one, both failing at the
  ends), traffic (signals against an open junction), households (a
  rota against nobody knowing who cooks).
ASKED-AS: freedom versus security tradeoff safety rules restrictions liberty balance too far

ESSENCE: a representative is handed one mandate and must then decide
  a thousand questions. Voters chose on three of them, cared about
  one, and are bound by all thousand — so representation is not a
  transmission of preferences, it is a delegation of judgement with a
  very lossy brief.
ROOT: people and power / delegating means handing over what cannot be
  said, so the work comes back done differently.
CANNOT: no mandate specific enough to cover a term — every
  government legislates mostly on matters nobody voted about. No
  fixing it with more elections; a referendum on everything asks
  people to rule on questions they have no time to learn.
THREAD: employment (hiring someone for a role, not for a task list),
  law (an agent's authority exceeding what was discussed), families
  (a proxy at a meeting whose agenda changed).
ASKED-AS: mandate voted for did not vote for this manifesto promises representative decides referendum

ESSENCE: the same ballots under different counting rules give
  different winners and, over time, different countries. Winner-takes-
  all in districts squeezes politics toward two parties;
  proportional counting produces many parties and coalitions. The rule
  shapes the party system more than opinions do.
ROOT: mathematics / a method of aggregating preferences is not
  neutral machinery, it is a decision made before anybody votes.
CANNOT: no counting rule that is fair in every sense at once — this
  is proved, not merely difficult. No changing the rule neutrally
  either: it is always proposed by people who can already calculate
  who wins under it.
THREAD: sport (a league table against a knockout, from identical
  matches), committees (the order of motions deciding the outcome),
  exams (a pass mark chosen before the papers are marked).
ASKED-AS: voting system first past the post proportional two parties coalition reform wasted votes

ESSENCE: where the lines are drawn decides who wins on identical
  votes. Concentrate your opponents into a few districts they win
  overwhelmingly and spread your own thinly across many, and a
  minority of votes becomes a majority of seats without a single
  ballot being altered.
ROOT: mathematics / an average taken over groups depends on how the
  groups were formed, and here somebody chooses the groups.
CANNOT: no neutral map — even honest rules must choose between
  compact districts, communities kept whole, and proportional
  outcomes, and those three conflict. No self-drawn boundary that is
  trusted; the drawer's interest is visible in every line.
THREAD: schools (catchment areas that decide intakes), sport (seeding
  that decides which strong sides meet early), statistics (a result
  that changes with how the data is bucketed).
ASKED-AS: gerrymander district boundaries drawn seats votes safe redistricting commission unfair map

ESSENCE: a majority can vote to do something terrible to a minority
  and be perfectly democratic while doing it. So every workable system
  puts some things outside the vote — and that is undemocratic by
  construction. The contradiction is not a flaw to be fixed; it is the
  design.
ROOT: this file / a constitution is a law that ordinary majorities
  may not contradict, and this is why anyone built one.
CANNOT: no protection of a minority from a majority by majority vote.
  And no limit that stays put unless something outside the majority
  enforces it — which means an unelected body, with all that follows.
THREAD: clubs (a rule that a two-thirds vote cannot expel members),
  families (a household vote that always outnumbers the youngest),
  companies (minority shareholder protections written into a charter).
ASKED-AS: majority rule minority rights tyranny of the majority vote to take away protections

ESSENCE: decide each thing at the smallest level that contains its
  consequences. Bin collection is local because the effects stop at
  the town; a river, a currency and an epidemic are not, because the
  costs cross every boundary they meet.
ROOT: premise — a decider who does not bear the consequences will not
  weigh them, so authority should be matched to where the costs land.
CANNOT: no local decision about a spillover — the upstream town has
  every reason to pollute and none to stop. And no central decision
  that keeps local knowledge; the further up it goes, the more the
  particulars are flattened into a form.
THREAD: farming (a shared aquifer against a private field), families
  (a decision that affects one child against one that affects all),
  business (a branch's pricing against a group's brand).
ASKED-AS: local council national government devolution which level decides subsidiarity spillover central

ESSENCE: ministers arrive and leave; the permanent staff hold the
  files, the procedures and the memory of what was tried in 1987. That
  is what makes a state able to act continuously — and it is exactly
  what makes it hard to steer, because the memory includes reasons the
  new arrival has not heard.
ROOT: people together / an institution recruits new humans into old
  shapes, and the shapes hold the knowledge that the arrivals lack.
CANNOT: no continuity of administration without a permanent staff. And
  no fast redirection of one either: a body that can outlast a bad
  minister can outlast a good one, and it uses the same muscles for
  both.
THREAD: hospitals (consultants outlasting managers), schools (a
  department that survives every head), families (a household routine
  a new partner cannot easily change).
ASKED-AS: civil service permanent officials minister blocked bureaucracy resists memory continuity mandarins

ESSENCE: a law is a wish until somebody with a desk and a budget
  carries it out. The clerk deciding what counts as evidence, the
  inspector deciding what to look at, the officer deciding whom to
  stop — their choices, repeated a million times, are the policy as it
  is actually experienced.
ROOT: power / procedure cannot remove persons entirely; every rule
  meets cases it never imagined and somebody judges the gap.
CANNOT: no policy delivered as designed — it arrives filtered through
  the incentives, workload and understanding of the last person in the
  chain. No fixing it from the top with more detailed rules; more
  rules produce more gaps, and the gaps are where the discretion sits.
THREAD: teaching (a curriculum as taught by one tired teacher),
  medicine (a guideline applied at three in the morning), retail (a
  refund policy as applied by the person at the desk).
ASKED-AS: policy on paper implementation front line staff discretion different in practice delivery

ESSENCE: an intervention lands on people who then rearrange
  themselves around it. Cap rents and landlords sell; pay by procedure
  and procedures multiply; ban a substance and a worse one appears. The
  effect on behaviour is not a side story — it is usually the main
  result.
ROOT: premise — behaviour follows incentives, so changing a rule
  changes the game and not only the score.
CANNOT: no policy whose effects stop at its intention — the subjects
  are not inert. No predicting the rearrangement from the rule alone;
  it depends on what people can substitute toward, which is discovered
  after the fact.
THREAD: farming (a subsidy per acre producing acres nobody farms),
  medicine (an antibiotic producing resistance), building (a tax on
  windows producing bricked-up walls).
ASKED-AS: unintended consequences backfired ban made it worse loophole behaviour changed policy

ESSENCE: the people who know an industry well enough to regulate it
  are the people who work in it. So the regulator's staff, its facts
  and eventually its assumptions come from the regulated — not by
  bribery, but because interested expertise is the only expertise
  available.
ROOT: this file / a regulator must be told what is technically
  possible, and only the industry can tell it.
CANNOT: no informed regulation without the industry's knowledge, and
  no way to take that knowledge free of its framing. No cure from
  rotating staff either: fresh officials know less, and are captured
  faster because they must ask more.
THREAD: medicine (guidelines written by specialists with industry
  ties), sport (rules written by the clubs), accounting (standards
  set by the profession being audited).
ASKED-AS: regulator captured industry insiders revolving door too close writes own rules

ESSENCE: a legislator cannot know a hundred industries, so somebody
  must tell them — and the telling is done by people with a stake.
  Lobbying is information and pressure travelling together, and the
  information is genuinely useful, which is precisely why it works.
ROOT: the mind / one head holds very little, so any decision at scale
  runs on other people's summaries.
CANNOT: no legislating without being briefed, and no briefing free of
  the briefer's interest. No fix from banning it either: the
  information would still have to arrive, and it would arrive
  privately instead of on a register.
THREAD: medicine (a representative who is also the source of the
  trial data), courts (advocacy as the way facts reach a judge),
  journalism (a story built from an interested source).
ASKED-AS: lobbying influence money politics access briefing interest groups information corrupt register

ESSENCE: the state ends up owning whatever cannot be sold — and that
  list is set by technology, not by ideology. Lighthouses were public
  until ships could be charged by beacon; roads were public until
  every vehicle could be tracked and billed. The boundary moves when
  the metering does.
ROOT: people and power / a benefit nobody can be shut out of will not
  be funded voluntarily, so compulsion or nothing.
CANNOT: no stable list of proper state functions — the same service
  is naturally public in one century and saleable in the next. No
  market provision of anything that cannot be withheld from
  non-payers, however strong the preference for markets.
THREAD: money (a good becomes a product when it can be packaged),
  computing (content freely copied until it could be gated), farming
  (common land enclosed once fencing was affordable).
ASKED-AS: public services privatise government provide roads lighthouse toll charging market state

ESSENCE: when an act costs somebody who is not party to it, three
  instruments exist: charge the causer, cap the total and let the
  quota be traded, or give somebody ownership so the cost lands on an
  owner. Each works, and each fails differently.
ROOT: premise — an unpriced cost is invisible to whoever causes it, so
  the whole job is making it visible in money or in a limit.
CANNOT: no charge set correctly without knowing the harm's true cost,
  which nobody does. No cap without deciding the total first, and no
  ownership solution where the thing cannot be bounded — nobody can own
  the atmosphere, which is why the hardest case has the weakest tools.
THREAD: farming (a grazing quota against a fee against enclosure),
  fishing (catch shares), cities (congestion charging as a price on a
  shared road).
ASKED-AS: pollution tax carbon price cap and trade property rights externality who pays

ESSENCE: the same payment can be argued two ways: insurance everybody
  buys against a risk anybody might face, or a transfer from those who
  have to those who do not. The two arguments answer different
  objections and produce different schemes — and the universal ones
  survive politically because everyone is a claimant.
ROOT: chance / a pooled risk and a redistribution move the same money,
  and differ entirely in who thinks they are paying.
CANNOT: no political durability for a scheme where the payers never
  collect — a benefit for other people is cut in the first hard
  budget. And no targeting without a means test, which costs to run
  and deters exactly the people it was built for.
THREAD: insurance (a premium against a charitable fund), pensions (a
  contribution record against a needs test), schools (universal
  provision surviving where vouchers for the poor do not).
ASKED-AS: welfare benefits insurance redistribution means tested universal handout contributions deserving

ESSENCE: whoever hands over the money is not who bears the cost. A
  tax on a firm becomes some mixture of lower wages, higher prices and
  smaller returns — and the split is decided by which side of the
  market can most easily go elsewhere.
ROOT: money / a price is where two valuations meet, and a tax moves
  the meeting point rather than staying with whoever writes the
  cheque.
CANNOT: no taxing a company as such — companies are arrangements, and
  every pound comes from a person somewhere in the chain. No
  predicting who bears it from the statute; only from how mobile each
  side is, which is why capital escapes and land cannot.
THREAD: shops (a duty passed to the till or absorbed, depending on
  competition), employment (an employer levy landing in wages),
  landlords (a property tax that cannot be passed on where tenants
  can move).
ASKED-AS: corporation tax who really pays passed on consumers wages incidence business burden

ESSENCE: public debt is mostly not owed to foreigners. It is a claim
  by future bondholders on future taxpayers — a transfer inside a
  country and across time, which is why it can be enormous and
  survivable, and why "we owe it to ourselves" is half true and half
  a dodge.
ROOT: money / a loan is a claim on work not yet done, and here the
  work will be done by people who were not consulted.
CANNOT: no borrowing from the future without the future producing
  enough to answer it — a debt that outruns growth ends in default,
  inflation or austerity, never in quiet payment. And no neutrality in
  "owing it to ourselves": the taxpayers and the bondholders are
  usually not the same people.
THREAD: families (a mortgage taken by parents and serviced by
  children), farming (borrowing against a harvest not yet planted),
  companies (debt-funded expansion answered by later earnings).
ASKED-AS: national debt future generations owe ourselves deficit borrowing sustainable interest payments

ESSENCE: two levers move an economy and they are held by different
  hands. Interest and money supply are fast, blunt, and pulled by
  unelected officials. Tax and spending are slow, targeted, and
  intensely political — so the fast lever gets used for everything,
  including problems it cannot reach.
ROOT: this file / an instrument's speed and its accountability pull
  in opposite directions, and urgency picks the fast one.
CANNOT: no fine targeting from a general interest rate; it is one
  number for every region and every industry at once. And no speed
  from a budget — by the time a spending programme reaches the ground,
  the condition it was aimed at has usually changed.
THREAD: medicine (a systemic drug against surgery), farming
  (irrigating the whole field against tending one row), driving (a
  brake against a steering wheel).
ASKED-AS: interest rates government spending stimulus recession central bank fiscal monetary policy

ESSENCE: a government that controls money can always print its way
  out of today at tomorrow's expense — so states hand the printing to
  a body they cannot direct. Independence is not a claim about
  competence; it is a promise put deliberately out of reach of
  whoever would be tempted.
ROOT: people together / the deepest rules are the ones fixed before
  anyone knows who they will favour.
CANNOT: no credible promise of monetary restraint from whoever faces
  the election — the temptation is structural, not personal. And no
  true independence either: the law that granted it can be amended,
  so it lasts exactly as long as the habit of not amending it.
THREAD: households (savings locked away to keep from spending them),
  sailing (a hand lashed to the wheel before the storm), sport (a
  drug testing body outside the federation it tests).
ASKED-AS: central bank independent politicians printing money inflation credibility governor rates pressure

ESSENCE: where everybody pays, refusing to pay costs you the service
  and buys nothing — the official still expects it and the queue still
  moves for others. So honesty is punished individually even where
  everyone would prefer a clean system. It is a trap, not a
  character.
ROOT: people and power / behaviour follows incentives, and here every
  individual's best move sustains an arrangement all of them dislike.
CANNOT: no cleaning it up one person at a time — the first honest
  official is simply poorer and often removed. No switch without
  changing what everyone expects everyone else to do, which is why
  successful campaigns look like sudden, visible, simultaneous shifts
  rather than gradual improvement.
THREAD: sport (doping where everyone assumes everyone does), traffic
  (a junction where only signalling makes you a victim), business (an
  industry where refusing to pay means losing every contract).
ASKED-AS: bribes everyone does it clean up corruption cannot refuse system expectation trap

ESSENCE: publishing what a body does is a real check and it has two
  hard limits. Whatever is measured and published becomes the target,
  so activity moves to what is counted. And forcing deliberation into
  the open moves the real deliberation to the corridor before the
  meeting.
ROOT: people and power / a measure that becomes a target stops
  measuring; and information flows only where it is safe to give.
CANNOT: no candid discussion in a fully published room — participants
  perform for the record, and the frank version happens somewhere
  else. No accountability without publication either, so every system
  ends up choosing which parts to expose and defending the choice.
THREAD: schools (published league tables reshaping teaching), medicine
  (surgeons declining hard cases once outcomes are public), meetings
  (the real decision made before the minuted one).
ASKED-AS: transparency freedom of information published minutes gaming figures secret meetings accountability

ESSENCE: censorship's real product is not silence about facts — it is
  uncertainty about what everybody else thinks. People act on what
  they believe others believe, so controlling the common picture is
  the lever, and private belief can be left alone.
ROOT: the mind / a person alone in an opinion behaves entirely
  differently from one who knows the opinion is shared.
CANNOT: no lasting control where people can see each other's
  behaviour — a crowd is common knowledge made visible, which is why
  gatherings are policed harder than opinions. And no persuading
  anyone against what they touch daily; the control works on the
  distant, not on the empty shelf.
THREAD: schools (a class where nobody knows the others also object),
  clothes (a fashion that changes the moment everyone sees others
  changing), stories (the child who says the emperor is naked).
ASKED-AS: censorship banned speech everyone secretly thinks nobody says crowd common knowledge silence

ESSENCE: between the household and the state sits a layer of clubs,
  unions, congregations, associations and teams. That layer is where
  people learn to run a meeting, keep accounts and organise strangers
  — which is why it is the first thing an anxious regime dismantles.
ROOT: people and power / organised numbers beat scattered ones, and
  organisation is a skill that has to be practised somewhere.
CANNOT: no organised society without somewhere to learn organising.
  And no negotiating with an atomised population either — a regime
  that destroys the layer eventually faces a crowd with no
  representatives, which is far more dangerous to it than a union
  was.
THREAD: farming (cooperatives that outlast governments), religion
  (congregations as the surviving civic structure under occupation),
  sport (a club that is also the town's committee).
ASKED-AS: civil society clubs unions churches associations banned organised community groups protest

ESSENCE: a protest's message is not on the placards. It is the number
  of people willing to bear a cost — time, weather, risk, a day's
  pay — because talk is free and turning up is not. That is why the
  count and the inconvenience are the whole signal.
ROOT: evidence / a claim is worth what it cost to make, and a costless
  claim carries nothing.
CANNOT: no signal from a costless demonstration — a petition anyone
  can sign in a second says almost nothing about how many would act.
  And no message beyond size and anger; a crowd cannot express a
  policy, which is why movements are read by whoever interprets them.
THREAD: religion (fasting and pilgrimage as costly signals), courtship
  (a gesture that means something because it was expensive), markets
  (a deposit that proves an intention).
ASKED-AS: protest march turnout numbers matter petition ignored strike costly signal disruption

ESSENCE: a coup is not a fight, it is a bet about what everybody else
  will do. The plotters do not need to defeat the state; they need
  officers, ministries and broadcasters to conclude that the thing has
  already happened. It succeeds by looking accomplished before it is.
ROOT: power / no ruler rules alone, so power is the standing
  expectation that the hands will obey — and a coup attacks the
  expectation directly.
CANNOT: no coup against a genuinely unified security apparatus; there
  is nobody to defect. And no coup surviving crowds in the street who
  make obedience visibly costly — which is why the first acts are
  always the broadcaster and the curfew, not the barracks.
THREAD: business (a boardroom removal that works only if it looks
  settled), sport (a captain replaced mid-match), families (an
  authority that ends when nobody enforces it).
ASKED-AS: coup military takeover failed generals refused crowds streets broadcaster seized legitimacy

ESSENCE: the biggest difference between two governments is usually
  not what they want but what they can actually do — collect a tax,
  run a school, deliver a vaccine, hold a fair election, get a road
  built for what it should cost. Capacity, not ideology, decides most
  outcomes.
ROOT: power / an order is executed by chains of other hands, and the
  quality of those hands is a real, measurable, slowly built thing.
CANNOT: no policy better than the machinery that will run it —
  ambitious design on weak administration produces worse results than
  modest design on strong. And no quick building of capacity; it is
  training, records and habits, and it takes a generation.
THREAD: medicine (a protocol that assumes staff and equipment that
  exist), building (a code enforceable only where inspectors are
  competent), armies (a plan that presumes logistics).
ASKED-AS: government cannot deliver corruption incompetence why does nothing work capacity administration state

ESSENCE: a state that cannot count its people, map its land or see
  its incomes cannot tax, conscript, police or deliver anything. The
  register comes before every other function, which is why collapsing
  states lose their information long before they lose their territory.
ROOT: measurement / nothing can be acted on that has not been made
  visible, and a state acts only on what its records contain.
CANNOT: no taxation of what cannot be counted — which is why states
  have always taxed grain, land and doorways rather than what they
  could not see. No welfare, no conscription and no epidemic response
  without a list of people; the list is the state's eye.
THREAD: farming (a harvest that can be measured at the threshing
  floor), shops (stock that cannot be managed unless counted),
  medicine (a population you cannot vaccinate without knowing who and
  where).
ASKED-AS: census records registry cannot count population failed state tax collect identity list

ESSENCE: to govern anything a state must first simplify it into
  countable categories — a person becomes a taxpayer number, a forest
  becomes cubic metres, a family becomes a household of a certain
  size. And then it governs the simplification, because that is all it
  can see.
ROOT: this file / a state acts only on its records, and a record is a
  reduction of something much larger.
CANNOT: no administering a reality that has not been flattened into
  categories. And no category that fits everyone — the people who fall
  between boxes are not edge cases to the state, they are invisible,
  and invisibility is where hardship collects.
THREAD: medicine (a diagnosis code that must be chosen), farming (a
  managed forest of one species because that is what could be
  counted), forms (a box for a family shape that does not exist).
ASKED-AS: forms categories do not fit my situation official classification simplified bureaucracy invisible

ESSENCE: the force that protects a state can take it, so every state
  must answer who guards the guards. The answers are always the same
  few: pay them well, split them into rival forces that watch each
  other, bind them with an ideology, or keep them small and far away.
ROOT: power / no ruler rules alone, and the armed agents are the ones
  whose refusal ends the argument immediately.
CANNOT: no defence against an army that decides to act, from inside
  the state's own machinery. And no free solution: every answer costs
  either money, or military effectiveness, or a rival force that must
  itself be watched.
THREAD: business (a security firm with the keys), families (a carer
  with power of attorney), computing (an administrator account that
  can undo every control above it).
ASKED-AS: military coup control army guards who watches loyalty rival forces praetorian

ESSENCE: much of what a modern state can do it learned under threat.
  Taxing incomes, registering every citizen, building railways and
  schooling everybody were all pushed hardest by the need to raise
  armies — and the machinery stayed when the war ended.
ROOT: history / capacity is built when something forces it, and
  nothing has forced states harder than the risk of being conquered.
CANNOT: no explaining state capacity by good intentions; the
  administrative apparatus arrived where survival demanded it. And no
  reversing it afterwards — an emergency capability, once built, is
  inherited by the next government with no emergency.
THREAD: medicine (surgery advanced by war), engineering (aviation and
  radio pushed by conflict), families (habits formed under scarcity
  that outlast it).
ASKED-AS: war built the state income tax conscription railways emergency capacity stayed afterwards

ESSENCE: everything a government says it values is in the speeches,
  and everything it actually values is in the budget. What is funded
  will happen, what is unfunded is an aspiration, and the ratio
  between two departments is the real policy, whatever the documents
  say.
ROOT: money / a commitment with no money behind it purchases nothing,
  so allocation is the only statement that binds.
CANNOT: no strategy without an allocation — a plan that does not
  change any budget line has not changed anything. And no honest
  reading of a government's priorities from its rhetoric; the two
  disagree routinely and the money is the one that acts.
THREAD: families (where the money goes rather than what is said),
  business (a strategy visible only in headcount), charities (a
  mission statement against a spending breakdown).
ASKED-AS: budget priorities funding cut announced but no money spending plans real policy

ESSENCE: the deep value of elections is not choosing the best ruler —
  voters cannot know that. It is removing a bad one on a schedule,
  cheaply, without violence, by a method everybody has agreed in
  advance to accept.
ROOT: power / succession is the perennial crisis, and a regular
  election is a crisis defused by being timetabled.
CANNOT: no peaceful removal without a losing side that expects to
  compete again — the whole system rests on defeat being survivable.
  And no good selection either: an election is a blunt instrument for
  choosing and a fine one for ejecting.
THREAD: business (a board that can remove a chief executive),
  sport (a manager sacked without the club dissolving), clubs (an
  annual meeting where the committee can be voted out).
ASKED-AS: elections choose best leader remove bad ones peaceful transfer power losing accept

ESSENCE: a voter's chance of deciding an election is almost nothing,
  while the effort of understanding twenty policy areas is real. So
  staying uninformed is individually rational — and a public made of
  rational individuals is collectively ignorant, which is not a moral
  failing.
ROOT: people and power / a diffuse benefit and a personal cost
  produce under-investment, and here the investment is attention.
CANNOT: no informed electorate produced by exhortation; the
  arithmetic of one vote does not change because somebody disapproves.
  And no substitute for it either — delegating judgement to experts
  returns the problem as who chooses the experts.
THREAD: shareholders (who do not read the annual report), consumers
  (who do not compare every tariff), tenants (who do not read the
  lease).
ASKED-AS: voters uninformed do not read manifestos single vote does not matter apathy rational

ESSENCE: parties exist because a voter cannot evaluate five hundred
  candidates. A party is a brand, a slate and a filter — it bundles
  positions, vouches for people, and gives an ignorant voter a usable
  shortcut. That is a genuine service, and it is also why politics
  bundles unrelated issues together.
ROOT: this file / a voter cannot be informed about everything, so
  something must compress the choice into one decision.
CANNOT: no mass democracy without parties or something that does
  their job; independents in a large legislature cannot form stable
