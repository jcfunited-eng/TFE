# 93 DEEP TIME AND MEASURE — counting the world
Numbers are not found lying in the world; they are made, by
somebody, with a tool, for a reason. This file is the workshop
where they are made, and the list of ways the making goes wrong.

ESSENCE: error travels — combine two measurements and their
  give-or-take combines with them, so a result is never firmer
  than its shakiest ingredient, and a long calculation can end
  vaguer than anything that went into it.
ROOT: evidence / every measurement wears error bars; arithmetic
  moves the bars along with the numbers.
THREAD: navigation (dead reckoning is this law walking), cooking
  (a recipe doubled twice drifts from the original), engineering
  (tolerances stack, which is why the last part will not fit).
ASKED-AS: calculation error adds up estimate rough numbers combined result uncertain accurate

ESSENCE: precision and accuracy are different virtues and fail
  separately — precision is how tightly your shots cluster,
  accuracy is whether the cluster sits on the target; a scale can
  be wrong by exactly the same two kilos every single time.
ROOT: two independent faults — random scatter and a standing lean;
  repeating measures the first and is blind to the second.
THREAD: shooting (a tight group off-centre is a sight problem, not
  a hand problem), the mind (a confident memory is precise, and
  precision is not truth).
ASKED-AS: scale off consistent wrong same every time tight grouping bullseye reliable valid

ESSENCE: calibration is maintenance, not an event — instruments
  drift with heat, age, and use, so a tool checked once and
  trusted forever is a tool telling a story that grows slowly
  falser while looking exactly the same.
ROOT: evidence / measuring is comparing against a standard, and
  the comparison must be renewed because the comparer changes.
THREAD: money (books reconciled against the bank, not against
  themselves), medicine (a blood pressure cuff nobody checks),
  mathematics (no system certifies itself — the same shape).
ASKED-AS: calibrate check scale thermometer drifting off standard reference certified trusted tool

ESSENCE: units are arithmetic's grammar — carry them through the
  working and they check the work for you: if the units of the
  answer come out wrong, the method is wrong, whatever the number
  says.
ROOT: evidence / a quantity without its unit says nothing, so the
  unit is part of the value and travels with it.
THREAD: cooking (grams against cups is a genuine translation, not
  a nuisance), engineering (a Mars probe lost to two teams' sticks
  never converted).
ASKED-AS: units convert metric imperial cancel check answer wrong dimensions miles hours kilograms

ESSENCE: get the power of ten right before polishing the digits —
  most real questions are answered by knowing whether the answer
  is nearer ten, a thousand, or a million, and being wrong by a
  factor of ten is a different species of error from being wrong
  by three percent.
ROOT: mathematics / quantities in the world span an enormous range,
  and the range is where the information lives.
THREAD: money (the difference between a million and a billion is
  the whole argument), astronomy (nothing about the sky can be
  held without this habit).
ASKED-AS: rough size ballpark magnitude ten times bigger million billion scale roughly

ESSENCE: counting and measuring are different acts — a count is
  exact and jumps (four chairs, never 4.3), a measurement is
  continuous and never exact (this table is 1.42 m, give or take);
  confusing them is where false precision is born.
ROOT: mathematics / counting matches things one to one, while
  measuring compares against a stick that always has a finer mark.
THREAD: computing (integers and decimals are two different kinds
  of number for exactly this reason), law (a legal age is a count
  imposed on something continuous).
ASKED-AS: exact number average children decimal people counting things measuring length precise

ESSENCE: some quantities have no single true value because the
  ruler decides them — measure a rugged coastline with a hundred
  kilometre stick and then a one metre stick and the second gives
  a far longer answer, because the small stick follows bays the
  large one skips.
ROOT: mathematics / a rough shape reveals more detail at every
  finer scale, so length grows without settling.
THREAD: computing (file size depends on how you count), the mind
  (how big a job is depends on how finely you break it down),
  maps (the scale chosen decides what exists).
ASKED-AS: coastline length border how long measure ruler detail zoom different answers

ESSENCE: no reading is not a reading of zero — a blank in the
  record can mean nothing happened, or nobody looked, or the
  instrument failed, and treating the three alike quietly invents
  data. The gaps are usually where measuring was hardest.
ROOT: evidence / a miss carries only as much information as the
  search's power, and a blank carries none at all until its cause
  is known.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (silence in an archive is not absence in
  the world), medicine (an untested patient is not a negative
  one), history (the unrecorded cannot testify).
ASKED-AS: missing data blank gaps no records unknown treated zero incomplete survey holes

ESSENCE: a count means nothing without its base — more accidents
  happen near home because nearly all driving happens near home,
  and the busiest hospital is not the most dangerous one. Always
  ask: out of how many?
ROOT: mathematics / a rate is a count divided by its exposure, and
  the division is where the meaning enters.
THREAD: money (profit means nothing without the capital it was
  earned on), sports (totals versus per-game), medicine (deaths
  per procedure, not deaths).
ASKED-AS: statistics per capita rate out of how many population compare counts totals

ESSENCE: comparing across time means undoing what changed anyway —
  more of everything happens in a bigger population, prices rise
  with the money supply, and shops sell more in December every
  year; a comparison that skips those corrections measures the
  correction instead of the thing.
ROOT: evidence / a difference is only evidence when the other
  differences have been held still.
THREAD: money (real versus nominal is exactly this), farming (a
  yield compared across a wet year and a dry one), sports
  (records across eras with changed rules).
ASKED-AS: inflation adjusted real terms record high seasonal comparison year over year growth

ESSENCE: "doubles your risk" is a sentence with a hole in it — a
  doubling of one in a million is still one in five hundred
  thousand, while a doubling of one in ten is a different life.
  The relative change and the absolute change are two facts, and
  only the pair means anything.
ROOT: mathematics / a ratio discards the size of what it divides,
  and headlines keep the ratio because it is the bigger-sounding
  half.
THREAD: medicine (a drug's benefit reported both ways looks like
  two different drugs), money (a doubled return on a tiny stake),
  persuasion (which half to quote is the whole trick).
ASKED-AS: risk doubles percent increase study headline chance cancer relative absolute small

ESSENCE: a percent and a percentage point are not the same
  quantity — a rate moving from 2% to 3% has risen one point and
  also risen by half, and both statements are true about the same
  event.
ROOT: mathematics / one is a difference, the other a ratio of
  differences, and mixing them silently changes the size of the
  claim.
THREAD: money (interest rates, fees, and tax rates all live in
  this confusion), shopping (stacked discounts never stack the way
  they read).
ASKED-AS: percent points interest rate rose sale discount stacked half off increase back

ESSENCE: rounding is a small lie repeated, and repetition is the
  danger — round each of a thousand entries and the errors mostly
  cancel, unless they were rounded the same direction, in which
  case they march.
ROOT: mathematics / independent small errors partly cancel while
  aligned ones add, so what matters is whether the rounding is
  even-handed.
THREAD: money (a fraction of a cent shaved off every transaction
  is the oldest theft there is), computing (the accumulated drift
  in long numerical calculations), cooking (a pinch, taken twenty
  times).
ASKED-AS: rounding up down totals do not match cents spreadsheet sum off pennies drift

ESSENCE: a number is too precise to be honest when it has more
  digits than its inputs earned — pace out a field roughly and a
  calculator will hand you an area to nine decimals; the extra
  digits are decoration that reads as authority.
ROOT: evidence / the answer inherits the uncertainty of the
  inputs, and no arithmetic adds information that was not measured.
THREAD: engineering (drawings state tolerances so nobody reads the
  digits as promises), money (a forecast to the dollar three years
  out), persuasion (false precision is a costume for confidence).
ASKED-AS: decimal places too many digits calculator answer converted exact estimate spurious rounding

ESSENCE: what a sample can speak for is set by how it was drawn,
  not by how big it is — a fair sample reports on exactly the
  crowd it was drawn from and no other, so a perfect national poll
  says nothing certain about your town.
ROOT: the part stands for the whole only through the drawing, and
  the drawing defines which whole.
THREAD: medicine (trial populations and who the drug was never
  tested on), law (a jury drawn from one district), teaching (a
  method proven in one kind of school).
ASKED-AS: sample represents group study applies me generalize population tested who results transfer

ESSENCE: the extremes live in the small groups — the highest and
  the lowest rates of almost anything will be found among the
  smallest towns, smallest schools, and smallest samples, purely
  because few draws scatter widely.
ROOT: chance / averages settle only as tries pile up, so a small
  count is free to wander far from the truth in either direction.
THREAD: money (the best-performing fund of the year is usually the
  smallest), sports (a hot week), medicine (rare-disease clusters
  in small counties that mean nothing).
ASKED-AS: small sample size unreliable best school town rates highest lowest tiny group

ESSENCE: precision is bought at four times the price — halving the
  uncertainty of an average needs four times the observations, not
  twice, because the scatter of a mean falls with the square root
  of the count.
ROOT: chance / independent errors partly cancel, and the
  cancelling improves slowly, not in step with the effort.
THREAD: money (diminishing returns on any effort), engineering
  (the last few percent of reliability costs more than the first
  ninety), polling (why sample sizes cluster near a thousand).
ASKED-AS: more data accuracy improve twice as good sample size cost survey diminishing

ESSENCE: an average alone is a rumour — the same average covers a
  tight lump, two separate crowds with nobody in the middle, and a
  long tail with a few giants; you cannot act on the number until
  you know which shape it summarises.
ROOT: mathematics / a single figure discards everything about the
  arrangement it came from.
THREAD: money (average returns hide the years that ruin), teaching
  (a class average with two groups in it), medicine (average
  survival with two very different diseases inside).
ASKED-AS: average misleading typical spread distribution range mean summary two groups shape

ESSENCE: in a lopsided pile the mean and the middle part company —
  a few very large values drag the average upward while the
  median stays with the crowd, so "average income" and "typical
  income" answer different questions and the gap between them
  measures the lopsidedness.
ROOT: mathematics / the mean weighs every value by its size, the
  median only counts heads.
THREAD: money (wealth statistics live or die on this choice),
  housing (average price versus median price), sports (average
  career length dragged by a handful of long ones).
ASKED-AS: average income median typical wage most people earn rich skew middle household

ESSENCE: co-movement is a licence to predict and never a licence
  to act — you may bet that the two move together, but the moment
  you push one to move the other, you have assumed a cause, and
  the assumption is the whole risk.
ROOT: evidence / correlation is a fact about a record, cause is a
  claim about intervening; only intervention tests intervention.
THREAD: money (a mined pattern that dies the day it is traded),
  medicine (habits of the healthy that do not make you healthy),
  management (a metric that predicted success until it was pushed).
ASKED-AS: correlation causation study linked predict acting on it intervention causes proves

ESSENCE: a split can flip the answer — a treatment can win in
  every single group and still lose overall, when the groups are
  unequal in size and in difficulty; the two readings are both
  arithmetically correct.
ROOT: mathematics / a combined ratio is not the average of its
  parts' ratios, because the parts carry different weights.
THREAD: medicine (a hospital with worse survival because it takes
  the worst cases), hiring (department-by-department fairness with
  an unfair total), sports (a better rate in every month and a
  worse season).
ASKED-AS: statistics paradox groups combined reversed overall better worse subgroups totals split

ESSENCE: regression to the middle is a fact about the measurement,
  not about the world — the noisier the measure, the harder the
  extremes are pulled back next time, because being extreme was
  partly luck and luck is redrawn.
ROOT: any score is true level plus a draw, and selecting the top
  of a list selects both, so the draw part cannot repeat.
THREAD: hiring (the star recruit who was partly a good interview
  day), teaching (streaming children by one noisy test), money
  (last year's best fund).
ASKED-AS: best performers next year worse selected top test scores luck repeat disappoint

ESSENCE: how common a thing is beats how well the test performs —
  before any evidence, ask how often this is true at all, because
  a rare condition starts so far behind that even good evidence
  rarely gets it in front.
ROOT: mathematics / a verdict combines the evidence with the prior
  frequency, and where the frequencies are lopsided they dominate.
THREAD: medicine (screening versus diagnosis), law (a matching
  detail in a city of millions), the mind (judging by resemblance
  rather than by frequency).
ASKED-AS: how likely rare common odds before test prior chance population frequency screening

ESSENCE: no test can miss less and cry wolf less at the same time
  — move the line to catch more of the real cases and more of the
  false ones come across with them, so every setting is a chosen
  balance between two different ways of being wrong.
ROOT: the two groups overlap on whatever is being measured, so any
  line drawn through them cuts into both.
THREAD: law (better that ten guilty go free is a threshold picked
  out loud), security (an alarm sensitive enough to catch
  everything gets switched off), medicine (screening set to miss
  little and therefore to frighten often).
ASKED-AS: test sensitivity threshold missed cases false alarms tradeoff catch everything tuning accuracy

ESSENCE: "significant" is a small word doing a small job — it
  means only that a result is bigger than the wobble luck alone
  would usually produce. It says nothing about whether the effect
  is large, useful, or real in your case.
ROOT: chance / any comparison of two draws shows some difference,
  so the first question is whether this difference is bigger than
  the ordinary scatter.
THREAD: evidence (replication is what significance is not), money
  (a strategy found by searching a thousand rules), medicine
  (clinically meaningful is a separate verdict from statistical).
ASKED-AS: significant study results proven means important statistics p value science headline

ESSENCE: the definition biases the number before any counting
  starts — change what counts as a crime, a case, a job, or a
  death, and the figure moves while the world stands still. Most
  arguments about numbers are arguments about definitions in
  disguise.
ROOT: measurement is comparison against a standard, and someone
  chose the standard, usually with something at stake.
THREAD: law (statutes are definitions with consequences), money
  (what counts as profit is a whole profession), history (records
  count what someone was paid to record).
ASKED-AS: statistics definition changed counted differently unemployment crime rate measured government figures

ESSENCE: any list assembled after the fact was filtered by
  survival — study only the ships that came home, the buildings
  still standing, the funds still open, and you learn the
  characteristics of survival, not of quality.
ROOT: the sample was drawn by the very outcome under study, so the
  cases that would have answered the question are the ones
  missing.
THREAD: engineering (armouring the bombers where the returned ones
  were holed), building (old buildings look well made because the
  bad ones fell), money (index histories that quietly drop the
  companies that died).
ASKED-AS: successful people habits secrets old buildings built better failures missing bias winners

ESSENCE: the ones who walk in the door are not the population —
  a clinic sees the sick, a complaints desk sees the furious, a
  repair shop sees the broken, so any pattern discovered there may
  be a pattern of who arrives rather than of what is true.
ROOT: sampling / the frame is whoever selected themselves, and
  self-selection is never blind.
THREAD: medicine (hospital data on diseases that rarely reach
  hospitals), reviews (only the delighted and the enraged write),
  dating (the pool is filtered before anyone meets).
ASKED-AS: patients clinic complaints reviews volunteered self selected who shows up skewed

ESSENCE: what is easy to measure gets measured instead of what
  matters — hours worked stands in for effort, test scores for
  learning, clicks for interest; the stand-in is never the thing,
  and the gap is where the mischief lives.
ROOT: measurement needs a countable handle, and important things
  frequently have none.
THREAD: teaching (the exam replacing the education), money (share
  price standing in for a company's health), medicine (a lab value
  standing in for how the patient feels).
ASKED-AS: metrics measure what matters proxy hours worked test scores clicks stand in

ESSENCE: a measure that becomes a target stops measuring — the
  moment a number decides pay, funding, or blame, effort flows to
  the number, and it detaches from what it once tracked while
  continuing to look healthy.
ROOT: people optimise what is scored; the measure was only ever
  correlated with the goal, and the correlation is not protected.
THREAD: hospitals (waiting-time targets met by renaming waits),
  policing (crime figures managed rather than crime), teaching
  (drilling the test), money (quarterly earnings shaped rather
  than earned).
ASKED-AS: targets gaming metrics bonus incentives measure becomes goal distorted quotas kpi

ESSENCE: measuring disturbs — a thermometer takes some heat from
  the drink, a tyre gauge lets out a little air, a question plants
  an opinion the asked person did not have, and people who know
  they are watched behave like people who know they are watched.
ROOT: any reading requires an interaction, and an interaction
  moves both parties.
THREAD: physics (at the smallest scale this stops being a nuisance
  and becomes a law), management (the visit that improves the shop
  for a day), medicine (blood pressure raised by the cuff).
ASKED-AS: observed changes behaviour watching survey question influences measurement affects results monitored

ESSENCE: a ranking is not a measurement — an order tells you who
  is ahead and hides by how much, so first and second may be a
  hair apart while second and third are a canyon; the list looks
  like equal steps and never is.
ROOT: mathematics / ranking discards distance and keeps only
  sequence.
THREAD: sports (league tables where the gap matters more than the
  order), schools (rankings driven by hairline differences),
  money (top-ten lists that hide the spread).
ASKED-AS: rankings league table position first second gap between close list order places

ESSENCE: a composite score is an opinion wearing a number — to
  combine unlike things into one figure you must decide how much
  each one counts, and those weights are chosen by somebody, then
  vanish inside the total.
ROOT: mathematics / unlike quantities cannot be added without a
  conversion rate, and the rate is a judgement, not a measurement.
THREAD: money (a stock index is a weighting decision), teaching
  (grade point averages across unlike subjects), law (sentencing
  scores that bury their assumptions).
ASKED-AS: index score ranking combines factors weighted overall rating best places methodology

ESSENCE: a measured relationship holds only across the range it
  was measured in — fertiliser improves yield up to a point and
  then poisons the field, and the straight line drawn through the
  tested range says nothing about what happens past the last data
  point.
ROOT: evidence / a finding describes the conditions it was found
  in; outside them, the finding is a guess wearing its authority.
THREAD: medicine (dose-response curves that turn), engineering
  (materials that behave until they suddenly do not), money (a
  growth rate carried forward for twenty years).
ASKED-AS: trend continues projection future straight line beyond data extrapolate growth chart

ESSENCE: whoever picks the starting date picks the trend — begin
  the chart at a peak and everything is falling, begin it at a
  trough and everything is climbing, and both charts use the same
  honest data.
ROOT: any short window samples one stretch of a wandering series,
  and the window is chosen by somebody.
THREAD: money (performance shown from a flattering year), climate
  (a decade picked to start hot), sports (form measured from a
  chosen match onward).
ASKED-AS: chart starting point cherry picked since year trend depends window data selective

ESSENCE: the same thing counted twice inflates quietly — one
  hospital transfer becomes two admissions, one product crossing a
  border three times becomes three times the trade, and totals
  built from overlapping lists count the overlap as new.
ROOT: mathematics / counting requires each item to belong to
  exactly one bucket, and real categories overlap.
THREAD: money (revenue counted at each step of a chain), keeping
  knowledge (the same document filed under three headings), law
  (one act charged as several offences).
ASKED-AS: counted twice totals overlap categories add up more than whole duplicates records

ESSENCE: a clock does not tell time; it counts something regular
  and we agree to call the count time — a swinging weight, a
  vibrating crystal, an atom's hum — so a clock is only as true as
  the regularity it borrows.
ROOT: measurement is comparison, and time's standard has to be a
  repeating physical event because nothing else is available.
THREAD: music (a metronome is a clock with a job), navigation
  (longitude waited on a clock that held true at sea), computing
  (machines that must agree what time it is before agreeing on
  anything).
ASKED-AS: clock accurate atomic quartz pendulum keeps time drift seconds standard measure

ESSENCE: "at the same moment" is a comparison that has to be
  shipped — you cannot check two distant clocks without sending a
  signal between them, and the sending takes time, so simultaneity
  is built, never observed.
ROOT: physics / no signal is instant, so every claim about two
  places at one instant contains an assumption about the delay.
THREAD: computing (distributed machines that can never fully agree
  on order), law (timestamps as evidence), astronomy (the "now" of
  a distant star does not exist for us).
ASKED-AS: same time two places sync clocks delay signal timestamp order events simultaneous

ESSENCE: a calendar is bookkeeping for an awkward orbit — the day,
  the moon's month, and the year do not divide into one another,
  so every calendar is a rounding scheme with a repair schedule
  built in.
ROOT: astronomy / three independent cycles with no common
  multiple; mathematics — the remainders must go somewhere.
THREAD: money (accounts that must be reconciled because the
  periods do not line up), farming (planting by the sun while
  festivals ride the moon).
ASKED-AS: leap year february calendar days months why 365 drift dates changed reform

ESSENCE: every clock in the rocks rests on two assumptions — that
  the starting amount is known and that nothing has come in or
  gone out since; a date is exactly as good as those two, and not
  a decimal better.
ROOT: evidence / a measurement's honesty lives in its assumptions,
  and buried samples are exposed to a great deal of history.
THREAD: evidence (replication in another form), keeping knowledge
  (a document dated by three unrelated marks), astronomy (tree
  rings and ice layers cross-checking the same centuries).
ASKED-AS: carbon dating accurate how old assumptions contamination reliable radiometric years tested wrong

ESSENCE: the mind extrapolates in straight lines and the world
  frequently does not — a thing that grows by a percentage looks
  flat, then modest, then impossible, and the impossible part
  arrives in a single stretch that feels like it came from
  nowhere.
ROOT: mathematics / repeated multiplication outruns any addition,
  and intuition is built for addition.
THREAD: medicine (an outbreak that seems small until it is not),
  money (debt at a steady rate), farming (a herd past its
  pasture).
ASKED-AS: exponential growth doubling sudden virus spread compound surprise fast slow start

ESSENCE: divide seventy by the percentage and you have the
  doubling time — 7% growth doubles in ten years, 2% in
  thirty-five; that single division turns any growth rate into a
  human timescale you can argue with.
ROOT: mathematics / a fixed proportional gain compounds at a fixed
  pace, and the rule of seventy is that pace, near enough.
THREAD: money (inflation and interest read the same way), farming
  (a population's real timetable), energy (demand growth in a
  finite system).
ASKED-AS: doubling time rule seventy growth rate percent years population interest how long

ESSENCE: proportional changes multiply rather than add, and that
  makes losses heavier than gains — a 50% fall needs a 100% rise
  to get back to even, so a sequence of ups and downs ends below
  the average of its percentages.
ROOT: mathematics / multiplying by 0.5 and then by 1.5 gives 0.75,
  not 1; the order does not matter but the multiplication does.
THREAD: money (why volatility itself costs, apart from any loss),
  health (weight and fitness regained is harder than lost),
  chance (why the wall at zero is absorbing).
ASKED-AS: lost half back even percent gains average return volatile down up recover

ESSENCE: some of the world is read in ratios, not differences —
  sound, earthquakes, star brightness, acidity, and money all
  register as multiples, so the useful scale spaces equal FACTORS
  equally, and each step is a tenfold jump.
ROOT: measurement should match the way the quantity behaves, and
  many quantities act on their own size rather than on a fixed
  amount.
THREAD: music (an octave is a doubling — the ear is logarithmic by
  construction), money (a raise means the same to two people as a
  percentage, not as an amount), astronomy (star brightness).
ASKED-AS: richter scale decibels magnitude ph log chart axis times stronger loudness steps

ESSENCE: you can build a usable number out of nothing but things
  you already know — break the question into factors you can each
  guess within a factor of two or three, multiply, and the
  independent errors partly cancel, so the chain lands closer than
  any of its links.
ROOT: chance / errors in different directions offset; mathematics
  — a product of rough factors is less rough than it deserves.
THREAD: engineering (a back-of-envelope check before any design),
  money (sizing a market before believing a forecast), teaching
  (the fastest cure for a wrong answer is estimating it first).
ASKED-AS: estimate roughly how many guess piano tuners work out first principles ballpark

ESSENCE: every number has a maker and a reason — someone chose to
  count this rather than that, funded it, and stopped counting at
  some point; a figure with no provenance is an assertion in
  numeric costume.
ROOT: measurement is an act performed by an interested party, so
  the act's history is part of the evidence.
THREAD: evidence (replication asks exactly this question), law
  (chain of custody), keeping knowledge (a citation is a
  provenance record, which is why it matters).
ASKED-AS: source statistic who says where from study funded cite figure quoted origin
