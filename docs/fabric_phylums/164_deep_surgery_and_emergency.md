# 164 DEEP SURGERY AND EMERGENCY — when time decides
File 100 holds what an untrained person should do and file 110 what
an operation costs the patient. This one holds the trade itself: the
order of work when minutes decide, what an operation can and cannot
be, and the failures that are made in the room rather than in the
body. Every entry carries what it forbids.

ESSENCE: when there are more casualties than hands, the goal quietly
  changes. Ordinarily every patient gets the best available care. In a
  mass casualty the goal becomes the greatest number of survivors, and
  those are different aims that give different answers — the person
  who would consume four hours and two theatres for a small chance is
  passed over, not because they matter less, but because the arithmetic
  has changed.

  obligation owed to a group are separate duties, and scarcity is what
  forces a choice between them.

  spent where it changes the most outcomes).

ESSENCE: sending too many people as urgent is its own harm. A
  receiving hospital has a handful of theatres and one blood bank, and
  every over-triaged patient occupies the team that the genuinely
  dying one needed. Getting it wrong upward looks safe and generous
  and is neither: the measure of a triage system is how few of both
  errors it makes, not how cautious it is.
ROOT: engineering / a queue with a fixed service rate is destroyed by
  false priority as surely as by missed priority.
THREAD: engineering (a system swamped by everything being marked
  urgent), computing (an alert threshold set so low the operator stops
  reading), money.
ASKED-AS: too many urgent hospital swamped over triage wrong category repeated again worse resources

ESSENCE: the golden hour is not magic and it is not sixty minutes. It
  names one true thing: the survival curve after serious injury is
  steepest at the start, so a minute early is worth many minutes
  later. And the clock starts at the injury, not at the ambulance, and
  it stops at the control of bleeding, not at the hospital door.
ROOT: this file / tissue starved of blood dies on its own schedule,
  so the only clock that matters runs from the moment supply stopped.
THREAD: engineering (a fire's first minute worth its next hour),
  movement of goods, strategy + conflict.
ASKED-AS: golden hour trauma time hospital ambulance bleeding stopped clock starts injury minutes matter

ESSENCE: the first pass over a badly injured person is not a search
  for the diagnosis. It is a search for the next thing that will kill
  in the next five minutes, taken in the order the plumbing fails —
  and each finding is treated before the next is looked for. Then it
  is done again. A patient who changes gets the whole sweep from the
  beginning.
ROOT: engineering / a chain fails at its first broken link, so
  everything downstream is wasted until that link is fixed.
THREAD: engineering (fixing a stalled process at the stall, not at
  the end), flight (a memorised sequence run under load), computing.
ASKED-AS: emergency assessment order airway breathing circulation treat as you find repeat deteriorates again

ESSENCE: the injury everybody is looking at is rarely the one that
  kills. An open fracture, a facial wound, a screaming patient — all
  of these pull the whole room's attention, and the quiet chest or the
  quiet abdomen kills while the team crowds round the dramatic one.
  Every trauma system has a rule for this, and every rule exists
  because it kept happening.
ROOT: the mind / attention is a single narrow beam, and it is pulled
  by what is vivid rather than by what is dangerous.
THREAD: the mind, flight (a crew fixated on a light while the fuel
  runs out), strategy + conflict.
ASKED-AS: obvious injury distracting broken leg missed chest bleeding attention team crowd quiet patient

ESSENCE: injury is a disease of energy, and asking how much energy
  went in and along what line predicts the damage better than the
  examination does. A fall from a height loads the spine and the
  heels. A side impact injures the organs on that side. A patient
  ejected from a vehicle has taken the whole energy twice. The
  mechanism is evidence, and it is available before the patient is.
ROOT: physics / energy is conserved and has to be absorbed by
  something, so what absorbed it is written in how it arrived.
THREAD: physics, engineering (reading a failure from the load path),
  evidence + measurement.
ASKED-AS: car crash fall height mechanism injury predicted looks fine scan anyway energy speed

ESSENCE: an airway is lost slowly and then all at once. Swelling from
  a burn, a spreading bruise in the neck, an expanding infection under
  the tongue — each gives hours of warning and then closes in minutes,
  and once it has closed it may be impossible to reopen. Which is why
  the airway is secured while it still looks unnecessary, and why
  waiting for it to be needed is the error.
ROOT: engineering / an access that will be needed later must be taken
  while access is still possible, because the failure removes the
  ability to respond to it.
THREAD: engineering (fitting the shut-off valve before the pipe
  bursts), flight, survival.
ASKED-AS: airway swelling burns neck throat closing early tube waiting too late talking fine

ESSENCE: air can get into the chest through a wound that acts as a
  one-way valve — in with each breath, none out. The lung collapses,
  and then the pressure keeps building and pushes everything sideways
  until the great veins are kinked and no blood returns to the heart.
  It kills by stopping the circulation, not by stopping the breathing,
  which is why it kills so fast.
ROOT: physics / a sealed space with a one-way inlet accumulates
  pressure without limit, and everything around it is soft.
THREAD: physics, engineering (a vessel with a stuck relief valve),
  medicine.
ASKED-AS: collapsed lung tension chest pressure needle veins kinked blood pressure falls fast emergency

ESSENCE: a young fit adult can lose a third of their blood and hold a
  normal blood pressure, by clamping every vessel that is not
  essential and squeezing the reserve out of their own veins. The
  pressure falls only when that is exhausted, and then it falls off a
  cliff. The compensation is the danger: it is what makes the patient
  look survivable right up to the moment they are not.
ROOT: living machinery / a system that defends a number will hold the
  number until it cannot, so the number reports the reserve rather
  than the loss.
THREAD: money (a business paying every bill up to the day it cannot),
  engineering, evidence + measurement.
ASKED-AS: blood loss pressure normal pulse young athlete elderly medication compensating crash suddenly hidden

ESSENCE: shock feeds itself. Low flow means cells burn without
  oxygen, which makes acid; acid makes the heart weaker and the
  vessels stop answering the body's own signals to tighten; that
  drops the flow further. Past a point the loop runs regardless of
  whether the original bleeding has been stopped, and stopping it is
  no longer enough.
ROOT: engineering / a feedback loop with gain above one runs away
  from its starting point, and the starting cause becomes irrelevant.
THREAD: money (a run that continues after the bad news is settled),
  engineering, oceans + weather.
ASKED-AS: shock spiral worse acid heart weaker vessels stop responding bleeding stopped still dying

ESSENCE: the modern answer to a patient who is bleeding to death is a
  deliberately unfinished operation. Stop the bleeding, stop the
  contamination, pack, close temporarily, and leave — the repair is
  not attempted. The patient goes to intensive care to be warmed and
  corrected, and comes back the next day for the real operation.
ROOT: this file / a body in the spiral cannot survive the physiology
  of a long operation, so the operation is cut to what the body can
  afford.
THREAD: engineering (a temporary repair to get a ship into port),
  building, money (stopping the bleeding before restructuring).
ASKED-AS: damage control surgery packed temporary closed back tomorrow intensive care warm second operation

ESSENCE: three things arrive together in a badly injured patient and
  each worsens the other two. Cold blood does not clot. Acid stops the
  clotting proteins and the heart working. Failure to clot means more
  bleeding, which means more cold and more acid. Any one alone is
  manageable and the three together are what kills on the table.
ROOT: chemistry / the clotting cascade is a chain of enzyme reactions,
  and enzymes stop at the wrong temperature and the wrong acidity.
THREAD: chemistry, engineering (three faults that each disable the
  fix for the others), cooking.
ASKED-AS: cold acid not clotting trauma triad warming blankets bleeding worse table temperature factors

ESSENCE: an injured patient is being cooled by everything around
  them — wet clothes cut away, a cold room, litres of fluid at room
  temperature, an open abdomen, and no shivering because they are
  anaesthetised. Theatres are kept cold for the staff. In trauma that
  is reversed: the room is heated, the fluids are warmed, and the
  patient is covered at every moment they are not being worked on.
ROOT: physics / a body loses heat by conduction, evaporation and
  radiation at once, and an anaesthetised body has lost every
  correction it would normally make.
THREAD: physics, survival, building.
ASKED-AS: patient cold theatre warm fluids blankets anaesthetic shivering stopped heat lost open abdomen

ESSENCE: what to pour in has changed more than once and is still
  moving. The old teaching was large volumes of salt water early. The
  current position is much less of it, accepting a lower blood
  pressure until the bleeding is controlled, and giving blood rather
  than fluid — because pressure pushes clots off and salt water
  dilutes the clotting proteins and cools the patient. Mark this
  UNSURE: the targets differ by injury and the evidence in head injury
  points the other way.
ROOT: this file / raising the pressure in a bleeding circuit
  increases the bleeding, which is the plainest fact in the argument
  and was ignored for decades.
THREAD: engineering (pressure-testing a leaking system), evidence +
  measurement, medicine.
ASKED-AS: fluids trauma how much saline blood pressure target head injury changed advice unsure

ESSENCE: blood is not one product. Massive bleeding loses red cells,
  clotting proteins and platelets together, so replacing red cells
  alone produces a patient who is well oxygenated and cannot clot at
  all. The modern practice gives the three back in roughly the
  proportion they were lost in, which is close to giving whole blood
  back — the thing that was replaced by components in the first place.
ROOT: engineering / replacing one component of a mixture changes the
  mixture, so proportional loss demands proportional replacement.
THREAD: engineering, money, chemistry.
ASKED-AS: transfusion units blood plasma platelets ratio massive bleeding whole stored old oxygen

ESSENCE: tourniquets were taught for a century as a last resort that
  cost a limb. Military data settled it: almost all the limb loss came
  from the delay before the tourniquet went on, not from the
  tourniquet, and a properly applied one can stay for two hours with
  no lasting harm. The device was rehabilitated by measurement against
  a doctrine that had never been measured.
ROOT: evidence + measurement / a harm attributed to an intervention
  has to be compared against the harm of not using it, and the
  comparison had never been made.
THREAD: evidence + measurement, war, engineering.
ASKED-AS: tourniquet lose limb myth military evidence two hours tight enough time written loose

ESSENCE: stopping bleeding is a ladder and every rung is tried before
  the next. Direct pressure on the point. Packing the cavity and
  pressing on the packing. A clamp on a vessel you can see. A tie or a
  repair. What is never done is reaching into a pool of blood with a
  clamp and closing it on something unseen — the structures beside a
  bleeding artery are a nerve, a duct and a vein, and they are what
  gets caught.
ROOT: craft / a repair is made under vision, and a blind action in a
  confined space damages what it cannot see.
THREAD: craft, plumbing, engineering.
ASKED-AS: stop bleeding pressure packing clamp blind grabbing nerve damaged see first exposure suction

ESSENCE: some bleeding cannot be pressed on. A broken pelvis bleeds
  into a space that no hand can reach and no dressing can compress,
  and the same is true of the chest, the abdomen and the space behind
  it. For the pelvis the answer is mechanical — a binder or sheet
  wrapped tightly closes the ring and reduces the volume the blood can
  fill.
ROOT: physics / bleeding into a space stops when the pressure in the
  space matches the vessel's, so a space that can expand indefinitely
  never reaches that point.
THREAD: physics, building (a ring under tension), engineering.
ASKED-AS: pelvis fracture bleeding binder sheet wrapped internal cannot press abdomen chest theatre scan

ESSENCE: a limb crushed for hours is not injured mainly by the crush.
  The trapped muscle dies and fills with potassium and breakdown
  products, held in place while the blood supply is cut off — and the
  dangerous moment is the release, when all of it arrives at the heart
  at once. People have died on being freed, having been talking
  throughout.
ROOT: this file / a poison confined to a compartment is harmless
  until the compartment is opened.
THREAD: chemistry, engineering (a pressure vessel vented into an
  unprepared system), survival.
ASKED-AS: crush injury trapped released died kidneys potassium muscle breakdown fluids before freeing earthquake

ESSENCE: muscle lives in closed sleeves of tough membrane. Swell the
  muscle inside one — from injury, from bleeding, from blood returning
  to a starved limb — and the pressure inside rises until it exceeds
  the pressure in the small vessels, and the muscle strangles itself
  with the artery still open and the pulse still present. The only
  treatment is to cut the sleeve open along its length.
ROOT: physics / flow through a vessel depends on the difference
  between its pressure and the pressure around it, so a rising outside
  pressure stops flow without touching the vessel.
THREAD: physics, building (a wall pushed by what is behind it),
  engineering.
ASKED-AS: compartment syndrome pain out of proportion pulse present cast tight cut open hours

ESSENCE: tissues have different clocks without blood. Nerve suffers
  in under an hour, muscle is dying by four to six, skin and bone last
  far longer — so a limb reattached at eight hours may be alive and
  useless. And restoring the flow is itself an injury: the returning
  blood delivers oxygen to damaged cells that turn it into a fresh
  wave of destruction.
ROOT: this file / the same oxygen that tissue is dying without is
  what the damaged machinery converts into poison when it arrives.
THREAD: chemistry, engineering, farming.
ASKED-AS: blood supply cut off hours limb bowel dead reattach amputate reperfusion injury clock

ESSENCE: wounds are sorted by what got into them, and the class
  predicts the infection rate before anything else is known. An
  untouched surgical incision through prepared skin. One that entered
  the gut or airway under control. One with a spill. And one already
  full of dead tissue, dirt or pus. The infection rate rises roughly
  tenfold across that range, and it is decided before the first
  stitch.
ROOT: this file / infection is a race decided by the starting number,
  so the count of germs present at the beginning dominates everything
  done afterwards.
THREAD: farming, cleaning, chance + risk.
ASKED-AS: wound clean dirty contaminated class infection risk gut opened pus stitches antibiotics prediction

ESSENCE: dead tissue is not treatable. It has no blood supply, so no
  drug reaches it and no defence patrols it, and it is the ideal food
  for anything growing. Removing it — cutting back to tissue that
  bleeds — is the single most important act in managing a serious
  wound, and it usually has to be repeated, because the zone of dying
  extends over the following days.
ROOT: living machinery / nothing is treated where blood does not
  reach, so tissue outside the circulation is outside medicine.
THREAD: farming (cutting out rot rather than treating it), cleaning,
  craft.
ASKED-AS: dead tissue cut away debridement repeated wound bleeding edge antibiotics not enough burn crush

ESSENCE: closing a wound is a bet that it is clean enough. If the bet
  is wrong the closure seals the germs in with warmth and a food
  supply, and the infection is worse than if nothing had been done. So
  a wound that is old, dirty, crushed or from a bite is left open and
  closed a few days later — not because time heals it, but because
  those days are the proof that it is clean.
ROOT: this file / a decision made on evidence you do not yet have can
  be deferred until the evidence exists, and here the wound supplies
  it.
THREAD: money (a payment held until delivery is verified), craft,
  farming.
ASKED-AS: wound left open closed later days dirty bite delayed closure proof clean tension

ESSENCE: two different ideas, often confused. Antisepsis is killing
  the germs that are there — the historic revolution, carbolic spray
  over an operation. Asepsis is not letting them arrive: sterilised
  instruments, drapes, gowns, filtered air, and a discipline about
  what may touch what. Modern surgery is almost entirely the second,
  and the first is what is left over.
ROOT: engineering / excluding a contaminant is far more reliable than
  removing it, wherever exclusion is possible.
THREAD: cleaning, building (keeping a site clean against cleaning it
  afterwards), engineering.
ASKED-AS: sterile field antiseptic scrub gloves gown touched something unsterile again instruments air theatre

ESSENCE: the things that actually change wound infection rates are
  unglamorous and mostly physiological. The patient kept warm. Enough
  oxygen given. Blood sugar controlled. The preventive dose given
  before the cut rather than after it. Hair clipped rather than
  shaved. And, above all, tissue handled gently and bleeding stopped,
  so nothing is left dead or pooled.
ROOT: this file / infection is decided by the starting number of
  germs and the condition of the tissue, so anything improving the
  tissue's defences beats anything applied to the surface.
THREAD: farming, evidence + measurement, cleaning.
ASKED-AS: infection after surgery prevent warm oxygen sugar antibiotic before shaving clipping gentle handling

ESSENCE: something left inside a patient is a systems failure, not a
  personal one. It happens in long operations, in emergencies, when
  the plan changes, and when the team changes over — precisely when
  attention is most loaded. The answer is not care but counting: two
  people, out loud, at fixed points, with the count reconciled before
  closure and a picture taken if it is not.
ROOT: engineering / a check performed by the same person doing the
  work checks nothing, so verification has to be independent and
  externalised.
THREAD: engineering, flight (a checklist because trained people
  forget), law.

ESSENCE: the three components of anaesthesia are measured with very
  different confidence. Stillness is measured directly and precisely.
  Pain relief is inferred from the body's responses. Unconsciousness
  is not measured at all — it is estimated from the dose, the age and
  the machine's readings. Which is why the rare failure is a paralysed
  patient who is awake, and why paralysis and depth are watched as
  separate things.
ROOT: evidence + measurement / a variable that cannot be measured
  directly is estimated, and estimates fail in the cases that differ
  from the average.
THREAD: evidence + measurement, right + wrong, engineering.

ESSENCE: sedation is not a category, it is a slope. A patient given
  something to take the edge off slides through drowsy, to not
  responding to speech, to not responding to pain, to not breathing —
  with no line crossed and nobody changing the plan. Most of the
  serious harm from sedation happens to people who were never intended
  to be anaesthetised.
ROOT: pharmacology / effects appear in an order as the dose climbs,
  and the ordering is continuous, so there is no natural boundary
  where a name changes.
THREAD: pharmacology, engineering (a control with no detent), flight.

ESSENCE: airway management is planned as a ladder with the failure
  written down in advance: the first attempt, the alternative, the
  device that goes in blind, and at the bottom a cut through the front
  of the neck. The rung that kills is the one where nobody moves down
  the ladder — repeated attempts at the same technique while the
  oxygen falls. The decision to cut is a timing decision, not a
  technical one.
ROOT: engineering / a plan that has no declared failure point turns
  every failure into improvisation under time pressure.
THREAD: flight (a decision height declared before the approach),
  engineering, strategy + conflict.
ASKED-AS: intubation failed cannot ventilate oxygen falling neck incision plan attempts limit decision moment

ESSENCE: most monitored numbers change late. Blood pressure holds
  until compensation fails; oxygen saturation falls only after the
  reserve in the lungs is used; the heart rate says a dozen things at
  once. The one that changes instantly and cannot be argued with is
  the carbon dioxide traced on every breath — it proves the tube is in
  the lungs, and it collapses the moment the circulation does.
ROOT: evidence + measurement / an instrument's worth is set by how
  early it moves, and most physiological variables are defended and
  therefore late.
THREAD: evidence + measurement, flight, engineering.

ESSENCE: an anaesthetised body makes no complaint. It will lie for
  four hours in a position no awake person would hold for two minutes,
  with an arm hyperextended, a nerve pressed against a rail, an eye
  unprotected, or the weight of the body on one heel. A meaningful
  share of surgical injury has nothing to do with the operation and
  everything to do with how the patient was left lying.
ROOT: living machinery / pain and fidgeting are what normally
  redistribute pressure, and anaesthesia removes both.
THREAD: engineering (a load left on one point), building, medicine.

ESSENCE: everybody underestimates blood loss, and by a lot. A litre
  on a floor looks like a catastrophe and a litre in the drapes and
  swabs looks like nothing. The reliable methods are unglamorous —
  weighing the swabs, measuring the suction, counting what went into
  the patient — and the estimate made by eye is consistently and
  substantially low.
ROOT: the mind / a quantity is judged by how vivid it looks, and
  spread thin over cloth is not vivid.
THREAD: the mind, evidence + measurement, cooking.

ESSENCE: how tissue is handled decides the outcome as much as what is
  done to it. Tissue that is crushed by an instrument, dried by long
  exposure, burnt by too much diathermy or strangled by a tight stitch
  is dead tissue in a wound, and it behaves exactly like dead tissue
  put there by an injury. Gentleness is not a courtesy; it is a
  measurable determinant of healing and infection.
ROOT: this file / the wound's fate is decided by how much dead tissue
  and pooled blood it contains, and the operation is capable of
  supplying both.
THREAD: craft (a joint made by a hand that respects the material),
  cooking, farming.

ESSENCE: a stitch's job is to hold edges together until the tissue
  holds itself, and every principle follows from that. Approximate,
  do not strangle. Take equal bites from each side or the edges
  step. Close in the layers the body built. Put the knot where it will
  not sit in the wound. And accept that the stitch material is a
  foreign body, so fewer and finer is better than more and stronger.
ROOT: craft / a clamp holds while the glue sets and is not the joint,
  so its only virtue is holding without harming.
THREAD: craft, textiles, building.
ASKED-AS: stitches how many tight strangle edges layers knot material foreign body scar tension

ESSENCE: a closed wound is at its weakest around the end of the first
  week — the temporary glue has been dissolved and the permanent
  material is not yet laid down, and that is when wounds burst. It
  then strengthens for months and never regains what it had: a mature
  scar reaches about four fifths of the original strength and stops
  there.

ESSENCE: a drain removes fluid that would otherwise collect, and it
  is also a tube from the outside world into the wound. The argument
  is old and mostly settled the same way: drains do not prevent the
  collections people put them in for, they do not substitute for
  stopping the bleeding, and in most operations they cause more
  infection than they prevent. Where they earn their place is warning
  of a leak.
ROOT: this file / every device through the skin is a route inward,
  and its benefit has to exceed that standing cost every day it stays.
THREAD: engineering, plumbing, security (an access left open for
  convenience).

ESSENCE: joining two ends of a tube is the operation's most fragile
  moment, and only three things decide whether it holds: both ends
  have a good blood supply, they are joined without tension, and the
  field is not infected. Technique matters much less than those three.
  A leak declares itself around the fifth to seventh day, after the
  patient has been getting better, which is exactly what makes it
  dangerous.

ESSENCE: the moment a patient is handed from one team to another is

  serious harm begins. The receiving team has no history, no sense of
  the trend, and no idea which findings were already considered and
  dismissed. What survives a handover is what was written down and
  spoken in a structured order — everything else evaporates.

ESSENCE: choosing not to operate is a surgical decision, made with

ESSENCE: for the sickest patients, resuscitation and operation are

ESSENCE: the same operation done as an emergency carries several

  night hours, the missing scan, the absent specialist, and the
  decisions taken with less information by people who are tired.
ROOT: this file / an operation's outcome is mostly set by the

THREAD: engineering (a repair in the field against one in the works),

ESSENCE: making a patient fitter before an operation works, and only
  where the delay costs less than the improvement. Correcting anaemia,
  stopping smoking for weeks, treating an infection, restoring
  nutrition — each measurably lowers the risk. Against that, most
  cancers and every acute condition grow worse while the preparation
  runs. The whole decision is which clock is faster.

ESSENCE: some operations are planned to be repeated. A contaminated

ESSENCE: a great many injured organs are now not operated on at all.
  A torn spleen or liver in a patient whose circulation is stable is
  watched, and most stop bleeding by themselves. But watching is not
  the absence of treatment — it requires a surgeon awake and nearby,
  repeated examination, and a theatre that can be reached in minutes,
  which is exactly why it is only safe in some hospitals.
ROOT: this file / an operation is an injury, so avoiding it is a
  benefit wherever the natural course is at least as good.
THREAD: farming (leaving a thing alone as a decision requiring

ESSENCE: a chest drain is a simple idea done carefully. A tube into
  the space around the lung, connected to a seal that lets air and
  fluid out and nothing back in, so the lung can re-expand and hold.
