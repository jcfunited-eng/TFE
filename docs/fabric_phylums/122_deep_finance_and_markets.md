# 122 DEEP FINANCE AND MARKETS — money that moves
Nothing here is advice. These are the laws that hold whenever money
is lent, pooled, priced, or handed to a stranger to look after —
what such an arrangement can do, what it cannot do at any price,
and who is left holding the bill when it comes apart.

ESSENCE: nobody pays extra for nothing. A promise of more money than
  the safe rate is the market telling you what it thinks could go
  wrong with that promise. When something offers far more and looks
  perfectly safe, you have not found a bargain — you have found a
  risk you cannot see yet.
ROOT: money / a price is where two valuations meet, so the price of a
  future payment falls exactly as far as buyers doubt it will arrive.
CANNOT: no extra return without somebody carrying a matching risk,
  and it is you, whether or not you have located it. And no reward
  simply for being brave — risk is the ticket price, never the prize,
  and most of the people who took large risks got poorer.
THREAD: chance (a bet's odds are its price), lending (a rate is a
  rating of the borrower written as a number), medicine (a drug with
  no side effects usually has no effect either).
ASKED-AS: safe investment guaranteed high interest too good risky returns promised secure double money

ESSENCE: hold twenty unrelated things instead of one and the swings
  partly cancel while the average return does not drop — one side of
  the bargain improves and the other does not get worse. It is the
  only place in money where that is true, which is why people call it
  the only free lunch there is.
ROOT: chance / unrelated wobbles cancel inside a sum, so many small
  independent holdings are steadier than one large one of equal size.
CANNOT: no cancelling without independence — twenty things that rise
  and fall together are one thing bought twenty times, and five funds
  holding the same companies are one fund with five fees. And no
  spreading away the part that moves everything at once.
THREAD: farming (fields scattered across a valley), the living world
  (a species tied to one food), insurance (a pool is this same law
  sold across a counter).
ASKED-AS: diversify spread money one stock portfolio eggs basket index fund holdings safer mix

ESSENCE: almost all the benefit of spreading arrives in the first
  handful — the second holding does most of the work, the tenth does a
  little, the hundredth does nearly nothing. Past that point you are
  paying real costs to chase a saving too small to find.
ROOT: mathematics / the wobble of an average shrinks with the square
  root of the count, so each addition buys less than the one before it.
CANNOT: no proportional gain from more names — one to four helps more
  than twenty-five to a hundred. And no escape by owning everything:
  a portfolio of the whole market still carries the whole market's
  swing, which was always most of the swing.
THREAD: evidence (a larger sample sharpens slowly for the same
  reason), making (four bolts where two would do), cooking (a stew of
  forty ingredients that tastes of nothing in particular).
ASKED-AS: how many stocks enough diversified funds overlap owning everything simple portfolio number count

ESSENCE: holdings that drifted apart for years move as one on the
  worst day. In a panic people sell whatever will sell, not whatever
  they dislike — so the spreading that was meant to protect you closes
  up in exactly the week you needed it open.
ROOT: premise — in a rush the seller's need decides what gets sold,
  and need has nothing to do with which company is any good.
CANNOT: no trusting a measured relationship through a crisis — it was
  measured in calm weather and the crisis is a different machine. And
  no hedge that has only been tested in ordinary conditions: it is not
  known to work until something has asked it in a storm.
THREAD: making (parts sharing one power supply share its failure),
  people (a bank run — everybody doing the sensible thing at once),
  dominoes standing safely apart until one leans.
ASKED-AS: everything fell together crash diversification didnt help correlation panic selling bonds stocks same

ESSENCE: liquidity is not a feature of a thing, it is a feature of the
  exit — how much you give up by having to sell today instead of
  whenever suits you. A house and a share can be worth the same on
  paper and be entirely different objects on a morning you must have
  cash by four o'clock.
ROOT: money / a price needs a buyer standing there; without one you
  have a valuation and no price at all.
CANNOT: no selling at the quoted price in a hurry — urgency is
  information, and the buyer reads it off you. And no liquidity in the
  hour it is worth paying for: markets are deepest when nobody needs
  to leave and thinnest when everybody does.
THREAD: property sales dragged out over months, work (a skill with one
  possible employer), farming (a crop that must move the week it
  ripens or rot).
ASKED-AS: sell quickly cash out house stock buyer hurry frozen market discount trapped need money

ESSENCE: a thing whose price is printed every second looks wild, and a
  thing valued once a year looks calm, and the difference between them
  can be nothing but the printing. Smoothness on a chart is often the
  absence of a quote rather than the absence of a danger.
ROOT: evidence / a measurement not taken is not a measurement of zero
  — a gap in the record reads to the eye as steadiness.
CANNOT: no judging risk from a price produced only occasionally. And
  no comfort from an appraisal: a valuation nobody had to trade
  against has never once been tested by a seller in a hurry.
THREAD: houses (a market with no ticker), private funds priced by
  their own managers, weather (a gauge read monthly shows no storms).
ASKED-AS: private equity real estate steady returns appraisal valuation smooth chart daily prices volatile

ESSENCE: borrowing to buy multiplies the outcome in both directions —
  a fifth your money and four fifths the bank's turns a ten per cent
  move into a fifty per cent one, upward and downward alike. Leverage
  never makes a decision good; it only makes it bigger.
ROOT: mathematics / the whole gain lands on your slice, so a small
  slice under a large position swings by a large multiple.
CANNOT: no borrowing that magnifies only the gains. And no surviving,
  when borrowed, the same percentage fall you would have shrugged off
  unborrowed — past a certain gearing an ordinary bad month stops
  being a loss and becomes an ending.
THREAD: mortgages, farming (a loan against next year's crop), physics
  (a lever moves more and needs more room to move in).
ASKED-AS: borrowing invest margin loan mortgage gearing amplify wiped out debt buy leverage

ESSENCE: a lender who may demand more money whenever the price moves
  against you owns the timing of your worst decision. You can be
  completely right about a thing and still be sold out of it before it
  comes back — the sale is made by the lender's rule, not by the
  market's verdict.
ROOT: money / a debt is a claim on a date, and when the loan is
  callable the date belongs to the lender.
CANNOT: no waiting out a fall on borrowed money — patience is a luxury
  of the unborrowed. And no call that arrives on a good day: it comes
  precisely when everyone else is being called too, which is what
  turns an ordinary fall into a cascade.
THREAD: pawnbrokers, farming (the loan called in during a bad
  harvest), people (exit removing exactly the members who would have
  stayed and argued for repair).
ASKED-AS: margin call forced sale broker sold position collateral top up liquidated crash borrowed money

ESSENCE: everything with a price is a bundle of future payments hauled
  back to today and shrunk for the waiting and the doubt. A field, a
  company, a bond, a rented flat — one method wearing four costumes.
  Valuation is not many crafts; it is that one sum, argued over.
ROOT: money / a sum now beats the same sum later, so a future payment
  must be reduced before it can be set beside a present one.
CANNOT: no two things with identical future payments holding different
  prices for long — somebody buys one and sells the other until they
  meet. And no value at all in a thing that will never pay anybody
  anything: there the price is only what the next buyer will pay.
THREAD: accounting (an estimate stating its basis), law (a settlement
  paid over years being worth less than its headline), pensions.
ASKED-AS: valuation worth company shares fair value cash flow discount future payments price model

ESSENCE: a payment far enough away is worth almost nothing today —
  discounting kills the distance. So an argument about a hundred years
  from now is settled almost entirely by which rate was chosen, and
  the choice of rate is a judgement wearing a decimal point.
ROOT: mathematics / dividing by growth over and over drives a number
  toward zero, and a small change in the divisor compounds across
  every one of the years at once.
CANNOT: no neutral discount rate — every choice states what a distant
  person's money is worth against yours today, and that is a moral
  claim, not an arithmetic one. And no long-horizon valuation that is
  not mostly assumption by weight.
THREAD: the environment (climate costs argued entirely through this
  one number), law (very long leases), family (a promise made for the
  grandchildren).
ASKED-AS: discount rate future value hundred years worth today assumption long term climate cost model

ESSENCE: one interest rate does three separate jobs at once — it pays
  you for waiting, for the chance of never being repaid, and for the
  unit shrinking while you wait. When a rate moves, nothing in the
  number says which of the three moved, and mixing them up is the
  commonest error in reading money.
ROOT: money / a price folds every reason into one figure and reports
  none of them back.
CANNOT: no reading the reason out of a rate. And no comparing two
  rates by size alone — a high rate on a shaky borrower and a high
  rate in a shrinking currency are two different diseases showing one
  symptom.
THREAD: medicine (a fever names no illness), engineering (one gauge,
  many causes), language (one word carrying several meanings at once).
ASKED-AS: interest rates why high compare loans risk inflation premium borrowing cost rate change

ESSENCE: the longer you must wait for your money, the more the price
  of waiting matters — a payment thirty years out swings several times
  as hard on a rate change as one due next year. Long-dated things are
  not merely slow; they are the sensitive ones.
ROOT: money / discounting multiplies the rate's effect by the number
  of years it acts over, so distance and sensitivity are one
  measurement seen twice.
CANNOT: no long-dated holding that is safe from rate moves — safe
  there means the payments arrive, not that the price holds still. And
  no funding a long promise with short money without one day
  refinancing at a rate nobody alive can know yet.
THREAD: pensions (long promises met with shorter assets), roads and
  reservoirs paid for over decades, the thirty-year mortgage funded by
  deposits repayable tomorrow morning.
ASKED-AS: bond prices fell interest rates thirty year long sensitive duration pension losses rose

ESSENCE: a bond's payments are fixed at birth, so the only way it can
  offer a new buyer today's higher rate is by costing less. Price and
  yield are one fact seen from two ends — rates up means prices down,
  with no opinion involved anywhere.
ROOT: mathematics / a fixed payment over a risen required return gives
  a fallen price, and nothing about the borrower has to have changed.
CANNOT: no fixed-payment bond rising in price while rates rise. And no
  loss at all from a sound bond held to its end — the loss is real
  only if you must sell, which is why the identical holding is a
  disaster for one owner and nothing whatever for another.
THREAD: accounting (marked to market against held at cost), a fixed
  wage in a rising market, a shop lease signed at last year's rent.
ASKED-AS: bond prices yields inverse rates rose lost money maturity fixed coupon sell early holding

ESSENCE: the ladder of rates from overnight out to thirty years is the
  market's written guess about the future — what money will cost and
  what prices will do. It is the largest forecast anyone publishes,
  and it is produced by nobody in particular.
ROOT: money / a long rate must roughly match the expected string of
  short rates, or lenders would simply roll short loans instead and
  the two would be pulled level.
CANNOT: no long rate independent of what people expect the short ones
  to become. And no reading the curve as certainty — it is a wager,
  wagers are wrong, and having money behind it makes a forecast
  serious rather than correct.
THREAD: weather forecasting, betting odds as a crowd's public
  prediction, evidence (a consensus is not a proof).
ASKED-AS: yield curve long short rates forecast recession predict market expects bond shape ladder

ESSENCE: the curve is also a price, so it carries things that are not
  forecasts at all — a hunger for safe collateral, a central bank
  buying, rules obliging pension funds to hold long bonds. Read it as
  pure prediction and you will hear a confident forecast where
  somebody was merely obliged to buy.
ROOT: money / prices compress every reason into one number, and
  compulsion looks exactly like belief by the time it reaches the tape.
CANNOT: no separating expectation from obligation inside a single
  price. And no indicator that keeps working once everybody trades on
  it — a watched signal is a bought and sold signal, and the trading
  changes the thing that was being measured.
THREAD: people and power (a measure made a target stops measuring),
  polls that move the vote they poll, medicine (a test everybody
  orders starts finding a different sort of patient).
ASKED-AS: inverted curve recession signal wrong central bank buying regulation forced demand indicator failed

ESSENCE: two borrowers can promise identical payments and be quoted
  wholly different rates; the gap between them is the market's
  estimate of the promise being broken. Taking the higher yield is not
  being paid more — it is being paid to hold a chance of getting less.
ROOT: money / a price is a meeting point, so the extra yield is simply
  the discount buyers insisted on for a doubt.
CANNOT: no lending without accepting some chance of no repayment — a
  genuinely certain loan pays the lowest rate there is, by definition.
  And no diversifying away a default risk shared by a whole class:
  weak borrowers fail together when the cycle turns, which is exactly
  when you are holding many of them.
THREAD: insurance (a premium as priced doubt), people (reputation
  standing in for collateral), tenancy deposits.
ASKED-AS: junk bonds high yield spread default risk corporate lending credit quality downgrade recovery

ESSENCE: a shareholder stands last in the queue — staff, suppliers,
  landlords, tax and lenders are all paid before anything is left.
  Owning shares is owning the leftovers, and that is why they swing so
  hard: the remainder of a business moves far more than the business
  does.
ROOT: money / fixed claims are paid first, so a small change in the
  whole becomes a large change in whatever survives the fixed part.
CANNOT: no shareholder paid ahead of a creditor in a failure — the
  order is the entire legal difference between the two. And no steady
  remainder from a business with heavy fixed costs: leftovers amplify,
  always, in both directions.
THREAD: a mortgaged farm (harvest varies a little, the farmer's income
  a great deal), tips paid after fixed wages, leverage in another suit.
ASKED-AS: shares stock owner bankruptcy wiped out creditors paid first equity residual profits leftover

ESSENCE: the shareholder's loss stops at what was put in — a company
  can fail owing more than it owns and nobody comes for the owners'
  houses. That single rule is what made strangers willing to fund
  ventures they cannot watch, and it is also what lets a company take
  risks whose worst case lands on somebody else entirely.
ROOT: law / a company is a person invented so that its debts belong to
  it and not to the people behind it.
CANNOT: no broad public ownership without a capped downside — nobody
  puts a hundred pounds into a stranger's business that could cost
  them everything. And no capped downside without somebody uncapped:
  losses past the company's assets land on creditors, on workers, or
  on the public.
THREAD: law (a fiction with teeth), power (a shield that also conceals
  a person), insurance, the ship owned by a company that owns one ship.
ASKED-AS: limited company shareholders liable debts personal assets bankruptcy owners protected creditors lost

ESSENCE: a company with spare cash can hand it out as a dividend or
  spend it buying its own shares — the same money leaving by two
  doors. The dividend pays every holder a little; the buyback pays
  only whoever sells and leaves the rest owning a larger slice of a
  slightly smaller thing.
ROOT: money / a share is a claim on a business, and paying money out
  reduces the business by exactly what left, whichever door it used.
CANNOT: no dividend that makes a holder richer on the day — the share
  falls by roughly what was paid, so it is a transfer between two of
  your own pockets. And no buyback adding value above what the
  business is worth: then the remaining owners have simply bought
  dearly on their own behalf.
THREAD: accounting (a transfer is not income), farming (eating the
  seed corn), family (an inheritance handed over early).
ASKED-AS: dividends buybacks share repurchase payout income yield company cash returning shareholders drop

ESSENCE: prices already hold what is widely known, because anyone who
  spotted a mispricing traded it away before you arrived — so the
  reachable news is in the number by the time you read it. The honest
  claim is not that prices are right; it is that being reliably righter
  than them, after costs, is very hard and never stays easy.
ROOT: people and power / where a gain is on offer and many are
  looking, the looking is precisely what removes it.
CANNOT: no lasting easy edge in public information — publish a working
  method and it stops working, which is why the working ones are not
  published. And no efficient market without somebody spending real
  money to make it so: efficiency is the residue of expensive
  searching, not a natural state of affairs.
THREAD: sports (a betting line as a crowd's price), evidence (a
  finding everyone has already checked), queues that all end up
  equally slow.
ASKED-AS: efficient market beat stock picking priced in news information edge random walk average

ESSENCE: efficiency thins wherever the searching thins — small
  forgotten companies, debt that certain funds are forbidden to hold,
  markets where the largest players are forced buyers or forced
  sellers, and the exact moments when everyone able to correct a price
  has run out of money.
ROOT: premise — the correcting force is somebody's trade, so where
  trading is blocked, dear, or prohibited, a price can sit wrong for a
  very long time.
CANNOT: no correction from a trader who cannot borrow the thing,
  cannot legally hold it, or cannot survive the wait. And no easy
  exception: the places prices go wrong are the awkward places, which
  is why they went wrong there in the first place.
THREAD: rules forbidding pension funds to hold downgraded bonds, index
  funds obliged to buy on an announced date, selling in December for
  tax reasons and buying it back in January.
ASKED-AS: mispriced small caps forced sellers index rebalance distressed illiquid anomaly inefficient opportunity

ESSENCE: every price is real news mixed with people reacting to each
  other, and nothing on the screen says in what proportion. Most daily
  movement is the second kind, which is why watching a price hourly
  teaches almost nothing while costing a great deal of attention.
ROOT: evidence / a signal buried in noise needs many observations
  before it shows, and one reading cannot be split into its parts.
CANNOT: no separating a day's news from a day's mood inside a single
  number. And no information in a small move — a change smaller than
  the usual wobble is not evidence of anything, however confidently
  the evening report explains it.
THREAD: measurement (reading past an instrument's precision), medicine
  (a single blood pressure reading), story (the explanation invented
  afterwards to fit).
ASKED-AS: stock moved why news explained market today noise random daily swings watching prices meaningless

ESSENCE: a mania turns into a catastrophe only when it was funded with
  borrowed money. Unborrowed, it is a fashion that costs its
  followers. Borrowed, the fall forces sales, the sales lower the
  price, the lower price forces more sales — and the collapse is a
  funding event wearing the story's clothes.
ROOT: money / a debt is a claim on a date, and falling collateral
  moves every date forward at the same moment.
CANNOT: no self-feeding crash without leverage somewhere in the chain
  — find the lending and you have found why this one was different.
  And no orderly unwinding at scale: everybody reduces borrowing by
  selling the same assets in the same fortnight.
THREAD: bank runs (this spiral on the funding side), fire sales,
  farming (mortgaged land in a price collapse), a crowded theatre.
ASKED-AS: crash bubble burst borrowed money margin debt forced selling spiral collapse credit housing

ESSENCE: the price of everything you own was set by the last person who
  traded, not by the average opinion of everybody holding it. So a
  small forced sale can re-price an enormous pile of assets that
  nobody involved wanted to sell at all.
ROOT: money / a price is a meeting point between two people, and it is
  then applied to everyone's accounts as though they had all agreed to
  it.
CANNOT: no market where every holder could sell at the quoted price —
  the quote is good for a slice, and the pile is vastly deeper than
  the buying beneath it. And no valuing a large holding at the screen
  price: size itself moves the number against you as you go.
THREAD: accounting (marking to market), a street of houses repriced by
  one desperate sale, auctions with two bidders and forty watchers.
ASKED-AS: last trade price everyone holdings valued forced sale small volume repriced whole market depth

ESSENCE: a bank run is everybody behaving sensibly at once — if you
  believe others will withdraw, withdrawing first is correct, and that
  same reasoning is available to every depositor simultaneously. A
  bank whose loans are perfectly good can still die of it, because the
  loans cannot be turned into cash by Friday.
ROOT: people and power / a coordination problem in which being right
  alone is being wrong, pointed at a queue outside a door.
CANNOT: no bank surviving a full run on its own resources — the money
  is out on loan by design, and calling it all back in is the one
  thing the business exists not to do. And no run stopped by proving
  solvency: the fear is about the queue, not about the books.
THREAD: petrol shortages created by the rumour of a petrol shortage,
  stampedes, people (trust as infrastructure, failing all at once).
ASKED-AS: bank run withdraw deposits collapse queue failed solvent liquidity panic savings branch guarantee

ESSENCE: a bank takes money that can be demanded this afternoon and
  lends it out for thirty years. That mismatch is not a flaw in
  banking, it is the product — turning the saver's need for access
  into the borrower's need for time — and the fragility arrives
  welded to it.
ROOT: money / a loan moves goods from the future into the present, and
  the saver and the borrower want completely different amounts of
  future.
CANNOT: no bank both entirely safe and useful — one holding every
  deposit as cash finances nothing and no house gets built. And no
  abolishing the mismatch by rule: push it out of banks and it
  reappears inside whatever is doing the lending instead.
THREAD: engineering (a buffer between two rates of flow), water
  towers, a library lending books it must be able to call back.
ASKED-AS: banks lend deposits long loans short savings mismatch narrow banking reserves cash safe

ESSENCE: most money was never printed by anybody. It is written into
  existence when a bank makes a loan and puts the amount into the
  borrower's account — the loan creates the deposit. Repayment
  destroys it again, which is why credit shrinking and money vanishing
  are one event with two names.
ROOT: money / a token works by agreement, and a bank's promise to pay
  is accepted like a token, so writing the promise is making money.
CANNOT: no controlling the quantity of money by controlling the
  printing press alone — the press makes small change and the banks
  make the rest. And no credit boom without a money boom, nor a credit
  bust without money disappearing out of accounts nobody touched.
THREAD: people (a promise everybody accepts is a currency), accounting
  (a new loan writing an asset and a liability in the same stroke),
  the schoolbook multiplier, which also runs backwards.
ASKED-AS: banks create money loans deposits printing reserves supply credit shrink recession lending multiplier

ESSENCE: promising every small depositor their money back removes the
  reason to queue, and it ended a century of regular bank collapses.
  It also means depositors stop caring which bank is careful — so the
  discipline that used to come from savers has to be rebuilt out of
  rules and inspectors, and paid for.
ROOT: chance / a guarantee does not delete a risk, it moves it, and
  behaviour follows wherever the risk went.
CANNOT: no guarantee without changed behaviour on both sides of the
  counter — the saver stops checking, and the bank can now buy
  deposits with a promise somebody else is standing behind. And no
  insurance fund large enough for every bank at once: it works by
  preventing the panic, not by being able to pay for one.
THREAD: seat belts and how people then drive, people and power
  (emergency measures that outlive their emergency), warranties.
ASKED-AS: deposit insurance guaranteed savings limit bank failed government covered protection scheme safe

ESSENCE: in a panic the only body that can lend without limit is one
  that can create the money — so it lends freely, against good
  security, at a rate high enough to sting. That last part is what
  separates rescuing a system from rewarding the people who broke it.
ROOT: money / a run is a shortage of cash rather than a shortage of
  value, so an unlimited lender against sound security ends it simply
  by making the shortage impossible.
CANNOT: no private institution able to be the last resort — whoever
  lends last must be able to make the money, and nobody else can. And
  no clean rescue: help offered mid-panic cannot be aimed only at the
  deserving, because sorting takes time the panic will not give.
THREAD: fire brigades that do not ask how the fire started, medicine
  (treat first, apportion blame later), people and power (crisis
  powers that never quite get handed back).
ASKED-AS: central bank bailout emergency lending panic liquidity collateral penalty rate rescue banks

ESSENCE: a bank's capital is the slice of its own money standing
  between a loss and the depositors. It is not a heap of cash in a
  vault — it is a measure of how much can go wrong before somebody
  else's savings begin paying for it.
ROOT: money / losses fall on the residual claim first, so the size of
  the residual is the size of the buffer, and nothing else is.
CANNOT: no bank safe on a thin buffer however good its loans look — a
  small error rate against thin capital is an insolvency. And no
  capital requirement that is free: shareholders' money costs more
  than depositors' money, so every extra point of safety turns up as a
  slightly dearer loan somewhere down the street.
THREAD: making (the margin between the load and the failure), ships
  (freeboard — how far the deck sits above the water), a household's
  months of expenses in the bank.
ASKED-AS: bank capital requirements equity buffer losses absorb depositors ratio lending cost safety

ESSENCE: once people expect prices to rise they act on it — wages are
  asked for in advance, contracts get escalators, sellers raise before
  their costs move. The expectation manufactures the thing expected,
  which is why the hard part of stopping an inflation is convincing
  people that it will stop.
ROOT: the mind / a belief about the future changes today's behaviour,
  and here today's behaviour is what builds the future.
CANNOT: no ending an inflation without breaking the expectation, and
  no breaking it cheaply once it has set — the proof people accept is
  a stretch of pain that cannot be faked. And no measuring the
  expectation directly: it is only ever read off wages, bond prices,
  and guesses.
THREAD: bank runs (a belief making itself true), stampedes, medicine
  (a placebo doing real work), a queue that forms because a queue
  formed.
ASKED-AS: inflation expectations wages rising prices spiral central bank credibility stopping recession cost

ESSENCE: when prices fall, waiting pays — so people wait, and the
  waiting drives prices down further. Worse, debts do not shrink: the
  mortgage stays the same size while wages and prices fall around it,
  so every borrower is quietly made poorer, and borrowers are where the
  spending lived.
ROOT: money / a debt is fixed in tokens while the value of a token is
  moving, so a growing unit hands wealth from borrowers to lenders
  with nobody deciding it should.
CANNOT: no easy escape downward — a central bank can always make more
  money and cannot make anybody spend it, so its tools are strong in
  one direction and feeble in the other. And no deflation that stays a
  price story: within a season it is a debt story.
THREAD: the 1930s, Japan's long flat decades, farming (the bumper year
  that beggars its growers), every repair postponed at once.
ASKED-AS: deflation falling prices depression waiting to buy debt heavier spiral worse than inflation

ESSENCE: an exchange rate is the price of one country's promises in
  another country's, and like any price it moves on relative interest,
  relative prices, and relative trust. A currency has no value of its
  own; it has a value against something, always, and never otherwise.
ROOT: money / value requires a comparison, and for a token the
  comparison is what it buys or what it swaps for.
CANNOT: no currency strengthening against everything at once without
  something else weakening — these are ratios, and ratios must agree
  all the way around the loop. And no telling a rise caused by your
  own strength from one caused by the other side's trouble.
THREAD: measurement (a reading taken against a moving reference),
  trade (imports and exports dragged opposite ways by one number),
  language (a word whose meaning is set by its neighbours).
ASKED-AS: exchange rate currency strong weak dollar euro travel money import export value

ESSENCE: a country may hold its exchange rate fixed, let money move
  freely across its borders, and set its own interest rates — but only
  two of the three. Attempt all three and traders simply push on the
  contradiction with real money until the fixed rate breaks.
ROOT: premise — a fixed price plus free trading means the price is
  being defended with cash, and a home interest rate that disagrees
  tells every trader which way to lean.
CANNOT: no fixed rate, open borders, and independent policy together —
  the third is always bought by surrendering one of the other two, in
  every country that has ever tried it.
THREAD: sterling in 1992, currency boards, the euro (member states
  handing over their rates to keep the other two), coordination
  problems generally.
ASKED-AS: pegged currency devaluation capital controls interest rates independent central bank speculators peg

ESSENCE: a weaker currency makes exports cheap and imports dear in the
  same instant. It is not a policy that helps a country; it is a policy
  that chooses who is helped. Exporters and hotels gain, and everyone
  buying fuel, food, or foreign parts pays for it.
ROOT: money / one ratio cannot move in only one direction of trade —
  the same number governs both sides of the border.
CANNOT: no devaluation raising competitiveness without lowering real
  wages: they are one event described from two different chairs. And
  no lasting advantage if home prices then climb to match — the gain
  leaks away at the speed the higher import prices travel inward.
THREAD: people and power (a concentrated benefit against a diffuse
  cost), farming, the tourist economy living off its own cheapness.
