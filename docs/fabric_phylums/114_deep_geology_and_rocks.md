# 114 DEEP GEOLOGY AND ROCKS — reading the ground
Stone keeps its receipts. Everything was made somewhere, by
something, at a speed, and none of that is thrown away when the
making stops. Reading is knowing which mark answers which question.

ESSENCE: a mineral is an arrangement more than a substance — the
  same atoms stacked in one repeating pattern out to the last
  edge. Diamond and pencil lead are both pure carbon, and every
  difference between them comes from the stacking alone.
ROOT: chemistry / atoms bond at fixed angles and fixed distances,
  so a stack that works once works forever, repeating.
THREAD: chemistry (a molecule's shape, not its ingredient list,
  does the work), building (a wall's strength lives in its bond).
ASKED-AS: crystal mineral rock difference diamond graphite carbon same atoms structure quartz salt cube

ESSENCE: a crystal's outside is the inside pattern made visible.
  Flat faces appear because growth adds whole layers, and the
  angles between the faces of one mineral are identical in every
  specimen on earth, from a sand grain to a fist.
ROOT: chemistry / growth stacks identical units, so the outer
  geometry can only repeat the inner one.
THREAD: craft (stacked bricks can only turn certain corners),
  design (a pattern repeated honestly to the edge of the cloth).
ASKED-AS: crystals shape pointy geode cave quartz grow angles same size big perfect clear

ESSENCE: a mineral breaks where its bonds are weakest, so mica
  peels into sheets and salt splits into cubes, while quartz —
  bonded equally in every direction — has no easy plane and
  shatters like glass. Hardness reads that same page.
ROOT: physics / a solid fails along its weakest line, and in an
  ordered solid the weak lines are ordered too.
THREAD: craft (splitting wood along the grain, never across),
  engineering (stiff and brittle keep each other company).
ASKED-AS: mica sheets flakes salt cubes scratch hardness diamond breaks chip glass tough brittle

ESSENCE: thousands of minerals have names and about a dozen build
  nearly every rock anyone will ever stand on. Almost all are
  silicates: silicon and oxygen locked into a four-cornered unit
  that links into chains, sheets and frameworks with metals in the
  gaps.
ROOT: chemistry / the crust's commonest atoms are oxygen and
  silicon, so the commonest rocks are whatever those two make.
THREAD: cooking (a whole cuisine standing on six staples),
  language (a few hundred words carrying most of what is said).
ASKED-AS: minerals how many kinds common rocks made of quartz feldspar mica identify learn list

ESSENCE: in a cooling melt, crystals grow only while atoms still
  have time to walk to a growing face — so slow cooling at depth
  makes coarse rock whose grains you can pick out by eye, and lava
  chilled in air makes rock too fine to resolve. Glass means there
  was no time at all.
ROOT: chemistry / ordering takes time, and the cooling rate
  decides how much time there was.
THREAD: cooking (slow-cooled fudge is grainy, fast-cooled smooth),
  metalwork (quenching freezes whatever structure was there).
ASKED-AS: granite basalt grain size crystals big small lava cooled fast slow obsidian glassy

ESSENCE: melted rock does not freeze all at once. It crystallises
  in an order, dropping the heavy early-forming minerals first and
  leaving a melt steadily richer in whatever stayed behind — which
  is how one starting material makes both dark heavy rock and pale
  light rock.
ROOT: chemistry / a mixture freezes across a range, and every
  crystal that leaves changes what remains.
THREAD: cooking (the pot changes character as it reduces),
  refining (each pass concentrates what refused to go).
ASKED-AS: magma cooling minerals order dark light granite basalt melt separate crystals settle rock

ESSENCE: a sedimentary bed is a snapshot of one place on one day —
  a storm, a flood, a quiet season — and the cliff is a run of
  them. So a sandstone does not merely say sand: it says beach, or
  dune, or river bar, and this is the only rock family that
  records the surface at all.
ROOT: deep earth / settling preserves the conditions at the moment
  of settling, and burial locks them in.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (only what got written has any chance),
  photography (a frame is a moment and never a story).
ASKED-AS: sandstone shale limestone how formed layers beach river desert ancient environment beds deposits

ESSENCE: squeeze and heat a rock without melting it and its
  minerals rebuild into ones that suit the new conditions, flat
  crystals growing sideways to the squeeze. That is why slate
  splits along planes that have nothing to do with the mud layers
  it was made from.
ROOT: chemistry / atoms rearrange into whatever is stable at the
  pressure and temperature they find themselves in, given time.
THREAD: craft (a hammered blade's grain runs where the hammer sent
  it), the body (bone thickens along the lines that load it).
ASKED-AS: slate marble schist gneiss pressure heat squeezed rock changed splits roofing quarry buried

ESSENCE: certain minerals form only inside narrow windows of heat
  and depth, so finding one is a reading rather than a curiosity.
  Walk from the edge of a mountain belt toward its heart and the
  minerals change in a fixed order — the same order every time,
  because it is an instrument and not a fashion.
ROOT: chemistry / each mineral is stable across a defined band of
  conditions, so its presence brackets what the rock went through.
THREAD: cooking (a sugar stage names its temperature exactly),
  measurement (an instrument reading a past state, not a present).
ASKED-AS: garnet minerals depth temperature mountain belt zones metamorphic grade indicator buried deep pressure

ESSENCE: layers are laid flat and continuous, so every tilt, bend
  and break came later. And a bed carries its own up-arrow: ripple
  crests point up, mud cracks open upward, a graded bed is coarse
  at its base. In folded country those arrows are the only thing
  that says whether the book has been turned over.
ROOT: physics / settling under gravity makes a flat sheet, and the
  features formed at a bed's top differ from those at its base.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (page numbers survive a dropped
  manuscript), evidence (an internal check beats an assumption).
ASKED-AS: rock layers tilted folded upside down cliff order older younger bedding canyon reading

ESSENCE: rocks of the same age look nothing alike a mile apart —
  sand at the shore, mud offshore, lime further out — so "the same
  layer" is a line of time and not a kind of stone. And when the
  sea creeps inland, those side-by-side places stack up: a cliff
  can be a whole coastline filed on its edge.
ROOT: deep earth / different materials settle at different depths
  and energies all at once, and a shifting shoreline turns that
  map into a column.
THREAD: language (one moment in a city sounds different street by
  street), history (one year read out of four countries' records).
ASKED-AS: same layer different rock sideways sea level rising cliff sequence shore mud sand correlate

ESSENCE: the loudest thing in a cliff is often a plain line where
  nothing was laid down at all, or where a whole stack was
  stripped away before the next arrived. At the famous ones, more
  time is missing at that single surface than every rock above and
  below it represents put together.
ROOT: deep earth / rock accumulates only where material is
  arriving, and most places, most of the time, are wearing down.
THREAD: accounting (the absent entry hides the theft, not the
  wrong one), archives (a silent decade is itself an event).
ASKED-AS: gap missing layers unconformity erosion surface time lost cliff record incomplete years between

ESSENCE: two rules settle almost every argument about order
  without a laboratory. Anything cutting across something is
  younger than what it cuts — a crack, a dyke, a fault. And a lump
  sitting inside a rock was already a rock before the rock formed
  around it.
ROOT: mathematics / logic — a thing must exist before it can be
  interrupted, and before it can be enclosed.
THREAD: law (a signature written over a fold proves the folding
  came first), archaeology (a post-hole cut through a floor).
ASKED-AS: which came first rock cracks intrusion dyke fault younger older cutting through pieces inside

ESSENCE: to match a layer in Kent against one in Kansas you need a
  fossil that was everywhere at once and did not last long —
  common, widespread, quick to change, easy to recognise. A
  long-lived species is useless for it: a creature that survived a
  hundred million years dates nothing.
ROOT: evidence / a marker's worth is set by how narrowly it pins
  the answer, never by how abundant or impressive it is.
THREAD: computing (a version stamp beats a description), medicine
  (a test is worth exactly how much it narrows things).
ASKED-AS: fossils dating layers matching worldwide index species trilobites ammonites short lived marker correlate

ESSENCE: the whole ordered list of periods — every name on every
  chart — was worked out by walking, matching and stacking, a
  century before anyone could put a year on any of it. The numbers
  were hung onto the order afterward, and any of them can be
  revised without disturbing the order at all.
ROOT: evidence / sequence and size are separate claims resting on
  separate methods, and the sequence came first.
THREAD: history (the order of reigns was certain long before the
  calendar), measurement (rank is cheaper and firmer than value).
ASKED-AS: geological time periods jurassic names order chart eras how decided before dating years numbers

ESSENCE: a clock inside a rock starts when a crystal cools enough
  to stop leaking — not when the mountain rose and not when the
  animal died. Which is why you cannot date a sandstone: its
  grains are older than the bed they lie in, and dating them dates
  the mountain they came from.
ROOT: chemistry / a clock reads from a sealed container, and the
  sealing is itself a physical event with its own date.
THREAD: accounting (an entry is dated by posting, not by the deal),
  evidence (a witness who only arrived after the event).
ASKED-AS: dating rocks how old fossil age ash layer radioactive clock sandstone grains reset accurate

ESSENCE: a fault names the direction the ground was being pushed.
  Pulled apart, one side drops. Squeezed, one side rides up over
  the other. Sheared sideways, the two grind past each other. Read
  the offset and you have read the force, and the same three kinds
  sit at the three kinds of plate edge.
ROOT: physics / rock breaks along a surface angled to its load, so
  the geometry of the break records the geometry of the load.
THREAD: engineering (a broken part shows the load that broke it),
  building (a crack's direction names the movement underneath).
ASKED-AS: fault line crack ground moved up down sideways normal thrust earthquake type offset slip

ESSENCE: the same limestone that snaps under a hammer lies folded
  like a blanket in a cliff. Nothing changed but rate and depth —
  pushed slowly, hot and buried, rock flows; pushed fast and cold
  and near the surface, it breaks. Every rock is both, and the
  conditions choose which.
ROOT: physics / how a material answers a force depends on how
  quickly the force arrives and how hard everything else is
  pressing in on it.
THREAD: cooking (toffee snaps cold and pulls warm), the body (bone
  breaks fast and remodels slowly under the same load).
ASKED-AS: folded rock layers bent wavy mountains cliff squeezed how bend without breaking pressure deep

ESSENCE: nearly every exposed rock is cut by sets of parallel
  cracks that carry no offset, opened as the miles of rock above
  were stripped away, or by cooling. They then decide almost
  everything: where water enters, where roots go, where a cliff
  will fail, where the quarryman puts his wedge.
ROOT: physics / rock squeezed for ages expands when the load comes
  off, and a brittle solid that expands cracks.
THREAD: building (a wall fails along its mortar lines), craft (a
  mason splitting stone the way the stone wants to go).
ASKED-AS: cracks rock cliff blocks columns basalt splitting quarry granite weathering water freeze joints

ESSENCE: magnitude measures the break — how much rock slipped over
  how large an area — and there is exactly one per earthquake.
  What you feel is a different quantity that changes street by
  street, and it is the one that matters, because damage follows
  the shaking and not the headline.
ROOT: measurement / a source and its effect are different things,
  and a scale for one is not a scale for the other.
THREAD: measurement (dose given against dose absorbed), sound (the
  power of a speaker against the loudness in your seat).
ASKED-AS: earthquake magnitude richter scale how bad damage felt shaking intensity energy stronger difference number

ESSENCE: soft ground shakes harder and longer than rock, because
  waves slow as they enter it and pile their motion up. And loose
  wet sand can lose its grip completely for a minute and behave
  like a liquid, so buildings sink or tip whole and buried tanks
  float to the surface.
ROOT: physics / a wave entering slower material grows, and loose
  grains carry load only through their contact points, which
  shaking breaks.
THREAD: building (foundation before frame, always), materials (a
  slurry is nothing but grains that stopped touching).
ASKED-AS: earthquake damage soft ground sand buildings sinking tilted fill liquefaction bedrock safer city

ESSENCE: nobody has sampled below a scratch, so the inside of the
  earth is a model — layer depths and speeds chosen so the arrival
  times of shaking match at every station on the planet. Its
  strength is that it must go on predicting the arrivals of every
  future earthquake everywhere, and it does, to the second.
ROOT: evidence / a model built to fit old data is worth little,
  and one that keeps predicting new data is worth almost anything.
THREAD: medicine (an image from echoes that must keep matching
  what surgery finds), evidence (prediction as the only real test).
ASKED-AS: how known inside planet never sampled deepest hole shallow model predicts stations timing

ESSENCE: the mantle is hot enough to melt and mostly does not,
  because pressure holds it solid. So melting happens by taking
  the pressure off, where plates pull apart, or by adding water,
  which lowers the melting point — and that water is wrung out of
  a slab sinking underneath. Heat is rarely what changes.
ROOT: chemistry / a melting point is set by pressure and by what
  is dissolved in the mixture, not by temperature by itself.
THREAD: cooking (salt lowering a freezing point — same handle),
  the body (pressure deciding what stays dissolved in blood).
ASKED-AS: why volcanoes there magma made melting mantle water subduction ridge pressure hot rock solid

ESSENCE: lava is the least of it. Lava is slow enough to walk away
  from and kills almost nobody. What kills is ash collapsing roofs
  miles off, hot clouds of gas and ash that run downhill faster
  than a car, and mud floods made when the heat melts a snowcap —
  and that last needs no eruption you could see from the valley.
ROOT: physics / danger is reach times speed, and a fluid you can
  outwalk has neither.
THREAD: fire safety (smoke kills, not flame), flooding (the water
  arrives where nobody thought to look).
ASKED-AS: volcano danger lava running away ash cloud pyroclastic mudflow pompeii evacuation deadly distance roofs

ESSENCE: a stationary source of melt under a moving plate writes
  its own history. The island above it is alive, and the ones
  behind are steadily older, cooler, lower and further off.
  Measure the spacing against the ages and you have the plate's
  speed; find the bend and you have the day it changed heading.
ROOT: physics / a fixed pen on a moving sheet draws a line whose
  geometry records the motion of the sheet.
THREAD: keeping knowledge (a dated stack of receipts tracking a
  shop's moves), measurement (a rate read off a distance and a time).
ASKED-AS: hawaii islands chain older younger plume hot spot plate moving speed direction bend seamounts

ESSENCE: copper sits in every rock anyone ever kicked, at a few
  parts in a hundred thousand, and nobody could afford to get it
  out. An ore body is somewhere a natural process already did the
  concentrating — hot water leaching metal from a huge volume and
  dropping it in one crack, a magma settling out its heavy grains,
  rain stripping a hillside for a million years.
ROOT: chemistry / a fluid that dissolves metal in one place and
  drops it at a change of conditions is a sorting machine, and
  time makes it a rich one.
THREAD: money (wealth gathering wherever a flow slows), cooking
  (reducing a stock down to a glaze).
ASKED-AS: gold copper mining ore vein where found concentrated deposit prospecting rich rock grade economic

ESSENCE: oil is never found where it was made. It forms in buried
  mud rich in dead plankton, cooks in a narrow band of depth, then
  squeezes out and floats upward through the pores of whatever is
  above until something stops it. Four separate things must line
  up, and missing any one of them gives you nothing.
ROOT: physics / oil is lighter than water and rock is porous, so
  migrating upward is the default and being contained is the rare
  exception.
THREAD: engineering (a chain of four links), plumbing (a tank
  without a lid is only a puddle).
ASKED-AS: oil gas found underground drilling reservoir trap cap rock source migrated seep leaked

ESSENCE: the same buried mud yields different things by how hot it
  got. Too cool and the organic matter stays waxy and useless. In
  a band around the temperature of a slow oven it cracks into oil.
  Hotter and the oil cracks again into gas. Hotter still and there
  is nothing left but carbon.
ROOT: chemistry / heat breaks big molecules into smaller ones in
  stages, and each stage owns its own band of temperature.
THREAD: cooking (one dough is bread, biscuit or charcoal by
  setting), chemistry (temperature choosing which product you get).
ASKED-AS: oil gas depth buried heat window why gas instead deep wells basin cooked temperature

ESSENCE: coal needs plants piling up faster than they rot, which
  means a swamp — waterlogged airless ground where decomposers
  cannot work. So a seam is not an ancient forest, it is an
  ancient bog, and every one of them records a stretch when the
  ground sank at exactly the rate the peat built up.
ROOT: chemistry / rotting needs oxygen, so shutting the oxygen out
  is the whole of what preserves the carbon.
THREAD: cooking (pickling and burying both preserve by excluding
  air), farming (peat bogs banking carbon by the same trick now).
ASKED-AS: coal formed swamp forest plants trees seams underground buried peat carbon mine layers thick

ESSENCE: coal is a road rather than a substance. Peat pressed and
  heated becomes brown coal, then house coal, then the hard bright
  kind, each step driving off more water and gas and leaving more
  carbon behind. How far a seam travelled is set by how deep it
  was buried, so its rank is a depth record.
ROOT: chemistry / heat and pressure drive off the volatile parts
  in order, and what remains grows steadily richer in carbon.
THREAD: cooking (the same meat dried, smoked and cured), fuel (the
  drier it is, the more of the flame belongs to you).
ASKED-AS: coal types brown anthracite hard soft quality burns hot smoky rank buried deep methane

ESSENCE: the top of the underground water is not flat. It is a
  gentler copy of the hills above, standing high under the ridges
  and low under the valleys, because rain arrives faster than the
  water can drain sideways. Where that surface meets the ground
  you get a spring, a marsh, or a stream that never dries.
ROOT: physics / water moves through pores far more slowly than it
  falls, so recharge builds a mound that never quite levels out.
THREAD: money (a balance topped up faster than it is drawn),
  plumbing (a header tank's level setting every tap below it).
ASKED-AS: water table spring river dry summer well depth hills valleys groundwater level stream flowing

ESSENCE: put a clay lid over a water-bearing layer and the water
  inside is under the pressure of wherever that layer reaches the
  surface, miles uphill — so a well drilled into it flows on its
  own with no pump. It also means the water being taken out is not
  replaced by anything falling on the field you are standing in.
ROOT: physics / a fluid in a sealed sloping pipe stands at the
  height of its supply, wherever along the pipe you tap it.
THREAD: plumbing (mains pressure comes from a tower you never
  see), law (a resource and the land above it are different things).
ASKED-AS: artesian well flowing without pump pressure aquifer confined clay layer recharge area rainfall elsewhere

ESSENCE: a rock's ability to hold water and its ability to give it
  up are two different properties, and the second is the one that
  matters. Clay is mostly holes and yields almost nothing, because
  the holes are too fine for water to travel through; coarse
  gravel holds less and pours.
ROOT: physics / flow through a passage is throttled savagely by
  the passage's width, so fine spaces store beautifully and
  transmit hardly at all.
THREAD: cooking (a sponge that refuses to wring out), engineering
  (capacity and throughput are separate ratings on the same box).
ASKED-AS: clay sand gravel water holds drains through soaks slow fast wet ground permeable pores

ESSENCE: pump a well and the water level around it dips into a
  funnel, because water must travel through the ground to reach
  the hole and travelling costs height. The funnel widens the
  longer you pump, so two wells a field apart eventually rob each
  other, and the shallow old one fails first.
ROOT: physics / flow needs a slope to drive it, so extraction
  manufactures the slope it needs by lowering the water at itself.
THREAD: money (a bank run where only the quick are served),
  farming (upstream abstraction arriving as downstream drought).
ASKED-AS: well dried up neighbour pumping deeper drilling water level dropping irrigation borehole competing farm

ESSENCE: a spill on the surface arrives underground years later,
  spreads as a long thin plume in the direction of flow, and
  stays. The ground filters bacteria rather well and dissolved
  chemicals hardly at all, and because the water creeps, cleaning
  it is a matter of decades of pumping — or of waiting.
ROOT: physics / slow flow means slow arrival and slow removal, so
  the same slowness that hides the problem forbids the cure.
THREAD: medicine (a slow poison with a long delay before signs),
  law (a liability surfacing after the company has gone).
ASKED-AS: groundwater pollution well contaminated spill petrol tank chemicals plume clean up slow drinking safe

ESSENCE: in limestone country the drainage runs inside the rock,
  so surface water vanishes down holes, travels miles through open
  passages, and reappears at a spring — arriving in hours,
  unfiltered, carrying whatever it picked up. A farm's slurry can
  reach a village tap the same week and look perfectly clear.
ROOT: chemistry / dissolution opens pipes rather than pores, and a
  pipe neither filters what it carries nor delays it.
THREAD: plumbing (a cross-connection delivering from the wrong
  source), evidence (clear water is not evidence of clean water).
ASKED-AS: limestone sinkhole stream disappears spring water pollution farm cave drinking clear contaminated dam

ESSENCE: the same water that ate the cave rebuilds inside it.
  Underground the water meets air, gives up its dissolved gas, and
  can no longer hold the stone it was carrying — so it lays it
  back down, drop by drop, a fraction of a millimetre a year.
  Dissolving and depositing are one reaction run both ways.
ROOT: chemistry / a reaction runs whichever way the conditions
  push it, and losing the gas pushes this one back.
THREAD: cleaning (limescale in a kettle is this exact deposit),
  chemistry (equilibrium as a road with traffic both ways).
ASKED-AS: stalactite stalagmite cave formations growing how fast broken dripping water limestone deposits air

ESSENCE: ice carries a boulder and a speck of clay at precisely
  the same speed, because it carries them inside itself rather
  than in a current — so what it drops is a jumble of every size
  at once with no layering whatever. Its meltwater, being water,
  immediately sorts what it is handed. The pair is the signature.
ROOT: physics / a solid carrier applies no sorting at all while a
  fluid carrier sorts by speed, so a place with both marks has had
  both.
THREAD: cooking (a tipped basket against a poured jug), evidence
  (a signature no other process can forge).
ASKED-AS: glacier boulders clay mixed till moraine erratic rock far from home gravel sorted meltwater

ESSENCE: a river cuts a narrow V because it works at a point; a
  glacier fills its whole valley and grinds the walls, leaving a U
  with a flat floor and spurs cut off square. It also scratches
  the bedrock as it goes, and the scratches, the valley lines and
  the ridges of dumped rubble all point back the way the ice came.
ROOT: physics / a wide stiff tool cuts to its own shape, and a
  tool dragging grit leaves a record of the direction it dragged.
THREAD: craft (a plane leaves flats where a gouge leaves curves),
  navigation (a scratch as a compass needle frozen in place).
ASKED-AS: valley shape u shaped glacier river carved fjord scratches direction ice came moraine ridges

ESSENCE: three things about a sand tell three different stories.
  How round the grains are records how far they travelled. How
  even the sizes are records how many chances the current had to
  sort them. And what the grains are made of records how long the
  whole journey was, because everything except quartz rots on the
  way.
ROOT: deep earth / transport abrades, sorts, and destroys the weak
  — three processes at three different rates on one cargo.
THREAD: language (a loanword's worn shape shows the miles it
  came), craft (a much-handled tool carrying its service record).
ASKED-AS: sand grains round angular sorted beach river source distance quartz white pebbles smooth worn

ESSENCE: sand piles up on the sheltered face of a ripple or a dune
  and is then buried, so the slanting internal layers of a
  sandstone lean the way the current or the wind was going. A
  quarry face becomes a wind rose from three hundred million years
  ago, readable in an afternoon.
ROOT: physics / a moving fluid strips the upstream side of a bump
  and drops its load on the downstream side, so the structure it
  builds is asymmetric and points.
THREAD: navigation (wind read off drifted snow), evidence (the
  internal structure carrying what the exterior has lost).
ASKED-AS: sandstone slanted layers cross bedding dunes current direction ancient wind river desert quarry face

ESSENCE: a delta grows by choking itself. The river raises its own
  bed as it drops sediment, until the channel stands above the
  plain — and then a flood breaks out and takes a shorter steeper
  route, abandoning the old lobe entirely. A delta is not a fan;
  it is a set of lobes built in turn and left in turn.
ROOT: physics / a stream takes the steepest path available, and
  depositing inside its own channel steadily destroys that path's
  advantage over the alternatives.
THREAD: engineering (a system whose success removes its own
  conditions), money (a business that fills in the niche it eats).
ASKED-AS: delta river mouth mississippi levees flooding land sinking new channel switch shortcut marsh louisiana

ESSENCE: most desert scenery was cut by water. Rain is rare but
  lands all at once on bare ground with nothing to slow it, so the
  canyons, fans and dry channels are all flood work. The wind's
  contribution is different and unmistakable: it moves only
  sand-sized grains, and so it sorts a desert into dune fields,
  dust plains and stony pavements.
ROOT: physics / a fluid carries what its speed permits, and air
  being thin can lift dust and roll sand but cannot budge gravel.
THREAD: risk (the rare event doing the work the common one gets
  credit for), sorting (a sieve with exactly one hole size).
ASKED-AS: desert canyon carved wind water flash flood dunes sand dust stones pavement dry riverbed

ESSENCE: dry out a body of salt water and the dissolved minerals
  come out in a fixed order, least soluble first and most soluble
  last. So a thick bed of gypsum or salt is a basin that
  evaporated, and the order of its layers reports how far the
  drying got before the water came back.
ROOT: chemistry / each dissolved substance leaves solution when
  its own limit is passed, and those limits are different numbers.
THREAD: cooking (a syrup crystallising its sugar first and the
  rest after), chemistry (solubility as an order of departure).
ASKED-AS: salt mine underground gypsum deposits sea dried up basin layers thick evaporated ancient desert

ESSENCE: rain moving down through soil carries fine particles and
  dissolved minerals with it, so a soil sorts itself into a stack:
  dark and organic on top, a pale washed-out band below, then a
  zone where everything that washed down has piled up, then broken
  parent rock. The sharpness of that stack reports rainfall and age.
ROOT: physics / percolating water is a one-way conveyor inside the
  soil, moving material from where it dissolves to where it stops.
THREAD: cooking (a stew separating out as it stands), keeping
  knowledge (layered filing revealing the traffic that made it).
ASKED-AS: soil layers topsoil subsoil digging pale sandy hard pan drainage roots profile horizons colour

ESSENCE: a slope stands while friction and roots beat the weight
  pulling downhill, and it fails when that ratio tips. Water tips
  it nearly every time — not by lubricating much, but by pressing
  the grains apart from the inside and taking away the friction
  they had been living on.
ROOT: physics / grains carry load through their contact points,
  and water pressure inside the pores carries part of the load
  instead, unloading exactly those contacts.
THREAD: engineering (a factor of safety is a ratio, not a
  promise), money (leverage that fails at the moment of stress).
ASKED-AS: landslide slope collapse rain heavy hillside houses cutting road digging bottom warning unstable wet

ESSENCE: most ground movement is far too slow to watch and never
  stops. A hillside creeping a few millimetres a year bends fence
  lines, tips gravestones, curves the base of every tree trunk,
  and cracks walls always in the same direction. The fast failures
  mostly happen on ground that was already doing this quietly.
ROOT: physics / a slope near its limit deforms continuously, and
  the amount of that deformation measures how near it is.
THREAD: building (a crack that reopens after repair is a message
  and not a defect), the body (a symptom returning to one place).
ASKED-AS: cracks house wall slope garden fence leaning trees curved gravestones tilted ground moving slowly

ESSENCE: ground sinks for a short list of reasons and each leaves
  its own fingerprint. Take water or oil out and a wide area drops
  evenly by feet. Mine a seam and a sharp trough appears over the
  workings. Drain peat and it shrinks and burns away in the air.
  Dissolve limestone or gypsum and the failure is a hole.
ROOT: physics / ground is held up by whatever fills its spaces, so
  removing either the solid or the pressure in the fluid lets it
  compact.
THREAD: money (drawing down a balance that cannot be restored),
  building (a repair that must match the cause or fail again).
ASKED-AS: ground sinking house subsidence mining water pumped city venice peat shrinking hole appears sudden

ESSENCE: some clays take water into the mineral itself and swell,
  then shrink hard when they dry, so the ground under a house
  rises in a wet winter and drops in a dry summer — and it does it
  unevenly, which is what cracks the walls. Frost does the mirror
  image, drawing water toward the freezing front and lifting the
  surface bodily.
ROOT: chemistry / a layered mineral lets water in between its
  sheets; physics / ice growing in soil pulls in more liquid from
  below and builds lenses that jack the ground up.
