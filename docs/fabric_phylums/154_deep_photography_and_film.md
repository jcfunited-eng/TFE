# 154 DEEP PHOTOGRAPHY AND FILM — capturing light and time
Two crafts on one floor. A photograph is a measurement of light through
a hole, and everything called technique is the handling of what that
measurement can and cannot hold. A film adds duration and order, which
is a different power entirely. Not gear, not taste — the machinery every
camera operator and editor is working inside whether or not they can
name it. Every law here also forbids something.

ESSENCE: a photograph needs a fixed amount of light, and there are three
  ways to supply it — how wide the hole is, how long it stays open, and how
  hard the signal is amplified. Change one and another has to pay it back.
  That is the whole trade.
ROOT: physics / a picture is a count of arriving light, so the count can be
  reached by area, by time, or by gain; mathematics / three factors
  multiplying to one product.
CANNOT: no free exposure change — each of the three buys light with a side
  effect, so there is no setting that is only better. And no correct
  exposure in general: it is correct for a chosen depth, a chosen rendering
  of motion and a chosen noise level, which are decisions and not
  measurements.
THREAD: cooking (heat, time and thickness trading against each other), money
  (three levers on one budget), engineering (a design where every gain is
  taken from somewhere else).
ASKED-AS: exposure triangle aperture shutter iso settings dark photo bright manual mode balance

ESSENCE: opening the lens wider lets in more light and shrinks the band of
  the scene that is sharp. That is the real bargain — depth of sharpness is
  what you sell to buy light, and the blurred background everybody wants is
  the receipt for a purchase.
ROOT: light / a wide opening gathers rays from many angles, and rays from
  many angles only meet again at one distance; this file / exposure is one
  product with three factors.
CANNOT: no wide aperture with deep focus at a given framing — they are the
  same physical fact, so a picture needing both must be lit brighter or
  taken from further back. And no fixing a missed focus by stopping down
  afterwards: the frame was recorded at the aperture used.
THREAD: the eye (a pupil in dim light losing its depth of focus the same
  way), microscopes (magnification bought with a vanishing focus band),
  engineering (a bearing clearance traded against load).
ASKED-AS: aperture f stop blurry background bokeh sharp depth wide open lens fast light

ESSENCE: the shutter decides how much of the world's movement gets packed
  into one frame. Short and everything is frozen mid-air; long and moving
  things smear while still things stay sharp. It is not a sharpness control
  but a control over how much time the picture holds.
ROOT: physics / a picture accumulates over an interval, so anything that
  moved during that interval is recorded along its whole path; this file /
  time is one of the three ways to buy light.
CANNOT: no motion frozen in a long exposure and no smear in a short one, so
  a dim scene with fast movement is the case the trade cannot solve — add
  light or accept a loss. And no telling camera shake from subject motion
  after the fact by looking at the word "blurry".
THREAD: hearing (a long listening window smearing a fast event), astronomy
  (a tracked telescope trading star trails for faint light), sport (a stride
  photographed against a stride understood).
ASKED-AS: shutter speed blurry motion freeze action fast slow waterfall silky handheld shake sports

ESSENCE: raising sensitivity does not gather more light, it multiplies what
  arrived. The picture gets brighter and so does the randomness in it,
  because light comes in countable arrivals and a small count is a rough
  count. Noise is thin light amplified, not a fault in the camera.
ROOT: physics / light arrives as discrete events, so the fewer collected the
  larger the proportional wobble; engineering / amplification raises the
  signal and the noise together.
CANNOT: no clean picture from too little light, so no setting that creates
  information nobody collected. And no judging noise from the number on the
  dial: a bright scene at a high setting is cleaner than a dark scene at a
  low one, because the count is what matters and not the label.
THREAD: polls (a small sample being a noisy sample), audio (turning up a
  quiet recording and meeting the hiss), medicine (a test run on too little
  material).
ASKED-AS: iso noise grainy high dark photos low light clean sensor amplify speckled setting

ESSENCE: the real limit is not brightness, it is the distance between the
  brightest and darkest thing one frame can hold. The eye handles a window
  and a room at once; a camera has to choose. Most failed photographs are
  not badly exposed — they are scenes that did not fit.
ROOT: engineering / any instrument has a floor it cannot see below and a
  ceiling it saturates at, and the span between them is fixed; the eye /
  which cheats by adapting locally and continuously.
CANNOT: no single frame holding a range wider than the sensor's, so no
  exposure that rescues a scene which exceeds it — the cures are changing
  the scene's range with light, or taking more than one frame. And no
  matching the eye: it never took a single exposure in its life.
THREAD: hearing (a recording that cannot hold a whisper and a gunshot), maps
  (a scale that cannot show a continent and a street), accounting (a report
  that cannot resolve pennies and billions).

ESSENCE: the two ends fail differently. A blown highlight recorded its
  maximum and nothing beyond, so nothing is stored to recover; a dark shadow
  recorded a little, so lifting works and drags the noise up with it.
  Exposure is protected at the top and repaired at the bottom.
ROOT: this file / a sensor saturates at its ceiling and merely gets noisy
  near its floor, which are two different failures; evidence / a measurement
  at the top of its range has lost its ordering.
CANNOT: no recovering detail from a clipped highlight by any process — the
  information was never written, so whatever appears there was invented. And
  no free shadow recovery: the noise comes up with the detail, so the repair
  is always visible somewhere.
THREAD: audio (a clipped recording that cannot be un-clipped), money (a
  ceiling hit against a floor approached), memory (a fact never encoded
  against one encoded badly).
ASKED-AS: blown out sky white recover shadows lifting noise editing raw highlights protect exposure

ESSENCE: how objects sit relative to each other is decided entirely by where
  the camera stands. A long lens compresses nothing; it crops. The reason
  long-lens pictures look stacked is that people step back to use them, and
  stepping back is what did it.
ROOT: geometry / relative size in an image depends only on the ratio of
  distances from the viewpoint, and a lens does not move the viewpoint;
  light / a lens sets the angle captured and nothing more.
CANNOT: no perspective changed by a lens, so no fixing a distorted portrait
  by choice of glass alone — the fix is to step back and crop. And no
  wide-angle distortion that is not simply being close: the stretched face
  is a proximity effect the lens merely permitted.
THREAD: maps (a projection's distortion set by where the eye is put),
  painting (foreshortening that follows the painter's position), arguing (a
  fact's apparent size depending on where you stand).
ASKED-AS: focal length perspective distortion wide angle telephoto portrait big nose zoom stepping back

ESSENCE: photograph something from far away and everything behind it looks
  stacked against it, because at long range your distance to the subject and
  to the background are nearly the same. The flattening is a fact about
  distance. The long lens only made it visible.
ROOT: this file / relative size is a ratio of distances, and at long range
  that ratio approaches one; geometry / differences that matter near the eye
  stop mattering far from it.
CANNOT: no compression without distance and no distance without losing the
  near-far difference — the same fact, so you cannot have a stacked
  background and a strong sense of depth in one frame. And no separating a
  subject from a compressed background except by focus or by tone.
THREAD: astronomy (stars in a constellation that are nowhere near each
  other), history (a decade's events looking simultaneous from a century
  away), management (a distant team's differences flattening out).
ASKED-AS: telephoto compression flat background looks close moon huge behind runner stacked long lens

ESSENCE: the band of acceptable sharpness thins as you open the lens, as you
  move closer, and as you use a longer lens for the same framing. Distance
  to the subject is much the strongest of the three, which is why a phone
  blurs a close flower and never a standing person.
ROOT: light / a point out of focus lands as a small disc whose size grows
  with the aperture and with the fractional error in distance; this file /
  framing and perspective are set by where you stand.
CANNOT: no shallow focus on a distant subject with a small lens, so no
  faking a large camera's separation optically on a phone — which is why
  phones compute it, and why the computed edges fail on hair and glass. And
  no equal depth from equal f-numbers across different formats.
THREAD: microscopes (a focus band of almost nothing at high power), the eye
  (reading distance being where your own focus is most critical), machining
  (a tolerance that tightens as the part gets small).
ASKED-AS: depth of field blurry background phone portrait mode close up sharp aperture distance sensor

ESSENCE: a lens is sharp at exactly one distance and everything else is a
  gradient of getting worse. There is no region in focus — there is a plane,
  and a tolerance either side that we agree to call sharp. Focus is a choice
  of distance, so it is a choice of subject.
ROOT: light / all rays from one point meet again at one place, so one object
  distance maps to one image distance; the eye / a tolerance for softness
  that depends on how large the picture is finally viewed.
CANNOT: no two distances truly in focus at once on a flat sensor, so no
  picture with the eyes and the far wall both actually sharp — one is merely
  inside the tolerance. And no fixed answer to what counts as sharp: enlarge
  the print and the tolerance shrinks, so a picture can be sharp on a phone
  and soft on a wall.
THREAD: the eye (focusing one distance at a time and hiding it by moving
  fast), attention (one thing held clearly while the rest is approximated),
  management (a plane of priority with everything else tolerated).
ASKED-AS: focus point plane sharp eyes back focus missed autofocus tolerance print size soft

ESSENCE: whether light is harsh or soft has nothing to do with brightness.
  It is the size of the source as seen from the subject. The sun is enormous
  and very far off, so it acts small and makes hard shadows; put a cloud in
  front and the whole sky becomes the source.
ROOT: geometry / a shadow's edge is blurred by the angular width of the
  source, so a wide source blurs it and a point source does not; light / a
  surface is lit from every direction its source occupies.
CANNOT: no soft light from a small source however powerful, and no hard
  light from a large near one however dim — so the only two controls are how
  big the source is and how close it is brought. And no softening by moving
  a small light further away: it gets smaller, which is backwards.
THREAD: sound (a large radiating surface against a point source), heating (a
  radiator against a fire), teaching (broad exposure against a single sharp
  example).
ASKED-AS: soft light harsh shadows cloudy window diffuser umbrella softbox flattering portrait sun bright

ESSENCE: shape is read from the pattern of light and shade across a surface,
  so the direction light comes from decides whether a face looks round, flat
  or carved. Light from beside the lens fills every hollow and deletes the
  form; light from the side puts every texture on show.
ROOT: light and seeing / the eye infers three dimensions from shading
  gradients, which exist only where light arrives at an angle; the mind /
  one assumed light direction is used to read every surface at once.
CANNOT: no modelling from light aligned with the lens — the shadows fall
  behind the objects that made them, so the picture flattens whatever the
  subject is. And no hiding texture under side light: skin, walls and fabric
  all confess, which is why it flatters stone and punishes faces.
THREAD: maps (relief shading that only works with a raking light),
  archaeology (crop marks visible only at low sun), design (an embossed
  shape that needs a light direction in order to exist at all).
ASKED-AS: lighting direction side front flat backlit shadows face texture skin wrinkles portrait

ESSENCE: every light source has a colour and the eye erases it — you never
  notice indoor bulbs are orange until a photograph shows you. White balance
  tells the camera which light to call white, and that is a choice: correct
  a sunset to neutral and you have deleted the sunset.
ROOT: the eye / perception discounts the illuminant so objects keep their
  colour under different lights; arts / colours belong together when one
  light has contaminated all of them.
CANNOT: no neutral answer — the camera has to call something white, so
  declining to choose lets a guess set the mood of the picture. And no
  single correction for a scene lit by two lights: fix the window and the
  lamp goes orange, fix the lamp and the window goes blue.
THREAD: memory (a bias corrected out so smoothly you deny having it),
  printing (paper white as the reference everything is measured from),
  translation (a neutral register that is itself a choice).
ASKED-AS: white balance orange indoor photos blue shade auto kelvin warm cool mixed lighting

ESSENCE: contrast is how a picture separates things, and it works at every
  scale at once — big light-and-dark masses that decide the layout, small
  local differences that read as texture. Raise one and you usually crush
  the other, which is why heavy editing looks loud and empty.
ROOT: light and seeing / the eye reports differences, so all visible
  structure is contrast at some scale; arts / a strong image survives being
  reduced to a few tone families.
CANNOT: no adding local detail by increasing overall contrast — the big move
  clips the small differences it was meant to reveal. And no picture with no
  contrast decision in it: a flat file is a decision to choose later, and a
  punchy one is a decision already spent.
THREAD: writing (emphasis on everything being emphasis on nothing), audio
  (compression that lifts the quiet parts and flattens the performance),
  maps (a legend that has to read at two zoom levels).
ASKED-AS: contrast flat photo punchy editing crushed blacks detail texture clarity sliders overdone

ESSENCE: a light meter measures the whole frame and assumes it averages to a
  mid grey. Point it at snow and it makes the snow grey; point it at a black
  dog and it makes the dog grey. The instrument is not wrong — it was asked
  a different question from the one you had.
ROOT: evidence / a measurement answers the question its method encodes, and
  averaging encodes "make this scene average"; this file / a frame has a
  fixed range, so what fits depends on where the subject is placed in it.
CANNOT: no automatic exposure that knows what the subject is, so no
  unusually bright or dark scene metered correctly by default. And no
  exposing for everything when the range does not fit: something has to be
  chosen and protected, and that choice is the photograph's content.
THREAD: statistics (an average describing nobody in the room), medicine (a
  reference range built on a different population), management (a headline
  number hiding the part that matters).
ASKED-AS: metering exposure compensation snow grey backlit face dark subject spot matrix camera wrong

ESSENCE: a photographer cannot move the world, only themselves and the
  moment. Every compositional decision comes down to where you stood, how
  high you held the camera, how wide you took it, and when you pressed. The
  thirds grid is a summary of results, not a method.
ROOT: arts / composition decides the order in which the eye arrives; this
  file / perspective is set by the viewpoint, so moving is the only tool
  that changes how things relate.
CANNOT: no composing without moving — standing still and changing settings
  changes framing and never arrangement, which is why beginners' pictures
  are all taken from standing height. And no rule that produces a picture:
  the grid can be satisfied perfectly by a photograph of nothing.
THREAD: negotiation (position deciding what is possible before any
  argument), chess (a move's value set by where the pieces already stand),
  reporting (proximity to the event being most of the story).
ASKED-AS: composition rule thirds boring photos moving around angle low high framing better position

ESSENCE: in a still frame the edge is a wall — whatever it cuts reads as
  cut. In a moving frame the edge is a door: things arrive through it and
  leave through it, so the space outside stays alive in a way it never does
  in a photograph.
ROOT: arts / a frame is a claim about what belongs, and its edges are active
  relationships; the mind / a thing seen leaving is still believed to exist,
  while a thing merely absent is not.
CANNOT: no offscreen presence in a still image without a cue inside it — a
  shadow, a gaze, a hand — because a photograph has no before and after for
  anything to have come from. And no film shot that can ignore its edges:
  whatever walked out is still in the audience's model of the room.
THREAD: theatre (the wings as a real place), radio drama (a world built
  entirely outside the frame), writing (a character kept offstage and made
  larger by it).
ASKED-AS: framing edges cut off cropping film offscreen someone walks out frame space still

ESSENCE: in a photograph the content is a slice of time whose edges you
  cannot see, so a fraction of a second decides everything — a foot planted
  or lifted, an expression forming or gone. Two frames a blink apart are two
  different photographs, and one of them is the picture.
ROOT: this file / a still image has no before and after, so all the
  narrative has to be carried by one configuration; the mind / a posture is
  read as a whole action, so the wrong instant tells the wrong story.
CANNOT: no recovering the moment afterwards by any amount of processing — it
  is the one thing not stored anywhere. And no catching it reliably by
  reflex: the decision has to be made before it arrives, which is why
  photographers pre-frame and wait.
THREAD: sport (the pass thrown to where the runner will be), comedy (timing
  being the whole of it), trading (an entry decided in advance and executed
  on sight).
ASKED-AS: decisive moment timing photo caught expression missed shot burst mode anticipating waiting street

ESSENCE: a moving picture has everything a photograph has, plus control over
  how long you look and in what order. That is a different power. A still
  image is examined at the viewer's pace; a film sets the pace, and setting
  the pace is how it does most of its work.
ROOT: the mind / attention over time can be led or left free, and only one
  of the two media can lead it; this file / a photograph carries an instant,
  a film carries duration and sequence.
CANNOT: no still image controlling how long it is looked at, so no
  photograph can build suspense — it can only depict it. And no film that
  can be scanned like a photograph: rewinding is not glancing back, so a
  detail missed is genuinely missed.
THREAD: music (a piece that must be taken at its own speed), reading (a book
  paced by the reader against a lecture paced by the speaker), guided tours
  (a route walked at somebody else's rate).

ESSENCE: the basic unit of film is not the shot, it is the join between two
  shots. A cut is an instantaneous jump in space and time that audiences
  accept without noticing, and every cut is a claim that these two views
  belong to one continuous event.
ROOT: the mind / perception stitches over gaps and builds one continuous
  world out of samples, which is what makes cutting possible at all; this
  file / film controls order and duration, and the cut sets both.
CANNOT: no film without the audience's willingness to bridge — the medium
  runs on it, which is why an audience trained to notice cuts is much harder
  to move. And no cut that means nothing: two shots joined are read as
  related, so an accidental adjacency is still an assertion.
THREAD: writing (a paragraph break crossed without effort), maps (adjacent
  sheets read as one territory), conversation (two remarks placed side by
  side implying a link).

ESSENCE: continuity errors — the glass full then empty, the jacket buttoned
  then not — matter exactly as much as they draw attention. Absorbed
  audiences miss nearly all of them, and the same error in a dull scene is
  all anyone remembers. It is an attention budget, not a rule.
ROOT: the mind / what is not attended is not encoded, so a change outside
  the focus of attention is genuinely unseen; this file / a cut is bridged
  by the audience, and the bridging is what forgives a mismatch.
CANNOT: no continuity fixing a scene that is not working — the errors become
  visible because attention wandered, so an error is a symptom of the real
  fault. And no ignoring it in the shots the audience studies: a face in
  close-up is examined, so its details are checked.
THREAD: writing (a plot hole invisible inside a gripping book), stage magic
  (misdirection making the impossible unnoticed), accounting (a small
  discrepancy found only when somebody was already looking).

ESSENCE: audiences build a map of the space from the first shots and then
  hold on to it. Shoot two people talking with one looking left and one
  looking right and they stay facing each other; cross the imaginary line
  between them and they suddenly seem to be looking the same way.
ROOT: the mind / a spatial model is built once from evidence and then
  defended, so contradicting evidence reads as a change in the world rather
  than in the camera; this file / a cut asserts that both views are one
  event.
CANNOT: no crossing the line without either wrecking the map or announcing
  the move with a shot that shows it happening. And no cheating an eyeline:
  with the gaze at the wrong height or angle the audience feels the two
  people are not in one room without being able to say why.
THREAD: maps (north kept consistent so a route can be held), stage (blocking
  that keeps the geography legible), instructions (left and right defined
  once and then kept).

ESSENCE: how much of a person fills the frame is a statement about how close
  the audience is standing. Wide is watching, medium is present, close is
  intimate or intrusive, and the extreme close-up is nearer than anyone is
  ever allowed in life. Shot size is a relationship.
ROOT: people together / physical distance is already how humans signal
  intimacy and threat, so the frame borrows a code the audience owns; arts /
  size is felt in the body before it is understood.
CANNOT: no emotional close-up that was not earned by distance beforehand —
  go close early and there is nowhere left to move, which is why films begin
  wide. And no neutral shot size: whatever was chosen has already placed the
  audience somewhere.
THREAD: conversation (a step closer changing the subject), portrait painting
  (a sitter's crop as social distance), interviews (a camera pushing in as
  the question sharpens).

ESSENCE: a moving camera is a second character with an opinion. Moving in
  commits and intensifies; moving out reveals and abandons; following says
  we are with this person. A camera that drifts with nothing motivating it
  only makes the audience seasick and vaguely anxious.
ROOT: the mind / self-motion is read as an intention, because in life a
  viewpoint moves only when somebody moves it; this file / film controls
  duration and viewpoint, and a move spends both.
CANNOT: no free camera movement — every move is read as meaning something,
  so a move for its own sake is a sentence that says nothing loudly. And no
  substituting movement for a reason to look: a restless camera on a static
  scene advertises that the scene is static.
THREAD: writing (a shifting point of view that has to be justified), driving
  (a passenger reading intent from the wheel), presentation (a speaker who
  paces for no reason).

ESSENCE: how long each shot is held is the film's pulse, and audiences feel
  it directly. Short shots raise arousal and cut comprehension; long shots
  let people look around and think. The cutting rate is the single strongest
  control over how a sequence feels.
ROOT: this file / film sets the audience's pace; the mind / every cut costs
  a moment of re-orientation, so frequent cuts spend attention on locating
  rather than on understanding.
CANNOT: no maximum clarity and maximum speed at once — fast cutting buys
  energy with comprehension, which is why chaotic action is exciting and
  unfollowable. And no tension from long takes by themselves: duration only
  builds pressure when something in the frame is unresolved.
THREAD: music (tempo doing the emotional work), speech (a fast talker who is
  hard to argue with), driving (speed reducing what can be read at the

ESSENCE: bad picture with good sound is watchable; good picture with bad
  sound is not. Sound reaches an audience without having to be looked at, it
  defines the space around the frame, and most of what people call the feel
  of a scene arrives through their ears.
ROOT: the body / hearing points in every direction and never switches off,
  so it is the sense that reports the environment; the mind / a threat is
  judged by ear before the eyes are pointed at it.
CANNOT: no scene rescued by pictures once the sound is broken — audiences
  forgive grain, shake and colour, and stop watching for hiss and echo. And
  no location saved in the edit: a room's acoustic is recorded into every
  word, so it cannot be taken out later.
THREAD: theatre (a hall's acoustic deciding whether a play works), music (a
  recording's room being part of the record), speech (a phone line that ends
  a conversation the moment it degrades).

ESSENCE: some sound exists in the world of the story — a radio, a door, a

ROOT: this file / film addresses an audience that is also watching a world,

ESSENCE: music tells the audience what kind of thing they are watching

ESSENCE: shooting a scene from several sizes and angles is not indecision,

  is the one nobody planned, and if it was never shot the scene cannot be
  fixed at all.
ROOT: chance and risk / a decision made later with more information is

ESSENCE: two different things get called sharp. Resolution is how fine a
  detail is recorded at all; edge contrast is how abruptly a boundary
  changes, and that is what the eye reads as sharpness. A low-detail picture
  with crisp edges looks sharper than a detailed soft one.
ROOT: light and seeing / the eye detects edges far more strongly than fine
  texture, so edge contrast dominates the impression; evidence / a measured
  quantity and a perceived one drifting apart.
CANNOT: no sharpening that adds detail — the process raises edge contrast on
  whatever was recorded, so it can only make existing information more
  visible and eventually make halos. And no sharpness that holds at every
  size: a picture is only ever sharp at a viewing distance.
THREAD: audio (a bright mix that sounds detailed and is not), typography (a
  typeface that reads crisply at low resolution), writing (a confident
  sentence with nothing in it).

ESSENCE: stopping the lens down keeps buying depth of field, and past a
  point it starts costing overall sharpness, because light squeezing through
  a small opening spreads. So there is a middle setting where a lens is at
  its best, and going smaller trades a gain for a loss.
ROOT: physics / a wave passing an aperture spreads by an amount that grows
  as the opening shrinks; this file / depth of field grows as the aperture
  closes, so the two curves cross.
