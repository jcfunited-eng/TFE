# 97 DEEP FOOD AND GROWING — feeding people
Middle floors from the seed to the plate to the market: what the
grower, the keeper, the cook and the ledger each know. Laws, not
recipes — and every one of them forbids something.

ESSENCE: good soil is about half empty space — crumbs of mineral
  and rotted matter glued into clumps by fungal threads and
  microbial slime, with pores between them holding air, water and
  room for roots; the holes are the fertility.
ROOT: farming / soil is a living bank — but the vault is its
  architecture, and the architecture is built by living things, not
  poured.
CANNOT: no root without air — a compacted or waterlogged soil
  drowns roots in a field full of water, and no fertilizer reaches a
  root that cannot breathe. And no rebuilding structure quickly: the
  glue is grown.
THREAD: building (concrete's strength lives in its pore structure
  too), the body (lungs are almost entirely hole), baking (a crumb
  is judged by its holes).
ASKED-AS: soil compacted hard clay digging crumbly worms roots drainage garden tilling healthy

ESSENCE: a nutrient sitting in the soil is not a nutrient in the
  plant — how tightly the soil grips it depends on how sour or
  chalky the ground is, so a field can be full of iron or phosphorus
  and starve the crop of both while the fertilizer bag says it has
  plenty.
ROOT: chemistry / a mineral is only available while it is dissolved,
  and how readily it dissolves is set by the water it sits in.
CANNOT: no feeding a crop past a locked soil — adding more of a
  nutrient the ground is holding shut wastes the money and can lock
  a second nutrient as well. The dial to turn is the sourness, not
  the sack.
THREAD: medicine (a drug present in the blood but bound to a
  protein does nothing), money (an asset you own and cannot sell),
  computing (data stored and unreachable is data lost).
ASKED-AS: soil ph acid alkaline lime testing yellow leaves deficiency fertilizer locked available nutrients

ESSENCE: the air is four-fifths nitrogen and almost nothing alive
  can touch it, because the two atoms are triple-locked together —
  so all fertility waited on a few bacteria that can break the lock,
  and beans, peas and clover house them in root nodules and pay them
  in sugar.
ROOT: chemistry / a strong bond is a high wall, and getting over it
  costs energy that has to come from somewhere.
CANNOT: no plant fixing nitrogen by itself — the crop cannot do it,
  only its lodgers can, so a clover field is a contract with
  bacteria. And no free lunch: the sugar paid to the lodgers is
  yield that was not grown.
THREAD: money (renting a capability instead of owning it), the body
  (gut bacteria making vitamins we cannot), industry (the factory
  version does the same job with heat, pressure and fossil fuel).
ASKED-AS: beans peas clover nitrogen fertilizer rotation legumes nodules roots soil fertility manure

ESSENCE: you do not make compost, you set conditions and the
  microbes make it — four dials and all of them matter: dry brown
  stuff against wet green stuff, damp but not soaked, air, and
  pieces small enough to work on. Heat is the receipt that the
  workers turned up.
ROOT: farming / rot is the return counter — composting is that
  counter given a corner of the garden and a schedule.
CANNOT: no hot heap without air — an airless pile still rots, but by
  a slower crew that stinks, so a sour smell means turn it and not
  add to it. And no compost from one ingredient: greens alone
  putrefy, browns alone just sit.
THREAD: brewing (the same four dials, indoors and fussier),
  management (you cannot order the work done, only supply the
  conditions), sewage works (industrial composting, of water).
ASKED-AS: compost heap turning smelly slimy grass clippings leaves kitchen scraps hot pile brown

ESSENCE: minerals leave a farm on every truck and used to come back
  through the people who ate them — the loop closed because everyone
  lived where the food grew. Cities broke it: the sewer now takes
  what the field needs, and bagged fertilizer is the patch over the
  break.
ROOT: physics / matter is never used up, only moved — so a nutrient
  is always somewhere, and the only question is whether it is where
  the roots are.
CANNOT: no farm exporting food forever and importing nothing.
  And no patching it forever either: nitrogen can be pulled from the
  air, but phosphorus is dug out of a finite number of holes in the
  ground and does not come back on its own.
THREAD: money (an account with withdrawals and no deposits),
  history (guano and saltpeter fought over as strategic goods),
  ecology (salmon carrying the sea's minerals up rivers into forests).
ASKED-AS: fertilizer manure sewage waste nutrients phosphate mined imported soil exhausted recycling cities

ESSENCE: a seed carries a packed lunch and has to reach the light
  before it runs out — so sowing depth is set by seed size, roughly
  two or three times its own width; a big bean can climb from deep,
  and a dust-fine seed sown a finger down starves in the dark.
ROOT: farming / sunlight is the only income — until a leaf reaches
  daylight, the plant is spending savings that were fixed at packing
  time.
CANNOT: no seedling arriving from deeper than its reserve allows —
  it does not come up weak, it does not come up at all. And no fine
  seed germinating on a dry surface either: shallow means exposed,
  so shallow sowing has to be kept damp.
THREAD: business (a startup's runway before revenue), climbing (the
  air you carry decides the summit), space flight (everything for
  the trip has to be aboard at launch).
ASKED-AS: seeds sowing depth planting deep shallow germination failed patchy row covering soil packet

ESSENCE: a plant does not pump water, it pulls it — water evaporates
  from the leaves and drags an unbroken thread of water up the stem
  behind it, so the plant's thirst is set by the sky: sun, wind and
  dry air decide the demand, and the soil only decides whether it
  can be met.
ROOT: physics / evaporation is the fastest escape, and water in a
  narrow tube holds together under pull rather than snapping.
CANNOT: no wilting cured by watering alone on a hot windy day — the
  leaves are losing faster than any root can supply, which is why
  shade and shelter work when a hose does not. And no growth without
  the loss: the same open pores that let water out let the air in
  that the plant eats.
THREAD: weather (a forest is a very large evaporator), the body
  (sweating for cooling and paying in water), engineering (a wick
  and a heat pipe running on the same pull).
ASKED-AS: wilting hot day watering shade windy leaves drooping evening greenhouse transpiration thirsty dry

ESSENCE: a plant drinks through root hairs finer than thread, and
  lifting it destroys nearly all of them — but the leaves above are
  untouched and go on losing water at full rate, so the plant wilts
  from a supply failure while looking perfectly healthy.
ROOT: the living world / intake and losses are separately sized, so
  an injury that halves one leaves the other running at its old
  speed.
CANNOT: no transplant without loss — the hairs regrow within days,
  but the gap has to be survived, which is why every remedy reduces
  demand instead of adding supply: evening planting, shade, shelter,
  and cutting the top back.
THREAD: medicine (fluid loss after a burn — the same imbalance),
  business (a firm that loses its supply line while orders keep
  arriving), moving house (routines cut and slowly regrown).
ASKED-AS: transplanting seedlings wilting drooping repotting shock roots watering shade planting out recovering

ESSENCE: a growing tip chemically holds down the buds below it, so
  cutting the tip does not remove growth — it releases it, into the
  buds you left, in the direction the top one is pointing; cut to an
  outward-facing bud and the branch grows outward.
ROOT: the living world / an organism has a fixed energy budget and
  internal rules for spending it — pruning edits the rules, never
  the budget.
CANNOT: no adding vigor with a saw — a hard prune redistributes and
  often answers with a thicket of useless shoots. And no shaping
  without knowing which bud you are handing the season to: every cut
  is an instruction, including the careless ones.
THREAD: management (removing a dominant person releases everyone
  below, for good and ill), money (a cut budget flows to whatever is
  still approved), teaching (dropping one habit frees the hour).
ASKED-AS: pruning cutting back hedge fruit tree shape branches buds shoots winter growth thicker

ESSENCE: a graft joins two plants so their thin growing layers touch
  and knit — roots chosen for the soil and the diseases, top chosen
  for the fruit — and it exists because almost no fruit tree comes
  true from seed; every apple of a named kind is a cutting of one
  original tree, kept alive for centuries.
ROOT: heredity / a seed shuffles two parents while a cutting copies
  one — sameness needs cloning, not breeding.
CANNOT: no named variety from a pip — plant the seed of a fine apple
  and you get a new tree, usually a poor one. And no graft without
  growing layer meeting growing layer: bark pressed to bark heals
  nothing.
THREAD: medicine (skin grafts and transplants, matched or
  rejected), computing (a copied codebase kept identical on
  purpose), wine (nearly every European vine now grows on American
  roots).
ASKED-AS: grafting fruit trees apple rootstock dwarf variety seed pip clone budding nursery cutting

ESSENCE: crossing two carefully separated parent lines gives a first
  generation that outgrows both — but its own seed scatters back into
  a ragged mixture, so the vigor cannot be saved and replanted; the
  farmer who wants it must buy seed again every year.
ROOT: heredity / a hybrid holds one copy of everything from each
  side, and the next generation deals those copies out again at
  random.
CANNOT: no keeping hybrid vigor by saving seed — the harvest is not
  the variety, and the loss shows up a year later in the field. And
  no vigor at all without keeping the two parent lines apart and
  weak in the meantime.
THREAD: money (a lease that looks like ownership until renewal),
  computing (a format you can read but not produce), governance
  (dependence created by a technical fact, not by a law).
ASKED-AS: hybrid seed saving seeds buying every year variety corn vigor open pollinated heirloom

ESSENCE: a great many crops cannot set fruit without a delivery
  service they neither own nor pay — insects that must be alive, in
  numbers, in exactly the fortnight of bloom, and which need forage
  and nesting through a whole year the farm never thinks about.
ROOT: the living world / a partnership is a shared fate, and each
  partner's needs run on its own calendar rather than the other's.
CANNOT: no fruit set without the visit — a perfect orchard in full
  flower with nothing flying is a crop of nothing, and no water,
  feed or weather substitutes.
THREAD: money (a business resting on one supplier's health), the
  body (gut flora we do not manage and cannot lose), logistics (a
  factory stopped by a missing part worth pennies).
ASKED-AS: bees pollination fruit orchard flowers blossom hives insects wild crop set apples yield

ESSENCE: bare soil is an unfilled job and something will take it —
  weeds are simply the plants that colonize disturbed ground fastest,
  so every act of digging is an invitation; mulch works not by
  poisoning them but by keeping the light off the seeds that were
  waiting for it.
ROOT: the living world / an unused resource is an opening, and
  openings are found quickly by whatever is built to find them.
CANNOT: no bare ground staying bare — clearing weeds without
  covering the soil only reissues the invitation, and the seed bank
  underneath holds decades of them. And no weed germinating in the
  dark: light is the trigger, which is why a covered bed stays clean.
THREAD: money (an unserved market fills within a season), people
  together (an authority vacuum), cleaning (an empty surface
  attracting clutter within a week).
ASKED-AS: weeds mulch bare soil digging seeds germinate covering ground cardboard hoeing garden coming back

ESSENCE: rotation works on the calendar, not on the soil — a pest or
  disease fitted to one crop builds up while that crop keeps
  returning, and starves if the crop is absent for a year or three;
  the target is the pest's life cycle, and the crop's absence is the
  weapon.
ROOT: the living world / a specialist population tracks its food, so
  the way to collapse it is to remove the food for longer than it
  can wait.
CANNOT: no rotation defeating a pest that outlasts the gap — some
  spores sit in the soil for a decade, and against those the gap
  would have to be longer than a farmer's patience. And no rotation
  at all in a field growing one thing forever, whatever is added to it.
THREAD: medicine (a drug holiday to let resistance fade), war (a
  siege breaking a supply line rather than a wall), computing (an
  attacker starved by changing the thing they had learned).
ASKED-AS: rotation crops same bed year disease build up potatoes tomatoes soil rest planning

ESSENCE: a broad spray kills the pest's enemies as well, and the
  enemies breed slower — so the pest comes back first, into a field
  with nothing left to eat it, in worse numbers than before; the
  second spraying was caused by the first.
ROOT: the living world / a population released from its predators
  grows at its own rate, and recovery speeds differ between the
  eaten and the eater.
CANNOT: no lasting control by killing alone — the survivors of every
  spray are the ones that tolerated it, so a poison also breeds its
  own defeat. And no standing army of predators without habitat:
  hedges and flowering strips are the barracks.
THREAD: medicine (antibiotics clearing the gut and letting something
  worse move in), policing (removing one gang and opening the
  territory), farming (a single-crop field removes the enemies by
  design).
ASKED-AS: pests aphids spraying insecticide ladybirds beneficial bugs organic worse resistance garden hedges

ESSENCE: modern high-yield varieties put more of the plant into the
  grain and less into stalk and root — which is exactly why they
  need more water, more feeding and more protection: the yield was
  bought by trading away the parts that used to make the plant
  self-sufficient.
ROOT: engineering / every design buys a virtue with a virtue — the
  plant's budget did not grow, it was reallocated.
CANNOT: no high yield without the inputs it was bred to expect —
  those varieties do worse than old ones on a poor unwatered field,
  so the package is the seed plus the water plus the fertilizer, and
  splitting the package fails.
THREAD: engineering (a race engine that needs the race fuel), money
  (leverage raising returns and removing the ability to survive a
  bad year), the body (a specialized athlete fragile outside the
  sport).
ASKED-AS: high yield varieties fertilizer irrigation green revolution old seeds drought failing inputs dwarf wheat

ESSENCE: all water carries dissolved salt, the crop drinks the water
  and leaves the salt behind — so irrigated land gathers it season
  by season until nothing grows; the only cure is spare water to
  wash the salt below the roots, and drainage to carry it away.
ROOT: chemistry / evaporation removes water and nothing else, so the
  dissolved load stays and concentrates, every single time.
CANNOT: no irrigation without drainage — water in with no water out
  is a slow sterilization with a lag of decades, and it has ended
  farming regions that never saw it coming.
THREAD: cooking (a pan boiled dry leaving a ring of salt), kettles
  and radiators (scale by identical arithmetic), money (a small
  accumulating cost invisible in any single year).
ASKED-AS: irrigation salt salty soil white crust desert drainage watering fields crops failing salinity

ESSENCE: a frost date is a probability dressed as a calendar — the
  day after which frost becomes unlikely, not impossible — and the
  growing season is the corridor between two such guesses; the plan
  is made by counting a crop's days to maturity backwards from the
  autumn one.
ROOT: chance+risk / a date taken off a spread of outcomes is not a
  promise, and the whole crop lives in the tail.
CANNOT: no crop finishing outside the corridor — sown so that it
  matures after the closing frost, it is not late, it is refused.
  And no gain from going far too early: cold soil holds the seed
  still while it rots.
THREAD: money (a deadline set from a forecast), building (a concrete
  pour that has to beat the winter), logistics (the whole plan
  back-calculated from a ship's sailing date).
ASKED-AS: last frost planting seeds spring garden tomatoes autumn first freeze timing calendar maturity days

ESSENCE: frost is a place before it is a night — cold air is heavy
  and flows downhill like water, pooling in hollows and behind
  hedges, so a still clear night with no wind to stir it and no
  cloud to hold the day's heat is the dangerous one, and it is
  dangerous at the bottom of the slope.
ROOT: weather / heat leaves upward as glow on a clear night, and
  cold air sinks — two ordinary facts that together draw a map.
CANNOT: no frost pocket protected by choosing hardier plants alone —
  the cold keeps arriving in the same yard every year. And no hard
  frost under thick cloud or in real wind: both are blankets, one
  holding heat in and one keeping the layers mixed.
THREAD: navigation (cold air draining down a valley as a night
  wind), building (a damp corner that is always the damp corner),
  wine (whole regions planted on slopes for exactly this).
ASKED-AS: frost pocket hollow valley bottom clear night still cold damage slope cloudy windy

ESSENCE: the danger is never the average year, it is the gap — the
  weeks before a new harvest when last year's store has run down and
  nothing is ready yet; every preserving craft and every fast in the
  old calendars sits over that hole in the year.
ROOT: farming / the harvest is a one-way gate on a moving clock — so
  a year's food arrives in bursts and is eaten continuously, and the
  difference has to be stored.
CANNOT: no surviving on an annual total — a full year's calories
  arriving in autumn feeds nobody in May, so storage is not thrift,
  it is the whole system. And no store that does not have to survive
  the longest gap, not the usual one.
THREAD: money (cash flow killing profitable businesses), water
  (reservoirs sized for the drought, not the rainfall), power grids
  (built for the coldest evening of the decade).
ASKED-AS: hungry gap stores running out spring lean season food supply harvest preserving famine

ESSENCE: the best day to eat and the best day to keep are different
  days, and they move apart — picked early a crop travels and stores
  and never quite develops; picked ripe it is at its best and has
  days to live. Every supermarket tomato is that trade made against
  the eater.
ROOT: farming / harvest is a one-way gate on a moving clock — the
  clock does not stop at picking, it only changes hands.
CANNOT: no fruit both fully ripe and long-keeping — sugar, softness
  and scent are the same processes as decay, one step further along;
  you can have the flavor or the fortnight.
THREAD: money (liquidity against return — the same shaped choice),
  wine (drink now or lay it down), people (a skill learned for this
  job against one kept general for later).
ASKED-AS: tomatoes picked green ripening supermarket flavor storing apples keeping shelf life harvest early taste

ESSENCE: some fruits go on ripening after picking and some stop
  dead — bananas, avocados, pears and tomatoes carry on, while
  grapes, citrus and strawberries are as good as they will ever be
  the moment they leave the plant. The ones that continue signal each
  other with a gas, so one ripe fruit does hurry a whole bowl.
ROOT: the living world / ripening is a program a plant runs, and
  some plants run it on a chemical announcement that spreads.
CANNOT: no ripening a fruit that does not carry the program — a sour
  orange stays sour on the windowsill forever, and no paper bag
  helps it. And no slowing the ones that do without either cold or
  taking away the air they are talking in.
THREAD: people together (one panicking person setting off a room),
  computing (a signal that spreads through a network faster than the
  thing it signals), medicine (a hormone released to coordinate many
  cells at once).
ASKED-AS: bananas ripening avocado hard paper bag apple bowl fruit gas ethylene oranges strawberries picked

ESSENCE: chilled food is protected by its warmest moment, not its
  average — every hour on a loading dock or a car seat is spent from
  a budget that never refills, and the damage does not show until the
  food is somewhere else entirely, in somebody else's kitchen.
ROOT: engineering / a system is as strong as its weakest single
  point, and here the weak point is a moment rather than a part.
CANNOT: no cooling back to safe — chilling again stops further
  growth but removes nothing that already grew, and no thermometer
  at the shop can report a warm hour last Tuesday.
THREAD: medicine (vaccines ruined by one failed refrigerator in a
  long chain), computing (a backup chain broken at one untested
  link), trust (a reputation set by its worst recorded moment).
ASKED-AS: fridge chilled shopping car trunk frozen thawed refrozen delivery temperature safe warm supermarket

ESSENCE: rot is other creatures eating your food first, and there
  are five ways to stop them — take their water away (drying,
  salting, sugaring), seal them out and heat the survivors dead
  (canning), slow them to a stop with cold (freezing), or let an
  acid-making crowd hold the ground so nothing worse can land
  (fermenting). Each one fails in its own particular way.
ROOT: farming / rot is the return counter — preserving is an
  argument with it, and every argument has its own weak point.
CANNOT: no method that stops everything — drying loses to mold at
  the one damp spot, canning fails silently when the seal or the
  heat fell short, freezing loses slowly through ice and fat, and a
  ferment fails when the tame crowd loses its grip.
THREAD: money (five ways to hold value, each with its own ruin),
  computing (backups by different methods so they fail differently),
  medicine (no single infection control does all of it).
ASKED-AS: canning jars drying jerky salting curing freezing pickling fermenting preserving spoiled mold seal

ESSENCE: freezing stops the microbes and does not stop the food —
  the vegetable's own enzymes keep working slowly in the freezer,
  turning color, flavor and texture drab over months, which is why
  vegetables are dipped in boiling water before freezing: the heat
  kills the enzymes, and the cold handles the rest.
ROOT: cooking / cooking proteins is one-way — an enzyme is a protein,
  so a brief heat shuts it permanently while barely cooking the food.
CANNOT: no freezer cold enough to stop chemistry — only microbes are
  stopped, and everything else is merely slowed. And no undoing the
  slow damage later: a badly frozen pea does not come back green.
THREAD: this file (a cut apple browning by the same enzyme, stopped
  the same way), medicine (a sample fixed with heat or chemicals
  before storage), photography (a film developed then fixed, or it
  keeps changing).
ASKED-AS: blanching freezing vegetables boiling first peas beans frozen taste dull color enzymes storing months

ESSENCE: food is mostly water, so shipping food is mostly shipping
  water — which is why, for all the centuries before refrigeration,
  the goods that crossed oceans were the dry, the salted and the
  concentrated: spices, sugar, tea, salt fish and spirits, all of
  them value squeezed small.
ROOT: movement of goods / freight is paid by weight and volume, so
  what travels is whatever carries the most worth per ton.
CANNOT: no long trade in cheap wet food before cold and speed
  existed — fresh milk had a range of a morning's cart ride, so
  every region ate its own perishables and traded only what dried.
THREAD: computing (compression before transmission, for exactly this
  reason), money (why gold rather than grain crossed continents),
  history (a spice trade that reshaped the world because pepper is
  light).
ASKED-AS: shipping food trade spices dried salted fish sugar heavy water transport fresh milk local

ESSENCE: about a third of all food grown is never eaten — lost in
  the field, the store and the road where handling is poor, and lost
  in the kitchen and the bin where food is cheap; so the cheapest
  way to add food to the world is usually to lose less of it rather
  than to grow more.
ROOT: engineering / a chain keeps only the product of its links, so
  a loss late in the chain wastes everything spent earlier on that
  item.
CANNOT: no waste that only costs the food — every wasted item also
  wasted its water, its fertilizer, its fuel and its land, and those
  are spent long before anyone decides to throw it out.
THREAD: engineering (efficiency won at the last stage saving all the
  earlier stages), money (a leak at the end of a process), medicine
  (a treatment that fails at the last step of delivery).
ASKED-AS: food waste thrown away bin expired storage losses harvest spoiled supermarket dates leftovers hunger

ESSENCE: the world eats grass seeds — wheat, rice, maize, barley —
  because a grass makes a dry, dense, storable package that keeps for
  years, can be measured, moved, taxed and lent against. You cannot
  build a state on cabbages: they will not wait, and they cannot be
  counted at the door.
ROOT: money / a good becomes a store of value when it keeps, divides
  and can be verified — grain does all three, and most food does not.
CANNOT: no granary of perishables, and therefore no tax on them —
  which is why the earliest states appear where storable cereals
  were grown, and not in the places with plenty of food that would
  not sit still.
THREAD: money (grain as the first widely accepted savings, and the
  first receipts as the first paper money), law (a debt needs a
  countable thing), war (an army travels on what can be sacked and
  carried).
ASKED-AS: wheat rice corn staple grain storing granary tax history bread civilization keeping cereals

ESSENCE: livestock is either an upgrade or a downgrade, and the feed
  decides which — an animal eating grass, scraps and stubble from
  land that grows nothing people can eat is turning the inedible into
  food; the same animal eating grain is eating what a person could
  have eaten, and giving back a fraction of it.
ROOT: farming / each eating step wastes most of the energy, so
  putting an animal in the chain only adds food when it started with
  something no human could use.
CANNOT: no grain-fed meat that does not compete for the grain — the
  loss at that step is real and large, so the choice is a use of
  land, not a matter of taste. And no meat at all from land already
  growing crops, without giving up the crop.
THREAD: money (borrowing to invest against borrowing to consume),
  computing (a cache that saves work against one that adds work),
  waste (a scavenger species converting loss back into value).
ASKED-AS: meat cattle grass fed grain feedlot land livestock efficient goats hillside vegetarian pasture

ESSENCE: no single plant food carries the whole set of protein
  pieces a body cannot build for itself — but a grain's gaps and a
  bean's gaps are different gaps, so nearly every old cuisine on
  earth pairs the two: rice and lentils, tortillas and beans, bread
  and chickpeas, and nobody had to be told why.
ROOT: the body / a builder needs every part on the list, and the
  supply is set by whichever part is scarcest, not by the total.
CANNOT: no diet built on one plant staple staying healthy for long,
  however much of it there is. And no repairing the gap with
  quantity: the missing piece is missing at every meal.
THREAD: building (a store with every material but the fixings),
  engineering (a system limited by its scarcest input, not its
  largest), cooking (why so many traditional pairs are the same pair
  everywhere).
ASKED-AS: rice beans lentils protein vegetarian diet corn tortillas complete meals traditional staples healthy

ESSENCE: bitter is the body's poison alarm, and plants make bitter
  chemicals because they do not wish to be eaten — so a surprising
  share of the world's staples are edible only because somebody
  worked out a treatment: acorns leached, cassava soaked and
  fermented, maize soaked in wood ash, olives cured.
ROOT: the living world / a plant that cannot run defends itself with
  chemistry, and an eater that cannot read labels defends itself with
  disgust.
CANNOT: no eating those staples raw and untreated — the process is
  not seasoning, it is the difference between food and poisoning,
  and the maize treatment also releases a vitamin whose absence
  caused disease wherever the technique was borrowed without it.
THREAD: teaching+learning (a technique kept alive for generations
  with nobody knowing the reason), chemistry (a toxin removed by
  water, heat, acid or time), medicine (dose is what separates a
  medicine from a poison).
ASKED-AS: bitter poison cassava acorns soaking olives cured maize lime raw beans toxic traditional

ESSENCE: cooking is digestion done outside the body — heat unwinds
  proteins and bursts starch so the gut gets far more out of the
  same food for far less work, which means fire did not only make
  food nicer, it raised the calories a human could actually take
  from a day's foraging.
ROOT: cooking / heat unfolds folded proteins one way — an unfolded
  protein and a burst starch grain are simply easier to take apart.
CANNOT: no getting a cooked food's full value from it raw — the
  energy is present and partly unreachable, which is why a raw diet
  needs far more bulk. And no undoing it: the softening is one-way,
  and so is overdoing it.
THREAD: the body (small human jaws and guts beside our relatives' —
  a body that assumes a fire), farming (grinding and soaking as
  cooking's cold cousins), industry (pre-treatment before any
  extraction process).
ASKED-AS: cooking raw food energy calories digest fire chewing softer starch heat nutrition easier

ESSENCE: appetite was tuned for a world where fat, salt and sugar
  were rare and worth going out of your way for — and it does not
  retune when they become cheap and constant, so the pull that once
  kept people alive now pulls in exactly the same direction with
  nothing in front of it.
ROOT: the living world / a preference is shaped by the conditions
  that selected it, and changing conditions does not change the
  preference on any human timescale.
CANNOT: no appetite that reads abundance — the signal that says
  "eat this while you can" cannot know the shelf will be full again
  tomorrow. And no arguing with it directly: the reliable moves
  change what is within reach, not what is wanted.
THREAD: money (saving instincts built for scarce years), the mind
  (attention tuned for rare danger and now farmed by machines),
  farming (a herd that will overgraze good grass because grass was
  never reliably good).
ASKED-AS: craving sugar fat salty snacks overeating junk food willpower diet hungry cheap tempting

ESSENCE: what a place eats is chosen by price per calorie, not by
  taste or by health — the cheap staples win, and as incomes rise
  people trade upward in a fixed order everywhere: first more
  calories, then more variety, then more meat and fat, then back
