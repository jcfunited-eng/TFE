# 132 DEEP FITNESS AND MOVEMENT — training a body
Living machinery holds the law that a body builds what is demanded
and dismantles what is not. This file is what follows from it once
somebody starts making demands on purpose: what each demand buys,
what it costs, and which of the gym's confident rules survive
looking at. Where the evidence is genuinely unsettled it says
UNSURE.

ESSENCE: a body answers the question you actually asked, not the one you
  meant. Ask for endurance and it gives up some force. Ask for a
  movement at one angle and it gets strong at that angle. Every training
  decision is the wording of a question, and the answer is always
  literal.

  asking for it — so the shape of the asking is the shape of what
  arrives.
THREAD: teaching (you get the exam you set, never the understanding you
  hoped for), farming (a crop bred hard for one trait), craft (a hand
  trained on one tool is clumsy with its cousin).

ESSENCE: recovery is not a day off, it is a rate. The body has one

ESSENCE: of everything sold as recovery, sleep is the one with a large

ESSENCE: more training is better, then it is not. The curve rises,
  flattens, and turns down, and where it turns depends on what you are
  asking for. For health most of the benefit arrives at a modest dose;
  for performance the useful dose is far higher and the turn is much
  closer to it.

ESSENCE: the first sign of too much is not soreness. It is that

  answering.

ESSENCE: the first weeks of strength training barely change the muscle.
  What changes is the nervous system's willingness — more fibres called
  at once, called faster, and the opposing muscles told to stop
  fighting. Train one arm and the untrained one gets measurably
  stronger, which is proof enough of where the gain sat.

ESSENCE: strength and size are cousins, not twins. Strength is force
  produced, and it improves with heavy loads, low repetitions and long
  rests. Size is tissue added, and it improves across a wide range of
  loads with more total work. The strongest lifters are not the biggest,
  and the biggest are not the strongest.

ESSENCE: how long the effort lasts decides which supply pays for it.
  Seconds run on what is already in the muscle. A half minute to a
  couple of minutes runs on fast burning that piles up its own waste.
  Anything longer runs on breathing. The three overlap constantly; the
  duration decides the mixture.

ESSENCE: the slow, dull, conversational miles build the parts nobody can

ESSENCE: intervals buy the top of the range and they buy time. Hard
  bouts with recovery between reach the ceiling of what the heart and
  lungs can deliver, which steady work never touches, and they produce a
  large share of the fitness in a small share of the hours. They also
  cost the most to recover from.

ESSENCE: two runners with identical engines can finish minutes apart,
  because one wastes less. Economy is how much fuel a given speed costs
  — bouncing, braking, gripping, fighting yourself — and in trained
  athletes it improves long after the engine has stopped improving.

ESSENCE: heart rate is a poor dial to steer by. It drifts upward through

  differently on caffeine or a drug. It measures the cost, and it
  measures it late.

ESSENCE: flexibility is how far a joint can be pushed. Mobility is how
  far it can be taken and controlled under your own power. They are
  different measurements, and the gap between them is where most trouble
  lives — a range you own passively and cannot command is not available
  when it is needed.

ESSENCE: static stretching does less than it is credited with. It does
  not prevent injury in the trials that looked, does not reduce next-day
  soreness, and briefly reduces power if done just before an effort. The
  range it adds seems to come largely from tolerating the stretch rather
  than lengthening the tissue. UNSURE at the mechanism, firm at the
  outcomes.

ESSENCE: posture predicts pain far more weakly than everyone expects.

  reliably help. The load and its duration matter; the shape matters
  less.

ESSENCE: hurting is not the same as harming. Some pain in training is

  moderate level, does not climb during the session, and is gone by the
  next morning is generally tolerable.

ESSENCE: for most injured tissue, load is the medicine and rest is the

  The dose is graded, not absent.

ESSENCE: injuries cluster where the load jumped, not where it was

ESSENCE: a warm-up does several small real things — raises tissue

ESSENCE: doing one thing exclusively loads one set of tissues in one

ESSENCE: fitness leaves at different speeds. Endurance falls first and

  layoff comes back far faster than it was built the first time, because
  some of the machinery was kept.
