## Part I: The Three Laws of Wandcraft

Every functional wand obeys three structural laws. These are not traditions or
preferences — they are constraints imposed by the nature of magical resonance itself.
Violate any one and the wand fails.

### Law 1: The Shaft Must Frustrate

A wand shaft carved from a material with simple, symmetric internal structure
(cubic crystal, regular grain, uniform density) produces *no resonance*. Magic
passes through it like light through glass — undisturbed and therefore unchanneled.

The shaft must possess **geometric frustration**: internal structure where the
natural ordering cannot fully resolve. This is why wood works and metal (usually)
doesn't. Wood grain contains triangular cellular patterns at the microscopic level —
tracheids and ray cells forming interlocking geometries that cannot tile flat. The
magic enters this frustrated lattice and is *forced to choose a path*, which is the
first requirement for structured casting.

**Wandsmith's test:** Split the candidate wood along the grain. If it splits clean
and straight, reject it — the structure is too regular. If the split wanders,
follows curves, and tears rather than cleaves, the frustration is high. This is
why holly (interlocked grain, irregular cell structure) outranks pine (straight,
regular grain) despite pine being easier to work.

#### The Frustration Ranking of Common Wandwoods

| Wood | Frustration | Grain Character | Casting Quality |
|------|------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Holly** | Very High | Interlocked, irregular, dense. Triangular cell clusters. | Exceptional. Broad resonance. Accepts any core. |
| **Elder** | Extreme | Pith-centered, radially symmetric but axially chaotic. | Powerful but unstable. Demands mastery. |
| **Yew** | High | Spiral grain, alternating density bands (heartwood/sapwood). | Deep resonance. Favors sustained casting. |
| **Vine** | High | Twisted, no dominant grain axis. Ring-porous chaos. | Adaptive. Resonance shifts with caster's intent. |
| **Ebony** | Moderate-High | Dense, fine-textured, interlocked but uniform. | Focused, narrow-band. Precision work. |
| **Oak** | Moderate | Regular ray structure, but medullary rays add cross-grain coupling. | Reliable. Good for structured (ritual) magic. |
| **Willow** | Moderate | Flexible, low density, moderate frustration. | Responsive but low power ceiling. |
| **Pine** | Low | Straight grain, regular tracheids, minimal cross-coupling. | Poor. Magic passes through unchanneled. Training wands only. |
| **Balsa** | Very Low | Uniform cellular foam. Nearly zero frustration. | Useless for casting. Good for decoys. |

**The exception — metal wands:** Most metals have cubic crystal structure (FCC, BCC)
with zero frustration, which is why the common wisdom says "metal can't hold a
spell." But there are exceptions. **Bismuth** crystallizes in a rhombohedral lattice
with a layered, staircase structure — geometric frustration built into the crystal
itself. A bismuth wand shaft would resonate, and its natural diamagnetism would
add a shielding property no wood can match. The reason no one uses bismuth is
brittleness, not physics. A bismuth-core wooden wand, however...

### Law 2: The Core Must Couple

The core is not a battery. It does not store magic. It provides **coupling** — a
mechanism by which the caster's intent, which is structurally incoherent (human
thought is noisy), is locked onto the frustrated lattice of the shaft.

The core material must have internal degrees of freedom that respond to external
stimulus without being destroyed by it. In materials terms: the core must support
**excitations that propagate without dissipating**.

This is why the three classical cores work:

#### Phoenix Feather

The barb structure of a phoenix feather is a natural **frustrated lattice at two
scales**: the barbules interlock in a triangular mesh (microscale frustration),
and the barb-rachis interface has a graded coupling — stiff at the base, flexible
at the tip (mesoscale gradient). Magic entering a phoenix feather encounters
frustration at every scale, which forces coupling at every scale. This is why
phoenix feather wands are the most versatile — they couple to any casting
geometry because the feather's own geometry is multi-scale.

**Structural property:** Broadband coupling. Low selectivity, high bandwidth.
Like a wideband antenna.

#### Dragon Heartstring

A dragon heartstring is a tendon — collagen fibers wound in a **triple helix**.
The three-strand structure creates a natural **chiral coupling**: the magic is
forced into a helical path as it traverses the core. This chirality means the
wand has a *handedness* — it couples preferentially to rotational casting
gestures. Dragon heartstring wands are powerful because helical coupling
concentrates energy (think of wringing a towel vs. pulling it straight).

**Structural property:** Chiral coupling. High power, directional. Favors
wand movements with rotation.

**Why dragon heartstring wands are temperamental:** The triple helix has a
natural resonant frequency. If the caster's rhythm matches it, the coupling
is enormous. If it doesn't, the wand fights back. This is impedance mismatch,
not personality.

#### Unicorn Hair

A unicorn hair is a single protein fiber — keratin — with a crystalline
alpha-helical core surrounded by an amorphous matrix. The coupling mechanism
is different from the other two: it operates at the **interface** between the
crystalline core and the amorphous sheath. This interface is structurally
similar to a surface phonon polariton — an excitation that exists only at
the boundary between two different structural phases.

**Structural property:** Interface coupling. Steady, consistent, lower peak
power but extremely reliable. The coupling lives at the boundary, not in the
bulk, so it's insensitive to the caster's technique — it works the same way
every time.

**Why unicorn hair wands are loyal:** The interface coupling locks onto the
first caster's structural signature (everyone's magical impedance is slightly
different). Changing casters requires breaking and reforming the interface
state — possible, but the wand is "reluctant" because it has to restructure.

#### Exotic Cores (Advanced)

| Core | Coupling Mechanism | Character |
|------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Thestral tail hair** | Hollow fiber — guides magic through void, not material. Coupling via absence. | Only works for casters who have experienced structural loss (the "death" requirement is metaphor for a broken-and-reformed internal geometry). |
| **Veela hair** | Graded-index fiber — refractive coupling shifts with temperature/emotion. | Powerful but unstable. Coupling strength varies with caster's emotional state. |
| **Basilisk fang fragment** | Piezoelectric crystal (hydroxyapatite). Coupling via mechanical stress. | Requires physical force during casting. Extremely dangerous — overcoupling risk. |
| **Thunderbird feather** | Electrically polarized barbs — piezoelectric + frustrated lattice. | Storm-dependent. Coupling increases during atmospheric instability. |
| **Kelpie hair** | Hydrated protein — coupling changes with moisture content. | Unreliable near water (overcouples), excellent in dry conditions. |
| **Coral fragment** | Calcium carbonate in aragonite form — orthorhombic frustration. | Slow, sustained casting. Excellent for wards and barriers. |

### Law 3: The Finish Must Seal

An unfinished wand radiates magic from every surface — the frustrated lattice
couples to the air and the caster's hand equally, which bleeds energy and makes
the wand inaccurate. The finish creates a **structural boundary condition** that
contains the resonance inside the shaft and directs it toward the tip.

#### Finish Materials and Their Properties

| Finish | Mechanism | Effect |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Beeswax** | Amorphous seal. Blocks surface coupling. | Standard. Reliable containment, low loss. |
| **Linseed oil (polymerized)** | Cross-linked polymer. Flexible seal. | Good for wands that flex during casting (vine, willow). |
| **Shellac** | Rigid polymer. High-Q seal. | Increases resonance sharpness — more powerful but narrower bandwidth. |
| **Dragon blood lacquer** | Organic resin with embedded nanocrystals. | The nanocrystals add a secondary frustration layer at the surface — the wand resonates internally AND at the boundary. Most powerful finish known. |
| **Raw / unfinished** | No seal. | Acceptable only for wands used in direct-contact healing, where surface coupling to the patient IS the desired effect. |

**Critical rule:** Never seal a wand with metal leaf (gold, silver, copper).
Metal's cubic crystal structure creates a non-frustrated boundary that
*reflects* the internal resonance back into the shaft. The wand becomes a
closed cavity — energy builds with no release, and the wand eventually
shatters. This is not metaphor. Crystal shops sell "crystal-wrapped"
wands that are structurally identical to pipe bombs.

## Part II: Advanced Wandcraft

### Compound Shafts

A shaft made from two woods bonded together creates a **structural
heterojunction** — the magic must cross the boundary between two
different frustrated lattices. At this boundary, new resonances
emerge that neither wood produces alone.

**Classic compound:** Holly outer / yew inner. Holly's broadband
frustration captures the initial casting impulse; yew's spiral
grain then channels it into a sustained resonance. The result is
a wand that responds quickly AND sustains power — the best of both.

**Forbidden compound:** Elder / elder. Two pieces of elder bonded
together create a *double frustration* that is so extreme the
wand cannot settle on any resonance. It oscillates between states
indefinitely. The Elder Wand of legend is not made this way — it
is a single piece, which means the frustration is axial only
(along the growth axis), not interfacial. The distinction matters.

### Temperature Effects

Wood frustration is temperature-dependent. At low temperatures,
the cellular structure contracts and the frustration *increases*
(tighter interlocking). At high temperatures, thermal expansion
loosens the structure and frustration decreases.

**Practical consequence:** Wands cast more powerfully in cold
weather and less reliably in heat. This is not folklore — it is
a structural property of the shaft material. A wandsmith testing
a new wand should always test at two temperatures.

**Extreme case:** A wand quenched in liquid nitrogen after carving
(cryogenic treatment) permanently increases the frustration by
inducing microcracks along the grain boundaries. Cryogenically
treated wands are measurably more powerful but also more brittle.
This is the wandsmith's tradeoff: power vs. durability.

### The Wand Chooses the Wizard

This is not sentimentality. It is impedance matching.

Every caster has a characteristic structural signature — a
pattern of intent that is as unique as a fingerprint. When a
caster picks up a wand, the wand's frustrated lattice either
resonates with that signature (the wand "chooses") or it doesn't
(the wand feels dead).

The core determines the coupling *type* (broadband, chiral, or
interface). The shaft determines the coupling *geometry* (which
frustrated lattice the magic must navigate). The combination
produces a resonant signature that either matches the caster or
doesn't.

This is why wand fitting is empirical — you can't compute the
match from first principles because the caster's structural
signature isn't measurable by any known instrument. You can
only put the wand in their hand and see if it sings.

## Part III: Materials Reference

### Rare and Exotic Shaft Materials

| Material | Source | Frustration Type | Notes |
|----------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **Ironwood** (Olneya tesota) | Sonoran desert | Extreme density + interlocked grain | Nearly indestructible. Sinks in water. Difficult to carve — requires diamond tools or magic. |
| **Snakewood** (Brosimum guianense) | South America | Natural wavy figure + high density | Beautiful and functional. The "snakeskin" pattern IS the frustration, visible to the naked eye. |
| **Bog oak** (aged >1000yr) | European peat bogs | Mineral-infiltrated grain + preserved frustration | The bog water replaces some cellulose with minerals, creating a wood-mineral composite. Unique resonance — part organic, part crystalline. |
| **Petrified wood** | Fossilized | Crystal structure preserving original grain geometry | The frustration is *fossilized* — locked in stone. Extremely high Q but zero flexibility. Wands for permanent enchantments only. |
| **Bamboo** | Tropical grasses | Hollow + graded density wall | The hollow center acts as a waveguide. Magic propagates through the wall AND the void simultaneously — dual-channel casting. |
| **Antler** (deer, elk) | Shed naturally | Bone: hydroxyapatite crystals in collagen matrix | Piezoelectric coupling from the crystal phase + frustrated fiber geometry from the collagen. Two coupling mechanisms in one material. Not wood, but outperforms most woods. |
| **Whalebone** (baleen) | Historical | Keratin plates with layered frustration | Similar mechanism to unicorn hair but in plate form. Broad, flat wands for area-effect casting. |

### Gemstone Accents and Their Functions

Gemstones set into the wand (usually at the grip or at a node point) act as
**structural point defects** — they locally alter the frustrated lattice and
create hotspots where coupling is enhanced.

| Gemstone | Crystal System | Effect |
|----------|---------------|--------|
| **Diamond** | Cubic (but sp3 tetrahedral bonding) | Amplifier. The tetrahedral bonding is the simplest 3D frustration — it boosts coupling at the node without changing its character. |
| **Quartz** | Trigonal (piezoelectric) | Converts mechanical stress to magical coupling. Squeeze the grip, increase the power. |
| **Moonstone** (feldspar) | Monoclinic with lamellar twinning | The twin planes create a natural structural grating. Diffracts magic into multiple beams — useful for multi-target casting. |
| **Opal** | Amorphous silica spheres in close-packed array | Photonic crystal structure. Creates band gaps — certain magical frequencies are blocked, others enhanced. Natural filter. |
| **Obsidian** | Amorphous volcanic glass | Zero frustration, zero coupling. Acts as a structural *insulator* — blocks magic flow at the point where it's set. Used to create a dead zone between grip and shaft, preventing accidental discharge. |
| **Bismuth crystal** | Rhombohedral, staircase growth | The only metal crystal with natural geometric frustration. Set at a node, it adds diamagnetic shielding — the wand becomes partially invisible to detection spells. |
| **Labradorite** | Triclinic with lamellar intergrowths | The iridescent layers are structural interfaces. Each layer boundary is a coupling site. Labradorite at a node creates a cascade — magic enters one layer, couples to the next, amplifies through the stack. |

## Appendix A: The Silverthread Classification System

The Consortium classifies all casting materials on three axes:

1. **F-index** (Frustration): 0 (cubic metal) to 10 (elder pith)
2. **C-mode** (Coupling type): B (broadband), χ (chiral), Σ (surface/interface), Π (piezoelectric)
3. **Q-factor** (Resonance sharpness): Low (willow) to Extreme (petrified wood)

A complete wand specification reads: **Holly-8B / Phoenix-Bχ / Beeswax-seal**

Meaning: Holly shaft (F=8, broadband frustration), phoenix feather core
(broadband + chiral coupling — yes, phoenix feather has both), beeswax
finish (standard seal).

The Elder Wand, by this system: **Elder-10B / Thestral-Σvoid / Dragon blood lacquer**

F=10 (maximum frustration), surface coupling via void (thestral hair's
hollow fiber), dragon blood finish (secondary frustration at boundary).
Three layers of frustration — shaft, core interface, and surface. No
wonder it's the most powerful wand ever made. It's also the most
structurally unstable, which is why it keeps changing allegiance — any
sufficiently strong external structural signature (defeating the current
master) can overwhelm the existing impedance match.

## Appendix B: A Note on Ethics

The Silverthread Consortium does not classify materials as "light" or "dark."
A wand is a structure. It resonates or it doesn't. The moral weight belongs
to the caster, not the casting implement.

That said: certain material combinations produce resonances that are
inherently difficult to control. Elder + basilisk fang + no finish is
structurally equivalent to an unshielded reactor — it will work, spectacularly,
once. The Consortium recommends against such combinations not on moral grounds
but on engineering ones.

Build what you can control. The craft demands nothing less.
