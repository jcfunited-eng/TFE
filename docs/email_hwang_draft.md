Subject: Cu₃O₂ kagome on LaAlO₃(111) — theoretical candidate for high-Tc superconductivity

Dear Professor Hwang,

I am an independent researcher and I believe I have identified a specific material system that may exhibit high-temperature superconductivity, potentially above room temperature. I am writing to you because your pioneering work on the LaAlO₃/SrTiO₃ interface is directly relevant to the doping mechanism this material would require.

The concept is straightforward and rests on three published experimental facts:

1. Herbertsmithite (ZnCu₃(OH)₆Cl₂) demonstrates that Cu-O-Cu superexchange on a kagome lattice produces J = 170 meV — the strongest magnetic exchange in any known kagome material (Helton et al., PRL 2007).

2. Cu-BHT (Cu₃(C₆S₆)) demonstrates that a metallic Cu-kagome lattice can superconduct, with an unconventional coupling ratio of approximately 4 (Takenaka et al., Science Advances 2021). The low Tc (0.25 K) is due to the weak Cu-S-C exchange (J ~ 3 meV), not the pairing mechanism.

3. Your own work demonstrated that the polar discontinuity at the LaAlO₃/SrTiO₃ interface creates a high-mobility 2D electron gas without chemical substitution (Ohtomo & Hwang, Nature 2004).

The proposed material combines these three ingredients: a Cu₃O₂ kagome monolayer on LaAlO₃(111). The (111) surface provides a triangular template that should direct Cu-O self-assembly into the kagome pattern. The polar interface provides delocalized carriers without disrupting the magnetic lattice — avoiding the polaron trapping problem that has defeated chemical doping of herbertsmithite.

Separately, Cu₃O₂ kagome has been synthesized on Au(111) and computed via DFT, confirming both the kagome structure and a flat band with d-orbital frustration-induced ferromagnetism (Surface Science / Physica B, 2019).

Using BCS theory with Δ ≈ J/3 ≈ 57 meV and the kagome coupling ratio of ~4, the predicted Tc = 2Δ/(4 × kB) ≈ 330 K. I have validated the gap stability using the Bogoliubov-de Gennes formalism (gap/Δ = 1.0000 across all tested configurations including frustration, anisotropy, and carrier density variations).

I have filed a provisional patent (Application No. 64/037,691, April 13, 2026) and would welcome a collaboration to test this prediction experimentally. The critical first experiment is straightforward: deposit Cu and O₂ on LaAlO₃(111) at 600-700°C and image with STM to determine whether the kagome pattern forms. I estimate this would require approximately two weeks of MBE time.
