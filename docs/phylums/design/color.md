## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- THE STRANGER — the person who has never seen it: the absent half
  of every design, acting only on what the thing itself says, with
  an attention that is a fixed budget everything present spends;
  also: people, teaching, mind.
- CONTRAST — difference, the only thing the eye reports; visibility
  is bought from the field, not from the mark; also: mind, music
  (the quiet bar makes the loud one), survival (camouflage kills
  the differences to kill the sighting).
- HIERARCHY — the order of arrival: the eye lands first, second,
  then gives up; there is exactly one first place to give away;
  also: story (the first sentence), governance.
- EMPTY SPACE — an active ingredient: the nothing around a thing
  gives it shape, air, and weight; margins carry load; also: music
  (the rest is a note), building (the room is bought for its
  hollow), language (silence lands the sentence).
- THE GRID — the hidden order freedom rides on: edges align,
  spacings repeat, sizes come in a few fixed steps, felt even when
  never seen; also: building (masonry courses), music (the beat
  under syncopation), language (meter under free lines).
- PROPORTION — a family of related sizes, one rule dividing the
  whole, the parts, and the details; also: mathematics, music (one
  key relates every chord), building.
- COLOR'S THREE DIALS — which color, how pure, how light; how-light
  does the heavy lifting; also: physics (light), body (the eye's
  channels), craft.
- LETTERS AND LABELS — the word on the button is the interface;
  people act on what they think the label means; also: language,
  law (the clause heading decides what was agreed).
- THE SHAPE'S PROMISE — a handle asks to be pulled, a slot asks for
  a card, a flat plate asks to be pushed (the trade's word is
  affordance); also: craft (a grip that teaches the hold), building
  (a door that says which way it swings), body.
- THE CONSTRAINT — the shape that excludes the error: a plug that
  fits one way needs no warning; also: engineering, medicine (a
  syringe that cannot fit the wrong port).
- THE DEFAULT — the choice made for everyone who makes none, which
  is most people; also: governance, law (the opt-out that decides
  participation), farming (the seed rate printed on the drill).
- THE STORY IN THE HEAD — the model every user invents from what
  they can see and then acts on; usually wrong, always used; also:
  mind, teaching, medicine (the patient's theory of the illness).
- THE CONVENTION — the platform's way, worth exactly as much as the
  number of people who share it; also: people, language, transport
  (a country's side of the road), measurement.
- ORNAMENT — decoration doing a job: marking where materials meet,
  taking wear at a corner, showing which door matters, carrying
  rank and information; also: culture, building, governance.
- MATERIAL AND ITS REFUSALS — what a medium forbids gives the form
  its character: watercolor cannot be undone, marble can only be
  taken away, film cost money by the second; also: craft, building.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- Attention goes where difference is, not where importance is;
  importance must be dressed as difference or go unseen. A work is
  a guided walk — the route authored or accidental.
- Visibility is bought from the field: a whisper in a silent room
  beats a shout in a crowd. A dominant needs elements built to
  lose — the usual failure is too few servants, not too many stars
  — and there is one first place; two firsts is none.
- No tone or color is judged alone: the same gray reads warm on
  blue, cool on orange, bright on black. Colors belong together
  when something is common to all — one light, one gray stirred
  through every mixture.
- What fails in gray fails in color: lightness is the skeleton. A
  strong image survives reduction to three or four flat tones
  because its maker enforced tone families; and the gaps between
  things are a second drawing, made whether meant or not.
- Freedom rides a hidden grid: alignment reads as intention,
  near-alignment as error — the same, or clearly different.
- Simplicity is bought by subtraction: remove everything whose
  absence does not break the message, stop one cut before it
  breaks. Condition: what looks removable is often a signal doing
  a quiet job (the mirror holds the purge that proved it).
- The shape tells the stranger what can be done to it; where it
  lies — a pull handle on a push door — people fail and think it
  their fault. What a thing can do but does not show is absent.
- A control belongs where its effect is and moves as the effect
  moves; matched layouts need no learning, mismatched ones are
  learned wrong for years.
- Every act needs an answer inside the attention window; silence
  reads as failure, never delay — the double press is the design's
  output. A physical control should confirm itself in the hand (a
  click, a detent), so the eyes never leave their work.
- A slip did the wrong thing meaning the right one (the hand went
  to the old place); a mistake did what was meant, having meant
  wrong. Warnings fix neither. A mode nobody can see is a trap
  with a delay — the more practiced, the faster they walk in.
- Undo beats care: a thing safe to explore is learned by using,
  the only way anybody learns anything.
- Defaults decide the majority: the default reads as a
  recommendation and changing it costs a decision; whoever sets it
  designs the behavior of most people.
- Consistency beats local best — learned once, not once per screen
  — and the platform's way beats your better way: the stranger
  knows the platform and spends minutes with you. The same law
  preserves mistakes (the mirror holds that side).
- Recognition beats recall: shown options, people choose well;
  asked to remember, they fail. Every recall demand is the
  design's work moved onto the user.
- You are not the user — arithmetic, not humility: knowledge
  cannot be un-had, so the one judgment a maker cannot make is
  whether the thing is obvious. And watch what people do: what
  they say they do is a story assembled afterwards.
- A thing that looks well made is judged to work better and
  forgiven more — visible care read as evidence of invisible care,
  usually rightly; the same reading can hide a bad design from
  everyone including its makers.
- Design has a job and can therefore be wrong — a chair that hurts
  is a bad chair whatever it looks like; decoration has no job and
  can only be liked. Form must not lie about use; but use alone
  does not pick the form — convention, meaning, and the material's
  refusals finish the choice.
- Everybody is disabled sometimes — one arm holding a child,
  glare, sixty years old; the wheelchair's ramp is used by prams,
  trolleys, and suitcases.
- Effort decides behavior more reliably than intention: whoever
  allocates the effort allocates the outcome. A pause before a
  permanent act serves the user; the easy signup with the buried
  cancel is the same tool aimed at them.
- New work is old parts in an arrangement nobody tried; style is
  what a maker refuses, visible only across works. And whoever
  pays shapes the form — canvases the size of walls somebody
  owned, songs the length of the disc.

## SCIENCE
The math and physiology of design, held — not mentioned. Each piece
is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it can follow, and where
it earns it, a WORKED case proving the rule runs.

### THE EYE — contrast arithmetic

LAW: legibility is a ratio of lights, not a feeling.
  contrast = (lighter + 0.05) ÷ (darker + 0.05), each side its
  share of white's light (white = 1.0, black = 0.0). The
  accessibility trade's measured floors: 4.5 for body text, 3.0
  for large type.
RULE: take each side's share of white's light, add 0.05 to each,
  divide lighter by darker, compare to the floor.
WORKED: black on white: 1.05 ÷ 0.05 = 21 — the ceiling. A
  fashionable pale gray at 0.32 of white's light, on white:
  1.05 ÷ 0.37 ≈ 2.8 — under the floor; calm to a young designer on
  a good screen, gone for an older reader in sunlight — and the
  reader does not complain, they leave. A dark gray at 0.10:
  1.05 ÷ 0.15 = 7.0 — passes with room for glare.

LAW: the eye weighs the dials unequally: of a color's share of
  white's light, green carries ~72%, red ~21%, blue ~7%.
WORKED: pure yellow (red + green) = 0.21 + 0.72 = 0.93; pure blue
  = 0.07. On black: yellow reads at 0.98 ÷ 0.05 ≈ 19.6 — nearly
  black-on-white; blue at 0.12 ÷ 0.05 = 2.4 — a failing pair at
  full purity. Two "full strength" colors, thirteenfold apart on
  the one channel that carries legibility: why pictures are tested
  in gray.

LAW: one strong difference is found in parallel — flat time however
  large the crowd (~a quarter second); a difference needing two
  features combined is hunted item by item (~25 thousandths of a
  second each, half the crowd on average).
RULE: give the first thing a single strong difference on several
  dials pointing the same way; never ask the eye to combine.
WORKED — what makes one thing read first: a poster of forty items.
  One item at 2.5× size, darkest tone, four times the empty space:
  every dial votes together — found in ~0.25 s regardless of the
  thirty-nine. Findable only as "large AND red" among large-only
  and red-only decoys: 0.25 + (40 × 0.025) ÷ 2 = 0.75 s — three
  times slower, and it feels like hunting. Three items boosted is
  no first at all; the landing falls to habit (top-left).

LAW: the eye detects edge misalignment ~10× finer than it resolves
  detail — the detector trips near 0.02 mm at reading distance, so
  a half-millimeter miss is ~25× threshold (0.5 ÷ 0.02 = 25). The
  grid is cheap order: the instrument convicting sloppiness works
  free in every viewer.

### SEEING AT SIZE AND DISTANCE

LAW: needed letter size = distance × angle. The bare floor
  (eye-chart conditions) is ~0.0015 of the distance; the sign
  trade's working rule ~0.008 — about 2.5 cm of letter per 3 m — a
  fivefold margin paid for motion, glare, age, low contrast.
WORKED: a title read across a 6 m room: floor = 6 × 0.0015 ≈ 9 mm
  (can be read, by someone trying); working = 6 ÷ 3 × 2.5 = 5 cm
  (is read). The gap between 9 mm and 5 cm is the gap between
  legible and used.
RULE: shrinking is renting distance — squint, or shrink the design
  to a stamp; detail is the first thing distance takes, and what
  survives is the large light-dark masses. Enforce three or four
  tone families and assign every part to one.

LAW: reading moves in jumps of ~7-9 letters; the sweep back to the
  next line is aimed from memory of the left margin and starts
  missing lines past ~75 letters. RULE: body text at 45-75 letters
  per line.

### THE HAND

LAW: time to reach a target grows with the log of distance over
  size: steps = log₂(distance ÷ size + 1), roughly 0.1 s per step.
  Bigger and nearer are the same sentence read backwards.
RULE: constant-use controls large and close; the one that should
  rarely be pressed, small and far — the law prices both.
WORKED: a 32 cm reach to a 1 cm target: log₂(33) ≈ 5.0 steps.
  Widen to 4 cm: log₂(9) ≈ 3.2 — about 0.19 s saved on every aim,
  forever; moving it to 8 cm at 1 cm wide (log₂(9)) buys exactly
  the same. A screen's edge stops the pointer, making corners
  bottomless targets — the fastest pixels on any screen.
- The fingertip flattens to ~1 cm and hides the target at the
  moment of touch. RULE: touch targets ≥ 1 cm, spaced so a near
  miss is not a disaster, the answer appearing from outside the
  fingertip. A pointer is a needle, a finger a blunt object; one
  layout cannot serve both.

### PROPORTION ARITHMETIC

LAW: an off-center split makes a dominant space and a servant
  space; a centered split makes two equal rivals. Thirds is the
  workable coarse cut.
WORKED: a 30 × 20 cm frame: vertical lines at 10 and 20 cm,
  horizontal at 6.7 and 13.3; subject on a line or crossing,
  facing the larger space. Held honestly: a rule of convenience,
  not an optimum — receipts favor off-center over centered and
  crown no exact fraction.

LAW: the golden ratio's true algebra. φ = (1+√5) ÷ 2 = 1.6180...;
  its square is 2.6180... (φ² = φ + 1), its reciprocal 0.6180...
  (1 ÷ φ = φ − 1). The only rectangle that loses a square and
  keeps its shape: 1.618 × 1 minus 1 × 1 leaves 0.618 × 1, and
  1 ÷ 0.618 = 1.618 — it nests without end; neighbor Fibonacci
  numbers close on it (8/5 = 1.600, 13/8 = 1.625, 21/13 ≈ 1.615).
  RECEIPTS EXIST for the algebra and the plant world's spirals;
  the beauty claims are MYTH (the mirror holds the record).
RULE (the honest one): a plain ratio repeated everywhere beats a
  famous ratio used once; proportion settles when ONE rule divides
  the whole, the parts, and the details.

LAW: a size family is a base times a ratio, step by step.
WORKED: base 16, ratio 1.25: 16 → 20 → 25 → 31.25 → 39.06 (16 ×
  1.25 = 20; 20 × 1.25 = 25; 25 × 1.25 = 31.25). Five sizes, one
  rule; every pair related, and the eye finds the pattern unasked.
  Spacing likewise in multiples of one unit; two grids in one work
  is no grid.

### THE CLOCK

LAW: delay is three things. Under ~0.1 s the result feels caused
  by the act — your own hand. Up to ~1 s the thread of thought
  holds. Past ~10 s the person has left, mentally, and returns
  needing to be told where they were.
RULE: over 1 s, show a sign at once; over 10 s, honest progress,
  allow leaving, keep their place. A progress sign that lies to
  look better spends its accuracy record — the only thing it had.
WORKED: a save taking 3 s with no sign: the press is repeated
  (silence reads as failure) and a second copy is made — the whole
  failure priced by one missing sign inside the 1 s window.

LAW: choosing among ordered options costs by the log; among
  unordered ones, by the count.
WORKED: 8 well-ordered options vs 64: log₂(9) ≈ 3.2 against
  log₂(65) ≈ 6.0 — eight times the options, under twice the time.
  Unordered, the reader scans half the list: 4 items against 32 —
  eight times the time, and past a screenful the true behavior is
  abandonment or take-the-top. The ordering, not the range, is the
  real decision.

### THE TESTING ARITHMETIC

LAW: watchers find shares of the same faults, because the faults
  are properties of the design. A fault one watcher surfaces with
  chance p escapes n watchers with chance (1−p)ⁿ; the measured
  average for a stranger session is p ≈ 0.31.
RULE: test with five strangers, fix what they hit, test five more
  — the second five meet the design that now exists.
WORKED: escape for five: 0.69² = 0.476; ×0.69 = 0.329; ×0.69 =
  0.227; ×0.69 = 0.156 — about 16 faults in 100 escape all five,
  ~84% found; the fifth session is mostly confirmation.

LAW: a comparison between two versions can only find the better of
  the two; run enough and you climb the nearest small hill, never
  once offered the mountain.
RULE: measure to refine what exists; originate by making, then
  measure the made things against each other.

LAW: translated text is a different shape, not a substitution —
  paragraphs grow ~⅓, single words up to double ("Save" →
  "Speichern", 4 letters to 9), some languages run the other way.
RULE: a third of slack in every container that holds words; a
  layout with none is broken before the translator goes home.

## METHODS
How things are done here — each with what governs it and what
breaks it. The prepositioned how-to-build.

- SUBTRACTION (governed by the attention budget): remove everything
  whose absence does not break the message; stop one cut before it
  breaks. Breaks when the cut thing was a signal doing a quiet job
  — the shadow that said "pressable."
- BUILDING THE READ ORDER: choose the one thing that reads first;
  give it converging differences; hold all else down ON EVERY DIAL
  — servants built to lose. Check with the squint. Breaks when
  everything is polished to full strength and nothing is left to
  be the ground.
- THE GRID: pick a unit and a ratio; sizes from the ladder, spaces
  in multiples, edges aligned exactly. Breaks when two grids share
  one work or near-alignment survives to print.
- MAKING IT STRANGE (governed by maker blindness — familiarity
  removes exactly the freshness being judged): the mirror, the
  week in a drawer, reading aloud, the stranger's face. One
  method: borrow the eyes of someone who wasn't there.
- SHAPING THE ERROR OUT: the plug that fits one way, the form that
  cannot take a date in the past. First sort the failure: a slip
  is repaired by position, spacing, distinctness; a mistake by
  fixing the story the design causes. Warnings repair neither.
- SETTING DEFAULTS ON PURPOSE: the default is the design for the
  majority; every switch added instead is an argument nobody
  settled, shipped to the person with the least information and
  least interest.
- SHOWING FEW, KEEPING THE REST ONE STEP AWAY: everyday tools out
  on the bench, the rest findable in a drawer. The beginner is
  confused by everything-at-once, not by power; the expert is
  served by the extras existing, not by their absence.
- THE CONSISTENCY AUDIT: the same act, the same word, the same
  place, everywhere; adopt the platform's way unless the local
  gain outweighs relearning charged to every stranger.
- WATCHING FIVE, FIXING, WATCHING FIVE: strangers, real tasks, no
  coaching; watch the hands, not the opinions. The prototype is a
  question built cheap — polish only as much as an honest answer
  needs, because polish buys attachment and attachment argues with
  the answer; then throw it away.
- GENERATING THEN SELECTING: make far more than will be kept — the
  first ideas are the common ones, spent to get past; quality is
  set by the ratio of made to kept. Mind the piece hardest to cut:
  the attachment records what it cost YOU — a fact about your
  history, not the work.
- THE EDGE-CASE WALK: walk the design past the name with no
  surname, the address with no postcode, the date before the
  system existed. Every rule about how people are shaped excludes
  somebody, who meets it as a locked door with no appeal.
- WRITING THE LABEL: name things in the stranger's vocabulary,
  never the organization's; an icon without a word is a guess —
  pair them. Group by the user's picture of the world, not the org
  chart; the failed searches are an inventory of the structure's
  mistakes.
- DESIGNING THE WHOLE LIFE: how it opens, cleans, repairs, and is
  thrown away is decided by the same pencil that decided its face;
  a glued seam is a decision about the object's old age.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE GRID AND THE SIZE LADDER: one unit, one ratio, few steps —
  order the eye finds for free.
- THE GRAY COPY: the cheapest honest instrument — strips the
  dials, leaves the skeleton every design must have.
- THE STRANGER: the only instrument that can be confused; five
  find ~84% of what is wrong. Cheap, replaceable, decisive.
- PAPER BEFORE PIXELS: the cheapest prototype that can still be
  wrong; costs minutes, answers what polish would hide.
- THE PATTERN LIBRARY AND PLATFORM CONVENTIONS: pre-learned
  material — every borrowed pattern arrives already taught.
- THE FINGERTIP AND THE ARM'S REACH: ~1 cm of blunt contact and
  the log-priced reach — the body as the unit system of every
  layout meant for hands.
- THE CONTRAST ARITHMETIC: the ratio-of-lights law as a checkable
  number; run it, don't eyeball it — young eyes on good screens
  are the wrong instrument.
- UNDO: the material safety is made of — converts mistakes into
  lessons; a thing safe to explore teaches itself.
- MOTION: the explanation that costs no words — a panel that
  slides shows where it went; one that blinks in and out is a
  fresh puzzle each time. Spend it carefully: a moving thing beats
  every other dial for the eye.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- BE USABLE WITHOUT TEACHING: the door that needs no sign, the
  grip that teaches the hold — the maker's knowledge built into
  the shape because the maker is not in the room.
- MAKE THE IMPORTANT READ FIRST: spend the one first place on
  purpose; an accidental route is an accidental meaning.
- MAKE THE WRONG ACT IMPOSSIBLE OR CHEAP: constraint first, undo
  second, warning a distant third.
- MAKE MANY PARTS ONE SYSTEM: learn once, use everywhere.
- INCLUDE EVERYONE IT WILL MEET: the tired, the old, the gloved,
  the glared-at, the color-blind, the person the categories
  forgot.
- MOVE SOMEONE: awe from size, intimacy from smallness, trust from
  visible care — persuasion and delight are jobs, with means.
- SAY WHO MADE IT AND FOR WHOM: identity, rank, and belonging in
  ornament and style — decoration doing government.
- KEEP IT ALIVE: repairable, cleanable, worth handing on.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- The craft era: maker and user were neighbors, and form was tuned
  by generations of small corrections — the scythe, the oar, the
  barn door. No manuals: the teaching was a person, and failed
  shapes died with their season.
- Industry split maker from user, and design as a subject was born
  in the gap: the shape now had to carry everything the absent
  maker knew. The manual was born the same day — the written
  confession of what the shape failed to say.
- Ornament's purges and returns, run over and over: plainness
  preached on moral grounds (one famous essay called ornament a
  crime), then ornament creeping back — it was doing jobs the
  purge never inventoried: marking joints, taking wear, showing
  rank. The latest round ran on screens: flat design stripped the
  shadows and with them the signal for "pressable"; uncertainty
  rose, the shadows crept back within a decade. Stripped on
  principle, returned by job — the fuller ledger is in the mirror.
- "Form follows function," the half-right banner: it won the war
  against ornament-that-lies, then overreached — function alone
  underdetermines form; the correction is in CLAIMS.
- The death of the average: ~4,000 pilots measured on ten body
  dimensions; average on all ten: zero. The adjustable seat was
  born from that zero — design stopped fitting the middle and
  fitted the range. The arithmetic lives in the mirror, where the
  average user cannot be.
- Errors re-charged from people to objects: identical adjacent
  cockpit levers kept raising the wheels instead of the flaps; two
  identical vials kept swapping drugs. For decades people were
  blamed and retrained; then the error rate was recognized as a
  property of the object, measurable before anyone is hurt —
  design's thermometer moment.
- Measurement enters the studio: watching five strangers replaced
  arguing about taste; the comparison test arrived and brought its
  trap (the hill with no mountain — in the mirror, with its speed
  limit).
- The dark turn, named: the color's laws aimed at the user instead
  of for them — the easy signup with the labyrinth cancel, the
  exit routed past the tills. Law began writing back; the ledger
  is in the mirror.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward. Each names
what runs along it.

- MIND: the largest border — attention as budget, difference as
  the only report, recognition over recall, the invented model
  acted on, habit clearing dialogs, maker blindness.
- BODY: the eye's channels and unequal weights, aging and glare,
  the fingertip's bluntness, reach's arithmetic, size felt in the
  body before it is understood.
- PHYSICS: light and its shares, the small-angle law, why printed
  woodgrain fails (real materials scatter light through their
  thickness; print reflects off its face).
- MATHEMATICS: ratios and ladders, the golden algebra, the logs of
  choice and reach, (1−p)ⁿ.
- MEASUREMENT: contrast ratios, error rates as properties of
  objects, the testing arithmetic, the honest floors.
- LANGUAGE: labels as the interface, names as promises, translation
  as reshaping, icons meaningless outside their community.
- CRAFT: material's refusals, the mark recording its movement,
  finish as evidence of hidden care, the hand closing the gap
  between meant and made.
- ENGINEERING: constraints and one-way fittings, the part that is
  not there cannot fail, the repairable life.
- BUILDING: proportion families, the door and the ramp, ornament
  at the joint — design at the scale that swallows the body.
- COMPUTING: modes, undo, defaults, the clock's windows — and the
  surface you do not control: a design is rules for arranging
  itself under conditions never seen, not a picture.
- ECONOMY: who pays shapes the form; a design is refusals made
  visible; features accumulate because the asker is present and
  the harmed user is not.
- CULTURE: taste, conventions, ornament as belonging, the canon
  and its filter, style as a run of refusals.
- PEOPLE: conventions as coordination (worth exactly their
  shared-ness), committee averaging, status read against the room.
- TEACHING: explaining itself in use beats the tour in advance;
  copying a master; taste arriving years before hands.
- MUSIC: the rest as a note, the beat under syncopation, one key
  relating every chord, the quiet bar making the loud one.
- MEDICINE: the identical vials; the device that must work for
  somebody frightened at three in the morning.
- FLIGHT: the cockpit as design's courtroom — levers, modes, the
  zero-average pilots.
- LAW: accessibility floors, the clause heading, dark patterns
  meeting regulation, the form that is a locked door.
- ETHICS: effort aimed for the user or at them — the same tool,
  the sign flipped; the honest progress bar as a promise kept.
