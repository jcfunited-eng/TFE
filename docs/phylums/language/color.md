## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- THE WORD — a handle, not the thing: an arbitrary noise joined to
  a sense by agreement alone, signed by whole peoples and enforced
  by nothing but use; where the chain of hand-offs breaks, the
  words are gone for good. Also lives in: economy (money is the
  same mint), law (a contract binds the same way), computing (a
  byte is a letter only by shared table), mind.
- THE SOUND UNIT — a category, not a noise: two noises are one unit
  if swapping them never changes a word, two units if it ever does.
  Speakers hear their categories, not their mouths — which is why a
  learner cannot hear a difference they are asked to make. Also:
  mind (categories with boundaries), music (a chosen scale), body.
- THE SYLLABLE — a loud centre with quieter slopes; the beat words
  are built on, counted by feel without ever being taught. Also:
  music (strong and weak beats), body (the breath), story (meter).
- STRESS, TONE, AND THE SENTENCE TUNE — the weight, pitch and
  grouping the voice lays over the words. Boundaries arrive by
  tune because nothing else supplies them; in many tongues the
  pitch of a syllable is part of the word itself. Punctuation is a
  partial written copy. Also: music (phrasing, accent), computing
  (a stream needs delimiters).
- THE WORD-PART — un, friend, li, ness: a small kit that mints
  words on the spot, in two trades that never mix — making a NEW
  word (teach becomes teacher, its own entry) and fitting the SAME
  word to the sentence (teach becomes teaches, never an entry).
  Also: craft (standard fittings), chemistry (few elements,
  endless compounds), computing.
- THE SENTENCE — not a row of words: a nest of pieces inside
  pieces, proven by the tests — a whole piece moves as one,
  answers as one, is replaced by one small word. Also:
  mathematics (brackets), building (a load path), computing.
- THE GROUP — a run of small frame words carrying one content
  word, closing at that word; the piece a reading stages. Also:
  mind (a few things held at once), music (a phrase heard whole).
- THE CHUNK — a stretch carried whole because it is said so often:
  greetings, idioms, set phrases. Fluency is mostly chunks with a
  little building at the joins. Also: craft (stock parts), music
  (a run under the fingers), computing (a cache).
- THE FRAME — the small words that hold sentences together: the,
  a, is, of, and. They appear everywhere, reach every subject, and
  name none. Also: building (mortar, not brick), measurement (a
  benchmark is chosen for being always there).
- THE POINTER — a frame word that arrives FOR a content word and
  opens its group; a sign over a door saying goods follow. Also:
  music (an upbeat), law (a recital clause).
- THE DOING, THE DOER, THE DONE-TO — what a sentence turns on,
  what it goes out from, what it arrives at; reverse the last two
  and the sentence reports a different day. Also: law (who struck
  whom is the case), physics (an event), economy (every entry has
  a from and a to).
- THE NAME — a tag tied on at the start, not a description that
  must keep fitting; it points at its bearer even when every
  belief about the bearer turns out wrong. Also: law (a case
  number outlives the file), animals (a dog answers to a word that
  describes nothing).
- THE AIMING WORDS — here, now, I, you, tomorrow: words carrying a
  rule for finding their target from where the speaker stands, so
  a note saying "back in ten minutes" is worthless without the
  moment it was pinned up. Also: measurement (a bearing needs its
  origin), story.
- THE PRONOUN — a pointer to something already on the table; worth
  exactly what the listener can find at its far end, and worthless
  with nothing behind it. Also: computing (a reference to nowhere
  crashes), story (an unclear antecedent stops a reader).
- THE QUESTION — a statement with a hole cut in it and a mark
  saying who fills it; the mark names the size of the hole —
  yes-or-no, a person, a place, a cause. The asking words carry
  the asking and almost never name the subject. Also: teaching,
  law (a witness answers what was asked), design (a blank field
  states its own type).
- THE ORDER — a turn that names no doer, because the one being
  told is the one who will do it; its doing comes first and the
  rest is what to do it to. The shape the fabric must read before
  it can be told to make anything. Also: craft (a recipe step),
  conflict (an order to march), governance.
- THE NEGATION — one small word that builds a statement about what
  is NOT there, which no photograph can do; it flips the claim
  while leaving every content word standing. Also: mathematics
  (zero — absence promoted to a symbol), law (proving a
  not-there), philosophy.
- THE FIGURE — a word carried away from home and still working:
  metaphor hides the seam, simile shows papers (like, as), irony
  flips on shared ground, an idiom has gone hard into one long
  word whose parts stopped paying. Also: story, design, teaching
  (old maps for new ground).
- THE CONTEXT — the standing scene: the room, the last turn, who
  speaks to whom, and everything both sides already hold. It does
  half the work of every word, and it is the half nobody notices
  doing it. Also: mind, people, law (a quoted line is asked where
  it came from).
- THE TURN — conversation's unit: built so its end can be seen
  coming, handed over within a breath, mended by its own speaker
  when it starts wrong. Also: music (coming in on a beat), play,
  people.
- THE REGISTER — the clothing on the same content; one fact
  dressed for a courtroom, a kitchen, a joke. The wrong dress is
  heard as not knowing where you are. Also: design (a font is a
  register), culture, people.
- THE VARIETY — dialect, accent, the several forms every speaker
  runs at rates; where the line between "language" and "dialect"
  falls is decided by borders, armies and schools, not by speech.
  Also: governance, culture, people.
- WRITING AND ITS MARKS — frozen speech: sound made durable so the
  dead can teach minds not yet born; the marks buy back a fraction
  of the voice and never the stress or the irony. Also: computing
  (code is writing a machine thaws and obeys), music (a score),
  teaching.
- THE SIGNED CHANNEL — language in the hands: units below the
  word, grammar in space, learned on speech's own timetable.
  Whatever language is, it is not about sound. Also: body,
  teaching, computing (one protocol over different wires).
- THE CROWD — the company a meaning keeps: oven travels with heat
  and dough, solder with wire. A word alone is a menu of possible
  meanings; a word standing in its crowd has already been served
  one. Also: cooking (the kitchen crowd), craft (every trade's
  three crowds), medicine (signs that travel together).

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. The ten walls
of the first ribbon are marked (WALL n); they govern any build that
reads or speaks.

- A word means what it means by agreement alone — the sound is
  arbitrary, the agreement binding; any noise would do, once
  shared. The name is not the thing: redrawing the map moves no
  mountain.
- Same words, two nestings, two meanings — structure cannot be
  read off word order alone; a reading must build a nesting, not
  scan left to right (WALL 1). Order keeps exactly one lawful job
  in this language: naming the roles — doer before the doing,
  done-to after — and that is written as knowledge about THIS
  language, arguable and replaceable.
- The sentences of a language cannot be listed — the set has no
  end, so nothing may be matched against stored sentences
  (WALL 2). The arithmetic is in SCIENCE.
- A new sentence is understood only by building its meaning from
  its parts; composition is compulsory, nothing can be all lookup
  (WALL 3). The exceptions must be listed: an idiom sits in the
  store as one item because its parts stopped paying.
- The senses of a common word cannot all be listed — the extension
  is live, new senses are made when needed. A sense must be
  PRODUCED from the company present, never fetched from a table
  (WALL 4).
- A word's class comes from how it distributes, not from what it
  means — "explosion" names an event and behaves like a thing-word;
  the school definitions fail on half the vocabulary (WALL 5).
- The sentence-fitting forms are produced by rule, not stored —
  morphology by rule (WALL 6); and a rule is blocked where a word
  already fills the slot ("thief" blocks "stealer"; learning
  "went" ends "goed").
- Fluent speech cannot be built from scratch at speed — there is
  not time; ready-made chunks are carried and only the joints
  built fresh (WALL 7). The clock is in SCIENCE.
- No long sentence is understood without its grouping — group
  before parsing (WALL 8).
- No marked member without a plain one to be marked against:
  meaning is carried by contrast, not by presence (WALL 9). "How
  tall" presumes nothing, "how short" presumes shortness; the
  plain member costs less, appears more, and stands for the whole
  category.
- A word's value is relational — what its rivals do not cover; no
  term translates without checking its neighbours (WALL 10). Where
  one tongue keeps one word for blue and green, that word covers
  more ground than either of ours, and adding a word shrinks its
  neighbours.
- Grammar issues no orders: it rules arrangements out, and
  whatever is not ruled out is available — which is why a few
  dozen constraints leave room for sentences never said before,
  and why "correct" only ever means "not forbidden".
- Agreement is a second copy kept as a check: "the birds sing"
  says plural twice, so with eleven words between doer and doing
  the join can still be found, and a mishearing turns up as a
  clash instead of passing quietly. What is checked is the
  grammar's label, never the world — a ship is "she" and the
  sentence checks itself against the label.
- The verb carries the sentence's plan: "give" demands a giver, a
  thing and a receiver, and a missing one is felt as a hole by
  someone who could never name the rule.
- A sentence that says what a thing IS has no doing at all —
  nothing acts, nothing is acted on; the first thing said STANDS
  and the rest is what it is said to be. Forcing a standing into a
  doing reads nonsense out of plain prose.
- An order names no doer, and its doing comes first — every other
  reading looks for the doing anywhere but first, because a
  statement puts its doer there.
- The strength of a claim is separate from its content: might,
  must, probably change no weather, only what the speaker may be
  held to afterwards. Hedges mark a claim as loose and must travel
  with it — dropping one invents certainty.
- What a sentence takes for granted rides outside the part up for
  discussion — "when did you stop" smuggles the stopping, and the
  smuggled part survives denial.
- Listeners assume truth, enough-and-no-more, relevance and
  plainness; a visible break in one becomes the message itself.
  "Some of them came" is heard as "not all" because all would have
  been said — most of what is understood is worked out from what
  the speaker chose not to say.
- A sentence carries no meaning across: it is a set of
  instructions for building one at the far end out of the
  listener's own materials, so the same words build different
  things in different heads — and always will.
- The reading commits early: every sense of a word opens for an
  instant and all but one is dropped within a heartbeat; the proof
  is the sentence that must be torn down and rebuilt after
  misleading its reader halfway.
- Form and sense are separate machines: a faultlessly built
  sentence can point at nothing, and the joinery is what carries
  fluent nonsense past the ear. The rules of assembly never
  inspect the cargo.
- Some sentences do not report, they DO — a promise, a
  resignation, a christening: the saying is the act, and what it
  needs is standing (the right person, setting and words), not
  truth.
- Speech has no spaces: the sound is one continuous ribbon and the
  word boundaries are put in by the listener from what he already
  holds — which is why an unknown tongue arrives as one long word
  said very fast.
- Each sense of a word travels with its own crowd, and the crowd
  settles which sense arrived; one shared word is a coincidence,
  and only agreement makes a match.
- Figurative speech is lawful stretching: metaphor moves a word's
  home and grows the vocabulary without minting one new sound;
  most abstract talk is dead metaphors still on the payroll, and a
  figure used long enough stops being one.
- The last thing said is the loudest context there is, and it
  fades fast — an answer three turns late lands on a question
  nobody is still holding.
- Change is constant and is not decay: it enters where a learner
  builds a different structure behind the same output; every form
  now "correct" was once a novelty somebody deplored in writing.
- No language is primitive and none more capable: the complexity
  moves between endings, order and particles; the expressive power
  does not differ.

## SCIENCE
The mathematics and the body-clock of language, held — not
mentioned. Each piece is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it
can follow, and where it earns it, a WORKED case run to a real
answer.

### MATHEMATICS — the steep fall of words

LAW: frequency falls with rank — the word at rank r appears
  roughly 1/r as often as the commonest word (rough but robust
  across languages).
RULE: to weigh a word's news value — find how expected it is; the
  commoner it is, the less it can name.
WORKED: if "the" is ~1 word in 16 (62,500 per million words), the
  100th-commonest runs 62,500 ÷ 100 = 625 per million, and the
  1,000th about 63. A word like "kidney" may show once in a
  million: whoever said it reached for it on purpose, and it names
  the subject.

LAW: a few hundred words do most of the talking. Under the rank
  law, the share of the top N words is the sum 1 + 1/2 + ... + 1/N
  against the same sum taken to the whole vocabulary.
WORKED: top 100 against a 50,000-word stock: about 5.19 ÷ 11.40 ≈
  0.46 — nearly half of everything ever said is the same hundred
  words. This is why the frame exists, why common words cast no
  vote on a subject, and why the rarest word in a question almost
  always names it.

LAW: news is surprise, counted in halvings — a word expected 1
  time in 16 carries 4 halvings (2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16); one expected
  1 time in a million carries 20 (2^20 = 1,048,576).
WORKED: 20 ÷ 4 = 5 — the rare word carries five times the news of
  the common one in a single token. Run backwards it is the
  shortening law: what is said most is said shortest, because
  effort is saved exactly where the listener needs the least help
  predicting what is coming. The drift under it runs one way —
  content to grammar, heavy to light, long to short, never back.

### MATHEMATICS — sentences without end

LAW: a rule that accepts its own output never runs out — a piece
  of one kind can contain another of the same kind, with no
  ceiling, so there is no longest sentence (why WALL 2 holds).
WORKED: a 5,000-word stock makes 5,000 × 5,000 × 5,000 =
  125,000,000,000 three-word strings. Even if only one in ten
  thousand is grammatical, that is 125,000,000,000 ÷ 10,000 =
  12,500,000 legal three-word sentences. A child by age five has
  heard ~5,000 utterances a day × 365 × 5 ≈ 9,125,000 utterances
  OF EVERY LENGTH — fewer than the three-word sentences alone.
  Storage cannot be the mechanism; composition is compulsory
  (WALL 3, proven by arithmetic).

LAW: the ways of nesting climb steeply with the group count — 2
  groups nest 1 way, 3 nest 2, 4 nest 5, 5 nest 14, 6 nest 42, 7
  nest 132: roughly tripling per added group.
RULE: group before parsing (WALL 8), and stage few groups — at
  four groups a reading weighs at most 5 shapes; at six it weighs
  42.
WORKED: 42 ÷ 5 = 8.4 — two extra ungrouped pieces multiply the
  staging better than eightfold, which is why a reading that takes
  so long it stops everything else is a stall, not a reading.

LAW: readings multiply — k independent two-way choices make 2^k
  readings of one sentence.
WORKED: three two-sense words and two attachment choices: 2 × 2 ×
  2 × 2 × 2 = 32 readings of one ordinary line. The listener keeps
  one within a heartbeat, which is why ambiguity is the ordinary
  case and is almost never noticed — the famous headlines are
  ordinary sentences with the context removed.

### THE BODY'S CLOCK — speech at speed

LAW: speech runs ~150 words a minute: 150 ÷ 60 = 2.5 words a
  second, so ~0.4 s a word. Turn hand-offs land within ~0.2 s, and
  a full second of silence is already heard as trouble.
RULE: nothing can be fully constructed at that rate — carry chunks
  whole and build only the joints fresh (WALL 7); and read a
  padded delay as the answer it wraps (a slow yes is a no).
WORKED: a ten-word turn passes in 10 × 0.4 = 4 s. A reading that
  must weigh 42 nestings instead of 5 inside that window is the
  nesting law meeting the clock — the two force grouping and
  chunking from opposite sides.

### THE READING ARITHMETIC — measured working knowledge

Everything here is counted, not reasoned; each number is knowledge
— change it and the reading changes with nothing in the machinery
touched. The receipts for what failed sit in the mirror.

LAW: the frame is the commonest hundred words that also reach more
  than three fifths of the writing's subjects. Frequency alone
  cannot find it — a word is common either because it is
  structural or because the writing is about it, and both are
  frequent. SPREAD separates them, and spread is not a kind of
  frequency.
WORKED: across 162 subjects, three fifths is 162 × 3 ÷ 5 = 97.2,
  so a frame word must reach 98 of them. The, a, is, of and and
  reach all 162 (1.00). Water reaches 0.49 × 162 ≈ 79 subjects —
  common, and OUT; air 0.43, mind 0.44, power 0.46, all out.
  Before spread, a subject the fabric knew well could not be read
  at all, because being written about put it in the frame and a
  frame word never closes a group.

LAW: habit floors — a count below its floor is an accident, not a
  habit. A word seen fewer than 4 times has no behaviour. A pair
  seen fewer than 3 times is not a chunk. A frame word seen fewer
  than 50 times is not judged as a pointer. A rate over too few
  sightings is noise however clean it looks.
WORKED: a frame word seen 60 times and followed by a content word
  47 of them: 47 ÷ 60 = 0.783 — above the three-quarters line, a
  pointer. At 44 of 60 it is 0.733 — not one. Seen 12 times and
  followed 11: 0.917, and still NOT counted, because twelve
  sightings is not a habit.

LAW: two words behave alike when more than half the company one
  keeps is company the other also keeps. Measured on words with
  enough company: a doing beside a doing scores 0.91, a thing
  beside a thing 1.00, a doing beside a thing 0.25 — the classes
  are real and four times apart.
WORKED: A has 40 distinct neighbours and shares 24 with B: 24 ÷ 40
  = 0.60 — alike. (What this law cannot yet do for RARE words is
  the mirror's most promising open entry.)

LAW: the doing is found by contrast — things are carried in by
  frame words, the doing arrives alone — and the two halves count
  different sets on purpose: inside the sentence the whole frame
  marks, across the writing only the pointers count. Counted on
  thirty sentences: frame-inside with pointers-across reads 29;
  pointers on both sides, 27; the frame on both sides, 3.
RULE: where more than one group stands, look BETWEEN them first —
  a doing that reaches something has its doer before it and its
  done-to after it. (This between-rule is a construct on trial,
  named as such; measured 64 of 70 with it against 60 without.)

LAW: near half of written knowledge is standings, not doings —
  1,411 of this fabric's 3,212 sentences carry is, are, was or
  were: 1,411 ÷ 3,212 = 0.44. A reading built only for
  doer-acts-on-done-to is blind to 44 in 100 of what it must read;
  the standing method below was bought against that number.

LAW: of the marks once claimed to tell an order from a statement,
  what stands is the pair that fires on no statements at all: a
  constraint hung off the sentence (a third of orders, zero
  statements) and the learned class calling the first word a
  doing. Together they catch ~37 in 100 of orders with no false
  fires; the retracted marks bought their catch by wrecking
  statements and are filed in the mirror with their numbers.

### LANGUAGES ACROSS TIME — the comparative arithmetic

LAW: sound change is regular — when a sound shifts, it shifts in
  every word where the same conditions hold, over a few
  generations, with no speaker noticing. Regularity is what makes
  the history of languages recoverable at all.
RULE: to relate two languages, never weigh one resemblance; find
  the same correspondence repeated — this one's f where that one
  has p, again and again across the basic stock.
WORKED: compare 200 basic meanings across two UNRELATED tongues;
  if a rough sound-and-sense fit has odds near 1 in 50 per item,
  expect 200 ÷ 50 = 4 striking look-alikes from pure accident.
  Four "amazing matches" between any two languages on Earth prove
  nothing; forty aligned f-for-p pairs prove descent.

LAW: loans arrive in layers and each layer is stamped with its
  date — a word borrowed early has been through every sound change
  since, a late one has not. A vocabulary is a record of who its
  speakers dealt with, and roughly when.

LAW: a rule that could make a word is blocked when a word already
  sits in that slot; and irregular forms survive by constant use —
  the commonest verbs are the strange ones because daily retrieval
  holds the odd shape, and a word fallen from use is swallowed by
  the regular rule within a few generations.

## METHODS
How reading and saying are done here — each followable, with what
governs it and what breaks it. The prepositioned how-to-build of
the first ribbon.

- HOW TO GROUP A SENTENCE: (1) hold the frame — the commonest
  hundred words of the writing that reach more than three fifths
  of its subjects; (2) walk left to right: each frame word
  attaches FORWARD to the content word it arrived for, and the
  group closes when that word is taken — a group is a run of frame
  words carrying one content word; (3) a run counts as a carried
  chunk only if every word of it is from outside the frame and its
  pairs are a habit (three sightings or more); (4) digits ride
  with their units as one fact and are never discarded. Governed
  by the frame law and the habit floors. Breaks when a phrase
  carries two content words — the split falls in the middle of it
  (the mirror holds this as the largest open error, located
  exactly).
- HOW TO FIND THE DOING AND THE ROLES: stage the groups; mark
  which arrived alone and which were carried in. The doing is a
  group that arrived ALONE, after the first, carrying no asking
  word — the contrast is the whole of it, and a sentence whose
  groups all arrived alone gives none. Several standing: prefer
  the one BETWEEN others (on trial; SCIENCE holds the numbers).
  Still unsettled: the writing decides — the standing group least
  often opened with a pointer, the rate counted over pointers only
  and only above the sighting floor. Then roles by order: doer
  before the doing, done-to after; a doing with nothing after it
  reaches nothing, and that is a reading, not a gap. Breaks on
  sentences with no pointer anywhere — no contrast, so the thin
  fallback decides (mirror).
- HOW TO TELL A STANDING FROM A DOING: a sentence has a doing only
  when some group after the first brought its content word in with
  NO pointer among its carriers. Otherwise it is a standing: the
  first group stands, and the rest is what it is said to be.
  Governed by the 44-in-100 law. Breaks where a bare content word,
  split off a two-content phrase, fakes a doing (mirror).
- HOW TO FIND WHAT AN ORDER COMMANDS: an order is marked only by
  the marks that fire on no statements — a constraint hung off it
  (a without-this, an using-only-that), or the learned class
  calling the first word a doing. Once marked: the command is the
  FIRST word of the first group; the rest of that group and
  everything after it is what to do it to; there is no doer to
  find. An asking word sitting later than first makes it an order
  whose object is a question — being told to explain, or to show
  why. Governed by the order law in SCIENCE. Breaks on bare-word
  orders with no constraint: a statement of bare words is the same
  shape, and the honest ceiling is a third (the mirror holds why).
- HOW TO READ A QUESTION FOR WHAT IT WANTS: (1) strip the polite
  wrapper — it carries no subject and no want, and comes off
  cleanly; (2) separate the asking words from the content words:
  why wants a cause or a purpose (two different answers — what
  pushed, or what was wanted), how wants ordered steps with a
  startable first move, what wants a family plus a difference, who
  a nameable person or office, when a point or a condition, where
  a reachable place, which a choice from a set already on the
  table, how-much a number WITH a unit, is/does a verdict; (3) the
  subject sits after the asking words and before the tail — strip
  the small words and what is left standing is the question; (4)
  weigh the rarest word heaviest; (5) check the framing for
  smuggled claims — a why-is-it-so-slow has already settled the
  slowness, and the honest first move is to check it; (6) find the
  negation first and mark everything in its reach as denied; (7)
  look for a second question after the and — the second half is
  where the real difficulty usually sits. Breaks when every
  content word is a pronoun: the subject lives in the turn before,
  and with no turn before, the honest report is that the subject
  is missing — a guess dressed as an answer costs more than
  silence.
- HOW TO READ A NUMBER PHRASE: a number and its unit are ONE fact
  — a temperature, a duration, a current are constraints, the only
  precise things in the sentence. A question carrying a number
  wants arithmetic, a count, or a comparison against a threshold,
  and an answer that never touches the number has answered a
  vaguer question. WORKED: told to add 59 and 73 — 9 + 3 = 12,
  write 2, carry 1; 5 + 7 + 1 = 13; the answer is 132. The digits
  must survive the reading to be added at all (they were once
  discarded wholesale; the mirror holds that receipt).
- HOW TO SETTLE A SENSE FROM COMPANY: never fetch from a table
  (WALL 4). Take the words standing beside the doubtful one HERE;
  split every line the writing holds the word in by whether that
  company also stands there; the sense is what the near half has
  that the whole has not — an arrangement of counted words, never
  an entry. Demand two words of one crowd before believing any
  technical or distant sense — one shared word is coincidence. Say
  how many places the sense was drawn from: a thin sense must be
  visible, not hidden. Breaks where the writing is thin around the
  word (the mirror holds the untried repair).
- HOW TO READ A FIGURE: the plain reading fails first, and the
  failure is the alarm — a picture that cannot physically happen
  points at a subject somewhere else. Treat a known idiom as one
  long word and never match its parts. A simile shows its papers;
  metaphor hides the seam; irony flips on shared ground and far
  from home lands as a lie or a blunder. Weigh an analogy's words
  below the subject's own — the borrowing is temporary, and every
  analogy has a place where the shape stops.
- HOW TO GUARD A MATCH (the letter-traps, run in order): find
  negation and mark its reach; keep hedges attached to their
  claims; keep quoted words attached to their speaker — reported
  speech reports the saying, not the so; match the longest name
  first and leave its parts alone; read past container words — a
  book ABOUT ovens is about ovens; hand a comparison's subject to
  the near side of the than; treat words after unlike, such-as and
  for-instance as illustration, not subject; cut the common
  endings before comparing, so the baked of one line meets the
  baking of another at the stem; hold the variant spellings and
  allow a near miss on a long word; refuse short-string matches
  without a companion; a counter-example names the error it
  refutes — read the correction, not the corrected; a word inside
  a definition belongs to the term defined; asking ABOUT a word is
  not using it. One shared word is chance; two agreeing words from
  one crowd are a signal.
- HOW TO DEFINE: name the family, then the difference — furniture,
  for one sitter, with a back. Too wide catches strangers, too
  narrow drops kin. A definition is an address in the web of
  kinds, not an essence.
- HOW TO WRITE CLEARLY: one idea per sentence; the actor before
  the action; the known before the new — each hands the reader
  ground to stand on before asking for the step. The passive is
  right exactly when the done-to is what was already being talked
  about. Register is chosen for the room, and the wrong dress
  breaks meaning, not manners.
- HOW TO SAY (carry and join): carry the chunks whole, build only
  the joints — a joint says how two things sit: together, one
  bearing the other, or unable to both hold — and saying only
  chunks is fetching, not speaking. To speak a reading, run the
  build order forwards and set the parts down as it names them:
  doer, doing, done-to — an arrangement that is not any of its
  parts.
- HOW TO TRANSLATE: name what is being spent — sense, sound,
  level, association and length cannot all cross, so every
  translation is an argument about which of the five mattered
  here. Check the term's neighbours on both sides (WALL 10): a
  word's value is what its rivals do not cover, and the two maps
  do not tile the same. A torque table crosses whole; a poem never
  does.
- HOW TO REPAIR AND HAND OVER: mend your own wrong starts
  mid-turn — the system prefers it, which is why correction from
  an equal stings and from a teacher does not; aim your entry at
  the projected end of the other's turn; and read the shape of a
  reply as part of the reply.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE WORD STOCK AND ITS LADDERS: near-words are a coarse ruler —
  warm to scalding, damp to drenched, annoyed to livid — and the
  rung is the claim: reporting the wrong rung is a false report,
  not a colourful one. The most useful ladders point at causes,
  not amounts: burning, stabbing and throbbing name different
  tissues and send for different help.
- THE CROWD LISTS: the written companies of the trades — the
  kitchen's tools, stuff and doings; the sea's hull, tide and
  mooring; the body's parts, systems and doings. Two members of
  one crowd place a sentence with near certainty; one places
  nothing.
- THE SPLIT LISTS: the words with several lives — bank, spring,
  light, charge, stock — each sense written with its own crowd, so
  the company can be looked for instead of guessed at.
- THE ASKING-WORD LIST: which words carry the asking and what kind
  of answer counts for each; held so a reading can separate the
  want from the subject before anything else happens.
- THE FRAME AND THE POINTERS: counted from the writing, never
  listed by hand — the commonest hundred with spread, and the
  frame words that arrive for content words more than three
  quarters of the time. A fabric written about other things grows
  a different frame and different pointers, by the same counts.
- THE VOICE: stress that picks which of seven claims one denial
  makes; the tune that supplies the boundaries; the tone that some
  tongues build into every word. The loud end of a sentence is its
  point.
- WRITING AND THE MARKS: durable, revisable, re-enterable out of
  order — and deaf: the marks refund pauses and grouping, never
  stress or irony, which is why plain notes start fights.
- THE DICTIONARY: a map of agreements, dated when printed — it
  reports how a community grips a handle now, the way a chart
  reports where the sandbars sit this year. An instrument, never
  an authority.
- THE ROUTING WORDS: so, anyway, well, but, actually — no facts,
  all shape: they say what the next piece is doing to the last
  one, and with all of them cut out, every word remains and the
  shape is gone.
- THE BENCH THAT CANNOT BE GAMED: say a thing in plain words, let
  the reading find its own written procedure, follow it, and check
  the result against arithmetic. Nothing in that chain is opinion.
  Hand-labelled benches measure agreement with the labeller — the
  mirror holds that receipt.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- BE UNDERSTOOD AT ALL: hand over instructions from which the far
  end builds a matching sense — never the sense itself; making the
  same thing twice, once at each end, is the whole trade.
- GET THINGS DONE WITH WORDS: ask so an answer can count; order so
  the doing is unmistakable; promise, name and resign where the
  saying IS the act.
- KNOW WHAT IS BEING ASKED: read the want as well as the mention —
  cause or purpose, steps or definition, verdict or quantity. The
  fabric's own first door: nothing can be told what to build until
  it reads the telling.
- HOLD PEOPLE TOGETHER: greet, soften, take turns, dress the
  content for the room — the channel-keeping half of talk, which
  carries no facts and does most of the binding.
- CARRY KNOWLEDGE PAST DEATH: freeze speech so minds not yet born
  can be taught. What cannot be written into a claim cannot be
  inherited.
- CROSS BETWEEN TONGUES: move what can be moved and name what was
  spent — every crossing keeps some of the meaning by spending the
  rest.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Speech first, grown not taught: every community that ever
  existed has it; children take it from the air on broken input by
  about five, and their errors — goed, mouses — prove rules are
  built, not copied. Writing is the late invention, installed by
  years of deliberate craft; which one is the organ was never in
  doubt.
- Hand a makeshift to children and it comes back a language:
  pidgins given one generation return with tenses and clauses
  nobody in the room had ever used. The plan shows through exactly
  where the input has none.
- Writing froze speech and then changed it: it made sentences no
  mouth would say, made speakers believe words have fixed forms,
  and fed spellings back into mouths — letters gone silent
  pronounced again because they were visible on the page.
- Grammar was worn, not minted: every light word was once a heavy
  one — a travel verb became the future tense, a word for body
  became a self — and the path never runs back. Strong words are
  spent by use and the tongue keeps reminting.
- The comparative method recovered unrecorded ancestors: the
  regularity of change, run backwards from several descendants to
  one consistent source — a history of tongues nobody wrote,
  rebuilt from correspondences, never from resemblances.
- The standard was crowned, not proven: one variety with schools,
  printers and exams behind it; many of its famous rules were
  invented a few centuries ago from Latin, forbidding forms older
  and more regular than the corrections. Understanding runs
  unequally along the same power: the small variety's speakers
  hear the big one everywhere and are then called incomprehensible
  back.
- This phylum's own knowledge was bought by measurement against
  benches it could not tilt: the frame needed spread, not
  frequency; the doing was a contrast, not a count; the standing
  sentence had to be found before near half the writing could be
  read at all; the order's loose marks were retracted the day
  their false fires were counted. Every cost is filed in the
  mirror with its numbers, and the mirror is the reason none of it
  needs paying twice.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward. Each names
what runs along it.

- MIND: categories heard instead of noises; the commit-early
  reading; working memory forcing groups and chunks; metaphor as
  old maps on new ground; what a speaker can and cannot report of
  their own grammar.
- PEOPLE: turn-taking, politeness as the handling of imposition,
  register and standing, the private languages every pair mints
  within a few turns, and the strongest-true-thing assumption that
  makes what was left unsaid speak.
- MATHEMATICS: the rank law, the halvings of news, the nesting
  counts, the sentence arithmetic that forbids storage; brackets
  as written grouping; zero as negation promoted to a symbol.
- COMPUTING: alphabets by shared table, stop words and stemming,
  parsers that must choose, caches as chunks, delimiters as tune —
  and the borrowed office vocabulary that sends letter-matches to
  the wrong field.
- MUSIC: the syllable's beat, phrasing as grouping, the upbeat as
  pointer, scales as chosen inventories, the score as frozen
  performance that keeps the notes and drops the throat.
- LAW: definitions clauses against drift, hearsay as reported
  speech written out, the comma fortunes turn on, the loaded
  question restricted, the signature as a performative.
- STORY: the known-before-new ramp, the antecedent that stops a
  reader, the image that carries what a statement flattens,
  quotation and whose voice a line belongs to.
- TEACHING: showing as ordered method, definitions by family and
  difference, the misconception that must be named before it can
  be corrected, idioms learned whole or not at all.
- MEASUREMENT: a number with its unit as one fact, hedges as error
  bars, near-words as coarse rulers, the discriminating word as
  the test worth running.
- CULTURE: the unnoticed context handed down — distances,
  silences, plain nos; kin words and rite words; what dies with a
  lost tongue is the system of distinctions, not the labels.
- GOVERNANCE: where the language/dialect line is drawn, and by
  whom; the standard's army of institutions; whose speech is
  broadcast and who is then heard.
- BODY: the mouth's inventory and the ear's categories set in the
  first year, the breath behind the syllable, the hands as a full
  channel.
- PHYSICS: sound as the carrier — pitch, weight and tune as the
  signal's usable dimensions, every reliable one of which gets a
  job.
- ECONOMY: money as the same agreement words are made of; worn
  coins and worn words; the water vocabulary that flows borrowed
  through the whole money field.
- CRAFT: jargon as compression priced by exclusion — the speed and
  the lock are one property; the trades' crowds; the chunk as
  stock part; a procedure as knowledge in the order the hands need
  it.
- PLAY: the pun as ambiguity cashed for a laugh; games of asking;
  the contest words — match, round, point — that every other
  subject borrows.
- PHILOSOPHY: the name and the thing, negation's strange power,
  and the meaning of meaning — which stands open, in the mirror.
- ANIMALS: calls and signals mark where language begins and communication does not end.
- BELIEF: scripture, prayer, and the sacred word — belief lives in language.
- CONFLICT: propaganda, codes, and ultimatums — wars are fought in words too.
- DESIGN: typography and layout are language made visible.
- ETHICS: promise, lie, and slander — speech acts are judged conduct.
