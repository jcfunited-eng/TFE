## THINGS
The units this subject is about; other homes named so a cascade can
branch.

- THE BODY AS MACHINE — bones as levers, joints as hinges, muscles
  as motors that only pull: force follows cross-section, speed costs
  force, and the fibres run slow-enduring to fast-tiring in largely
  inherited proportions; also: body, physics, engineering, animals.
- TENDON AND BONE — the spring and the frame: tendon returns energy
  like a bow and adapts in months where muscle takes weeks; bone
  thickens for loads that are large, fast, and unfamiliar; also:
  body, medicine, building (columns).
- THE NERVOUS SYSTEM — the commander: how many fibres are called,
  how fast, whether opponents stand down; early strength and all
  skill live here; also: mind, body, teaching.
- THE GROUND — the third fighter, the only partner too big to move
  by pushing on it; every step and punch is thrown by it, every fall
  is a strike it throws back; also: physics, earth, building.
- BALANCE — the center of mass kept over the base; everything
  upright is this held, and every throw is a negotiation to end it;
  also: physics, building (a tower is a stance held for centuries).
- FUEL AND HEAT — three supplies chosen by duration (a phosphate
  charge worth seconds, fast burning worth minutes, breathing
  beyond), and the furnace: ~three-quarters of every calorie spent
  leaves as heat; also: body, chemistry, weather, survival.
- THE SKILL — a movement compiled by repetition from slow deliberate
  steps into one fast automatic piece; runs below awareness, and
  runs alone under fear; also: mind, teaching, music, craft.
- THE OPPONENT — force to borrow, a reader of your timing, owner of
  the contested distance; also: conflict, play.
- THE CONTEST AND THE RECORD — fair start, shared rules, an ending
  built in: an instrument, and its high-water mark; also: play,
  measurement, law, culture.
- THE LOAD — weight, mileage, sparring round: the question asked of
  the body, whose exact wording decides the exact answer; also:
  teaching (the exam you set), farming (what a field is asked).
- PAIN AND SORENESS — signals, not measurements: soreness reports
  novelty, pain a protection verdict, neither reports merit; also:
  body, mind, medicine.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query.

- The body answers the question actually asked, not the one meant:
  ask for endurance, lose some force; ask for one angle, get that
  angle. The subject's first law; half of METHODS is corollary.
- A load repeated becomes silent: a demand already met asks nothing
  and builds nothing; progress requires the question to keep moving.
- The gap between what you are and could be is the fuel, and
  training spends it: beginners improve weekly on anything, the
  trained fight for a percent a year, and nothing went wrong.
- Recovery is a rate, not a day off: one repair budget serves the
  session, the job, the argument, the short night. Of everything
  sold as recovery, sleep is the one with a large effect.
- The session is a stimulus; the performance is built afterwards out
  of food, sleep, and time — the visible half is the smaller half.
  And performance = fitness − fatigue, cleared on different clocks,
  which is why a taper uncovers percent that was already there.
- The first weeks of strength training change the commander, not
  the muscle — more fibres called, faster, opponents stood down;
  train one arm and the untrained arm gets measurably stronger.
- Strength and size are cousins, not twins: force is command times
  cross-section, tissue is added by total work near failure, and the
  strongest lifters are not the biggest.
- Structure beats muscle: bones stacked in line pass force to the
  ground like a wall stud; a bent frame fights it with meat. And
  relaxation is speed: clenching fold- and straighten-muscle at once
  parks the limb — the fast strike travels loose, hardens at arrival.
- The opponent's push is a gift: steering costs a fraction of
  stopping (SCIENCE holds the arithmetic); his commitment becomes
  your throw.
- Distance and timing are the real weapons — every strike has a
  range where it lives and a beat when the door is open — and every
  attack spends the guard that made it: offense and safety draw on
  one budget, and the counter lives in the overdraft.
- Momentum goes through the target: force feeds the ball only during
  contact — the swing that stops at the ball was braking before
  arrival (SCIENCE prices it).
- Practice rewires before it strengthens: repetition moves a
  movement to the fast automatic track, and under fear only that
  track runs — drill exists because battle deletes thinking. And
  automation frees attention: amateurs think about their feet,
  masters about the opponent.
- Fatigue takes judgment first: late in a game players run nearly as
  fast and choose much worse — decisions must be practised tired;
  fatigue itself is several failures wearing one word. And choking
  is a well-learned skill taken back under conscious control:
  pressure moves attention onto the parts, exactly what the
  automatic system does not need.
- Performance during practice is not learning: the same drill twenty
  times looks like progress and almost all of it is temporary; the
  jumbled, worse-looking session retains far better a week later.
- Elite reading is anticipation, not reflex: experts are no faster
  at a laboratory light — they read the shoulder before the pass,
  answering an earlier question.
- Effort is regulated against an expected finish, not against empty:
  athletes slow before anything runs out and sprint at the end; the
  limit is managed, not reached.
- Lactate is not the villain: a fuel other muscles and the heart
  burn, an excellent marker of hard work outrunning the slow supply,
  mistaken for its cause.
- Injuries cluster where the load JUMPED, not where it was heaviest:
  capacity is a record of recent weeks. And the best predictor of an
  injury is the same injury, earlier.
- For most injured tissue, load is the medicine and total rest the
  placebo that weakens everything else: tissue reorganises along the
  force it is given. There is no forbidden exercise, only unprepared
  tissue — the question is never the movement but this body's record.
- Hurting is not harming: pain below moderate, not climbing, gone by
  morning is generally tolerable; the two-day ache measures novelty,
  not merit.
- The contest is an instrument: it measures nothing unless it starts
  fair (the fair start is its zero); one game belongs to luck and a
  season to skill (SCIENCE); and home teams win more everywhere ever
  measured, roughly 54-60% — the total solid, the split argued.
- Belief moves performance measurably: told of an aid, athletes
  produce more — part of how effort is regulated, and why unblinded
  training studies are worthless.
- Elite athletes look alike because the sport removed everyone who
  did not: rowers are tall because tall rowers won. Copying a
  champion buys little — and identical training returns improvement
  from enormous to none; the own record beats any group average.
- At the top, men's and women's records differ ~10-12% in most
  events (body composition, heart and lung size, oxygen carrying) —
  smaller than the spread inside either group; a group mean says
  nothing about any individual.
- The ten-thousand-hour finding, held honestly: top violinists
  AVERAGED more deliberate practice than good ones — one domain,
  wide spread. Deliberate practice is specific, effortful, feedback-
  rich work; hours are required, not decisive (the myth's grave is
  in the mirror).

## SCIENCE
The math, physics, and physiology of movement, held — not
mentioned: LAWs to compute with, RULEs to follow, WORKED cases
proving the rule runs.

### PHYSICS — the body as levers

LAW: turning force = force × lever arm. Muscles insert close to the
  joint, loads sit far — the muscle pays a multiple of what the hand
  holds; the hand moves a multiple of the muscle's speed.
RULE: to price a held load — load newtons × distance from joint ÷
  the muscle's insertion distance.
WORKED: 10 kg (98 N) held 35 cm from the elbow; the flexor inserts
  ~4 cm from the hinge. 98 × 0.35 = 34.3 N·m; 34.3 ÷ 0.04 = 857.5 N
  — about 87.5 kg-force, nearly nine times the weight held (8.75×,
  the same ratio as the speeds below). Backwards, the
  gift: the hand travels 0.35/0.04 = 8.75× faster than the muscle
  shortens — geared for speed, paying in force. Every joint lock is
  the same purchase reversed: a grip at the wrist works the elbow
  through ~30 cm while its flexors answer through ~4 — the answer to
  a lock is escape from the geometry, never strength.

LAW: you stand while the center of mass stays over the base; the
  lean you own is the angle whose tangent is edge distance ÷ center
  height.
WORKED: center ~1 m up. Feet together (edge ~10 cm): arctan 0.10 ≈
  5.7° of lean. Shoulder-wide (~25 cm): ≈ 14°. Crouch to 0.8 m,
  widen to 30 cm: ≈ 20.6° — the wrestler's crouch more than triples
  the allowance. Every throw is a negotiation to move the opponent's
  center past his edge.

LAW: force = momentum change ÷ time. A fall's total is fixed;
  spreading it over time and area is what surviving means.
WORKED: 70 kg falling 1 m lands at √(2 × 9.8 × 1) = 4.43 m/s,
  carrying ~310 kg·m/s. Stopped rigidly in 0.02 s: 15,500 N — 23×
  the 686 N body weight, hip-breaking. Spread by slap and roll over
  0.2 s: 1,550 N, a bruise. Ten times the stopping time is a tenth
  the force — breakfalls, bent-knee landings, and crumple zones are
  one law in three coats.

LAW: borrowed force — steering costs only the sideways momentum
  change; stopping costs all of it.
WORKED: an 80 kg opponent at 3 m/s carries 240 kg·m/s. Stopping in
  0.5 s: 480 N. Deflecting 15°: 240 × sin 15° ≈ 62 kg·m/s sideways,
  ÷ 0.5 ≈ 124 N — a quarter of the price, and his 360 J of motion
  stays his for the throw to spend. Add your little to his much;
  supply only the curve.

LAW: force is thrown by the ground — no shove without a shove back;
  power sums up the chain: foot's push, hip's turn, stiff trunk, arm
  last (the last car of the train, not the engine). A trunk that
  bends leaks the force it was passing — the middle's muscles mostly
  exist to STOP movement, why one-sided carries train them best.

LAW: energy of motion = ½ × mass × speed²; small speed losses cost
  square-law energy.
WORKED, follow-through priced: a swing braking to 24 m/s from 30:
  30²/24² = 900/576 ≈ 1.56 — over a third of the payload (36%) given
  up before contact. WORKED, the sprint's cheap energy: 80 kg at 10 m/s holds
  ½ × 80 × 100 = 4,000 J ≈ 1 kcal — nearly free as ENERGY, brutal as
  POWER; the marathon is the reverse (below).

LAW: power = force × speed of producing it, and muscle cannot peak
  both — the faster it shortens, the less force it makes; peak power
  sits in the middle. And maximal force takes ~0.3 s to build while
  a sprinting foot touches ~0.1 s: the ceiling never arrives; what
  counts is force in the first fraction of a second, trained
  separately.
RULE: train both ends (heavier-slower AND lighter-faster) plus the
  ramp; who only lifts heavy stays slow, who only moves fast stays
  weak.

LAW: strength follows cross-section (length²), weight follows
  volume (length³) — relative strength falls as size rises.
WORKED: scale an athlete 1.2× in every direction: strength ×1.44,
  weight ×1.728, strength-per-kilogram ×0.83 — 17% weaker for his
  size. This one computation is why climbers are small, heavyweights
  slow off the mark, weight classes exist, and the flea's jump does
  not scale up to a horse.

LAW: tendons are springs in series with the motors — a stretched
  elastic returns most of the work put in, so a bounce costs far
  less than a standing start; ~a third to a half of each running
  stride is recoil, not muscular work. Stiffness, not only strength,
  makes an athlete fast.

### PHYSIOLOGY — fuel, heat, adaptation

LAW: duration picks the fuel mixture (seconds: the phosphate
  charge; minutes: fast burning; beyond: breathing) — and the fast
  stores are REFILLED by the slow system, so the boxer between
  rounds and the footballer between sprints recover on their aerobic
  base. Train the mixture the event buys; train the refill for any
  stop-start sport.

LAW: effort above the sustainable rate spends a fixed reserve; time
  to empty = reserve ÷ (power above sustainable).
WORKED: a rider holding 250 W indefinitely with a ~20 kJ reserve:
  at 350 W, 20,000 ÷ 100 = 200 s; at 300 W, 400 s; at 275 W, 800 s.
  Every race is that reserve spent once; "blowing up" is this
  division done badly.

LAW: moving a body costs ~1 kcal per kg per km; the carbohydrate
  store is ~500 g ≈ 2,000 kcal; fat is ~50,000 kcal even lean —
  far more for most — but burns slower per minute.
RULE: multiply body mass by distance; past 2,000 kcal, plan to eat —
  and train the gut (absorption ~60-90 g carbohydrate/hour, itself
  trainable).
WORKED: 70 kg runner, 42.2 km: ≈ 2,950 kcal. The store covers
  2,000 ÷ 70 ≈ 28.5 km — the famous wall at thirty kilometers is
  this division run once; past it the body drops to fat's slower
  burn and the pace goes with it. The sprint cost one kcal; this
  costs three thousand — same body, opposite constraint.

LAW: only ~20-25% of spent energy becomes movement; the rest is
  heat, and evaporated sweat is the only big drain (~580 kcal per
  liter; dripped sweat cools nothing).
WORKED: a runner spending 1,000 kcal/h makes ~800 kcal/h of heat —
  shedding it needs 800 ÷ 580 ≈ 1.4 L/h evaporated. Un-shed (humid
  air blocks evaporation), the body's ~59 kcal/°C capacity climbs
  800 ÷ 59 ≈ 13.6°C per hour — lethal in well under one. The body
  knows this arithmetic and derates pace from the first minute in
  heat: the slowdown is protection. Heat adaptation is fast (a week
  or two) and specific — it prepares no one for altitude or cold.

LAW: adaptation = a demand above what is kept, plus repair that
  overshoots. Its clocks: muscle answers in weeks; tendon in months
  to a year (poorly plumbed) — the classic injury is strength
  arriving before its rope catches up; bone thickens for large,
  fast, unfamiliar loads (runners dense, swimmers not); endurance
  fades in a fortnight, strength holds weeks, skill lasts years,
  muscle regained returns far faster than first built.
RULE: to keep a capacity, keep asking; to rebuild, load gradually;
  to return to sport, test capacity (strength against the other
  side, the movement at speed, done without thinking) — never the
  calendar: six weeks is a description, not a test.

LAW: the dose-response curve rises, flattens, turns down: for
  health most benefit arrives at a modest dose (walking's curve
  flattens well below the famous ten thousand steps — a pedometer's
  brand name); for performance the useful dose is far higher and the
  turn closer. Past the turn: performance falls while effort rises,
  sleep lightens, mood shortens. And eating too little for the
  training shuts down the non-urgent — hormones, bone, immunity, in
  men as well as women — performance holding for months, then
  collapsing, the athlete praised for discipline all the way down.

LAW: the body's clock is part of performance — strength,
  temperature, reaction cycle daily; time zones cost ~a day per
  hour, east worse. Thin air costs endurance at once and pays back
  over weeks, but hard training is impossible up there: live high,
  train low.

### MATHEMATICS — chance, records, the ledger

LAW: an average settles toward the true rate as tries multiply; a
  single try says almost nothing.
WORKED: a team winning any game 60%, in a best-of-seven: 4-0 =
  0.6⁴ = 0.1296; 4-1 = 4 × 0.216 × 0.4 × 0.6 = 0.2074; 4-2 = 10 ×
  0.216 × 0.16 × 0.6 = 0.2074; 4-3 = 20 × 0.216 × 0.064 × 0.6 =
  0.1659; sum ≈ 0.71. Seven games turn a 60% edge into 71%; a season
  turns it into near-certainty — and still leaves any night to luck.

LAW: gains shrink like the room left to gain in — records fall in
  shrinking slices toward walls set by levers, muscle physics, and
  heat shedding: the mile 4:14 (1913) → 3:59 (1954) → 3:43 (1999),
  standing since; the 100 m 10.6 (1912) → 9.95 (1968) → 9.58 (2009).
  Where the wall sits is the mirror's bounded unknown.

LAW: adaptation compounds slowly and cannot be front-loaded.
WORKED: three sessions a week for four years = 3 × 50 × 4 = 600;
  six a week for two months = 48, then an injury or a job ends it.
  The modest rate held long beats the heroic rate abandoned. Same
  ledger for the hours myth: 3 h/day × 365 × 10 years ≈ 11,000 — the
  famous number is a decade of daily afternoons, an average, never a
  threshold.

LAW: an endurance result is three factors multiplied — delivery's
  ceiling, the holdable fraction, what a speed costs — so one number
  never ranks athletes, and at the elite level the ceiling test
  saturates. Economy is the quiet factor: identical engines finish
  minutes apart because one wastes less.

## METHODS
How movement is built — what governs each, what breaks it.

- PROGRESSIVE OVERLOAD, WRITTEN DOWN: keep the demand moving and
  keep a log — an unrecorded load settles at what felt right,
  reliably less than last month. Breaks when the jump outruns recent
  weeks (the injury law).
- BASE BUILDING (long, slow, conversational volume): buys the parts
  nobody feels — vessels, fat-burning machinery, a fuller-filling
  heart — and decides how much hard work can later be absorbed.
  Bought with volume, not effort.
- INTERVALS (hard bouts, recovery between): reach the ceiling steady
  work never touches; most fitness per hour, most recovery cost per
  session. Breaks when stacked without the base to absorb them.
- STRENGTH: heavy, few reps, LONG rests (the phosphate store refills
  in minutes — rest between sets is a variable, not an
  intermission). SIZE: any load near failure, more total work,
  protein, months. Two questions; each answered literally.
- BRACING AND STRUCTURE: fill the chest, tighten the trunk — a
  sealed pressurised column carries what the spine would take alone
  (cost: the pressure squeezes vessels; heavy lifts grey people
  out). Stack the bones so the ground pays what meat would.
- SKILL PRACTICE, BUILT TO RETAIN: coach to the stage (understand;
  roughly right under attention; automatic). Practise VARIED —
  variation forces the skill to rebuild into a rule, not a memorised
  copy — accepting worse sessions for better performers. Feedback
  sparse and delayed: a learner handed the answer every rep builds a
  skill that needs the answerer. Practise decisions tired. For
  transfer, practise the whole task at its own speed, load, and
  position (the weighted-bat trap is in the mirror).
- BREAKFALLS AND BALANCE, LEARNED BEFORE NEEDED: spread the planet's
  strike over time and area; one-leg stands, heel-to-toe, deliberate
  catches on uneven ground — unglamorous, among the largest effects
  in the field, because modern life asks nothing of balance and a
  fall in an old body is often the beginning of the end.
- WARM-UP AND MOBILITY: warm-up is rehearsal and preheat, not
  insurance — raises tissue temperature, opens supply, wakes the
  movements about to be used. Mobility is built by LOADING the end
  of the range — a range owned passively is not available when
  needed; the ankle that gives way asks for reflexes at its edge,
  not loosening.
- REHABILITATION BY GRADED LOAD: the medicine is the dose of force,
  risen on the tissue's own clock; protect only what must be; gate
  the return by measured capacity, not weeks elapsed.
- TAPER: cut volume sharply, keep intensity, the last week or two —
  fatigue clears faster than fitness fades. It uncovers, not builds.
- AUTOREGULATION: adjust the day's work to the day's body — a
  program is a forecast made last month about a body it has not met
  today. A PLANNED easy week is cheaper than the one an injury
  chooses.
- PACING AND FUELING: spend the reserve evenly against a KNOWN
  finish (unknown endpoints wreck the forecast governor); eat before
  the wall's arithmetic says so; drink to thirst (the schedule
  doctrine's grave is in the mirror); practise the eating.
- PRE-PERFORMANCE ROUTINE (serve, putt, lift): a fixed rehearsed
  sequence claims attention before the crowd or the monitoring can —
  cousin of the expert's long quiet look at the target, the fixation
  that shortens under pressure.
- CONTEST-CRAFT: own the distance and the timing; convert advantages
  at good rates (space for time, position for tempo); start fair, or
  the measurement is of the handicap.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- GRAVITY, THE GROUND, THE LOAD: the free resistance and anything
  that words a demand precisely — the barbell's virtue is a numeric,
  repeatable question.
- THE LOG AND THE CLOCK: a quantity not recorded is not managed; the
  stopwatch made the record possible and the fair start checkable.
- THE OWN-BASELINE TEST: a simple jump test taken regularly tracks
  readiness better than most expensive measures — same movement,
  own history as reference.
- HEART RATE, USED KNOWING ITS LIES: drifts up at unchanged effort,
  reads high on heat or a bad night, lags every change by a minute,
  shifts on caffeine — it measures the cost, late. Steer by pace,
  feel, and the log.
- SLEEP AND FOOD: the actual recovery products — extend athletes'
  sleep and sprint times, accuracy, and reaction improve; restrict
  it and young athletes' injuries climb steeply. The supplement
  short list that survives testing: caffeine, creatine, nitrate,
  buffers for hard few-minute efforts — modest effects; the rest of
  the market is in the mirror.
- EQUIPMENT AND SURFACE: shoes, suits, tracks — real, measurable,
  the quiet author of many "human" improvements.
- RULES, REFEREES, WEIGHT CLASSES: the means of fair contest — rules
  do not keep themselves past a friendly; weight classes exist
  because of the square-cube law; return-to-play criteria take the
  decision out of the pressured moment and hand it to a rule agreed
  in advance.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- MOVE THROUGH THE DAY: carry, climb, reach, catch yourself — the
  want modern life quietly stopped asking for; activity (everything
  on your feet) outweighs exercise (the scheduled hour).
- BE STRONGER, FASTER, LONGER-LASTING: each a different literal
  question to the body, answered by METHODS.
- WIN THE ARGUMENT BETWEEN TWO BODIES: fight, defend, throw, escape
  — settled by balance, leverage, distance, timing.
- PLAY AND CONTEST: enter a small world of rules on purpose, with a
  scoreboard.
- SURVIVE THE FALL AND KEEP INDEPENDENCE: the ground is the last
  opponent. Ageing takes power first — fast fibres shrink first, and
  most in those who never ask for anything fast; old muscle still
  answers a louder question (more protein per serving, genuinely
  heavy demands), and balance is a skill trainable at any age.
- COME BACK FROM INJURY: graded load, measured gates, the fear
  handled as seriously as the tissue — fear of re-injury is among
  the strongest predictors of who returns; a serious injury takes
  away what the athlete IS, not only what they do.
- BE WATCHED AND BELONG: team, crowd, record — a team's output is
  not the sum of its players (effort falls where contribution is
  invisible; roles are a performance variable).

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost.

- The martial arts as physics found by bruises: centuries of
  fighters discovered balance, leverage, borrowed force, structure,
  and the breakfall empirically — every art's secret teaching is a
  physics line paid for before the physics was written; drill
  predates the science of automaticity as bread predates
  microbiology.
- The fair start and the stopwatch: contests are ancient,
  measurement of them recent — standard distance and level start
  turned "faster" into a number, and the record was born.
- The lactic-acid century: the burn's companion promoted into its
  cause, taught for generations, then demoted to marker and re-hired
  as fuel — the subject's cleanest correlate-for-mechanism case.
- The hydration reversal, paid in deaths: drinking ahead of thirst
  hardened into doctrine and the doctrine killed; thirst was the
  regulator all along (the mirror holds the grave).
- No-pain-no-gain's long half-life: soreness-as-merit and play-
  through-it built generations of tendon ruptures and stress
  fractures before load science separated hurting from harming; half
  survived honestly — hard effort near failure IS part of the
  demand; the pain was never the signal.
- Stretching's demotion and rest's with it: universal practices
  finally tested — no prevention, no soreness relief, brief power
  loss; replaced by loading the range, and by load-as-medicine once
  measurement showed tissue heals into what is asked of it.
- The evidence turn, still arriving: talent scouts, movement
  screens, cycle programs, recovery gadgets, and most of the
  supplement market walked into testing; a short list walked out —
  the record of what did not is the mirror's whole cargo.
- The technology jumps: surfaces, swimsuits, shoe foams — records
  leaping in a single season across many athletes; era comparison
  has carried an asterisk since.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths. Each names what runs
along it.

- PHYSICS: levers, momentum and impulse, square-cube, elastic
  storage, energy and power, the tipping law — half this phylum's
  SCIENCE is physics wearing skin.
- BODY: muscle's machinery, tendon's slow clock, bone's listening,
  recruitment, fuel, sweat — this phylum is the body ASKED FOR
  something; that one is the body as it is.
- MIND: skill compilation, attention's one channel, choking,
  arousal's curve (the lifter nearly enraged, the putter calm), the
  effort forecast, fear of re-injury, belief moving output.
- MEDICINE: injury and rehabilitation, concussion protocol, the
  under-eating collapse, the placebo's real effects; load-as-
  medicine is a shared law.
- TEACHING: the exam you set is the answer you get; stages, varied
  practice, sparse feedback, performance-now versus retained-later —
  coaching is teaching under a stopwatch.
- MATHEMATICS: luck settling over many tries, shrinking slices,
  compounding sessions, products of factors, the reserve division.
- PLAY: the contest entered on purpose; rules ARE the game — remove
  the constraints and only motion remains; strategy as currency
  exchange, space for time, material for tempo.
- CONFLICT: distance and timing as weapons, drill because battle
  deletes thinking, home ground the oldest advantage — war is
  contest without the whistle's mercy.
- MUSIC and CRAFT: the loose hand plays faster than the clenched
  one; scales free the hands so the head can phrase; ten thousand
  ordinary repetitions behind one good piece.
- ENGINEERING: the chain limited by its weakest member (grip fails
  before the back), crumple zones as breakfalls in steel, derating
  before overheating.
- MEASUREMENT: the fair start as the instrument's zero; own-baseline
  series beating population norms; measures that saturate in
  selected populations; blinding, because belief moves the measured.
- WEATHER: heat and humidity setting the sweat arithmetic;
  altitude's thin air; the home team knowing its wind.
- ANIMALS and LIFE: square-cube ruling every gait and jump; the
  tendon spring is older than we are; adaptation — a capacity kept
  only while asked for — is life's rule, and this phylum its
  gymnasium.
- ECONOMY: leverage kept the name and the danger; every position is
  safety spent; doping and weight-cutting are rule-created arms
  races only a rule change exits.
- LAW / GOVERNANCE: referees past a friendly; criteria agreed in
  advance to bind the pressured moment; category rules justified at
  the tail, applied to everybody.
- CULTURE and SURVIVAL: the game as gathering, the record as shared
  story; the fall survived and the heat respected.
