## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- FORCE — the push or pull every design must carry from where it
  enters to the ground; also lives in: physics (its home law),
  body (muscle), weather (wind and snow are loads).
- THE LEVER FAMILY — lever, wheel-and-axle, pulley, screw, wedge,
  ramp: six costumes for one bargain (force traded for distance);
  also: physics (work), body (every limb is one), farming (the
  plow is a wedge), history.
- THE WHEEL AND THE BEARING — the near-abolition of dragging;
  worthless without the smooth hard surface under it (the pair is
  the invention, not the disc); also: physics (friction), economy
  (roads and rails), culture.
- THE BEAM AND THE COLUMN — the carriers: the beam takes load
  sideways and lives or dies by its depth; the column takes it
  straight down and fears bowing more than crushing; also: physics,
  body (bones are both), life (a tree is a column wearing beams).
- THE TRIANGLE AND THE ARCH — the two honest shapes: the triangle
  cannot change form without a side changing length; the arch turns
  a span into pure squeeze so stone (which crushes well and tears
  badly) can cross a river; also: mathematics, culture (Rome).
- MATERIALS — steel, iron, concrete, wood, aluminum, glass: not
  substances but ARRANGEMENTS — the same atoms stacked differently
  are a different material (see SCIENCE); also: chemistry, economy
  (price decides more than strength), farming (wood grows).
- THE ENGINE — heat turned into motion, always paying a toll set by
  temperatures alone; also: physics (the second law's showroom),
  economy, history (it made the modern world).
- THE PRESSURE VESSEL — canner, boiler, tank, reactor: one law,
  one danger, every size; also: cooking (the canner IS one),
  physics, medicine (the sterilizer).
- THE CONTROLLER — governor, thermostat, float valve: the loop that
  measures its own output and pushes against the error; also:
  body (its thermostat runs at 37°C), cooking (the oven), life
  (every cell is full of these loops).
- THE FASTENER AND THE JOINT — bolt, rivet, weld, glue: where parts
  meet is where designs fail, so the joint is a first-class thing;
  also: chemistry (the weld is local melting), body (joints).
- THE GAUGE AND THE TOLERANCE WINDOW — a dimension stated as a
  range, and the tool that answers fit/no-fit without a number;
  also: mathematics (intervals), economy (interchange built the
  factory).
- THE SAFETY FACTOR — a number that is a thing: humility written
  into arithmetic, the ratio held between what a part can bear and
  what it will be asked to; also: economy (it is bought by weight
  and cost), medicine (dosage margins are the same idea).
- THE PUMP AND THE PIPE — moving water uphill is civilization's
  oldest engineering want; also: farming (irrigation), body (the
  heart is a pump rated ~1.5 W of useful work), weather.
- THE CODE — the written rulebook: every clause a compressed
  accident, the profession's memory of what failure cost; also:
  economy (law and insurance), culture, history.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- No machine creates work. It trades force for distance or distance
  for force; the product is conserved at best, and friction taxes
  every real trade. Every "more out than in" claim ever tested
  resolved into an unmeasured inflow (the mirror holds the ledger).
- A material fails by load PER AREA, not load. The same chain that
  hoists an engine snaps as a thin wire; strength is a property of
  substance times cross-section, and the weakest section speaks
  for the whole part — a chain is exactly its weakest link.
- Stress crowds at sharpness. A small round hole in a loaded plate
  triples the local stress; a sharp inside corner does worse.
  Cracks are born at corners, which is why aircraft windows are
  round (the mirror holds the tuition receipt).
- Repeated load kills below the one-time strength. Steel endures
  forever only below roughly half its breaking stress; aluminum
  has no such floor — any repeated stress cracks it eventually,
  so aluminum parts are retired by count, not by condition.
- A beam's stiffness lives in its depth, cubed. Material far from
  the middle does the work; that one fact is the entire I-beam.
- Long thin columns fail by bowing, not crushing, at loads far
  below the material's strength — and the bowing load falls with
  the square of free length: double the unbraced run, quarter what
  it carries.
- Scale is not neutral: weight grows with the cube of size,
  strength of sections with the square. Everything built has a
  size beyond which its own weight breaks it, and a model proves
  shape, never strength.
- No engine beats the temperature-difference ceiling, ever, by any
  cleverness of mechanism (the formula is in SCIENCE). The cold
  side matters as much as the hot: cooling towers are half the
  power station.
- Pressure is blind and patient: it pushes on every square
  millimeter equally, so big lids at modest pressure carry
  crushing forces (worked in SCIENCE), and stored compressed gas
  is a spring wound to bomb tightness.
- Feedback beats strength. To hold a thing steady, do not build a
  stronger pusher — build a measurer and let the error command the
  push. But correction that arrives late and hard turns holding
  into hunting: the loop oscillates, and gentler gain, not more,
  is the cure.
- Parts interchange when dimensions are windows, not numbers, and
  every window is guarded by a gauge. Fit is a relation between
  two windows; no part need ever meet its partner before assembly.
- Everything is returning to ore. Iron rusts, and rust flakes off
  to expose fresh metal, so the reaction feeds itself; aluminum's
  oxide seals its own wound and stops. Maintenance is not upkeep
  of a finished thing — it is the toll the finished thing pays to
  keep existing.
- The safety factor is sized by ignorance, not by strength: the
  better the loads and material are known, the smaller it may be
  (aircraft fly at ~1.5); the worse they are known or the higher
  the price of failure, the larger it must be (ropes carrying
  people run ~10 and up).
- Design the failure. Every part will someday fail; the honest
  question is what happens next. A brake held open by the cable's
  own tension closes BY the cable breaking — the failure operates
  the safety (fail-safe). Denying failure is the failure.

## SCIENCE
The math, physics, and chemistry of engineering, held — not
mentioned. Each piece is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it
can follow, and where it earns it, a WORKED case proving the rule
runs.

### PHYSICS — the machine's bargain

LAW: work = force × distance. W = F·d, joules, newtons, meters.
  A machine conserves it: F₁d₁ = F₂d₂, minus friction's cut.
  Lever advantage = effort arm ÷ load arm. Pulley advantage =
  count of rope strands holding the load. Screw advantage = the
  circle the hand travels ÷ the thread's pitch.
RULE: to find the force a lever asks of the hand — load times its
  arm, divided by the hand's arm; the hand then travels the same
  ratio farther. Nothing is free; distance pays for force.
WORKED: a 900 N rock sits 0.5 m from the pivot; the crowbar's
  hand end is 2 m out. Moment to match: 900 × 0.5 = 450 N·m; the
  hand supplies 450 ÷ 2 = 225 N — a quarter of the load. The
  receipt: lifting the rock 4 cm moves the hand 16 cm, and
  900 × 0.04 = 36 J = 225 × 0.16. Work in equals work out, to
  the joule.
WORKED: a screw jack, 30 cm handle, 5 mm thread pitch. The hand's
  circle: 2π × 0.30 ≈ 1.88 m per turn; the load rises 0.005 m.
  Advantage: 1.88 ÷ 0.005 ≈ 377. A 50 N hand presses with
  50 × 377 ≈ 18,850 N — near two tonnes, ideal. Friction keeps
  half or more, and that is a FEATURE: the same friction holds the
  load when the hand lets go. A frictionless jack would unwind
  under the car. One arm lifts an automobile because of this
  arithmetic, and the automobile stays up because of the tax.

LAW: efficiencies multiply down a chain, never add.
WORKED: motor 0.90 × gearbox 0.95 × pump 0.70 = 0.60 — the well
  receives sixty percent of what the wall pays, and each stage's
  loss arrives as heat somewhere a designer must carry away.

### PHYSICS — friction and the wheel

LAW: friction force = grip coefficient × pressing weight.
  F = μN. Sliding μ: dry steel on steel ~0.6, wood on ground
  ~0.4, greased metal ~0.1. ROLLING μ: tire on road ~0.012,
  iron wheel on iron rail ~0.002.
RULE: to price a haul on the level — weight times the coefficient
  of the chosen contact. Changing the contact, not the strength of
  the puller, is the engineering move.
WORKED: move 1,000 kg (weight 9,800 N). Dragged on wood: 9,800 ×
  0.4 = 3,920 N — four strong people at their limit. On a cart on
  dirt (~0.06): 588 N — one person. On steel rails: 9,800 × 0.002
  ≈ 20 N — two fingers keep a tonne rolling. Same load, two
  hundredfold difference; the wheel-and-rail pair IS that
  arithmetic, and it is why railways moved the world's freight
  before engines were any good.

### PHYSICS — stress and the shape of strength

LAW: stress = force ÷ area. σ = F/A, in N/mm² (megapascals).
  Give points (~): mild steel yields 250, breaks 400 · aluminum
  alloy yields 270 · cast iron tears at ~150 but crushes at ~600 ·
  concrete crushes ~30, tears ~3 · wood along the grain ~40.
  Read the asymmetries — they decide what shape each material may
  take: stone and concrete must arch or be reinforced; steel may
  hang.
RULE: to size a member — allowed stress = strength ÷ safety
  factor; needed area = load ÷ allowed stress.
WORKED: hang a 2,000 kg machine (19,600 N) from mild steel at
  factor 5: allowed 250 ÷ 5 = 50 N/mm²; area 19,600 ÷ 50 =
  392 mm²; a round rod needs diameter √(4 × 392 ÷ π) ≈ 22.3 mm —
  call the catalog and buy 25.

LAW: a rectangular beam's stiffness goes as width × depth CUBED.
RULE: to stiffen, add depth, not width; to save weight, move the
  material to the top and bottom flanges and leave a thin web —
  that is the I-beam, derived, not invented.
WORKED: a 20 cm × 5 cm plank. Laid flat: 20 × 5³ = 2,500. Set on
  edge: 5 × 20³ = 40,000. Ratio 16 — the same plank, sixteen
  times stiffer turned on edge. Every floor joist stands on edge
  because of this number.

LAW: scale betrays. Weight ∝ size³, section strength ∝ size².
  Stress from self-weight therefore grows ∝ size.
WORKED: a footbridge scaled 3× in every dimension: 27× the
  weight carried by 9× the section — triple the self-stress. Keep
  scaling and the bridge fails under nothing but itself. This is
  why the elephant's legs are thick out of proportion, why a
  cathedral is mostly holding itself up, and why a tested model
  certifies a SHAPE and never a SIZE.

LAW: stress concentrates at geometry: ~3× at a small round hole
  in a pulled plate; more at sharper corners, without bound as
  the radius goes to zero.
RULE: round every inside corner; drill a round hole at a crack's
  tip to blunt it (the stop-drill — adding a hole to SAVE the
  part, correct and provable by this law).

### PHYSICS — the pressure vessel

LAW: the wall of a cylinder feels hoop stress = pressure × radius
  ÷ wall thickness (σ = P·r/t); a flat cover feels force =
  pressure × its whole area.
RULE: wall thickness = pressure × radius × safety factor ÷
  strength. The law says danger scales with SIZE at the same
  pressure — a big mild vessel outguns a small fierce one.
WORKED, the same law at three sizes:
  · kitchen canner: 1 atmosphere gauge (0.1 N/mm²), radius
    150 mm, wall 3 mm → stress 0.1 × 150 ÷ 3 = 5 N/mm² — the
    aluminum holds ~20× more; that margin is why the pot is
    boring, and boring is the achievement.
  · factory boiler: 10 atmospheres (1.0 N/mm²), radius 750 mm,
    allowed 50 N/mm² → wall = 1.0 × 750 ÷ 50 = 15 mm of steel.
  · the lid: a 40 cm round cover at those 10 atmospheres carries
    π × 0.2² × 1,000,000 ≈ 125,700 N — nearly thirteen tonnes
    sitting on the manhole. Bolts, not habit, hold it down.
WORKED, why a boiler is a bomb and a burst pipe is a nuisance:
  a tonne of water held liquid at 180°C carries 80° × 4.18 kJ/kg
  = 334 kJ/kg above the open-air boil — 334 MJ in the tank,
  roughly 80 kg of TNT. Break the wall and ~15% flashes to steam
  instantly (334 ÷ 2,257), about 150 kg — a quarter of a
  thousand cubic meters of steam born in a room in a heartbeat.
  Water stores the energy; compressed gas IS the energy — which
  is why proof tests are done with water (it barely compresses,
  so a failure spits, not detonates) and never with air.

### PHYSICS — the engine and its ceiling

LAW: no heat engine converts more than the temperature drop
  allows: best fraction = 1 − T_cold ÷ T_hot, temperatures in
  kelvin (°C + 273). Not a friction problem, not a design
  problem — a law. Real machines get half to two thirds of it.
RULE: to bound any engine before believing any claim about it —
  convert its hottest and coldest to kelvin, divide, subtract
  from one. A claim above the ceiling is a perpetual-motion claim
  wearing overalls (the mirror holds that ledger).
WORKED: old locomotive, steam 180°C (453 K) against 100°C
  exhaust (373 K): ceiling 1 − 373/453 = 0.18 — and real ones
  delivered 6-8%, hauling a century of freight on less than a
  tenth of the coal's heat. Modern steam plant, 550°C (823 K)
  against a 27°C river (300 K): ceiling 1 − 300/823 = 0.64,
  real ~0.40. The whole history of power is the hot side
  climbing and the materials (see below) being begged to allow
  it. "Waste heat" is not sloppiness; it is the law's rent.

### CHEMISTRY — materials as arrangement, not substance

LAW: iron with under ~2% carbon dissolved in it is steel; more is
  cast iron. Tenths of a percent of carbon move strength by
  hundreds of N/mm² — composition is a dial, and the material is
  the SETTING, not the element.
LAW: heat-treatment is chemistry run on a clock. Quench hot steel
  (from ~800°C, fast, in water or oil) and the carbon is trapped
  in a strained arrangement — glass-hard, glass-brittle. Reheat
  gently — temper, 200-350°C — and trade hardness back for
  toughness by the degree. The smith reads the temperature by the
  oxide film's color: straw ~230°C, blue ~300°C — a thermometer
  grown by the reaction itself. One bar of steel is a family of
  materials, chosen after the shape is made.
LAW: rust is iron + oxygen + water, and the product occupies
  several times the metal's volume, so it swells, flakes, and
  exposes fresh iron — a self-feeding reaction. Aluminum oxidizes
  instantly into a film that seals; that difference in the OXIDE,
  not in the strength, decides what survives outdoors. Zinc on
  steel corrodes first, by chemical preference, spending itself
  to protect what it coats — the sacrificial layer.
LAW: concrete sets by reaction with water — hydration — not by
  drying; it hardens under water and gains strength for weeks.
  It crushes at ~30 N/mm² and tears at ~3, so alone it may only
  arch; steel bars laid inside carry the tension, and the pair
  works ONLY because their heat-expansions nearly match
  (~12 vs ~10 ×10⁻⁶ per °C) — a lucky coincidence of chemistry
  that underwrites every reinforced floor on earth.

LAW: things swell with heat: growth = coefficient × length ×
  degrees. Steel: 12 ×10⁻⁶ per °C.
RULE: give the growth somewhere to go, or it becomes force:
  locked-in stress = stiffness × coefficient × degrees
  (steel's stiffness E = 200,000 N/mm²).
WORKED: a 500 m steel bridge, winter −20°C to summer +40°C
  (ΔT = 60): growth 12e-6 × 500 × 60 = 0.36 m. The expansion
  joint under the roadway is that 36 cm, built in. Weld the
  bridge solid instead and the season delivers 200,000 × 12e-6
  × 60 = 144 N/mm² — over half of mild steel's give point, from
  weather alone, before the first truck arrives.

### MATHEMATICS — the safety factor, the window, the count

LAW: safety factor = what it can bear ÷ what it will be asked.
  The going rates (~): aircraft structure 1.5 · machines 2 ·
  pressure vessels 4 · cranes and hoists 5 · ropes that carry
  people 10-12. The factor prices IGNORANCE and CONSEQUENCE, not
  strength.
WORKED: a passenger elevator, car plus load 2,000 kg (19,600 N),
  rope factor 11: the ropes together must hold 19,600 × 11 ≈
  216,000 N — twenty-two tonnes for a two-tonne job — split over
  several ropes, each typically able to hold the car alone, with
  a brake underneath that needs no rope at all. Elevator falls
  went from common to nearly extinct on this arithmetic, and the
  arithmetic costs only steel.

LAW: a dimension is a window: 25.00 ±0.02 mm names every part
  from 24.98 to 25.02, and fit is a relation between two windows.
RULE: to guarantee assembly of parts that never met — keep the
  shaft's whole window below the hole's whole window (clearance),
  or wholly above it (press). Check the two worst cases only;
  the middles take care of themselves.
WORKED: hole 25.00 to 25.02, shaft 24.96 to 24.98. Tightest pair:
  25.00 − 24.98 = 0.02 mm of air; loosest: 25.02 − 24.96 =
  0.06 mm. Every shaft from the line fits every hole from the
  line, forever, sight unseen. That small subtraction is
  interchangeable parts, the spare-parts trade, and the assembly
  line — the factory system is two intervals that do not overlap.

LAW: fatigue is a count, and counts compound fast: a part
  stressed once a second sees 86,400 × 365 = 31,536,000 cycles a
  year.
RULE: rotating and vibrating parts are designed to the endurance
  floor (~half of breaking stress, for steel), never the one-time
  strength; aluminum parts get an inspection calendar or a
  retirement count instead, because no floor exists for them.

### THE CONTROL LOOP — the governor of everything

LAW: error = want − is; the actuator pushes against the error.
  The loop: measure the output, compare to the setpoint, act,
  measure again. The machine that measures ITSELF needs no
  operator.
RULE: to hold any quantity — temperature, speed, level, course —
  build the loop, then respect its lag: act too hard on stale
  news and the correction overshoots, the overshoot begets a
  counter-correction, and the system hunts. Soften the push or
  quicken the news.
WORKED: an oven set to 200°C with a ±5° switch: heater on below
  195, off above 205, and momentum in the hot element carries the
  swing a few degrees past each trip — the oven actually lives
  between ~192 and ~208. The device promises the BAND, not the
  number, and every on/off controller from freezer to furnace
  draws this same sawtooth. The flyball governor (1788) closes
  the same loop with no electricity: engine speeds up → whirling
  weights fly outward → the linkage chokes the steam → the
  engine slows itself. Thermostat, governor, float valve,
  autopilot, the body at 37°C: one loop, many costumes.

## METHODS
How things are done and built here — each with what governs it and
what breaks it. The prepositioned how-to-build.

- FOLLOW THE LOAD PATH: trace every force from where it enters to
  the ground; size every member it crosses by stress = force ÷
  area. A force with no traced path to ground finds its own, and
  its choice is never good. Breaks when: a path is assumed
  instead of traced (the mirror's collapsed roofs).
- TRIANGULATE: pin-jointed squares fold; triangles cannot without
  a side changing length. A truss is triangles all the way down,
  turning a spanning problem into pure push-and-pull members that
  the σ = F/A arithmetic sizes directly.
- DESIGN TO THE WEAKEST SECTION: the part IS its weakest
  cross-section, its sharpest corner, its worst weld. Strengthen
  there or nowhere; metal added elsewhere is dead weight.
- ROUND THE CORNERS, BLUNT THE CRACKS: fillet inside corners;
  stop-drill a running crack. Governed by the concentration law;
  breaks when a "cosmetic" sharp notch is let into a loaded skin.
- BUILD THE LOOP, NOT THE MUSCLE: hold steady by feedback. Choose
  the on/off switch where a band is tolerable (ovens), smooth
  proportional push where hunting costs (steering); always honor
  the lag rule.
- PROOF-TEST: load the real thing past its working load before
  service — pressure vessels to 1.5× with water, never air, the
  crane with dead weight before live ones. Measurement outranks
  confidence; the test is cheap and the alternative is the
  mirror.
- GAUGE, DON'T MEASURE, ON THE LINE: two gauges per window — the
  GO end must fit, the NO-GO must not. A yes/no answered in one
  second by anyone, no number read, no skill required: quality
  made mechanical.
- TEMPER TO THE JOB: quench for hardness, then temper back
  toughness by temperature — the file left hard and brittle, the
  spring drawn to blue. Breaks when hardness is worshiped: the
  hard part that meets a shock shatters (the mirror's brittle
  fractures).
- MAINTAIN AGAINST THE RETURN TO ORE: paint, grease, inspect,
  replace on schedule. Corrosion and fatigue both compound
  quietly and show late; the calendar, not the appearance, is
  the instrument.
- MAKE FAILURE OPERATE THE SAFETY: hold the brake OPEN with the
  hoist rope's own tension, so the rope's break IS the brake's
  application; melt the fuse BY the overload it protects
  against. The failure mode is a design input, not an
  embarrassment.
- GIVE MOVEMENT SOMEWHERE TO GO: expansion joints sized by the
  swell law; slots, not holes, where seasons pull; flexible
  couplings where shafts disagree. Forbid the movement and
  receive the force.
- ITERATE ON RECEIPTS: version two is designed by version one's
  failures. The method is honest only when the failure is
  measured and filed with its conditions (the mirror is the
  other half of this method — that is what it is FOR).

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE MEASURING TOOLS: the rule, the level, the plumb line; the
  micrometer reading to 0.01 mm; gauge blocks true to a tenth of
  a thousandth of a millimeter, stacked to build any length —
  measurement is the ground truth under interchange, and the
  gauge room is the factory's supreme court.
- THE DRAWING: the carrier of intent — every dimension with its
  window, every material named, every corner's radius stated. A
  part can be made a century later on another continent from a
  good drawing; that permanence is the point.
- THE MOTHER MACHINES: the lathe makes rounds and threads — and
  can make the parts of another lathe, the trick that
  bootstrapped the machine age; the mill makes flats and slots;
  the forge moves hot metal; the caster pours what cutting
  wastes. Each holds its work stiller than a hand ever could —
  precision is mostly rigidity.
- THE POWER LADDER, in watts: a laboring person sustains ~75; a
  horse ~750; a water wheel thousands; a small car engine
  ~75,000 — a thousand people under the hood — and a power
  station a billion-plus. Each rung rebuilt what work meant (the
  economy phylum holds that side).
- THE FASTENER SHELF: the bolt clamps and can be undone — its
  thread is a wedge wound in a circle, holding by the screw's
  friction tax; the rivet is permanent and, filling a hole,
  arrests a running crack at its seam; the weld makes two parts
  one — strongest, and crack's open highway (the mirror holds
  the ships that taught this). Choose by which failure and which
  repair you can afford.
- THE CODES AND STANDARDS: written margins, tested details,
  mandatory inspections — the profession's memory made
  enforceable. A young engineer's access to everyone's dead (the
  boiler code was written in the ash of exploded factories; the
  mirror keeps that ledger).

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- MULTIPLY THE ARM: move what muscle cannot — the lever family's
  door, the oldest one; pyramid stones to engine hoists.
- CARRY ACROSS AND HOLD UP: span the river, roof the hall, raise
  the tower against wind and its own cube-law weight.
- HOLD IN, HOLD OUT: vessel, dam, hull, wall — pressure and water
  told where they may and may not be.
- TURN HEAT AND FALL INTO WORK: engine, mill, turbine, motor —
  the door where physics' ceiling law collects its rent.
- MAKE MANY, ALL ALIKE: interchange and the tolerance window —
  the door through which repair became a swap and craft became
  industry.
- MAKE IT RUN ITSELF: the control loop's door — from the float
  valve to the autopilot, attention replaced by feedback.
- MAKE FAILURE SURVIVABLE: the safety factor, the proof test, the
  fail-safe, the code — engineering's promise to strangers who
  will never know whose arithmetic they stand on.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- The lever family first, before writing: ramps, wedges, levers
  raised the monuments; the bargain (force for distance) was used
  for millennia before it was stated once.
- The wheel, and the surprise about it: the disc is easy; the
  PAIR — wheel on smooth hard road, iron on iron rail — is the
  invention. The 200× arithmetic in SCIENCE was harvested only
  where surfaces were built to collect it.
- The arch, compression's loophole: stone tears at a tenth of its
  crushing strength, so antiquity learned to make every load a
  squeeze — aqueducts, domes, bridges standing twenty centuries
  without a gram of steel in tension.
- Steel's price collapse: cheap air-blown steel (1856) dropped
  the price of a ton to roughly a sixth within a generation, and
  structure changed species — rails, frames, towers, all from
  one process change. Materials history IS price history.
- Power and self-control born together: the steam engine needed
  the governor (1788) within its first generation — the machine
  strong enough to wreck itself demanded the loop that watches
  it; power and feedback have been a pair ever since.
- The boiler dead and the written code: a century of explosions —
  in the worst years, one every few days somewhere — until one
  factory's 58 dead (1905) bought the rulebook (1915): margins,
  materials, inspections, mandatory. Safety became arithmetic
  with the force of law; the mirror keeps the full ledger.
- Interchange, chased for decades: armories wanted swappable
  parts long before shops could hold the windows; the gauge, not
  genius, finally delivered it, and mass production followed as
  a corollary.
- Fatigue learned in the air: the count-not-condition law was
  paid for at altitude by the first jet airliners — the mirror
  holds the receipt; every round window since is the law flying.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward. Each names
what runs along it.

- PHYSICS: force, work, energy conservation, friction, the
  engine's temperature ceiling, pressure — engineering is physics
  with a budget, a deadline, and consequences for being wrong.
- CHEMISTRY: what materials ARE — carbon's dial in iron,
  tempering's clock, rust's self-feeding loop, cement's set,
  combustion's heat; along this path travels every answer to
  "why did it corrode, harden, burn, or stick".
- MATHEMATICS: the ratio (mechanical advantage), the square and
  cube (scale's betrayal, the beam's depth), intervals (the
  tolerance window), compounding counts (fatigue) — the phylum
  engineering computes WITH.
- COOKING: engineering's export floor — the canner is a pressure
  vessel at 1 atmosphere gauge, the oven thermostat is the
  control loop, the knife is force ÷ area; cooking's altitude and
  pressure laws come back along this path as field data.
- ECONOMY: price picks the material more often than strength
  does; interchange created the factory and the spare-parts
  trade; the safety factor is bought in steel and fuel; codes
  are insurance written as law.
- BODY: bone is a beam that thickens along its stress lines,
  muscle is a ~75 W engine, joints are bearings with a friction
  coefficient machines still envy, the body's 37°C is a control
  loop — and the cube law caps animal size exactly as it caps
  towers.
- MEDICINE: implants must survive salt water at 37°C for decades
  (corrosion's hardest exam), the sterilizer is a small
  autoclave-boiler, dosage margins are safety factors on flesh.
- FARMING: the plow is a wedge, the mill and the pump are the
  first big machines, irrigation is pipe-and-head arithmetic,
  and the tractor's 75,000 W retired the horse's 750.
- WEATHER: wind and snow are the loads that size roofs and
  towers; the seasonal ΔT sizes every expansion joint (worked in
  SCIENCE); humidity drives the rust reaction; freeze-thaw is
  water's 9% expansion demolishing masonry on a yearly clock.
- LIFE: the other iterating designer — evolution runs the same
  loop (variant, test, keep receipts) with more time and no
  fresh starts; trees are pre-tensioned columns, honeycomb is
  material moved away from the middle; what transfers and what
  does not is an open border (the mirror holds its dotted line).
- CULTURE: the cathedral, the bridge, the tower as statements;
  the engineer's ethic — margins owed to strangers — as a moral
  artifact; and the code as culture's way of remembering its
  accidents after the witnesses die.
- ANIMALS: hooves, wings, and spider silk out-engineer us; biomimicry copies them.
- ASTRONOMY: telescopes and spacecraft are engineering at its precision limit.
- BUILDING: structures are engineering's oldest branch; the load path is shared law.
- COMPUTING: the chip is engineering's smallest artifact; control code runs the machines.
- CONFLICT: fortification and armament drove materials and precision for centuries.
- CRAFT: engineering is craft with calculation; the prototype is handwork.
- DESIGN: form and function negotiate in every made thing.
- EARTH: foundations, tunnels, and dams engage rock on its terms.
- ELECTRONICS: power and signal are engineering's nervous system.
- FLIGHT: the aircraft is the integrated exam: structures, engines, control at once.
- GOVERNANCE: codes and standards are engineering law; infrastructure is public works.
- MEASUREMENT: tolerance and calibration — engineering is measurement enforced.
- MOVEMENT: prosthetics and sports gear engineer the body's levers.
- MUSIC: halls, speakers, and instruments are acoustic engineering.
- PHILOSOPHY: the safety factor is institutionalized doubt — engineering's epistemology.
- PLANTS: timber's grain and fiber composites — grown materials engineered.
- TEACHING: the worked example and the design review are engineering's classrooms.
- TRANSPORT: vehicles and their ways are engineering in motion.
- UPKEEP: inspection schedules and fatigue life — maintenance is designed in or paid for.
