## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- SPEECH — the first carrier: knowledge moving mouth to ear, alive
  only while a living head holds it; also lives in: the body (the
  voice, the childhood learning window), life.
- WRITING — the technology that lets the dead teach: knowledge
  parted from its holder and parked on a material; also:
  engineering (the media), economy (born from accounting),
  mathematics (notation is writing for quantities).
- THE STORY — knowledge shaped for memory: cause, actor, and
  consequence packed so a head keeps it whole; also: the body
  (memory's native format), cooking (every recipe was one first).
- METER, RHYME, AND SONG — speech given a checksum: a wrong word
  breaks the beat and is HEARD; the oral world's error
  correction; also: the body (rhythm), mathematics (period).
- RITUAL AND FESTIVAL — knowledge performed on schedule so it
  refreshes; the calendar driving rehearsal; also: farming (the
  harvest feast is the year's exam), the table.
- THE RECIPE — the inherited claim in its purest form: do this,
  under these conditions, and the outcome repeats; law, craft
  lore, and cooking instructions are one thing in three coats;
  also: cooking, medicine (the prescription), engineering (the
  specification).
- LAW — recipes for disputes: inherited claims with conditions,
  written so strangers can predict each other; also: economy
  (contract), medicine (the dose that is also a limit).
- THE TABLE — the bond made of shared food; the one ceremony
  every culture keeps: trust is built by eating together before
  it is built any other way; also: cooking, the body.
- THE ROAD, THE SHIP, THE CARAVAN — the carriers: knowledge moves
  at the speed of whatever carries its holders; trade routes are
  knowledge routes; also: engineering, economy, weather (the
  monsoon sets the sailing schedule).
- THE SCHOOL AND THE APPRENTICESHIP — transmission made
  deliberate: batch copying (school) and high-fidelity single
  copying (master to heir); also: economy (the guild), the body
  (hands learn what books cannot yet say).
- THE LIBRARY AND THE ARCHIVE — copies gathered and kept; the
  species' external memory, exactly as strong as its upkeep;
  also: engineering (the media), economy (upkeep is a budget
  line, and that fact has killed libraries).
- THE CALENDAR — time cut into agreed pieces so strangers can
  meet, plant, pay, and remember on the same day; also: farming
  (its first customer), mathematics and physics (the year's true
  length), weather.
- STANDARDS — shared weights, measures, coin, and spelling: the
  agreements that let two strangers' numbers add up; also:
  economy, engineering, mathematics.
- LANGUAGE — the medium itself: thousands of them, drifting,
  splitting, borrowing; the map of who talked to whom; also: the
  body (learned young or hardly at all), life.
- MONUMENTS, GRAVES, AND MIDDENS — the unintended record: what a
  people threw away, built, and buried testifies after their
  chronicles are gone; also: chemistry and physics (the decay
  clocks), farming (pollen and seeds in the layers).
- TOOLS — frozen knowledge: a plow or a pot is a method cast in
  material, readable by anyone who can hold it; also:
  engineering, cooking.
- MEASUREMENT — the instrument that replaces lore: it turns
  "until it feels right" into a number that can be written,
  argued, and inherited; also: physics, cooking (the
  thermometer), medicine (the trial), every craft in turn.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- Lore dies with its holders. Unwritten knowledge survives only
  as long as an unbroken chain of living teachers; break the
  chain once and it is gone as if it never was. The holder
  arithmetic in SCIENCE prices the risk; the mirror holds the
  graveyard.
- Writing lets the dead teach. A clay tablet from ~3,750 years
  ago still gives cookable stew instructions; the writer is dust
  and the method works tonight. Condition: someone must still
  read the script — an unreadable record is lore that died
  differently (the Rosetta entry in METHODS).
- Knowledge moves at carrier speed — and methods slower than
  goods. Cargo changes hands at every relay; a method moves only
  when a person who can DO it moves and stays. Paper crossed
  Asia as cargo in months and as a craft in ~1,300 years
  (SCIENCE holds the numbers).
- Trade routes are knowledge routes. Spice roads moved methods
  with the goods: numerals, paper, noodles, gunpowder, crops,
  and diseases all traveled the same tracks as the pepper —
  at holder speed.
- Method outruns why, usually by centuries. Bread rose ~5,000
  years before microbes were named; smiths quench-hardened steel
  ~2,000 years before carbon's role was found; sailors drank
  lime juice 185 years before vitamin C had a name. A working
  method needs no theory to feed people — and cannot defend
  itself against fashion until the why arrives.
- Measurement replaces lore in the same order in every craft:
  the instrument arrives, the number replaces the proverb, the
  argument ends, and the knowledge stops dying with its holders
  — because a number files and a thumb does not. Cooking (the
  thermometer), navigation (the chronometer), medicine (the
  trial) all walked this stair, each at its own century.
- Verbatim memory is a writing property. Oral tradition keeps
  the story's SHAPE — plot, names, formulas — while every
  telling rebuilds the words; singers recompose, not recite.
  Meter narrows the drift but never stops it.
- Redundancy, not toughness, is what survives. What outlived
  every burning was whatever had been copied widely and
  scattered; the strongest vault with the only copy is one fire
  from silence. The dispersion arithmetic is in SCIENCE.
- Standards let strangers cooperate. Shared weights, coin, and
  calendar turn a stranger into a trading partner because both
  sides can check the deal without trusting each other; every
  empire's first exports are its measures.
- Cities compound knowledge; villages keep it. Meeting-pairs
  grow with the square of the people (SCIENCE), so density
  multiplies the collisions ideas breed by — and burns through
  traditions a village would keep for centuries.
- A surplus buys the specialists. Every full-time scribe, smith,
  or teacher is fed by someone else's extra harvest; where the
  surplus fails, the specialists disband and their unwritten
  knowledge follows the holder law down.
- Adoption lags proof by a generation or two when the proof
  indicts the practitioners. The scurvy cure waited 48 years
  from trial to fleet; the pattern recurs wherever accepting the
  number means admitting the old way cost lives. Condition: the
  lag shortens when the proof flatters instead of accuses.
- Conquest changes rulers faster than kitchens. Language, faith,
  and law impose from above only through children and schools,
  over two to three generations; the food and speech of the
  majority usually outlast the conqueror's decree. The mirror
  holds decree's failures and the drain condition beside them.
- The ground audits the chronicle. What people buried, ate, and
  threw away is testimony they never edited; where written
  record and midden disagree, dig into the claim, not the
  midden.

## SCIENCE
The math, physics, and chemistry of keeping and moving knowledge,
held — not mentioned. Each piece is a LAW a query can compute
with, a RULE it can follow, and where it earns it, a WORKED case
proving the rule runs.

### MATHEMATICS — surviving the copy

LAW: fidelity compounds. survival = f^n, where f is the fraction
  of detail kept per hand-off and n the hand-offs in the chain.
  Rough carrier grades: retelling f ≈ 0.9 · careful scribal copy
  f ≈ 0.99 · print within an edition f = 1 (every copy is the
  first copy).
RULE: to price a transmission chain — raise the carrier's
  fidelity to the power of the chain's DEPTH (hand-offs in
  sequence, not copies in parallel).
WORKED: 100 details through 10 retellings: 100 × 0.9^10 ≈ 35
  survive — two-thirds gone by the tenth teller. The same 100
  through 10 scribal copyings: 100 × 0.99^10 ≈ 90. Through a
  print run of 10,000: 100 — the run is depth 1, however wide.
  This one law is why oral genealogies bend, why old manuscripts
  disagree, and why print ended a whole class of argument.

LAW: copying errors ride depth, not width. A scribal lineage
  stacks errors generation on generation; a press makes its
  errors once and repeats them identically — which makes them
  FINDABLE against rival editions, and a correction fixes all
  future printings at a stroke.
WORKED: a careful scribe slips ~1 error per 10 pages; a 200-page
  book takes ~20 new errors per copying, so ten generations of
  copies carry ~200 accumulated errors — and no two lineages the
  same errors, which is how editors rebuild lost originals:
  where independent lines agree, the ancestor spoke.

LAW: a craft's survival is a redundancy bet.
  P(one generation) = 1 − (1−p)^k, for k independent holders
  each with chance p of leaving a trained heir; over g
  generations, raise it to the g.
RULE: to price a craft's future — count its true holders, not
  its admirers; raise the one-generation survival to the
  generations ahead.
WORKED: one master, 90% likely to train an heir each generation:
  0.9^10 ≈ 35% odds of surviving ten generations — a coin flip
  against oblivion. Three independent masters: per-generation
  survival 1 − 0.1³ = 0.999, ten generations 0.999^10 ≈ 99%.
  Two extra holders move a craft from likely-dead to nearly-safe
  — the arithmetic under every guild and seminary that insisted
  on multiple lines, and under the mirror's lost-knowledge
  ledger, where the counts were low.

LAW: erasure must beat dispersion. An idea in N copies survives
  a purge catching fraction q of them with N × (1−q) seeds left,
  and any survivor recompounds by the spread law below.
WORKED: 10,000 scattered pamphlets, a 99% seizure: 100 survive —
  the idea returns. Twenty sacred books, all in known temples: a
  single decree reaches every copy. Erasure works exactly where
  copies are few and central — the mirror's burning ledger runs
  this arithmetic case by case; the four surviving Maya books
  are its receipt.

### MATHEMATICS — the spread of what is known

LAW: adoption compounds, then meets its ceiling.
  While rare: N = N₀ × 2^(t ÷ doubling time). Near the ceiling K
  (everyone who ever would adopt), growth slows and stops — the
  S-curve.
RULE: to project an idea's spread — count the doublings that fit
  the time while adopters are scarce; stop trusting the doubling
  as N approaches K.
WORKED: the printing press: one town (Mainz, ~1450) to ~250
  towns with presses by ~1500 — eight doublings in fifty years,
  one per ~6 years, compound the whole way. The same law runs
  rumor through a market square: one teller, converts doubling
  by the hour, 2^10 ≈ 1,000 heads by evening — why a lie must be
  answered the same day, and why the mirror files "each new
  medium will make everyone wise" among the claims that never
  held: the law moves whatever copies well, true or not.

LAW: knowledge speed = carrier speed; methods at holder speed.
  Rough carrier grades, km per day: foot ~30 · camel caravan ~40
  · horse relay ~250 · ocean sail ~150-300 · telegraph and
  after: the day collapses to minutes.
WORKED: the Silk Road's ~7,000 km at caravan pace is 175 days of
  travel — yet paper, invented in China ~105 CE, reached
  Samarkand's workshops ~751 (the story says with captured
  papermakers — a method jumping when holders moved), Spain
  ~1150, Italy ~1270s, Germany ~1390: ~1,300 years for the CRAFT
  to walk a road its cargo crossed in a season — about 5 km a
  year. Goods relay hand to hand; a method waits for a maker to
  move, settle, and train. The relay's other proof, computed
  from the grades above: a horse post at ~250 km a day covers
  the same 7,000 km in 28 days — fast enough for orders, never
  for crafts.

LAW: meeting-pairs grow with the square.
  Possible pairs among n people = n(n−1) ÷ 2.
WORKED: a table of 6 holds 15 possible conversations; a village
  of 150 holds 11,175; a city of 100,000 holds ~5 billion. Ideas
  breed in collisions, so the city out-collides the village by
  five orders of magnitude — the whole history of why invention
  clusters in cities, and why six is the size where everyone can
  still hear everyone.

LAW: the surplus arithmetic. specialists supportable ≈
  households × surplus fraction.
WORKED: ten farm households each raising 10% beyond their own
  need free exactly one household from farming: 10 × 0.10 = 1.
  That household is the village's smith OR its priest OR its
  teacher — not all three. A town of 5,000 households at the
  same surplus frees 500: a school, a guild hall, a scriptorium.
  Every archive in history stands on someone else's extra grain,
  and falls when the grain does.

### MATHEMATICS — language's own numbers

LAW: word frequencies follow rank's inverse (Zipf).
  frequency of rank r ≈ frequency of rank 1 ÷ r.
WORKED: English's top word ("the") is ~7% of running text; rank
  2 runs ~3.5%, rank 70 ~0.1%. Consequence, computable: a small
  core does most of the talking — the top ~1,000 words cover
  roughly three-quarters of ordinary text — why a phrasebook
  works at all, and why translation's true cost hides in the
  long tail where words are rare, local, and loaded (the
  mirror's standing cannot lives there).

LAW: related languages drift apart on a rough clock.
  Two tongues from one ancestor each keep ~86% of a fixed
  100-word core list per 1,000 years; shared core after t
  millennia ≈ 0.86^(2t). A band, not a constant — the mirror
  holds where the constant-rate assumption broke.
WORKED: 1,000 years after the split, sisters share ~0.86² ≈ 74
  of the hundred; after 2,000 years, ~0.86⁴ ≈ 55. Runs backward
  as an instrument: count the shared core between two languages
  and read a rough age for their parting — lore about "old
  tongues" replaced by arithmetic with stated error.

LAW: literacy's entry price is the sign count.
  Alphabet ~26 signs · syllabary ~50-100 (Japanese kana: 46) ·
  logographic ~3,000 for a newspaper.
WORKED: 3,000 ÷ 26 ≈ 115 — the alphabet cut the memorization
  toll a hundredfold, and mass literacy followed the cheap
  scripts wherever schooling met them. Condition: the sign count
  prices ENTRY only; the logographic script pays back later by
  crossing dialects that share writing but not speech.

### MATHEMATICS AND ASTRONOMY — the calendar's arithmetic

LAW: the year is 365.2422 days; any whole-number calendar drifts
  by its remainder. drift = years × |calendar mean − 365.2422|.
RULE: to audit a calendar — compute its mean year from its leap
  rule, subtract the true year, multiply by the centuries in its
  service.
WORKED: the Julian mean is 365.25 — error 0.0078 day/year, one
  full day per ~128 years. From the year 325 to 1582 that is
  1,257 years × 0.0078 ≈ 9.8 days — precisely the ten days the
  Gregorian reform struck from October 1582 (the 4th was
  followed by the 15th). The repaired rule (drop 3 leap days per
  400 years) gives a mean of 365.2425 — error 0.0003, one day
  per ~3,300 years. A civilization's timekeeping audited and
  fixed with arithmetic a farmer could check: measurement
  replacing lore at the scale of centuries.

### PHYSICS AND CHEMISTRY — reading the dead directly

LAW: living things hold carbon-14 at the air's level; death
  stops the intake and the stock halves every 5,730 years.
  age = 5,730 × log₂(starting amount ÷ remaining amount).
RULE: to date organic remains — measure the remaining fraction,
  count the halvings, multiply by 5,730. Useful to ~50,000 years
  (nine halvings leave ~1/500, the practical floor).
WORKED: hearth charcoal holding ¼ of its carbon-14: two halvings
  × 5,730 = 11,460 years old. This instrument, arriving in 1949,
  did to the deep past what the thermometer did to the kitchen:
  king-lists stopped being the only clock, and arguments about
  "older than" ended in a number.

LAW: a tree lays one ring per year; ring-width patterns overlap
  from log to older log into an unbroken year-exact ladder
  ~12,000 years back.
RULE: to correct the carbon clock — date wood twice, by rings
  and by carbon, and use the rings (exact) to calibrate the
  carbon (drifting with the air's history). Measurement auditing
  measurement — the mature form of the instrument stair.

LAW: deeper is older, unless disturbed — the layer rule that
  turns any undisturbed ground into an ordered archive.

LAW: media trade lifespan against capacity, all down the record:
  fired clay ~millennia (fire HARDENS it — the burning that
  killed a palace baked its library durable) · stone ~millennia,
  words per ton: few · parchment ~1,000+ years · rag paper
  ~centuries · acid wood-pulp paper (1850-1990) ~50-150 years,
  eating itself · tape and film ~decades · digital media ~5-20
  years PLUS a format that dies on a ~10-year clock and takes
  its readability with it.
RULE: to keep something a century, pick the medium by this table
  or pick many media at once; the mirror holds the
  eternal-medium want, still open.

## METHODS
How keeping and moving are done here — each with what governs it,
when it applies, and what breaks it.

- MEMORY WITH A CHECKSUM (meter, rhyme, formula, song): bind the
  words to a beat so damage announces itself. Preserves shape,
  not wording (the claim above). Breaks silently when the
  language drifts under the poem until the checksum lines stop
  scanning and nobody hears the alarm.
- APPRENTICESHIP (watch, copy, be corrected, repeat): the
  high-fidelity channel for knowledge hands hold and words miss.
  Governed by the holder law — one master, few heirs, the whole
  line a redundancy bet. Breaks when the heirs die first, the
  guild hoards (secrecy lowers k by design — the mirror's Greek
  fire entry is the receipt), or the market fails before the
  training finishes.
- WRITING IT DOWN: park the knowledge outside the skull.
  Conditions, all three load-bearing: a script someone will
  still read, a medium that outlives the need (the media table),
  and copying upkeep — writing is not preservation; writing PLUS
  recopying is.
- COLLATION (compare the copy-lines): where independent lineages
  agree, the ancestor spoke; where they differ, an error shows
  its generation. The depth law made into a repair tool.
- PRINT (depth to width): compose once, copy identically,
  correct once for all future copies. Broke the scribal error
  stack and collapsed a book's labor price ~300-fold (a
  1,000-copy edition: ~137 scribe-years against ~167 press-crew
  days). Scribes kept the unique and the ornate — the surpassed
  method stays for the queries it still answers best.
- TRANSLATION: recruit a text into another language at a loss
  priced by the word's neighborhood (the mirror holds the
  standing cannot). Travels best: numbers, parts, procedures.
  Travels worst: jokes, prayers, poems. Method: translate
  meaning-first, keep the original alongside, say what was paid
  — a translation that hides its losses is a copy error wearing
  a diploma.
- PROVENANCE (the citation chain): every claim carries where it
  came from, so any reader can walk back and audit. The
  difference between inheritance and rumor; the journal (1665)
  made it an institution, and this fabric's own HOLDS lines are
  its descendant.
- SCHOOLING (batch transmission): one teacher, ~30 pupils, every
  year — fidelity below apprenticeship, reach beyond it a
  hundredfold; the only method that ever made literacy a
  majority condition. Needs the surplus arithmetic paid first.
  Breaks when it teaches the checksum without the cargo —
  recited formulas, empty of the method they carried.
- STANDARDIZING (weights, measures, calendar, spelling): agree
  once so every record afterward adds up across distance and
  time. Breaks local fit on purpose and pays for it in reach;
  half of empire is this method wearing a crown.
- THE ARCHIVE'S RULE (many copies, many places, many materials):
  survival by dispersion, per the erasure law. What survived the
  ancient libraries was what had been copied OUT of them; the
  unique holdings died with the buildings. Breaks when budget
  fails — an archive is upkeep, forever.
- THE AUDIT OF THE GROUND (archaeology): read middens, layers,
  rings, and isotopes as testimony nobody edited; check the
  chronicle against it. The measurement stair applied to history
  itself — the past got its instruments last, in the same order
  as every craft.
- THE TRIAL (comparison replacing testimony): 1747, HMS
  Salisbury: twelve scurvied sailors, six pairs, six treatments,
  same diet otherwise — the citrus pair recovered, and the
  method that ends argument-by-anecdote was on deck. Adopted
  fleet-wide 48 years later; the lag is itself a filed claim.
- RITUAL AS SCHEDULED REHEARSAL: the calendar drives
  re-performance so stored knowledge refreshes before it fades —
  the feast, the recitation, the drill. Breaks into empty form
  when the rehearsal outlives its cargo; the mirror holds the
  unsettled question of why the empty form persists anyway.
- KEEP WHAT YOU CANNOT READ (the Rosetta method): an unreadable
  record is a locked room, not garbage. Egyptian went unread
  ~1,400 years; the stone carrying one decree in three scripts
  opened every hieroglyph at once (1822). Condition for a key: a
  bilingual text, or a living relative of the dead language
  (Coptic served). The mirror lists the rooms still locked and
  what each key would be.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE MEDIA, by the lifespan table in SCIENCE: clay and stone
  (durable, heavy, tiny capacity), papyrus and paper (cheap,
  fast, flammable), parchment (a millennium, at a herd per book
  — one great Bible cost ~170 calfskins), print runs, film,
  tape, and the digital stack (vast, fastest to rot, blind
  without its reader device).
- THE PRESS AND ITS HEIRS: movable type, steam press,
  mimeograph, photocopier, network — each a further collapse of
  copy cost, each widening who can publish, each pushing the
  erasure law's threshold further from any censor's reach.
- THE CARRIERS: road, ship, caravan, rail, cable. The monsoon
  deserves its name here: a wind reversing on a half-year clock
  scheduled the whole Indian Ocean's exchange of goods, faiths,
  and methods — weather as the timetable of knowledge.
- THE INSTITUTIONS: school, guild, library, museum, monastery,
  journal — each is the holder law answered with an
  organization: keep k high, train the next line, budget the
  upkeep.
- THE STANDARDS CHEST: the standard weight and measure, the
  coin, the agreed calendar, the dictionary — small objects that
  let masses of strangers act as one record-keeping body.
- THE INSTRUMENTS OF THE PAST: the carbon clock, the tree-ring
  ladder, the layer rule, and lately the old bones' own DNA —
  migration stories audited from inside the migrants; lore about
  origins replaced by measurement, the stair's newest step.
- THE RECORDER (1877 and after): sound, then image, then both —
  the first means that keeps the PERFORMANCE rather than a
  description of it. The master's hands can be watched after the
  master is gone; the part of lore that words always dropped
  finally files.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- LET THE DEAD TEACH: keep what was learned past the death of
  everyone who learned it — the purpose the whole phylum orbits.
- TEACH CHEAPER THAN REDISCOVERY: a child inherits in a decade
  what cost the species millennia; every school is this bargain.
- COORDINATE STRANGERS: law, standards, calendar, and coin let
  people who will never meet act on each other's word.
- BIND THE GROUP: the table, the feast, the story, the song —
  belonging built by shared performance; the bond is also the
  channel the knowledge rides.
- AUDIT POWER: archives, provenance, and the ground's testimony
  keep the record from belonging to whoever currently rules it.
- RECOVER THE LOST: locked scripts, buried cities, silent crafts
  — the door queries enter carrying half a key; the mirror's
  ledger is this purpose's worklist.
- KNOW WHEN: order the past (the clocks in SCIENCE) and the
  future (the calendar) so events can be reasoned about at all.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Speech first, before any record — which is exactly why its
  cost and age are unknowable: the tool that would have recorded
  its own birth is the thing being born.
- Meter, ritual, and the trained rememberer next: the oral world
  built its checksum and its specialists long before script, and
  fed everyone on inherited method for tens of millennia.
- Writing arrived ~5,200 years ago as ACCOUNTING: the earliest
  tablets are receipts — grain, beer, sheep, debts. The dead
  learned to teach because the living needed to be dunned;
  poetry, law, and scripture moved into a house bookkeeping
  built.
- The alphabet (~3,000 years ago) cut literacy's entry price a
  hundredfold (the sign-count law), and reading began its long
  walk out of the scribal guilds.
- Paper walked west at holder speed — ~1,300 years from Chinese
  workshop to German mill — the standing proof that methods
  travel as people, not parcels.
- Print (1450s) flipped depth to width: ~250 press towns in
  fifty years, the scribal error stack broken, erasure priced
  out of most censors' reach; every argument afterward could
  assume both sides held the same text.
- The journal (1665) made provenance an institution: claims
  filed with their sources, disputes conducted in the open
  record — knowledge grew a citation chain and stopped losing
  its receipts.
- Mass schooling (1800s) paid the surplus arithmetic at nation
  scale and made literacy a majority condition for the first
  time anywhere; within three generations the
  decree-plus-schools method was rewriting whole language maps
  (the mirror holds decree-alone's failures beside it).
- The recorder (1877 on) filed the performance itself; the part
  of every craft that died with the hands began surviving them.
- The past got its instruments last: layers read in the 1800s,
  tree rings, then the carbon clock (1949), old DNA in this
  century — history climbed the same stair it had watched every
  other craft climb: instrument, number, end of argument.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward. Each names
what runs along it.

- COOKING: the oldest working inheritance — recipes cookable
  after ~3,750 years; the table as the bond that carries the
  rest; the thermometer lesson is this phylum's founding claim
  ("what cannot be written into a claim cannot be inherited"),
  learned in a kitchen. Cuisine is the memory of scarcity
  survived, kept as method.
- ECONOMY: writing born from accounting; money as portable
  memory of who owes whom; trade routes as knowledge routes; the
  surplus arithmetic funding every specialist, school, and
  archive; standards as the grammar of trade.
- MATHEMATICS: every law in SCIENCE is its lending; and the
  numerals themselves are cargo history moved — positional
  digits walked from India through Arab hands to Europe along
  the trade roads, and calculation's price fell with them.
- PHYSICS: the decay clocks (carbon's halving), the layer rule,
  the carrier speeds, the year's true length under the calendar
  arithmetic.
- CHEMISTRY: what ink, clay, parchment, and paper are made of
  decides what survives (the media table); acid paper's
  self-destruction; fire hardening clay while eating libraries.
- ENGINEERING: every carrier and every medium is engineered —
  press, ship, road, cable, vault; carrier speed sets knowledge
  speed, so engineering sets this phylum's tempo.
- MEDICINE: the same measurement stair (trial replacing
  testimony — the scurvy case is a shared worked receipt);
  plague records among the first public data; the adoption-lag
  claim runs both directions along this edge.
- FARMING: the calendar is farming's contract with the sky and
  culture's first shared standard; crops moved with peoples and
  remade every kitchen (the Columbian exchange rewired the
  world's cuisines in ~200 years); the surplus that funds all
  keeping grows here.
- WEATHER: the monsoon as the Indian Ocean's timetable; climate
  shifts as movers of peoples and their knowledge; tree rings as
