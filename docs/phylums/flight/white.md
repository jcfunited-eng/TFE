# FLIGHT — THE WHITE
Built 2026-08-30 to the ratified model (fabric/MODEL.md). The
anti-mirror of docs/phylums/flight/color.md: same parts, opposite
sign. Seed authored by Claude from
docs/fabric_phylums/05_flight_fluids.md and
docs/fabric_phylums/147_deep_transport_and_flight.md. Every
impossibility is certified UNDER CONDITIONS, its why in
quantities, so it recomputes when new knowns move a condition.
This phylum carries the model's own founding example: wings on
arms fail at Earth's gravity and work at a third of it.

## THINGS
Units wondered about or attempted that are not yet real, or cannot
be under stated conditions.

- THE VACUUM BALLOON — the perfect filling is none: empty space
  would lift the full 1.20 kg/m³, beating hydrogen's 1.12. CANNOT
  BE under current materials: the shell must hold off ten tonnes
  per square meter of crush and still weigh less than the 1.2
  kg/m³ it buys — no written material's strength-per-weight
  reaches it. Would drain on: a shell beating that ratio; the
  arithmetic is waiting, not wrong.
- THE FLYING CAR — attempted for a century; each one is a poor
  car bolted to a poor aeroplane. WHY, from the color's medium
  law: air rewards light, large, and fast; the road rewards
  small, dense, and stoppable — one machine cannot sit on both
  optima. Would drain on: the want redefined (a small aircraft
  allowed on roads is buildable; a car that flies well is not
  the same wish).
- THE EVERYDAY JETPACK — flies today, for half a minute. The
  color's hover law prices a person on a back-worn disc at
  ~23,000 W ideal; the fuel for an hour of that cannot be worn.
  Would drain on: energy per kilogram far beyond kerosene, or a
  disc too large to wear — at which point it is a helicopter.
- FLIGHT THAT PAUSES CHEAPLY — air cannot hold anything still
  except at hover's triple rate; the want stands open between
  the balloon (pauses, cannot steer) and the rotor (steers, pays
  triple). Nothing certain — but a possibility: buoyancy carrying
  the weight while small rotors carry only the steering; the
  hybrid airship keeps being re-attempted along exactly this line.

## CLAIMS
What was claimed and didn't hold — with why — and what is still
unsettled.

- "HEAVIER-THAN-AIR FLIGHT IS IMPOSSIBLE" — declared by eminent
  authority within a decade of its happening. WHY believed: the
  power arithmetic was run on the wrong engines — arms and steam
  both sat below the watts-per-kilogram line, and the petrol
  engine's crossing was not in the accounts. Filed as the
  standing warning: an impossibility is certified UNDER
  CONDITIONS, and an engine is a condition.
- "THE BUMBLEBEE CANNOT FLY BY THE MATH" — backwards: the bee
  flies, so the math used was the wrong math. The smooth-flow
  wing law was applied to a wing that flaps, twists, and rides
  its own shed swirls; unsteady air gives lift the steady law
  never priced. Applied science stretched past its conditions —
  filed in SCIENCE below.
- "THE TOP AIR HURRIES TO REUNITE WITH THE BOTTOM AIR" — the
  equal-transit explanation of lift, taught for decades: false by
  wind-tunnel measurement — the upper flow arrives EARLY, not on
  time. The honest ledgers stand in the color; kept because
  queries still arrive carrying it.
- "THE SOUND BARRIER IS A WALL" — a steep drag hill mislabeled;
  crossed in 1947. WHY believed: drag and control trouble rise
  sharply approaching it. A limit is not a wall until the
  conditions say why.
- "A WING STALLS AT ITS STALL SPEED" — the misfiled version of
  the color's angle law; a limit misremembered as a number. It
  kills in turns, where the needed angle arrives at a higher
  speed than the quoted one.
- UNSETTLED: WHY A KITE NEEDS A TAIL — standing in the fabric's
  own white ledger (docs/fabric_phylums/99_the_white.md).
  Nothing certain — but here is a possibility: the tail is a
  drag anchor hung behind the tow point; when the nose yaws, the
  dragged tail is pulled back into line — steadiness bought with
  a little drag; bowed kites fly tailless because their shape
  does the same job. Would drain on: a measured comparison, tail
  against bow, same kite, same wind.
- UNSETTLED: THE SHOWER CURTAIN'S LEAN INTO THE SPRAY — still
  argued (the source marks it UNSURE): spent-pressure flow, a
  driven vortex, warm rising air — each grips partly. The
  phylum's homeliest open question.

## SCIENCE
The science this subject still lacks, named; and where applied
science broke.

- LACKING: TURBULENCE AT DEPTH — smooth flow is the exception on
  a leash: push air fast enough past a big enough thing and
  orderly layers shatter into self-feeding swirls no equation can
  track, only the averages staying lawful. An honest open wound
  in physics, and this phylum lives inside it — the bumpy flight,
  and, through weather, the forecast dying near ten days. Would
  drain on: a mathematics of the swirls; none is written.
- LACKING: A FULL PRICE LIST FOR FLAPPING — the unsteady-lift
  tricks insects and birds run on are demonstrated and filmed,
  and only partly written as computable law. Until then the
  color's smooth-flow ledgers underprice small fliers, and every
  claim about tiny flying machines carries a "~".
- BROKE WHEN APPLIED: the smooth-flow law on flapping wings (the
  bumblebee file) — a law used outside its conditions reads as a
  paradox; the paradox was the misapplication, not the bee.
- BROKE WHEN APPLIED: the equal-transit story — a wrong WHY
  attached to a right HOW MUCH; it computed nothing and taught a
  generation to explain lift without the thrown air.

## METHODS
What was tried and didn't work, carrying its conditions — waiting
for their planet.

- WINGS ON ARMS — MUSCLE-POWERED FLAPPING AT EARTH GRAVITY (the
  flagship; the model's own founding example). CERTIFIED CANNOT
  under these conditions: gravity 9.8 m/s², air 1.2 kg/m³, human
  arm muscle ~150 W sustained. The recompute, held in full: a
  90-kg flier (person plus wings) weighs 882 N; at ~10 m/s on a
  crude flapping wing (lift-per-drag ~8, flapping losses ~1.3)
  the bill is 882 × 10 ÷ 8 × 1.3 ≈ 1,400 W SUSTAINED. Arms hold
  ~150 W — nine times short; the whole body's ~250-300 W is
  still five times short; a champion's minute at 450 W is three
  times short. A burst of 1,000 W buys two wingbeats — exactly
  what every tower jumper got.
  THE SHIMMER, in numbers — flight power scales with weight to
  the 3/2:
  · at ONE-THIRD GRAVITY, same air: ÷ 3^1.5 ≈ 5.2 →
    1,400 ÷ 5.2 ≈ 270 W — a fit person's legs-driven hour. Wings
    on arms fail at Earth's gravity and work at a third of it.
  · on a Titan-like world (gravity ÷7, air ×4): ÷ (18.5 × 2) ≈
    ÷37 → ~40 W — arm power alone flies, easily.
  · and the drain already CASHED ON EARTH, by moving a condition
    nobody thought movable — the method: legs on a propeller, a
    ~30-m wing, ~300 W, and the Channel crossed in 1979 in under
    three hours. The want was never wrong. The arms were.
  RECOMPUTE ON: any change to gravity, air density, or sustained
  watts-per-kilogram of the flier.
- THE TOWER JUMPERS' LEDGER — centuries of strapped wings and
  broken bodies (a monk of ~1000 AD glided a couple hundred
  meters and broke both legs (~); most got less). WHY, run
  honestly: ~4 m² of arm-wing under 90 kg is ~220 N/m² of load,
  needing ~19 m/s just to hold level — a speed reachable only by
  falling — and ~4,000 W to keep, against arms' 150. They failed
  twice over, on speed and on watts, before control was even
  asked. WHAT THE LEDGER BOUGHT: lift was never the missing
  piece; power and control were — and the gliding pioneers
  succeeded precisely by dropping the flapping and keeping the
  fall. A failure measured is a discovery's first half.
- ORNITHOPTERS AT HUMAN SCALE — flapping machines. Flapping WINS
  at small scale (the unsteady bonus; toys fly beautifully;
  every bird is one) and LOSES with size: the size law raises
  speed and loading while a flapping spar must oscillate its own
  growing mass and survive the reversals — the rotating wing
  does the same job at steady stress. Human-carrying attempts:
  brief, towed, marginal (~). CONDITION: scale. Would drain on:
  materials and actuators making oscillation as cheap as
  rotation at the tonne scale — none written.
- STEAM-POWERED AEROPLANES — full-size machines built and
  lavishly funded before 1903; the boiler's watts-per-kilogram
  sat below the flight line at any size carrying its own water.
  DRAINED by petrol, then turbines — the cleanest case of an
  impossibility whose condition was the engine.
- HYDROGEN FOR CARRYING PEOPLE — worked as lift (the extra tenth
  over helium), failed as safety, paid in fire in 1937. Not a
  cannot: a conditions entry — hydrogen still lifts where nothing
  rides but the bag. RECOMPUTE ON: containment that puts the burn
  out of reach, or helium's scarcity biting harder.
- FLYING BY FEEL IN CLOUD — tried by every early pilot; the inner
  ear integrates turns into a lie within a minute; the graveyard
  spiral is its signature. CANNOT under the condition "no
  external reference"; drained by the gyroscope — measurement's
  strongest kill in the fabric.

## MEANS
Means that fell short, and means not yet found.

- MUSCLE AS AN AIRCRAFT ENGINE — ~3 W per kg of flier sustained
  (~), against the ~15 W/kg a human-scale flapper demands: short
  by five at the whole-body best. Fell short at Earth's numbers;
  the flagship holds where it stops being short.
- BATTERIES FOR THE LONG HAUL — ~20× behind kerosene per
  kilogram carried after crediting the motor (~); and since fuel
  is load, the shortfall compounds. Partial drain already real:
  short-range electric flight works. Would drain further on:
  storage nearing fuel's energy-per-kilogram.
- THE VACUUM SHELL — the material that is not there (THINGS
  holds the arithmetic).
- A WORN FUEL FOR AN HOUR'S HOVER — the jetpack's missing half;
  nothing in the written price list packs it.
- NOT YET FOUND: AN INSTRUMENT THAT SEES TURBULENCE AHEAD —
  clear-air turbulence arrives unannounced. Possibility: light
  scattered off the moving air itself, read at range — partly
  demonstrated, not yet a cockpit means (~).

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject cannot yet answer.

- PERSONAL DAILY FLIGHT — flight's energy per tonne-kilometre is
  the worst of the modes and hover's bill is triple cruise; the
  car it would replace wins on every number but romance. Blocked
  on the MEANS shortfalls, not on any law.
- FLIGHT WITHOUT NOISE — thrust is thrown air and thrown air is
  sound; the gentler throw is quieter, and a silent helicopter
  remains a contradiction in the written physics. Would drain
  on: lift without throwing — which is the balloon, which cannot
  hurry.
- THE TEN-DAY BUMP FORECAST — knowing tomorrow's rough air the
  way tides are known; blocked on turbulence (SCIENCE), shared
  with weather.
- FLIGHT AT SAILING'S PRICE — cheap, slow, buoyant air freight
  keeps being proposed (the airship's return) and keeps losing
  to the ship's still-cheaper water; open, and honestly stated
  as economics rather than physics.

## HISTORY
The record of the trying itself — the portrait's side of the story.

- The tower jumpers, the phylum's uncounted dead: centuries of
  arms flapped against an arithmetic written only later. The
  color's HISTORY says gliders learned control on hillsides;
  this page is where the ones who jumped first are acknowledged
  as the price of learning WHICH question to ask.
- Lilienthal's death (1896): two thousand glides bought the
  first real lift tables; the stall took him before he could buy
  the control lesson too. The next builders added the wind
  tunnel and the rudder — his ledger, continued.
- Langley's two crashes (1903): a funded, full-size, properly
  powered aeroplane thrown twice into a river days before the
  Wrights flew — power without control, the tower jumpers'
  second lesson, paid this time at the top of the establishment.
- The airship's long dying: decades of hydrogen successes, each
  fire filed as bad luck until 1937 filed it as a condition. The
  color's density table is that record compressed.
- Icarus, the oldest filed try: the story blamed pride and the
  sun; the arithmetic blames watts and wax. Filed because a
  moral that misnames the constraint can hide a whole phylum's
  real white for centuries.
