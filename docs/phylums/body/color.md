## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- WATER — ~60% of the body's weight; the solvent every reaction
  runs in and the coolant the engine sweats; also lives in: cooking
  (the medium), physics (phase change), chemistry, weather.
- BLOOD — ~5 liters of transport fleet: fuel out, oxygen out,
  waste back, heat wherever it is needed; also: medicine, physics
  (pumps and pressure), chemistry.
- THE HEART — a double pump in one housing: one side loads oxygen
  at the lungs, the other side delivers it everywhere else; also:
  engineering (every pump is its imitation), medicine.
- THE LUNGS — the gas exchange: a tree of tubes ending in ~400
  million tiny bags, ~70 m² of wet surface folded into the chest;
  also: physics (diffusion, pressure), chemistry.
- MUSCLE — the engine's moving parts: fibers that pull, never
  push, always mounted in opposing pairs; also: physics (levers,
  work), farming (the ox before the tractor), economy (sold labor).
- BONE — the frame: living mineral that rebuilds itself along its
  load lines all life long; also: engineering (struts and beams),
  physics, medicine.
- SKIN — the wall, the radiator, and the largest sense organ at
  once; ~1.8 m² on an adult; also: medicine, weather (the boundary
  where climate meets body).
- NERVES — the wiring: salt-charge signals, not electron current;
  also: physics (voltage, signals), chemistry (ions), medicine.
- THE BRAIN — ~1.4 kg, ~2% of the body's weight, burning ~20% of
  its fuel — a dim light bulb's worth, never switched off; also:
  culture, medicine.
- THE GUT — mouth to exit, a staged chemical works: grind, acid
  bath, neutralize, absorb, reclaim water; also: cooking (its
  outsourced first stage), chemistry, life (its microbe tenants).
- THE LIVER — the refinery: stores sugar, repackages fat, breaks
  down poisons, builds blood proteins; also: chemistry, medicine.
- THE KIDNEYS — the water-and-salt accountants: filter ~180 L of
  blood plasma a day and hand ~99% of it back, keeping the books
  on every ion; also: chemistry (osmosis), medicine.
- SUGAR — glucose, the fast fuel; the blood holds it in a narrow
  band; also: cooking, chemistry, farming, medicine.
- FAT — the deep tank: the densest fuel the body stores, ~38 MJ
  per kg; also: cooking (flavor carrier), chemistry, medicine.
- SALT AND THE IONS — sodium outside the cells, potassium inside:
  a charged battery at every cell wall that nerves and muscles
  spend; also: cooking (seasoning, brines), chemistry, economy.
- OXYGEN — the one fuel line with no tank: minutes of reserve,
  total; also: chemistry (the burn), physics, weather.
- THE SENSES — eye (light), ear (pressure waves, ~20 Hz to
  20 kHz), tongue (five channels), nose (~400 receptor kinds,
  most of flavor), touch, heat, pain, balance, body-position;
  also: cooking (taste and smell), physics, culture.
- THE IMMUNE ARMY — patrols, checkpoints, memory of past
  invaders, and fever as its scorched-earth option; also:
  medicine (vaccination drills it), life.
- MICROBES — trillions of tenants, mostly in the gut, part
  workforce (digesting what we can't) and part threat held at the
  walls; also: life, cooking (the same workers hired), medicine.
- HORMONES — slow messages in the blood where nerves are fast
  wires: hunger, growth, fear, sleep, sugar control; also:
  chemistry, medicine.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- The body is a heat engine idling near 100 watts. The famous
  "2,000 calories a day" and the 100 W figure are the same number
  in two costumes (the arithmetic is in SCIENCE).
- Core temperature is held at ~37°C by a thermostat in the brain;
  the skin is allowed to wander so the core doesn't have to.
- Fever is not the thermostat breaking — it is the thermostat
  deliberately raised, cooking the invaders faster than the host.
  Conditions: a tool below ~40°C, an alarm at 41, an emergency
  above — because of the ceiling below.
- Above ~42°C the body's own proteins begin to unfold, and
  unfolding is one-way — the same law that sets an egg. The whole
  temperature defense exists to keep the core off that ladder.
- Sweat cools ONLY when it evaporates. Condition: dry air takes
  the vapor; humid air refuses it — the same heat is deadlier wet.
- Flavor is mostly smell: the tongue reports five channels (sweet,
  salt, sour, bitter, savory); the thousands of distinctions ride
  on aroma through the back of the nose. Pinch the nose and an
  apple and an onion nearly trade places.
- Digestion is staged chemistry, and each stage's workers die at
  the next stage's conditions: neutral spit, then acid bath, then
  neutralized again — the stations are the method.
- Cooking is the body's outsourced first stomach: cooked starch
  and protein hand over more energy for less chewing; the species
  budgeted its small gut and big brain on that outsourcing.
- Blood makes its full loop in about a minute at rest — every
  cell is a minute from the lungs and the liver.
- Nerve signals are fast for chemistry and slow for wires: ~1 to
  ~100 m/s depending on the fiber. Everyone has felt the speed
  table: the sharp report of a stubbed toe arrives well before
  the dull ache (numbers in SCIENCE).
- Muscles turn roughly a fifth to a quarter of their fuel into
  work; the rest is heat. This is why effort warms you, and why
  shivering — muscle working against nothing — is pure furnace.
- Water follows salt across every cell wall it can cross. This
  one law is thirst, swelling, the drip bag's 0.9% number, and
  why drinking seawater dries you out.
- The body rebuilds itself while running: gut lining in days,
  skin in ~a month, red blood cells in ~4 months, the skeleton in
  ~10 years. You are a standing wave, not a statue.
- Every dial — temperature, sugar, salt, water, acidity — is held
  in a narrow band by opposing pushes, correction by opposition,
  never by erasure. Blood acidity is the narrowest: 7.35-7.45,
  and ±0.3 is an emergency.
- Use shapes structure both ways: loaded bone thickens along its
  load lines, idle muscle melts away at ~a few percent a week of
  strict bed rest. The body budgets; it keeps nothing idle.
- The heart's output is demand-priced: ~5 L/min at rest, ~5× that
  under hard effort, throttled by rate and by stroke together.

## SCIENCE
The math, physics, and chemistry of the body, held — not
mentioned. Each piece is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it
can follow, and where it earns it, a WORKED case proving the rule
runs.

### PHYSICS — the heat engine and its fuel

LAW: energy = power × time. The resting body runs at ~100 W.
  One food calorie (kcal) = 4,184 J.
RULE: to convert a body's watts to its daily food bill — multiply
  by 86,400 seconds, divide by 4,184.
WORKED: 100 W × 86,400 s = 8,640,000 J; ÷ 4,184 = ~2,065 kcal.
  The "2,000-calorie day" IS the 100-watt engine. Backwards it
  runs too: 2,000 kcal × 4,184 ÷ 86,400 ≈ 97 W.

LAW: food energy densities: fat 9 kcal/g · protein 4 · starch and
  sugar 4 · alcohol 7.
WORKED: a 30 g pat of butter (~24 g fat): 24 × 9 = 216 kcal. The
  resting engine burns ~86 kcal/hour (2,065 ÷ 24), so one pat is
  ~2.5 hours of idling. Eating outruns burning effortlessly —
  the stairs arithmetic below says the same thing louder.

LAW: warming what you swallow costs Q = m · c · ΔT (the kitchen's
  heating law run inward; the body is mostly water, c = 4.18).
WORKED: half a liter of ice water, 0°C to body's 37: 500 × 4.18 ×
  37 = 77,330 J ≈ 18.5 kcal. Real, and tiny — a glass of ice
  water "burns" about one percent of the day. The law kills the
  slimming legend politely, with its own numbers.

LAW: evaporating sweat carries off ~2,400 J per gram (a touch
  more than the kettle's 2,257, because skin is cooler than a
  boil). Sweat that drips instead of evaporating cools ~nothing.
RULE: to price a workout's sweat — find the heat to dump (fuel
  burned minus work done), divide by 2,400 per gram.
WORKED: a cyclist holding 200 W of real output at ~22.5%
  efficiency burns 200 ÷ 0.225 ≈ 889 W of fuel; 889 − 200 =
  689 W is heat. One hour: 689 × 3,600 ≈ 2,480,000 J; ÷ 2,400 ≈
  1,030 g. About a liter of sweat per hard hour — which is what
  riders actually lose. Three laws welded: muscle efficiency,
  energy bookkeeping, evaporation's price.

LAW: chemistry roughly doubles its speed per 10°C (the kitchen's
  speed law; bodies obey it too): factor = 2^(ΔT ÷ 10).
WORKED, both directions:
  · fever at +2°C: 2^0.2 ≈ 1.15 — the machinery runs ~15% hotter
    pace; measured sickbeds bill ~10-13% more fuel per degree,
    the law's prediction plus the army's overhead.
  · cold water at 20°C core (17 below normal): 2^1.7 ≈ 3.2×
    slower — the brain's few-minute oxygen clock stretches
    threefold, which is why cold-water drownings are sometimes
    survived long past the warm-water limit, and why surgeons
    chill a body on purpose before stopping its heart.

LAW: the cost of raising the core — the fever's climb is a
  heating job like any other. Body heat capacity ~3.5 J/g°C.
WORKED: 70 kg raised 2°C: 70,000 × 3.5 × 2 = 490,000 J. Spare
  heating power from shivering and a raised burn, say ~100 W:
  490,000 ÷ 100 = 4,900 s ≈ 80 minutes. That is why a fever
  climbs over an hour or two of chills — the chills ARE the
  climb: the thermostat moved first, so normal feels cold.

### PHYSICS — the body's mechanics

LAW: work against gravity = m · g · h; muscle pays for it at
  ~20-25% efficiency.
RULE: to price a climb in food — mgh, divide by ~0.22, convert
  by 4,184.
WORKED: 70 kg up one 3 m floor: 70 × 9.8 × 3 = 2,058 J of work;
  fuel cost 2,058 ÷ 0.22 ≈ 9,350 J ≈ 2.2 kcal. One medium apple
  (~95 kcal = 397,480 J) buys 397,480 ÷ 9,350 ≈ 42 floors of
  stairs. One apple, a skyscraper — the fork moves the scale a
  hundred times more easily than the feet do. The law itself
  says: weight is lost at the table and kept off on the stairs.

LAW: most of the body's levers trade force for speed — the
  muscle attaches close to the joint, the load rides far away.
WORKED: the forearm: hand ~35 cm from the elbow, biceps tendon
  ~5 cm — a 7:1 lever against the muscle. Holding 10 kg (98 N) in
  the hand costs 98 × 7 ≈ 690 N at the tendon — seventy kilos of
  pull to hold ten. The trade bought speed: a small muscle
  shortening slowly whips the hand fast — throwing, striking,
  playing — at the price of tendon loads engineering would
  flinch at.

LAW: surface grows by the square, volume by the cube; heat loss
  rides surface, heat production rides volume.
WORKED: an adult carries ~0.026 m² of skin per kg; a newborn
  ~0.07 — nearly 3× the radiator per kilo of furnace. That is
  why babies chill in a room adults find mild, why small animals
  eat constantly, and why the very large grow radiators (the
  elephant's ears are this law made visible).

LAW: pressure and the pump. Stroke work ≈ pressure × volume.
WORKED: mean arterial pressure ~13,300 Pa (100 mmHg) × 70 mL
  (0.00007 m³) ≈ 0.93 J per beat; × 70 beats/min ≈ 65 J/min ≈
  1.1 W. The pump that pushes ~7,000 liters a day around the
  body runs on about one watt of mechanical work — burning ~5 W
  of fuel for it at muscle's usual efficiency. Engineering has
  no pump so cheap that lasts thirty years, let alone eighty.

### PHYSICS — nerves as charge signals

LAW: nerve speed by fiber class: bare thin fibers ~1 m/s (dull
  pain), insulated fast fibers ~60-100 m/s (touch, muscle
  command). The signal is all-or-nothing; loudness is coded as
  spikes per second, never as taller spikes.
WORKED: stub a toe 1.8 m from the brain: the sharp report at
  60 m/s arrives in 1.8 ÷ 60 = 0.03 s; the dull ache at 1 m/s
  arrives 1.8 seconds later. The two-pain delay everyone knows
  is the speed table, felt.

LAW: the signal is a falling row of salt gates, not current in a
  wire: sodium floods in, potassium leaks out, the wall's voltage
  swings ~0.1 V (−70 mV rest to ~+30 peak) and the swing hops
  down the fiber.
WORKED: 0.1 V across a wall ~7 nanometers thin is 0.1 ÷
  0.000000007 ≈ 14 million volts per meter — several times the
  field strength at which air itself breaks down and sparks.
  Every cell wall in the body holds, all the time, a field no
  air gap could.

WORKED, the reaction budget: total human reaction time ~0.2 s —
  a few hundredths of travel, the rest is the brain deciding. A
  car at 100 km/h covers 27.8 m/s × 0.2 = 5.6 m before the foot
  even starts to move. And sprint rules encode the limit: a
  start under 0.1 s after the gun is scored a false start —
  judged faster than human wiring, therefore a guess.

### CHEMISTRY — digestion, the staged works

LAW: the stations and their conditions:
  mouth — neutral spit, a starch-cutting enzyme, and the teeth
    running the kitchen's square-cube law (smaller pieces, more
    surface per amount for every enzyme downstream);
  stomach — acid at pH ~1.5-3.5: strong enough to unfold protein
    (what lime juice does to fish, run internally) and to kill
    most swallowed microbes; the acid-born protein-cutter works
    only here;
  small intestine — acid neutralized, the main enzyme suite, and
    absorption across ~30-40 m² of folded wall;
  large intestine — water reclaimed, the microbe tenants paid in
    leftovers.
LAW: pH is a logarithm, each step 10×. Stomach at pH 2 against
  blood at 7.4 is 10^5.4 ≈ 250,000× more acid — two rooms apart.
RULE: each stage's enzymes are keyed to that stage's pH and die
  outside it; a swallowed enzyme pill that ignores the acid bath
  arrives dead. Conditions are part of the method here too.

LAW: the burn itself: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
  + ~2,870 kJ per 180 g of glucose ≈ 16 kJ/g ≈ 3.8 kcal/g — the
  food label's "4" for carbohydrate, derived from the reaction.
LAW: oxygen buys energy at ~20 kJ per liter breathed in and
  used.
WORKED: at rest the body uses ~0.25 L of oxygen a minute:
  0.25 × 20,000 = 5,000 J/min ≈ 83 W — the 100-watt engine
  measured a second, independent way (breath instead of food).
  Two roads, one number: that agreement is why "the body is a
  heat engine" is a claim and not a metaphor.

LAW: cooking as pre-digestion, with the why: raw starch granules
  resist the enzymes until heat has burst them (the kitchen's
  60-70°C starch law is the condition); cooked food yields
  ~10-30% more usable energy for some staples and cuts chewing
  from ape hours (~5 a day) to human minutes. The gut saved by
  the fire is roughly the budget the big brain spends.

### CHEMISTRY — salt and water at the cell wall

LAW: water crosses any wall that passes water but not salt,
  toward the saltier side — the kitchen's brine law running at
  every one of ~37 trillion cells, all the time.
- Blood plasma sits at ~0.9% salt-equivalent. That is the drip
  bag's number: 9 g per liter, matched so dripped water neither
  bursts nor shrivels the blood cells it meets. Pure water in a
  vein bursts them — the law, enforced in seconds.
WORKED, seawater: the sea is ~3.5% salt, four times blood. The
  kidney's most concentrated urine tops out near ~2%
  salt-equivalent — and its other wastes must ride in the same
  water. Every liter of seawater drunk therefore costs more than
  a liter to excrete: the books can only balance negative. The
  sailor surrounded by water dies of thirst by osmosis alone.
- Sweat is salty but weaker than blood (~0.1-0.3%): long sweating
  loses water faster than salt, so thirst-driven plain water can
  overshoot — dilute the blood enough (marathon aid stations
  have done it) and cells swell; the brain has no room to swell.
  The fix is in the law: replace both, in sweat's proportions.

### MATHEMATICS — the body's own arithmetic

LAW: the pump's numbers multiply out: ~70 mL per beat × ~70
  beats/min = 4,900 mL/min ≈ 5 L — the whole blood volume, once
  around per minute at rest. Hard effort: ~×5, 25 L/min.
WORKED, a lifetime: 70/min × 60 × 24 = 100,800 beats a day;
  × 365 = ~36.8 million a year; × 80 years ≈ 2.9 billion beats,
  no service intervals.
LAW: microbes in a body compound like microbes anywhere:
  doublings every ~20-60 min in a warm wound. The immune army's
  speed matters because the enemy's arithmetic is exponential —
  a day's head start is not additive.
LAW: the fuel tanks, priced in idle days:
  · sugar (glycogen): ~500 g ≈ 2,000 kcal — ONE day's idle;
  · fat: 10 kg ≈ 90,000 kcal ÷ 2,065 ≈ 43 days — the deep tank;
  · protein: the body's own furniture, burned only in emergency.
WORKED, the marathoner's wall: running costs ~1 kcal per kg per
  km — a 70 kg runner spends ~70 kcal/km. The sugar tank alone:
  2,000 ÷ 70 ≈ 29 km. The marathon is 42.2. The wall stands near
  30 km because that is where the fast tank runs dry and the
  slow fat tank must carry a pace it cannot feed alone. Training
  moves the wall by teaching fat to co-burn earlier — the
  arithmetic says exactly what training must accomplish.

### THE 42°C CEILING (chemistry + the thermostat, welded)

LAW: body proteins begin unfolding at ~42°C, one-way — the
  bottom rungs of the kitchen's ladder, and this kitchen cannot
  buy new eggs. The working band:
  36.5-37.5 normal · 38-40 fever's tool range · 41 alarm ·
  42+ the cook cooks.
RULE: judge any heat exposure by whether the core can shed heat
  faster than it gains: air temperature, humidity (sweat's exit),
  work rate (the engine's own furnace), and water to spend. A
  wet-hot day at hard labor closes every exit at once —
  heatstroke is the four conditions stacking, and it kills at
  air temperatures a dry idle body would shrug off.
- Cold side, same thermostat: below ~35°C core the machinery
  slows by the speed law; shivering is the furnace's last gear
  (up to several times resting heat, briefly); below ~30 the
  thermostat itself fails and the body stops defending.

## METHODS
How the body does its work, and how we work with it — each with
what governs it and what breaks it.

- HOLDING 37 (the standing method): blood rerouted to the skin
  opens the radiator, sweat spends water for evaporation's
  2,400 J/g, shivering burns fuel for zero work — pure heat.
  Breaks when: humidity blocks the sweat exit, water runs out,
  or the gap is too large too long (the ceiling above).
- THE FEVER PLAY: raise the thermostat, chill and shiver up to
  the new mark, hold, then sweat down when the all-clear sounds.
  Fighting the climb from outside (cold bath at the wrong hour)
  triggers harder shivering — work WITH the thermostat: reset it
  (that is what fever medicine does) or wait it out below 40.
- EATING (the staged works, run as a method): chew small (the
  square-cube law is the reason chewing matters), stations in
  order, ~a day mouth-to-exit. Breaks when: stages are bypassed
  or hurried — the stations are the method.
- BREATHING: the diaphragm pulls a partial vacuum ~12-16 times a
  minute at rest, half a liter a pull; the gas swap itself is
  pure diffusion across the lungs' ~70 m². No pump touches the
  oxygen — only surface, gradient, and the folded area do it.
- CIRCULATION AS LOGISTICS: one fleet, demand-routed — vessels
  widen where work happens, narrow where it doesn't; after a
  large meal the gut outbids the muscles (the sleepy lunch is a
  routing decision, not a weakness).
- MOVING (pairs and levers): muscles only pull, so every joint
  gets an opposing pair; the levers trade force for speed (the
  7:1 forearm in SCIENCE). Breaks when: one side of a pair
  shortens or weakens — posture and joint pain are often a
  pair's argument, not a part's failure.
- HEALING IN STAGES: clot (minutes), inflame and clean (days),
  rebuild rough (weeks), remodel along load lines (months) —
  bone finishes near its old strength in ~6-12 weeks IF loaded
  gently; unloaded bone remodels itself away. Breaks when:
  stages are rushed (re-tear), or infection turns the clean-up
  stage into a siege.
- TRAINING (overload then repair-with-interest): effort tears
  slightly; the rebuild overshoots; the growth happens in the
  rest, not the effort. Governs muscle, bone, tendon, and the
  heart alike, on different clocks (muscle weeks, tendon months,
  bone longer). Breaks when: load without rest (the interest is
  never paid), or rest without load (the budget office reclaims
  ~a few percent a week).
- DEFENDING (the army's method): walls first (skin, acid,
  mucus), then patrols, then memory — a beaten invader's
  description is filed and the second visit is crushed in hours.
  VACCINATION is that filing done on purpose: show the army a
  wanted poster instead of the criminal. Breaks when: the enemy
  changes its face faster than filing (each winter's flu), or
  the army mistakes home for enemy (the mirror holds that).
- MEASURING THE BODY (the honest instruments): the thermometer,
  the pulse, the pressure cuff (~120/80 mmHg healthy at rest),
  the scale — each replaced a proverb with a number, and doneness
  arguments with degrees, exactly as in the kitchen.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE FUELS, by tank: blood sugar (minutes, held in a narrow
  band), glycogen (~one idle day, the sprint and the marathon's
  first 29 km), fat (~six idle weeks, the deep reserve), protein
  (the furniture, burned last). Oxygen: NO tank — the brain's
  reserve is ~4 minutes; it is the only supply line that cannot
  pause, which is why the heart and lungs get no rest days.
- WATER: ~42 L in a 70 kg body. Lose ~2% and thirst plus a
  performance drop arrive; ~10% is grave danger. The kidneys
  balance it daily: ~180 L filtered, ~99% reclaimed, ~1.5 L
  spent — precision plumbing, not disposal.
- THE BATTERY: sodium kept outside, potassium kept inside every
  cell, at constant pumping cost — a standing charge the nerves
  and muscles spend and the pumps rebuild. A large share of the
  resting 100 W is just keeping this battery topped up.
- THE SURFACES (the body's real estate): lungs ~70 m², gut wall
  ~30-40 m², skin ~1.8 m² — exchange scales with area, so the
  body folds cathedral floors into a chest and a belly.
- THE SENSES AS INSTRUMENTS: the tongue's five channels are a
  coarse gate (safe/fuel/spoiled); the nose's ~400 receptor
  kinds do flavor's fine work; the ears span a thousandfold
  pitch range; the eye adapts across a millionfold of light.
  All report by rate — spikes per second — the one signal
  currency the wiring has.
- THE SELF-REPAIR KIT: clotting cascade (seconds), immune army
  and its memory files, remodeling crews in bone and skin —
  running maintenance on a machine that never stops to be fixed.
- THE MICROBE WORKFORCE: gut tenants digesting what our enzymes
  cannot, paid in leftovers, crowding out worse tenants by
  occupying the ground — the kitchen's fermentation fence, run
  as a permanent internal policy.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Anatomy before books: learned at wounds, hunts, and butchery —
  the parts list came first and cost nothing extra to read.
- The long wrong model: for ~2,000 years the four humours ruled
  (the mirror holds that reign and its fall); it explained
  everything and forbade nothing, which is why it held.
- Vesalius cuts and looks (1543): anatomy rewritten by the
  radical method of checking — the book against the body, and
  the body won.
- Harvey does the arithmetic (1628): the heart's output per hour
  — run with today's numbers, 70 mL × 70 × 60 ≈ 294 L — is
  ~60 bodies' worth of blood. The body holds 5 L. Therefore the
  blood must go around. Circulation was forced by multiplication
  before any capillary was ever seen — a worked case standing as
  a monument: arithmetic can settle what dissection cannot show.
- Vaccination before understanding (1796): the method worked a
  century before germ theory could say why — like bread rising
  for millennia, HOW outran WHY and saved lives anyway.
- Germ theory arrives (1860s-80s): invisible enemies named;
  Semmelweis's ignored hand-washing counts vindicated (the
  mirror holds the price of the delay); surgery and childbirth
  stop being gambles against the unseen.
- The calorimeter weighs life (1780s-1900s): a body in a
  measuring box obeys energy conservation exactly — fuel in,
  heat plus work out, no vital exception. The 100-watt claim
  was earned here, and the old vital force went to the mirror.
- The instruments enter: thermometer, cuff, microscope, X-ray,
  and the body's proverbs became numbers, as the kitchen's did.
- The single-molecule fixes (1920s-40s): insulin and the
  antibiotics — proof that one right substance can stand in for
  a failed organ or rout an army; medicine reorganized around it.
- The genome read (2000s): the parts list's parts list — read,
  but far from computable; its gap is filed in the mirror.
