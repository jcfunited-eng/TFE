## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- ATOM — the smallest piece that keeps an element's name; about a
  hundred kinds make everything; also lives in: physics (its
  insides), mathematics (chemistry is counting them).
- ELEMENT — one kind of atom; its identity is the count of protons
  in its nucleus, and the nucleus is territory chemistry cannot
  touch (the mirror's largest entry stands on this); also: physics,
  economy (gold, iron, oil's carbon), farming (the soil's stock).
- MOLECULE — atoms joined into a unit with its own character: two
  gases make water; a metal that explodes in water and a poisonous
  green gas make table salt; the parts never predict the whole by
  inspection alone; also: life, body, medicine.
- BOND — the charge pull that joins atoms: electrons SHARED
  (covalent — most molecules), electrons GIVEN AND TAKEN (ionic —
  salts), electrons POOLED as a common sea (metals — why they
  bend and conduct). Between molecules, weaker pulls — water's
  hydrogen bonds chief among them; also: physics (all of it is
  electric charge).
- THE ELECTRON — chemistry's only currency. Every reaction is
  outer electrons traded, shared, or stolen; the nucleus never
  enters the transaction; also: physics, engineering (current is
  the same particle marching).
- WATER — the solvent most of chemistry happens in: polar, sticky
  with hydrogen bonds, strange in ways life depends on (see
  SCIENCE); also: cooking, weather, life, the body, farming.
- SOLUTION — one substance dispersed molecule-by-molecule in
  another; concentration is its one number; also: cooking (brine,
  syrup), medicine (every dose), body (blood).
- ACID AND BASE — proton givers and proton takers; their meeting
  makes a salt and water; their strength runs on the pH logarithm;
  also: cooking (the 4.6 line), body (blood held to a tenth of a
  unit), farming (soil pH decides the crop), medicine.
- SALT — the child of acid meeting base: charged atoms stacked in
  a lattice, dissolving back into its ions in water; also: cooking,
  body (nerves run on its ions), economy.
- OXYGEN — the great electron thief; one appetite behind fire,
  rust, breath, and the browning of cut fruit — same theft at four
  speeds; also: life, body, weather (a fifth of the air),
  engineering (corrosion, the standing tax).
- CARBON — the four-handed atom: bonds to itself in chains, rings,
  and sheets without end; every molecule of life is built on its
  skeleton; also: life, body, farming, economy (coal, oil, and the
  price of both).
- METALS — the electron lenders: shiny, bendable, conducting,
  eager to be stolen from (rust is the theft); also: engineering,
  economy, culture (ages are named for them).
- POLYMER — small units chained into giants: cellulose, starch,
  proteins, DNA, and every plastic; the chain's length and tangle
  set the material's feel; also: life, engineering, economy,
  cooking (dough's gluten net).
- CATALYST — the matchmaker: lowers a reaction's threshold, speeds
  it thousands-fold, and walks away unspent. Life's are called
  enzymes; industry's feed and clothe the world; also: life, body,
  engineering, economy (one catalyst puts bread on half the
  world's tables — see HISTORY).
- ENERGY — the ledger every reaction pays into or draws from;
  bonds broken cost, bonds formed pay; the difference leaves as
  heat or must be supplied; also: physics (whose conservation law
  this is), cooking, engineering.
- THE FLAME — combustion made visible: fuel's electrons handed to
  oxygen at speed, the payout bright and hot; also: cooking (fire),
  physics, culture (the first chemistry commanded).
- CHIRAL TWINS — molecules built of the same atoms joined the same
  way but as mirror images, like hands: identical to plain
  chemistry, opposite to any handed receiver. The body is a handed
  receiver; also: medicine (one twin cures, one can harm), life
  (life itself is one-handed), body.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- An element's identity lives in its nucleus (the proton count);
  chemistry rearranges only outer electrons. Therefore NO chemical
  process turns one element into another — not fire, not acid, not
  any reagent. Conditions: chemical means; the nuclear road is
  physics's and is real (the mirror holds the full entry).
- Bonds are charge pulls and nothing more. Breaking one always
  costs energy; forming one always pays energy out. A reaction's
  heat is bookkeeping: bonds formed minus bonds broken (the ledger
  is in SCIENCE, with numbers).
- Every reaction needs a threshold shove first — even the downhill
  ones. Paper and air sit together for centuries; the match's
  moment of heat lifts enough molecules over the wall and the
  payout then feeds the climb. No threshold crossed, no reaction,
  however willing.
- Reaction speed roughly doubles for every 10°C climb, because
  warmth is molecular motion and more collisions clear the
  threshold. This single law runs the refrigerator, the pressure
  cooker, spoilage, and the glow stick (SCIENCE holds the rule).
- Like dissolves like: charged-and-polar dissolves in
  charged-and-polar (salt and sugar in water); oily dissolves in
  oily. A molecule with one end of each (soap) bridges the two
  refusals — the same mediator wager cooking makes in mayonnaise.
- Mass is conserved in every chemical reaction: atoms are never
  made or destroyed, only re-partnered. The books balance to the
  atom, and to any balance ever built. Conditions: chemical change;
  nuclear processes trade mass itself for energy, on physics's
  ledger, at rates no kitchen scale sees.
- What burns or rusts GAINS the air's oxygen: burned metal weighs
  more than the metal, and the CO₂ from a tank of fuel outweighs
  the fuel about three to one. The gained mass came from the air
  (worked in SCIENCE — and it is the measurement that killed
  phlogiston; the mirror holds the corpse).
- Acids free protons in water, bases take them; pH is a logarithm
  where each step is tenfold. Below pH 4.6 the deadliest food
  spoiler cannot grow — chemistry's number, cooking's law.
- Oxidation is one family at many speeds: fire (seconds), the
  browning of cut fruit (minutes, enzyme-driven), rust (months).
  Same electron theft; the speed difference is threshold and
  catalyst, and each speed has its own counter-levers (METHODS).
- A catalyst lowers the threshold, is not consumed, and never
  moves the destination — it speeds the arrival at the same
  equilibrium from both directions. What it changes is the clock,
  never the answer.
- Reactions run both ways at once; what looks finished is a
  balance of rates. Push on a balance — add an ingredient, remove
  a product, squeeze a gas — and it leans away from the push.
  Removing the product as it forms drives a reluctant reaction to
  completion; this is a working method, not a metaphor.
- Concentration drives rate and equilibrium both; dilution's
  arithmetic is exact (C₁V₁ = C₂V₂, worked in SCIENCE) and
  dilution is the only undo mixing has — and it costs everything
  else, chemistry's version of the kitchen's line.
- Mirror-twin molecules pass every ordinary chemical test as
  identical — same melting point, same formula, same reactions —
  and part company only before a handed judge: polarized light, or
  a living receptor. One twin of carvone smells of spearmint, the
  other of caraway; the nose is a handed instrument. Medicine's
  hard rule follows: test the twins separately (HISTORY holds the
  ink it was written in).
- Hot water holds more solid, less gas: sugar's ceiling more than
  doubles from kitchen to boil, while warm soda goes flat and warm
  seas hold less breath for their fish. Two solubility laws,
  opposite signs, one cause (motion helps a lattice apart, and
  helps a gas escape).
- Water is an odd molecule and the oddities are load-bearing: it
  soaks up heat stubbornly (4.18 J per gram per °C — weather's
  flywheel), it climbs and clings (hydrogen bonds), and its solid
  FLOATS — ice is ~9% roomier than the water it froze from, which
  bursts pipes and cells and keeps lakes liquid under their lids.

## SCIENCE
The math and physics of chemistry, held — not mentioned. Each
piece is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it can follow, and
where it earns it, a WORKED case proving the rule runs.

### THE COUNTING — conservation made arithmetic

LAW: atoms are conserved; a balanced equation is the books
  balanced. The counting unit is the mole: 6.022 × 10²³ pieces,
  chosen so one mole of a substance weighs its formula mass in
  grams. Formula masses from the atoms (g/mol): H 1 · C 12 · N 14
  · O 16 · Na 23 · Cl 35.5 · Fe ~56. Water H₂O = 2 + 16 = 18.
RULE: to convert mass through a reaction — grams ÷ formula mass =
  moles; multiply by the equation's ratio; multiply back by the
  product's formula mass.
WORKED: burning methane, CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O.
  16 g of methane (one mole) takes 64 g of oxygen (2 × 32) and
  yields 44 g of CO₂ plus 36 g of water (2 × 18).
  Books: 16 + 64 = 80 in; 44 + 36 = 80 out. Balanced to the gram.
WORKED: rust, 4 Fe + 3 O₂ → 2 Fe₂O₃. Fe₂O₃ = 112 + 48 = 160.
  224 g of iron (4 × 56) + 96 g of oxygen (3 × 32) = 320 g of
  rust (2 × 160). The rust outweighs the iron by 96 g — the
  gained mass is the air's oxygen, weighed. One such weighing
  ended a century of phlogiston (mirror, THINGS).
WORKED: a car's exhaust — octane C₈H₁₈ (114 g/mol) burns to
  8 CO₂: 8 × 44 = 352 g of CO₂ per 114 g of fuel, a ratio of
  ~3.1. A 40 kg tank of fuel becomes ~120 kg of CO₂; the extra
  80 kg is oxygen taken from the air. Conservation is why the
  exhaust outweighs the fuel — nothing was created, oxygen was
  annexed.
WORKED: fermentation's books — glucose C₆H₁₂O₆ (180 g/mol) →
  2 alcohol (2 × 46 = 92 g) + 2 CO₂ (2 × 44 = 88 g).
  92 + 88 = 180. Balanced. Bread keeps neither (the gas leavens
  and leaves, the alcohol bakes off); beer keeps the 92 and vents
  the 88 — cooking's two products are one equation's two lines.

### CONCENTRATION — how much in how much

LAW: dilution conserves the dissolved amount. C₁V₁ = C₂V₂, any
  units, kept consistent.
RULE: to dilute to a target — multiply target strength by target
  volume, divide by the stock's strength; that is the stock to
  take, topped up to the target volume.
WORKED: household bleach at ~5% down to a 0.5% sanitizer, one
  liter wanted: V₁ = (0.5 × 1000) ÷ 5 = 100 mL of bleach, water
  to the liter mark. Tenfold down, one-tenth the volume — the
  arithmetic IS the recipe.
LAW: molarity = moles per liter of solution.
WORKED: table salt NaCl = 23 + 35.5 = 58.5 g/mol; 58.5 g in a
  liter of water = a 1 molar solution. Seawater runs ~0.6 molar
  — 35 g of salts per liter, most of it this one.
LAW: neutralization is mole-for-mole — moles of acid meet moles
  of base at the endpoint (one proton traded each, for the
  one-proton pairs).
WORKED: baking soda NaHCO₃ = 23 + 1 + 12 + 48 = 84 g/mol.
  8.4 g (0.1 mol) neutralizes 0.1 mol of stomach-strength acid —
  100 mL of a 1 molar solution — giving salt, water, and CO₂ (the
  burp is the third product). This same drip-and-count, run with
  a measured burette, is how any unknown strength is read
  (METHODS: titration).

### THE pH LOGARITHM

LAW: pH = −log₁₀ of the hydrogen-ion concentration; each unit
  step is tenfold. Pure water sits at 7; below is acid, above is
  base. Landmarks: stomach acid ~1.5-2 · lemon ~2 · vinegar ~2.5
  · the canning line 4.6 · milk ~6.6 · blood 7.35-7.45 · baking
  soda in water ~8.3 · household ammonia ~11.5 · lye ~13-14.
RULE: to compare two acidities — subtract the pH values, raise
  10 to the difference.
WORKED: lemon (pH 2) against coffee (pH 5): 10^(5−2) = 1,000×
  more hydrogen ion. Blood drifting from 7.4 to 7.0 — a mere 0.4
  — is 10^0.4 ≈ 2.5× the ion count, and a medical emergency; the
  body spends real chemistry (its bicarbonate buffer) holding
  that tenth of a unit. A buffer is an acid-base pair that
  absorbs additions from either side — the resistance is
  stoichiometric, not magic, and it runs out when the pair does.
LAW: the 4.6 line — below pH 4.6, botulism cannot grow. This one
  number divides boiling-water canning from the pressure
  canner's 121°C; cooking holds the method, chemistry holds the
  why (the spore's machinery fails in that much acid).

### THE THRESHOLD AND THE CLOCK

LAW: every reaction has an activation threshold — an energy wall
  molecules must clear by collision before even a profitable
  reaction can run. Warmth raises the clearing fraction steeply;
  the practical form: rate roughly doubles per 10°C.
  factor = 2^(ΔT ÷ 10).
RULE: to trade temperature for time in any reaction — hotter by
  ΔT, divide the time by 2^(ΔT/10); colder, multiply.
WORKED: kitchen (20°C) to refrigerator (4°C): 2^(16/10) ≈ 3×
  slower spoilage; to the freezer (−18°C), 2^(38/10) ≈ 14×
  slower before ice locks the water and stops the game outright.
  A glow stick in the freezer runs the same law in reverse of
  the want: dimmer light, lasting the whole night — brightness
  is rate, and rate was traded for duration.
LAW: rate also follows contact — surface area and concentration.
  Halve the piece, double the surface per amount (the cube-square
  law, physics's loan).
WORKED: a steel beam rusts in years; steel wool sparks and burns
  in a flame; flour in a heap smoulders while flour DUST in air
  explodes — same substances, same reactions, surface multiplied
  ten-thousand-fold. Hand warmers are rust run fast on purpose:
  iron powder, salt, and air.
LAW: the catalyst clause — a catalyst lowers the threshold and
  is returned unspent; rate rises because more collisions clear
  the lower wall; the equilibrium destination does not move.
WORKED: hydrogen peroxide keeps in the bottle for months (high
  wall), yet foams within seconds on a cut — the blood's
  catalase enzyme drops the wall, and 2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂ runs
  ~million-fold faster. Industry's version: nitrogen's triple
  bond (~945 kJ/mol, among the strongest in nature) yields at
  scale only over an iron catalyst at ~450°C and hundreds of
  atmospheres — the reaction that fixes air into fertilizer
  (HISTORY holds what that catalyst fed).

### THE ENERGY LEDGER OF BONDS

LAW: reaction heat = energy of bonds broken (cost) − energy of
  bonds formed (payout). Average bond energies (kJ/mol):
  C–H 413 · C–C 347 · O=O 498 · O–H 463 · C=O in CO₂ 799.
  Averages hold to roughly ±10% (the mirror files where that
  slack bites).
RULE: to estimate a reaction's heat — sum the broken bonds' cost,
  sum the formed bonds' payout, subtract.
WORKED: methane burning. Break: 4 C–H (4 × 413 = 1,652) + 2 O=O
  (2 × 498 = 996) = 2,648 kJ cost. Form: 2 C=O (2 × 799 = 1,598)
  + 4 O–H (4 × 463 = 1,852) = 3,450 kJ payout.
  Net: 3,450 − 2,648 = 802 kJ released per mole — ~50 kJ per
  gram (measurement says ~890 per mole; the averages' slack, as
  warned). Fuel is bonds waiting to be traded up: the C–H bond's
  payout when swapped for O–H and C=O is the entire fossil
  economy in one subtraction.
LAW: energy density follows the bond inventory. Fats ~38 kJ/g,
  sugars and starches ~17 kJ/g — fat carries more C–H bonds per
  gram (less oxygen already aboard). The body's choice of fat
  for storage is this arithmetic, made by evolution.

### DISSOLVING — like into like

LAW: polar and charged dissolve in polar; oily in oily. Water
  pries salt's lattice apart ion by ion and shells each in a
  polar crowd; oil offers ions nothing and refuses them.
LAW: solids dissolve more when hot, gases less.
WORKED: sugar in 100 mL of water: ~200 g at 20°C, ~490 g at the
  boil — the candy window; cooled undisturbed it hangs
  supersaturated until a seed crystal or a scratch calls the
  excess out (rock candy is this patience). Salt's ceiling
  barely moves (~36 → ~39 g) — not every solid climbs alike.
  Gases run opposite: cold streams hold the most oxygen, warm
  soda goes flat, and a warming ocean exhales.
LAW: the mediator molecule — one end polar, one end oily —
  dissolves the refusal itself: soap's oily tail buries in
  grease, its charged head faces the water, and the droplet
  sails away wearing a water-loving coat. Same physics as the
  kitchen's egg-yolk mayonnaise; soap is fat boiled with lye
  until the fat itself becomes the mediator.

### THE HANDEDNESS

LAW: a carbon holding four different partners has two ways to
  hold them — mirror images that no rotation superposes. Same
  formula, same bonds, same energies; plain chemistry cannot
  tell them apart. Handed judges can: polarized light twists
  opposite ways through them, and every receptor of the living
  body — itself built one-handed — greets the two twins
  differently.
WORKED: carvone — identical atom lists, mirror joins: one hand
  is spearmint, the other caraway. Life runs one-handed
  throughout (its amino acids all left, its sugars all right —
  WHY is in the mirror, unsettled), so a drug's two hands are
  two different drugs to the body. The rule medicine bought at
  terrible cost: make, separate, and test the twins as two
  substances, and check whether the body converts one to the
  other (HISTORY).

## METHODS
How things are done here — each with what governs it, when it
applies, and what breaks it. The prepositioned how-to-build.

- MIXING, GRINDING, HEATING (rate control by contact): raise
  surface, concentration, or temperature by the clock laws.
  Breaks when: heat outruns the vessel — a reaction making heat
  in its volume sheds it only by its surface, so what simmers in
  a flask can run away in a drum (the cube-square trap, filed
  with a burn scar in the mirror).
- DISSOLVING AND CRYSTALLIZING (purity by solubility): dissolve
  hot, filter what never dissolved, cool slowly so the wanted
  lattice rebuilds itself and excludes strangers. The crystal is
  a purification, not a decoration. Breaks when cooled fast:
  strangers get walled in.
- FILTERING, SETTLING, EVAPORATING (separations by phase): the
  undissolved caught in paper; the solvent walked off to leave
  the solid (sea salt in pans — the oldest works); the heavy
  settled from the light.
- DISTILLING (separation by boiling point): heat the mixture,
  take the vapor of the lower boiler, cool it back — alcohol at
  78°C leaves water's 100 behind. Governs spirits, solvents, and
  oil's whole refinery (crude cut into gases, gasoline, diesel,
  tar by boiling range). CONDITION: some pairs board a joint
  boil and stop the trick — alcohol-water travels as one above
  ~96%, and the last water must leave by another road.
- CHROMATOGRAPHY (separation by clinginess): a mixture carried
  along a surface parts by how hard each member holds on — the
  ink dot on wet paper unbraiding into dyes is the whole
  principle; slowed and instrumented it resolves almost
  anything.
- TITRATION (the measured drip): to read an unknown strength,
  pay it a counted equivalent — drip the known solution in until
  an indicator turns, then read moles off volume (the arithmetic
  in SCIENCE). The kitchen has no need of it; the brewery,
  clinic, and water works run on it.
- DRIVING EQUILIBRIUM (the lean exploited): flood an input,
  remove a product as it forms (vent the gas, freeze out the
  water, precipitate the salt), or squeeze where fewer gas
  molecules stand on one side. Reluctant reactions are completed,
  not begged.
- ELECTROCHEMISTRY (the reaction split in two): part a willing
  electron transfer into two rooms and make the electrons
  commute through a wire — that is every battery; run a current
  backwards through an unwilling reaction and buy it — that is
  electrolysis, plating, and aluminum (a metal that was
  jewelry-rare until bought electrically, then cheaper than
  tin — economy holds the crash).
- PROTECTING FROM OXYGEN (the rust methods, one law four coats):
  exclude it (paint, oil, airless packing), outrank it (zinc on
  steel corrodes first on purpose — the sacrificial layer),
  outrun it (dry the surface: rust needs water AND air; either
  absent, iron keeps), or intercept it (antioxidants take the
  theft on themselves — lemon juice on the cut apple adds acid's
  enzyme-stop besides). Choose by which condition is cheapest to
  deny.
- SYNTHESIS BY STEPS (building molecules): assemble large from
  small, each step its own threshold, catalyst, and yield —
  and yields MULTIPLY: five steps at 80% each deliver
  0.8⁵ ≈ 33% overall. The multiplication law is why long routes
  are dear, why a one-step catalytic route dethrones a fine
  ten-step one, and why process chemists are paid to delete
  steps.
- HIRING ENZYMES (life's catalysts borrowed): ferment, brew,
  leaven, cheese — and now industry's kettles run on purified
  enzymes at body temperature where bare chemistry would demand
  a furnace. Breaks when: heat or pH strays outside the narrow
  window the protein folds in — an enzyme is a shape, and the
  ladder that unfolds cooking's egg unfolds it too.
- READING BY LIGHT (analysis without touching): elements sign
  their names in color — sodium's yellow streetlamp flare,
  copper's green flame, strontium's red firework — and every
  substance absorbs its own signature wavelengths. Instrumented,
  this reads what stars are made of from their light alone: the
  chemistry of places no sample will ever leave.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- GLASS — transparent, nearly inert, formable, cheap: chemistry's
  home material; it refuses almost every reagent (lye hot and
  one etching acid are its short list of enemies).
- THE BALANCE — conservation's own instrument: mass is the
  bookkeeper, and the science's founding revolution was weighing
  EVERYTHING, gases included (HISTORY). A modern bench balance
  reads to a ten-thousandth of a gram.
- THE THERMOMETER AND THE BURNER — the clock law's two handles:
  read the rate's driver, set it.
- pH PAPER AND METERS — the logarithm made visible: a strip's
  color to half a unit, a meter to hundredths.
- THE FUME HOOD AND THE LABEL — the means of staying alive at
  the bench; the label is chemistry's claim-with-conditions
  stuck to its own bottle.
- CATALYSTS AS STOCK-IN-TRADE — iron (fertilizer), platinum and
  its kin (the car's exhaust cleaner, burning the unburned at
  the tailpipe), enzymes (life's, hired). A working catalyst is
  worth more than the metal it is made of — it is a method cast
  as a material.
- THE STILL, THE COLUMN, THE CENTRIFUGE — separation's hardware:
  boiling point, clinginess, and weight, each given an engine.
- THE ELECTRICAL SUPPLY — electrons by the ampere: the reagent
  that never arrives in a bottle.
- THE SPECTROSCOPE — matter interrogated by light; the only
  instrument that assays the sun.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- MAKE NEW SUBSTANCES: materials, medicines, dyes, fuels,
  plastics — matter to order; the purpose alchemy wanted and
  chemistry, differently, delivers.
- KNOW WHAT A THING IS: the assay — the ore's worth, the
  poison's name, the water's safety, the counterfeit's tell.
  Analysis is half the subject.
- KEEP AND PROTECT: against oxygen, rot, and time — rust-
  proofing, preservation, stabilizers; cooking and engineering
  both enter here.
- FEED THE MANY: fixed nitrogen from the air — roughly half the
  people alive are built of fertilizer nitrogen from one
  catalytic reaction. No purpose on this page outweighs it.
- CURE: medicine's molecules, keyed to the body's handed locks;
  dose is concentration arithmetic, and the twins are tested
  separately.
- STORE AND MOVE ENERGY: fuels (bonds banked) and batteries
  (the transfer split and wired); the gap between them is a
  standing want in the mirror.
- CLEAN: soap and solvents — the mediator molecule set loose on
  the world's grease.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Fire and metal came before understanding: cooking, pottery,
  smelting — chemistry commanded for millennia by lore, method
  outrunning why (the same proof cooking's history holds, from
  the other side).
- Alchemy's long try bought the bench: centuries chasing
  transmutation and elixirs failed at both (the mirror holds why,
  in numbers) — and invented distillation, the acids, the
  balance-work, and the habit of writing procedures down. The
  failed goal's equipment became the science's means; nothing
  surpassed was discarded.
- The balance made it a science: weigh everything, gases
  included, and the books balance — combustion is joining air's
  oxygen, not releasing phlogiston; mass is conserved. One
  instrument, honestly applied, retired a century's theory
  (the mirror keeps the corpse as a lesson in how theories
  die — by a number).
- Atoms counted by ratios: fixed proportions in every compound
  said matter comes in pieces; the mole gave the pieces a
  ledger.
- The periodic table dared its gaps: elements ordered by weight
  showed repeating character; the orderer left holes and
  PREDICTED the missing elements' properties — and they arrived
  as described. Pattern promoted to law by successful prophecy.
- The vital wall fell: urea — an animal's molecule — synthesized
  from mineral stock proved life's chemistry is chemistry.
  The relation to LIFE was rebuilt on that spot (RELATIONS).
- Fixed nitrogen fed the century: the iron-catalyzed pull of
  fertilizer from the air ended the manure ceiling on
  agriculture — and armed the same century's wars with
  explosives from the same kettle. One reaction, both ledgers.
- The handed lesson, paid in harm: a sedative sold as one
  substance was two twins, one of them a maimer of the unborn —
  and the body converts between them, so purity alone was no
  rescue. Test the twins separately, and their interconversion:
  law written in that ink.
- The inert turned on its users: refrigerant gases chosen
  PRECISELY for their harmlessness — unburnable, unbreathable-
  safe, unreactive — survived unreacted all the way to the sky's
  ozone and thinned it. "Inert here" is not "inert everywhere";
  the fuller pattern (lead, radium, and kin) is filed in the
  mirror where it belongs.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward. Each names
what runs along it.

- PHYSICS: the whole foundation — bonds ARE the electric pull,
  temperature IS molecular motion, reaction heat obeys energy
  conservation, and the nucleus (where element identity lives)
  is physics's territory: the wall that certifies alchemy's
  impossibility is physics reaching into this phylum.
- MATHEMATICS: the balanced books (ratios and proportion), the
  pH logarithm, the rate exponential, the yield multiplication,
  the dilution equality — chemistry computes with borrowed
  grammar throughout.
- COOKING: browning, caramel, ferment, the 4.6 line, dissolving,
  emulsions, the unfolding ladder — cooking is this phylum's
  kitchen branch, and the traffic runs both ways (the kitchen
  found by lore what the bench later explained).
- LIFE: life is carbon chemistry run by enzyme catalysts on
  one-handed parts; photosynthesis banks the sun into bonds and
  respiration spends them — the two halves of one equation run
  in opposite directions.
- BODY: digestion is hired hydrolysis, blood pH is a buffer
  holding a tenth of a unit, nerves run on salt's ions, breath
  is controlled combustion at 37°C — the body is a reactor with
  opinions.
- MEDICINE: drugs are molecules keyed to handed receptors; dose
  is the concentration arithmetic; interactions are competing
  equilibria. The twins rule runs along this edge.
- FARMING: soil pH decides which crops thrive and which
  nutrients lock up; fixed nitrogen is the yield ceiling's
  release; the pesticide and its residue both travel this road.
- WEATHER: the atmosphere is a reacting mixture — ozone made
  and unmade, acid rain from sulfur's oxidation, CO₂'s slow
  ledger; water's odd heat capacity is the climate's flywheel.
- ECONOMY: oil, ore, fertilizer, plastics — the chemical
  industries; gold's price is its unreactivity (the metal that
  refuses oxygen keeps); aluminum's price crash when
  electrolysis replaced chemistry proper is the textbook case
  of a method change repricing a commodity.
- ENGINEERING: materials are frozen chemistry — steel's carbon
  tenths of a percent, cement's slow hydration, polymers by the
  ton, the semiconductor's doped lattice; corrosion is the
  standing tax every structure pays this phylum.
- CULTURE: dyes and pigments (purple once worth its weight in
  silver, then synthesized from coal tar and democratized),
  photography's silver chemistry, alchemy's stories, the
  firework's strontium red — color itself, for most of history,
  was a chemical possession.
- ANIMALS: venoms, pheromones, digestion — animal life run as chemistry.
- ASTRONOMY: spectra name the elements in stars; the periodic table was forged in them.
- BUILDING: cement's cure, steel's rust, mortar's set — building stands on reactions.
- CRAFT: glazes, dyes, tempering, tanning — craft is applied reactions by hand.
- EARTH: rock weathering, ore formation, and the slow chemistry of soil and sea.
- ELECTRONICS: doping, etching, batteries — electronics is chemistry made switchable.
- FLIGHT: fuel energy per kilogram sets what can fly; combustion is the engine's chemistry.
- THE MIND: transmitters and drugs — the mind's dials are molecules.
- PLANTS: photosynthesis, alkaloids, scents — the plant is a chemical factory rooted.
- UPKEEP: soap on grease, acid on scale, bleach on stain — cleaning is bench chemistry at home.
