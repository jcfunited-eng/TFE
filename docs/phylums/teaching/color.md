## THINGS
The units this subject is about; each names its other homes so a
cascade can branch.

- THE PRIOR — what already stands in the learner's head; new
  knowledge holds only where it hooks onto this. No mind is an
  empty vessel; also: mind, farming (the soil decides what the
  seed can do), building (the foundation).
- MEMORY — a web, not a shelf: kept by connections, strengthened
  by being pulled out, weakened untraveled; also: mind, computing.
- THE WORKING SPACE — the bench holding a problem while worked: a
  few items, no more; everything not yet automatic passes through
  it; also: mind, computing (a machine out of working space).
- ATTENTION — one beam; whatever else runs (feed, layout, dread,
  a disorderly room) is subtracted from the problem; also: mind,
  movement (choking), design.
- THE ERROR — the only event marking exactly where the model is
  wrong, which no success can do; also: engineering (the fallen
  bridge), craft, measurement.
- THE MODEL — the learner's working picture; the wrong one is not
  replaced by teaching but joined by it unless found and
  compared; also: mind, story, physics (the ball "with force in
  it").
- PRACTICE — repetition with correction at the edge of ability;
  without correction, hours; also: movement, music, craft.
- FEEDBACK — the return signal, aimed at the work or the person;
  the aim decides everything; also: engineering, people (face).
- THE TEACHER — chooser of the next task, finder of the wrong
  model; among what a school controls this matters most, by a
  distance; also: people, craft, governance.
- THE TUTOR — one adult, one learner: a correction loop closing
  in seconds instead of a week; also: engineering, medicine.
- THE CLASS — thirty heads at one pace, which fits somebody and
  misses almost everybody else, by arithmetic; also: mathematics,
  transport (one timetable, many trips).
- THE CHAIN — a subject as links in order; a missing link stops
  rather than slows, and every lesson built over the gap widens
  it; also: building, computing.
- THE TEST — an act of learning wearing a measurement's clothes;
  also: measurement, mind.
- THE MARK — the incentive that actually arrives; students learn
  whatever it is given for; also: economy, law.
- THE EXAM — makes claims about strangers comparable, at the
  price of one syllabus on one clock; also: measurement (a shared
  standard as treaty), governance.
- MOTIVATION — a product of believed-possible and worth-having; a
  zero in either gives zero; also: mind, economy.
- SLEEP — the filing shift: studied-then-slept beats the same
  study followed by a night awake; also: body, mind.
- INDEPENDENCE — the teacher's real product: the work succeeds
  when no longer needed; also: craft (the masterpiece made
  alone), medicine (the patient who stops coming), people
  (parenting as planned obsolescence).

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
applicability to the query at hand, never absolute.

- Learning builds on what stands: new knowledge holds only where
  it hooks onto old, and the wrong model must be found before the
  right one can land. A missing hook is not a difficulty but an
  impossibility (priced in SCIENCE).
- Doing well DURING a lesson and KEEPING after it are different
  quantities, and arrangements raising the first often lower the
  second: smooth, blocked, well-supported practice looks like
  excellent teaching all week and is gone by the holidays.
- The feeling of knowing tracks how easily a thing came to mind
  NOW, not whether it returns tomorrow — nothing else is
  available to consult. This is why learners abandon the methods
  that work and defend the ones that fail them.
- A test is an act of learning: pulling an answer out changes
  what is stored more than putting it in again — the pulled gets
  stronger, the near-misses pushed aside get weaker. Pulling
  beats re-putting everywhere they race (receipts in SCIENCE).
- Spaced beats crammed; the gap is set by how long the knowledge
  must last, and must be long enough that the pull takes real
  work — the work is the whole mechanism.
- Mixing kinds beats twenty of one kind: the block hides the
  hardest part — deciding WHICH method this is — and the mix puts
  that decision back at the start of every question. Slower, more
  errors, far better when questions arrive unlabelled.
- Guessing before being told beats being told, even wrong: the
  attempt finds the hooks and shapes a hole the answer lands in.
  An error plus fast correction is the fastest teacher — landing
  best while the miss is warm.
- Feedback aimed at the work continues the learning; aimed at the
  person it stops it — "this step went wrong" sends the learner
  back in; "careless" and "brilliant" both send them out. And a
  grade beside a comment eats the comment: the number closes the
  question, so the reasoning is never read.
- What is learned in one dress does not walk to another: the
  percentage skill stays in the lesson when the sum appears on a
  wage slip, unless teaching crosses the costume and names what
  the cases share. A single example teaches the example.
- There is no general thinking skill apart from something to
  think about: judgement in a field is mostly a large store of
  its cases — the puzzle club improves the puzzles and nothing
  else.
- Effort is a product, not a sum: work happens when success seems
  possible AND worth something; the confident who see no point
  and the eager who expect to fail both do nothing. Visible
  progress beats applied pressure (~ as iron law; pressure-only
  schooling's wreckage is the evidence): pressure buys compliance
  and burns the wish.
- Students learn whatever the marks reward — grade the essay on
  neatness and length and by winter the class writes neat long
  essays with no argument. Teaching aims at the SHAPE of the
  test, so changing the paper changes classrooms, meant or not.
- Praise strategy and persistence, never the person: "clever"
  hands the child a label to protect, and the hard task becomes
  its threat. Two learners work equally hard for opposite reasons
  — getting better (a mistake is information) or looking able (a
  mistake is a disaster to hide) — and a comparison room
  manufactures the second kind. And every learner meets the
  struggle; what decides the outcome is the explanation the room
  supplies: this is what learning feels like, or the suited find
  it easy. Both are taught, usually without a word.
- Skill climbs stages — rules (slow, spoken), then feel, then
  wordless flow — none skippable. Fluency and understanding are
  not rivals; each buys the other, because automatic pieces free
  the bench that understanding needs.
- Experts cannot remember not-knowing: practice merges steps into
  units no longer separately inspectable — one pattern where the
  beginner sees nine features — so the explanation starts three
  moves in. The best knowers are often the worst explainers (the
  tapping game: obvious to the tapper, noise to the listener).
- Hours are not practice: doing what you already can, slightly
  wrong, unknowingly, produces twenty years of the same year — in
  medicine, driving, and music alike.
- Sleep files the day: the hard passage is smoother in the
  morning unplayed (~ overnight gains on practised sequences run
  to a fifth better).
- Reading is decoding × subject knowledge — a comprehension
  problem is often a knowledge problem wearing its clothes.
  Writing is not recording a finished thought; it is the
  operation that finds out whether there was one.
- The teacher matters most among what a school controls, and no
  hiring proxy predicts it — not degree, certificate, or years;
  only watching the teaching does.
- Attention is one beam: studying beside a feed is switching with
  a restart cost — longer, shallower, and the person feels fine.
  Dread takes the same equipment, then feeds itself through
  avoided practice.
- Everything a teacher does works only through what the learner
  does — one question governs: what is the learner made to think
  about? A lesson leaving it unanswered has entertained a room.

## SCIENCE
The math of teaching, held — not mentioned. Each piece is a LAW a
query can compute with, a RULE to follow, and where it earns it, a
WORKED case proving the rule runs.

### THE CHAIN ARITHMETIC — why the gap must be small

LAW: a lesson holds only for the mind holding every link it leans
  on; the chance of arriving is the product of per-link chances:
  P = p^N for N links each standing with probability p.
RULE: count the links, estimate the fraction of the room holding
  each, multiply through — then CHECK prerequisites, never assume.
WORKED: ten links at 95% each: 0.95² = 0.9025 · squared = 0.8145
  · squared = 0.6634 · × 0.9025 = 0.599 — ~6 in 10 arrive with
  the whole chain, in a lesson that felt reasonable at every
  step. At 80% a link: 0.8¹⁰ = 0.107 — 9 in 10 lost. Backwards:
  for 9 in 10 to arrive across ten links, each must stand at ~99%
  — "check, don't assume" is the arithmetic's only exit.
LAW: the coverage debt compounds — move on over a gap and every
  later lesson leaning on it fails too, while looking like a
  lesson about something else. RULE: fix a failing learner by
  diagnosing the missing link and teaching THAT; more exposure at
  the same speed treats the chain instead of the break, and fails.

### THE FORGETTING CURVE AND THE SPACING LADDER

LAW: memory falls steeply, then flattens (~ the first measured
  numbers, still holding shape): after 20 minutes ~58% remains ·
  an hour ~44% · a day ~34% · a week ~25% · a month ~21%. Most
  loss is in the first day; what survives the week mostly
  survives the month.
LAW: each successful pull near the edge of loss flattens the
  curve — the climb back is the strengthening — so the stable gap
  multiplies with success (~ ×2 to ×2.5 per pull is what working
  schedules run on).
RULE: size the gap at roughly a tenth of the horizon — hold a
  week, review in a day; hold a year, leave a month — then let
  gaps climb. A pull that costs nothing strengthens nothing.
WORKED: the ×2.5 ladder from 1 day: gaps of 1 · 2.5 · 6.25 ·
  15.6 · 39.1 · 97.7 days — six pulls of a minute carry the fact
  across 1+2.5+6.25+15.6+39.1+97.7 ≈ 162 days. Six minutes, five
  months. The crammer's hour buys the exam and is on the curve by
  nightfall — ~a third left in a day. Exam in 60 days → gaps of
  ~6 · 15 · 37: three visits inside the window, against thirty
  rereadings that walk the eye and not the memory.

### THE RETRIEVAL RECEIPTS

LAW: pulling beats re-putting, and the advantage REVERSES with
  delay — why the losing method keeps its customers.
WORKED (~ the standing measurements): same passage, same total
  time; at five minutes the rereaders lead ~81% to ~75% — and
  predict they will keep leading. A week later the tested group
  holds ~56% against ~42%. The felt and the real ranking cross
  between the lesson and the holidays; every "it went well" read
  at five minutes is an instrument error.
WORKED (~): foreign vocabulary learned to one standard, then kept
  in the pull rotation or merely restudied: a week later ~80%
  against ~35%. Restudying the known adds almost nothing;
  retrieving it again roughly doubles what survives.

### THE WORKING SPACE BUDGET

LAW: the bench holds about four chunks — a chunk being whatever
  practice has merged into one unit. Capacity is fixed; chunk
  size is not. This is the whole economics of expertise.
RULE: count what a task makes a beginner hold; everything not yet
  automatic is a separate item, and past about four the thinking
  stops. Cut steps, automate pieces first, keep the reference in
  view — whatever a presentation wastes (the key on the next
  page, the slide read aloud, the split example) is paid from the
  same account as the problem.
WORKED: B B C F B I C I A — nine letters is past the bench and
  drops; BBC · FBI · CIA is three chunks and trivial. Same marks,
  same order: the difference is entirely what the reader already
  holds. This is the beginner's condition — and why the child who
  must reconstruct 7×8 spends a bench slot the fluent child
  spends on the reasoning.

### THE MOTIVATION PRODUCT

LAW: effort ∝ believed-chance-of-success × worth-of-success — a
  product, so a zero in either term zeroes the whole.
RULE: for the learner doing nothing, find which term is the zero
  and raise THAT: visible progress raises the first, a use
  actually wanted raises the second; pressure raises neither.
WORKED: on 0-to-1 scales — sure but pointless: 0.9 × 0 = 0.
  Wanted but hopeless: 0 × 0.9 = 0. Middling both: 0.5 × 0.6 =
  0.30, which BEATS certain-but-nearly-worthless 1.0 × 0.1 =
  0.10. Raise the low term first; nothing multiplies out of a
  zero.

### THE SIGMA SCALE AND THE TUTOR LOOP

LAW: a sigma is the class's own spread-step; on the usual bell
  +1σ stands at the ~84th percentile of the untouched class, +2σ
  at the ~98th — gains stated in sigmas compare across measures.
LAW: the tutor's power is loop speed, not affection: each error
  sensed as it happens, the next thing said changed — a loop
  closing in seconds against the classroom's week.
WORKED (~ the famous measurement, caveats attached): mastery
  pacing alone ~+1σ — the average student to the ~84th percentile
  of the lectured class; tutoring plus mastery ~+2σ — above ~98%.
  Caveats that stay attached: small studies, short units, tests
  close to the taught material. Large real-world tutoring
  programs measure ~+0.3 to +0.5σ — still the ~62nd to ~69th
  percentile for the middle student, among the largest levers
  ever measured. The gap between +0.4 and +2 is the scaling
  problem, filed in the mirror.
WORKED, the contrast (~): cutting a class from ~23 to ~15 — a
  third fewer chairs — buys ~+0.2σ at nearly a second teacher's
  price. Chairs do not teach; what teaches is what the learner is
  made to do, and empty chairs change that only if the teaching
  changes.

### THE PACE AND THE SPREAD

LAW: one speed cannot match a spread. A class must be paced for
  somebody, and whoever that is, it is wrong for almost everybody
  else — arithmetic, not a failure of the teacher.
WORKED: model the room as the usual bell; a single pace reaches
  learners within half a spread-step of its target — a band
  holding ~38% of the room. ~31% sit above it bored, ~31% below
  it drowning. Every remedy — setting, differentiated work,
  self-pacing — is a different way of paying this one law.

### THE COMPOUNDING GAP

LAW: a lead buys the means of keeping the lead — the economy's
  compounding, run in vocabulary: words are learned mostly by
  meeting them in reading, and reading runs on words already
  held, so the gap widens on its own, fastest when school is
  closed.
WORKED (~): the child reading ~20 minutes a day meets ~1.8
  million words a year; the child who barely reads meets under
  ~100,000 — ~18-fold before any lesson is counted. By
  adolescence the gap outruns what a lesson can close; the
  working intervention is early, where compounding is cheapest to
  interrupt.
LAW, the reading consequence: comprehension = decoding ×
  knowledge, another product — the fluent reader who knows
  nothing of cricket understands nothing of a match report, and
  no strategy lesson raises a zero knowledge term.

## METHODS
How teaching is done — each with what governs it and what breaks
it. The prepositioned how-to-build.

- RETRIEVAL PRACTICE: closed-book recall, low-stakes quizzing,
  telling, doing — the pull made routine. Breaks when stakes
  climb (dread takes the bench) or the pull is free.
- THE SPACED SCHEDULE: gaps ~a tenth of the horizon, climbing
  with success. Breaks when gaps make the pull free, or let the
  fact fully go — the edge of loss is the target, not the ditch.
- INTERLEAVING: kinds shuffled so every question begins with
  "which method is this?" Feels worse, tests better. Breaks on
  absolute beginners who own no methods to choose between.
- GUESS FIRST, THEN TELL: minutes of attempt before explanation.
  Breaks when the guess goes uncorrected — a wrong method
  practised is a wrong method learned.
- WORKED EXAMPLES, THEN FADE: beginners study completed solutions
  (no method yet to retrieve); the practised attempt, and are
  slowed by walkthroughs. The same support is a ladder at one
  stage, a weight at the next; breaks when the stage is misread.
- SCAFFOLDING WITH REMOVAL SCHEDULED: prompts, sheets, the
  teacher nearby all become part of the performance, and nothing
  removes help by itself. A skill only ever done with the aid has
  not been learned yet.
- FEEDBACK ON THE WORK: name the failing step, keep the person
  out of the sentence, comment WITHOUT the grade when improvement
  is the goal (~ measured: comment-only improved later work ~30%;
  grade-only and grade-plus-comment improved nothing).
- WHOLE-CLASS CHECKS: everyone answers at once — boards, cards, a
  written line — because hands-up samples the confident four and
  cannot report on the rest.
- WAIT TIME: hold the silence (~ most teachers wait under a
  second; three to four changes who answers, lengthens answers
  severalfold, draws questions back). A silence filled by the
  teacher is a retrieval interrupted.
- VARIED EXAMPLES, SHARED PART SAID ALOUD: cases agreeing in
  structure, differing in everything else. Breaks when variation
  is cosmetic or the shared bone goes unspoken.
- CROSSING THE COSTUME: the same idea met in lesson, wage slip,
  recipe, game — its bone named each time. Transfer is built this
  way or not at all.
- FLUENCY AND UNDERSTANDING TOGETHER: drill pieces to automatic
  (freeing the bench) AND build the whole; either alone strands
  the learner — the argument between them is false.
- DELIBERATE PRACTICE, four parts, none optional: a task just
  past reach · immediate specific correction · repetition of the
  failing piece, not the whole · somebody choosing the next task.
  Ordinary work supplies none — why experience alone plateaus.
- DIAGNOSIS, THEN REMEDIATION: find the broken link, teach the
  link. Repeating the year at the same speed re-runs the exposure
  that already failed; the problem was never the amount.
- MASTERY PACING: the next topic waits until this one is held —
  paying the coverage debt up front, because the chain arithmetic
  charges compound interest on every gap moved over.
- ROUTINES FIRST: nothing is learned in a room that cannot be
  settled; order is bought with routines in the first weeks, not
  volume or punishment (those are what its loss looks like).
- THE RELATIONSHIP AS CHANNEL: learners work for adults they
  believe are on their side; the same lesson from someone who has
  given up on them lands as nothing.
- HOMEWORK AS PRACTICE OF THE KNOWN: spacing and retrieval on the
  understood earn their keep; a new topic sent home yields a
  wrong method practised alone, a parent's answers, or an
  argument — nobody there to catch the miss.
- NOTES IN OWN WORDS: the value is almost all in the deciding, so
  a rough own summary beats a transcript, and complete slides
  handed out delete the listener's most useful work.
- MATCH WORK TO KIND: a fact is laid down by retrieval at
  spreading gaps; a procedure by varied cases with correction.
  Treat either as the other and the practice quietly does nothing.
- TUTORING: the loop at full speed. The strongest measured method
  and the least scalable; its scaling white is the mirror's
  largest open entry.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE TEACHER — matters most among what a school controls,
  predicted by no paper proxy, judged only by watching; their
  scarcest resource is the loop.
- THE TUTOR — the loop at one-adult-per-learner prices; why the
  strongest method waits in the mirror for a scalable form.
- THE TEST — double instrument: as measurement it reads the room;
  as exercise it IS the learning. Low stakes keep the bench
  clear; high stakes hand it to dread.
- THE MARK — the loudest statement of what is wanted; spend it
  exactly on what should be learned, for it is obeyed to the
  letter.
- THE EXAM PAPER — the strongest lever outside the classroom: its
  shape steers a thousand rooms, meant or not.
- CARDS AND SCHEDULERS — the spacing ladder made mechanical;
  minutes a day, gaps climbing on success. The cheapest strong
  means in the subject.
- BOARDS IN EVERY HAND — the whole-class check's instrument;
  thirty guesses become one reading.
- THE WORKED EXAMPLE — the beginner's borrowed method, faded as
  their own grows.
- THE ROOM AND ITS ROUTINES — the container of the attention
  budget; settled early or spent forever after.
- SLEEP — free, universal, filing nightly; the plan ending in a
  short night spends its own gains.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- MOVE KNOWLEDGE BETWEEN HEADS: what one mind holds, another can
  come to hold, with neither copied.
- MAKE THE LEARNER INDEPENDENT: the work succeeds the moment it
  is no longer needed; performance needing the teacher present is
  borrowing, not learning.
- KEEP KNOWLEDGE ALIVE ACROSS GENERATIONS: what cannot be taught
  dies with its holders.
- MAKE CLAIMS ABOUT STRANGERS COMPARABLE: the exam's genuine
  product, its price (one syllabus, one clock) filed with it.
- CLOSE THE GAPS THAT COMPOUND: the vocabulary loop, the coverage
  debt — teaching as the deliberate interruption of compounding
  disadvantage.
- MAKE THE MANY CAPABLE: reading, counting, writing as the
  infrastructure every other phylum's queries quietly assume.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Apprenticeship first, before writing: watch, attempt, be
  corrected at the bench — the tutor loop is older than any
  school, ending in a masterpiece made alone, judged by
  strangers: independence the stated product from the start.
- Writing detached teaching from presence: knowledge outlived its
  holder, at the price of the loop — the page cannot see the
  reader's error. Every method since partly buys the loop back.
- The lecture was born of book scarcity: one manuscript, one
  voice, many copying hands. The press removed its reason and the
  lecture stayed — its measured demotion as transfer is in the
  mirror; its remaining uses (orientation, demonstration, the
  scarce expert's reach) stay here.
- Mass schooling: reading for everyone, one adult per thirty —
  the pace-and-spread arithmetic became the standing problem;
  years, sets, syllabi, and exams are its payments.
- Forgetting was measured (~ the 1880s): one researcher, nonsense
  syllables, his own memory — the curve's numbers still hold
  shape. First proof that learning obeys laws a schedule can
  compute with.
- The testing effect was noticed (~ the 1900s), shelved a century
  as folklore, remeasured hard (~ the 2000s) — retrieval crowned
  over rereading after generations of highlighters.
- The two-sigma measurement (~ the 1980s): tutoring plus mastery
  moved the average student past ~98% of the lectured class — and
  was published as a PROBLEM: find what scales. Still open in the
  mirror.
- The great pruning (~ the 2000s-2010s): learning styles,
  discovery-for-novices, lecture-as-transfer, brain training —
  measured and cut one by one. The white grew more in that
  stretch than the color: what a field growing honest looks like.
