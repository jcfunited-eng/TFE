## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- THE CELL — the unit of life, no exceptions found: a fenced drop
  of busy water that eats, copies, and comes only from another
  cell. Sizes: bacteria ~1-2 µm, our own cells ~10-20 µm; a human
  is ~37 trillion of them cooperating. Also: body, medicine,
  chemistry (it is a bag of reactions).
- MICROBES — bacteria, archaea, yeasts, molds: the invisible
  majority. A gram of soil holds ~a billion; a square centimeter
  of skin ~a million; a person carries about as many microbe
  passengers as body cells (~40 trillion, mostly in the gut).
  Workers and spoilers, decided by control. Also: cooking,
  medicine, farming.
- VIRUSES — recipe fragments in a coat, running only inside cells;
  they stand at the door of "alive" (the mirror holds the
  argument). Also: medicine, chemistry.
- ENZYMES — the cell's molecular tools: each one a folded protein
  shaped for one job, speeding it a millionfold to beyond a
  trillionfold. Each has a temperature window and a pH window and
  unfolds one-way past its edge — the same ladder cooking climbs
  on purpose. Also: chemistry, cooking, body, medicine.
- DNA — the recipe: a four-letter text, copied on every division,
  read constantly. Recipe, NOT blueprint — it states procedure,
  and outcome depends on conditions (see CLAIMS). Also: chemistry,
  medicine, farming.
- PROTEINS — what most of the recipe names: the tools, walls,
  pumps, and motors of the cell, each folded from a chain of
  amino-acid parts. Also: chemistry, cooking, the body.
- SUGAR AND STARCH — the common fuel and its stored form; the coin
  both sides of the energy ledger trade in. Also: cooking,
  chemistry, farming.
- FAT — the dense store (about twice sugar's energy per gram) and
  the stuff of every cell's fence. Also: body, cooking, chemistry.
- WATER — the medium of all of it; life's chemistry runs only
  where water is liquid. Also: chemistry, weather, cooking.
- OXYGEN AND CARBON DIOXIDE — the breath pair: one kind's exhaust,
  the other kind's intake, both directions (see the ledger in
  SCIENCE). Also: chemistry, weather, the body.
- LIGHT — the energy source at the bottom of nearly every food
  chain; captured by the leaf, spent by everything downstream.
  Also: physics, weather, farming.
- NITROGEN — the scarce ingredient: air is mostly nitrogen yet
  almost nothing can eat it raw; the few microbes that fix it feed
  the rest. Usually the field's limiting factor. Also: farming,
  chemistry, economy (fertilizer).
- POPULATIONS — the compounding unit: any kind, counted, growing
  by doubling until something runs out. Also: mathematics,
  economy, medicine (an outbreak is one).
- SPECIES AND LINEAGES — populations shaped by selection over
  generations; the units history flows through. Also: farming
  (breeds), culture (domestication).
- SEEDS AND EGGS — beginnings in a package: recipe plus packed
  lunch plus a wall, waiting for conditions. Also: farming,
  cooking.
- ECOSYSTEMS — the ledger at landscape size: who eats whom, where
  the energy leaks, what cycles and what flows through. Also:
  weather, farming, economy.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- Every living thing is made of cells, and every cell comes from a
  cell. Condition: certified since the sealed flask (HISTORY); the
  one open exception is the first cell ever, which is the mirror's
  origin entry — impossibility in the flask, unknown at the
  beginning of the world.
- Living things eat and excrete, each by its kind — and one kind's
  exhaust is another kind's food. Plants exhale the oxygen animals
  burn; animals exhale the carbon dioxide plants build with; yeast
  eats sugar and pays in alcohol and gas (cooking's hire); the
  decomposers eat the dead and pay in soil. Nothing leaves the
  loop; the loop is the claim.
- Microbes are everywhere that is not deliberately made sterile —
  soil, skin, gut, air, ocean, boiling springs, brine at ~−15°C.
  "Clean" surfaces are merely sparse. This is why sterile
  technique must deny EVERYTHING and then admit one thing (see
  METHODS), and why food keeps only where their free water is
  denied (cooking holds that law).
- Enzymes work only in their windows: rate roughly doubles per
  10°C of warmth UP TO the unfolding edge (~40-60°C for most
  kinds), then falls off a cliff, one-way. Heat-lovers rebuild the
  same tools sturdier and grow at up to ~122°C. Cold does not
  break the tools; it slows them — the refrigerator's whole
  mechanism, seen from the microbe's side.
- Populations compound. Unchecked, any kind doubles on its own
  clock — and nothing stays unchecked for long: food, space, or a
  predator runs out first, and WHICH runs out governs the count.
  The limit, not the doubling, is what a stable field shows.
- Evolution needs exactly three things: variation (the copies
  differ), selection (the differences change who leaves
  offspring), inheritance (the differences pass on). Where all
  three are present it is arithmetic, not opinion — days in a
  flask, ages in a forest. Worked on resistance in SCIENCE.
- Selection has no foresight and no ladder: it fits a population
  to CURRENT conditions only. Change the conditions and yesterday's
  best is today's burden (the mirror files the ladder belief).
- DNA is a recipe, not a blueprint: no part of the text maps to a
  part of the body the way a drawing maps to a machine. Receipts:
  one caterpillar recipe also builds the butterfly; a queen bee
  and her sterile worker share one recipe and differ by feeding;
  identical twins share every letter and differ in fingerprints.
  Recipe plus conditions makes the organism — never recipe alone.
- Copying the recipe makes typos at a known rate, and the typos
  are the variation selection sorts. Mutation is not rare
  catastrophe; it is a steady drizzle (~3 letters per human cell
  division — the arithmetic is in SCIENCE).
- Energy flows one way through life; matter goes around. Sunlight
  enters at the leaf, degrades to heat at every step, and leaves;
  carbon, water, and nitrogen cycle and are used again. A food
  chain is one-way spending (the ~10% law in SCIENCE); a compost
  heap is matter turning the corner.
- Death is part of the build: a body sculpts itself partly by
  scheduled cell death — human fingers are carved from a paddle by
  the webbing dying on time — and an adult retires ~50-70 billion
  cells a day by program. Cell death is a tool, not only a defeat.
- Sex shuffles the recipe deck: two parents each give half, so
  every child is a new hand. Clones share one fate; shuffled
  offspring spread their bets. (WHY shuffling is worth its cost is
  unsettled — the mirror holds it.)
- Life runs only in the liquid-water window and pays constantly to
  stay ordered: every cell spends fuel to hold itself together
  against decay, and the spending stops only at death. An adult
  human turns over roughly their own body weight in the cell's
  energy coin (ATP) every day — remade, not stockpiled.

## SCIENCE
The math, physics, and chemistry of life, held — not mentioned.
Each piece is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it can
follow, and where it earns it, a WORKED case proving the rule runs.

### MATHEMATICS — the compounding law

LAW: N = N₀ × 2^(t ÷ doubling time).
  Doubling times, warm and fed: gut bacteria ~20 min (the
  rich-broth best case; in the gut itself, hours) · yeast
  ~90 min · human cells ~24 h · human populations — by the rule
  of 70 below.
RULE: count the doublings that fit in the time, raise 2 to that
  power. Handy anchors: 2^10 ≈ a thousand, 2^20 ≈ a million,
  2^30 ≈ a billion.
WORKED: one bacterium, warm broth, 6 hours = 18 doublings =
  2^18 = 262,144 cells. Ten hours = 30 doublings ≈ a billion.
  Unchecked at 20 minutes a doubling, one cell's line (~a
  trillionth of a gram each) would outweigh the Earth in ~133
  doublings — under two days. It never happens: the broth runs
  out. The gap between that arithmetic and any real count IS the
  limiting factor, made visible.

LAW: the rule of 70 — steady growth of g% per step doubles in
  ~70 ÷ g steps. (Same law as money's compound interest; the
  economy phylum holds that accent.)
WORKED: the human population grew ~1.8% a year through the late
  1900s: 70 ÷ 1.8 ≈ 39 years to double — and it did: 3 billion in
  1960, 6 billion in 1999. The law, run once, on us.

LAW: an outbreak compounds by its spread number R — cases next
  generation = R × cases now; it grows when R > 1 and dies when
  R < 1.
RULE: to stop a spreading kind without killing it everywhere —
  push R below 1. Immunizing a fraction f cuts R to R₀ × (1−f),
  so the threshold is f > 1 − 1/R₀.
WORKED: measles spreads at R₀ ≈ 15 in an open crowd: threshold
  1 − 1/15 = 14/15 ≈ 93% protected. At R = 2, one case is ~2^10 =
  1,024 cases ten generations later; at R = 0.9 it is 0.9^10 ≈
  0.35 — a fading echo. The whole difference is which side of 1.

### MATHEMATICS — the selection arithmetic

LAW: selection compounds like interest. A variant leaving s% more
  offspring per generation multiplies its share by (1 + s/100)
  each generation; by the rule of 70 it doubles its share every
  ~70/s generations.
WORKED: a mere 1% advantage doubles the variant's share every ~70
  generations — invisible in any one lifetime, unstoppable across
  ten thousand. Speed selection up (big s, short generations) and
  it runs before your eyes; that is the next entry.

WORKED IN FULL — antibiotic resistance, the standing live case:
  · copying typos happen at ~1 in 10^8 per gene per division, so
    a flask of 10^9 bacteria already holds ~10 cells carrying a
    resistance typo BEFORE any drug arrives (10^9 ÷ 10^8 = 10);
  · the drug kills the sensitive billion and misses the ~10;
  · the 10 double every ~20 minutes: 10 × 2^27 ≈ 1.3 × 10^9 —
    27 doublings, nine hours, and the flask is full again, now
    entirely resistant.
  The drug created nothing. It SELECTED what variation had
  already written. This is why resistance follows every antibiotic
  on schedule, why finishing a course and fencing the drug's use
  matter (METHODS), and why Fleming predicted the whole story in
  1945 before it happened (HISTORY).

### MATHEMATICS — the inheritance ratios

LAW: inheritance is particulate — each parent carries two copies
  of each recipe entry and deals ONE, at random, to each child.
  Copies come in variants; some variants (dominant) show whenever
  present, others (recessive) show only when both copies match.
RULE: to predict a cross — write each parent's two letters, pair
  every letter with every letter (four equally likely hands),
  count the hands that show each way.
WORKED: cross two carriers Rr × Rr: hands RR, Rr, rR, rr — three
  show the R way, one the rr way: 3:1. Mendel's actual peas:
  5,474 round to 1,850 wrinkled = 2.96 : 1. The ratio is the
  particle theory, counted on a table — and it is why a trait can
  skip a generation whole (two carriers, neither showing, deal an
  rr hand one time in four).

### CHEMISTRY AND PHYSICS — the energy ledger

LAW: photosynthesis and respiration are one equation run in two
  directions:
  6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light energy ⇌ C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) + 6 O₂
  Forward (the leaf): stores ~2,800 kJ per mole of glucose made —
  ~16 kJ per gram. Backward (everything that breathes): releases
  the same, never more. Life adds no energy; it captures, stores,
  and spends sunlight.
RULE: to trace energy through any living system — follow the
  sugar: made in the leaf, burned wherever there is breath;
  match grams × 16 kJ against the work claimed.
WORKED: a person on 2,000 kcal a day = 2,000 × 4.184 = 8,368 kJ =
  ~523 g of sugar-equivalent burned (8,368 ÷ 16). The oxygen bill:
  each 180 g of glucose burns with 192 g of O₂, so 523 × 192/180
  ≈ 560 g of oxygen a day ≈ 390 liters — about 0.27 liters a
  minute, which is just what a resting body measures. The ledger
  balances to the breath.

LAW: fields capture ~1% of sunlight as sugar (leaves' theoretical
  best ~11%; real crops run near 1).
WORKED: summer sun delivers ~18 MJ per m² per day (~5 kWh); 1% =
  180 kJ = ~11 g of sugar per m² per day (180 ÷ 16). A 100-day
  season: ~1.1 kg per m² ≈ ~11 tonnes per hectare of made
  plant-stuff — the right size of a real harvest. Farming's yield
  ceiling is this arithmetic, not effort.

LAW: eating up the chain keeps ~10% per step; ~90% is spent as
  heat and living costs at each level.
WORKED: 1,000 kg of grain → ~100 kg of chicken → ~10 kg of the
  eater of the chicken. Eating the grain directly feeds ~10× more
  people per field — the economy and farming phylums price this;
  life states it.

### PHYSICS — the size laws

LAW: surface grows by the square, volume by the cube: for size s,
  surface ∝ s², demand ∝ s³, so supply-per-demand ∝ 1/s. A cell
  feeds and breathes only through its surface.
WORKED: double a cell's width — 4× the skin, 8× the hunger:
  supply per demand HALVES. That is why cells stop at tens of
  microns and why an elephant is trillions of cells and not one
  big one. (Cooking's potato obeys the same law in the oven.)

LAW: metabolic rate scales as mass^(3/4) (Kleiber): per-gram fire
  slows as the fourth root of the mass ratio.
RULE: to compare per-gram burn between kinds — divide the masses,
  take the fourth root: that is the slowdown.
WORKED: elephant (5,000 kg) over mouse (20 g) = 250,000×; fourth
  root: √250,000 = 500, √500 ≈ 22. Each mouse gram burns ~22×
  hotter than an elephant gram — and the hearts agree, ~550 beats
  a minute against ~30, about the predicted twentyfold. Both
  spend ~a billion heartbeats in ~3 years and ~65 years
  respectively: fast fire, short wick. (A correlation with famous
  exceptions — the bat — filed in the mirror under aging.)

### CHEMISTRY — enzymes, the tools with windows

LAW: reaction speed roughly doubles per 10°C (the same law
  cooking holds), UP TO each enzyme's unfolding edge; past the
  edge the tool unfolds one-way and the rate goes to ~zero. Each
  enzyme also has a best pH: the stomach's pepsin works near
  pH 2, the gut's trypsin near pH 8 — each tool built for its
  room.
RULE: to place any organism's speed — know its temperature; halve
  or double per 10°C from its normal; respect the cliff.
WORKED: fever, 37 → 40°C: 2^(3/10) ≈ 1.23 — the body's chemistry
  runs ~23% hot. Cold, 37 → 4°C (the refrigerator): 2^(33/10) ≈
  10× slower — spoilage at a crawl, exactly cooking's cold-box
  number, derived here from the microbe's own tools.

### CHEMISTRY — the recipe's arithmetic

LAW: DNA is a 4-letter text read 3 letters at a word; each word
  names one amino-acid part; a chain of parts folds into one
  protein tool. A gene of 3n letters names a tool of n parts.
LAW: the human recipe is ~3.2 billion letters. At 2 bits a letter
  that is ~800 megabytes — a recipe smaller than a film, building
  and running a body of ~37 trillion cells for decades. The
  compression alone proves it is procedure-plus-conditions, not a
  part-for-part drawing.
LAW: copying, after proofreading, errs at ~1 in 10^9 letters.
WORKED: 3.2 × 10^9 letters ÷ 10^9 ≈ 3 typos per cell division.
  Every division a few; almost all silent or fatal to the one
  cell; the rare useful one is evolution's entire feedstock. The
  drizzle is slow per cell and vast per world.

### THE KILL ARITHMETIC (life's side of cooking's safety law)

LAW: heat kills microbe populations by equal FRACTIONS in equal
  times, not equal numbers — each minute at a given temperature
  cuts the survivors tenfold after the first log; so sterility is
  a probability, never an absolute.
LAW: the time-for-temperature trade is tenfold per ~7°C for
  ordinary microbes (held jointly with cooking: t₂ = t₁ ÷
  10^(ΔT ÷ 7)); the toughest spores trade nearer tenfold per
  ~10°C — the number the canning case below runs on.
RULE: to certify a jar — decide the survivor odds you can live
  with, count the tenfolds from the starting load to those odds,
  hold the temperature that long.
WORKED: canning's actual standard for the deadliest spore is
  "12 tenfolds": start even 10^6 spores, end at expected 10^-6 —
  one survivor per MILLION jars. That number, at botulism's
  toughness, is why low-acid canning requires the pressure
  canner's 121°C: the spore's 12 tenfolds take ~3 minutes at
  121°C but ~5 hours at 100°C — 21°C colder is 10^2.1 ≈ 130× the
  time; the spore laughs at a boil. Same law,
  same jar, as cooking's — restated here because the microbe, not
  the pot, is what the law is about.

## METHODS
How things are done and built here — each with what governs it,
when it applies, and what breaks it.

- STERILE TECHNIQUE (deny everything, then admit one thing):
  flame, alcohol, 121°C under pressure; then seed ONLY the kind
  you chose. Governed by microbes-everywhere and the kill
  arithmetic. Breaks on one touch, one open lid, one skipped
  minute — sterility is a probability you spend.
- CULTURING (grow the chosen microbe): food + warmth + time on a
  jelly it cannot eat (agar — chosen exactly because it is
  indigestible to almost everything). Governed by the doubling
  law: overnight, one invisible cell is a visible million-cell
  dot. Limit honestly held: most wild microbes refuse the dish
  (the mirror holds the uncultured majority).
- SELECTIVE BREEDING (evolution steered by hand): variation +
  CHOSEN parents + generations. Receipts: corn from a grass of
  5-12 hard kernels to ears of hundreds, over thousands of years;
  dairy yield ~5× in one human lifetime; every dog from one wolf
  stock. Governed by the selection arithmetic; breaks when
  variation runs out — then wait for the mutation drizzle or
  bring in outside stock.
- HIRING MICROBES (fermentation and manufacture): fence with salt,
  acid, or airlessness; feed sugar; the fenced-in workers pay in
  acid, alcohol, gas (cooking's whole fermented shelf) — or, with
  an edited recipe, in medicine: human insulin has been brewed by
  engineered microbes since 1982. Breaks as cooking states: when
  the fence fails, the spoilers eat first.
- VACCINATION (show the body the coat, not the enemy): the body's
  own learning defense drills on a harmless piece and remembers.
  Governed by the outbreak law — protect past 1 − 1/R₀ and the
  chain breaks for everyone, including those who cannot drill.
  Receipts: smallpox gone from the world (1980), cattle plague
  gone (2011).
- CROSSING AND COUNTING (Mendel's method): choose parents, cross,
  COUNT the offspring by kind, compare to the ratio table. The
  method that turned inheritance from lore into arithmetic; still
  the floor under every breeding and gene question.
- COPYING DNA BY CYCLES (PCR): heat to split the text's strands,
  cool to let primers land, let a heat-proof enzyme copy; repeat.
  The doubling law in its purest form: 30 cycles = 2^30 ≈ a
  billion copies from one. The enzyme that survives the hot step
  was borrowed from a boiling-spring microbe — the extremophile's
  window, made into a tool.
- READING THE RECIPE (sequencing): the letters of any organism,
  read end to end; cost fell from ~$3 billion for the first human
  read (2003) to ~hundreds of dollars. What reading does NOT yet
  give — the body from the letters — is filed in the mirror.
- COMPOSTING (hire the decomposers): pile carbon-rich with
  nitrogen-rich near ~30:1, keep damp and turned (air), and the
  microbes run it hot to ~60-70°C — hot enough to kill seeds and
  most spoilers by the kill arithmetic. Breaks: too wet or
  unturned goes airless and stinks; too carbon-poor won't heat.
- ANTIBIOTIC STEWARDSHIP (spend a depleting resource slowly):
  dose full, finish the course, fence use to real need — the
  resistance arithmetic runs EVERY time the drug runs. Breaks by
  the worked case in SCIENCE: blanket use anywhere breeds
  resistance everywhere (the mirror files the blanket method).
- QUARANTINE AND ISOLATION (starve the spread number): separate
  cases until each infects fewer than one. The only method that
  works with NO knowledge of the microbe — it needs only R < 1 —
  which is why it is the oldest one and still the first one.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE MICROSCOPE — the instrument that made the unit visible:
  ~400-1,000× shows bacteria; light's own wavelength sets a floor
  near 0.2 µm (below it, the electron microscope — the mirror
  holds how that wall fell). Leeuwenhoek's single bead lens
  started the whole subject in the 1670s.
- THE DISH AND THE JELLY — agar from seaweed: solid, clear,
  water-holding, indigestible to nearly all — the perfect stage
  BECAUSE nothing eats the stage.
- THE AUTOCLAVE — cooking's pressure cooker, aimed at spores:
  121°C wet, the kill arithmetic's practical instrument.
- THE INCUBATOR — holds the window (human-body warm for our
  microbes) so the doubling law runs on schedule.
- DEEP COLD — −80°C freezers and liquid nitrogen (−196°C): life
  paused, not killed; with anti-ice-spear additives (glycerol),
  cells return decades later. Quality's enemy is the ice crystal
  — the same spear cooking's freezer fights.
- THE HEAT-PROOF ENZYME AND THE CYCLER — Taq, borrowed from a
  hot spring, plus a machine that rides the temperature ladder up
  and down: together, the doubling law as a desktop tool.
- THE SEQUENCER — the recipe reader; its cost collapse (billions
  to hundreds) moved reading from a moonshot to a routine.
- THE MODEL ORGANISMS — fly, worm, mouse, yeast, gut bacterium:
  small, fast, shared, so any lab's result can be re-run in any
  other. The worm C. elegans has exactly 959 body cells, every
  one mapped — a whole animal held as a known quantity.

## PURPOSE

- FEED — every crop and herd is steered life: breeding, soil
  microbes, nitrogen, the yield ceiling (the 1% law). Farming is
  this phylum with its boots on.
- HEAL — know the cell to fix the body; kill the invader and
  spare the host; the resistance arithmetic and the outbreak law
  are medicine's working tools from this phylum.
- KEEP FOOD — cooking's preservation IS applied microbiology:
  deny free water, deny warmth, deny the pH window, or kill and
  seal. The microbe's side of that story lives here.
- MAKE — bread, beer, cheese, kraut; then antibiotics; then
  insulin and beyond: hired life as a factory, from lore to
  recipe-editing.
- UNDERSTAND DESCENT — why children resemble parents and differ
  from them; where kinds come from; what we are made of and where
  we came from. The oldest questions with working answers here.
- KEEP THE PLANET'S LEDGER — the oxygen in every breath is life's
  own exhaust, accumulated over ages; soil, rain-seeding, the
  carbon loop. What lives sets what the air and fields ARE —
  weather and farming cash this connection daily.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Life used long before understood: bread rose, beer brewed,
  cattle and wheat were bred toward the useful for thousands of
  years — method outran science by millennia, and fed everyone
  while it did (the same proof cooking files).
- The microscope opens the invisible (1670s): Leeuwenhoek's
  "little animals" in a drop of pond water — the census of the
  world suddenly wrong by a factor of everything.
- Spontaneous generation dies in stages: Redi screens the meat
  jars and no maggots come (1668); Pasteur's swan-neck flask lets
  air in but dust out and the boiled broth stays clear — snap the
  neck, it teems in days (1859-61). Every cell from a cell — the
  claim that made sterile technique, canning, and surgery's
  cleanliness possible. The belief's full record is the mirror's.
- Vitalism dies at a bench (1828): Wöhler makes urea — a
  kidney's own product — from mineral stuff, no kidney, no life.
  Life's chemistry is chemistry; the whole catalogue followed.
- Selection is named (1859): Darwin and Wallace put variation,
  selection, and inheritance together; the arithmetic has since
  been watched running live (resistance, moths, beak sizes).
- Mendel counts peas (1866) and is ignored for ~34 years — the
  right answer, filed and unread until 1900 (the mirror weighs
  that cost).
- Germ theory rewrites two subjects at once (1860s-80s): disease
  becomes microbes, not vapors; cooking's safety becomes
  arithmetic; surgery washes its hands (the mirror holds the man
  who said so first and was destroyed for it).
- Antibiotics arrive (1928, mass-made 1943) — and resistance is
  observed within years, exactly as the arithmetic says it must
  be. Fleming's own 1945 warning predicted it publicly.
- The recipe itself: structure 1953, code cracked through the
  1960s, a whole human read by 2003, editing tools from 2012 —
  the subject's texts became readable and then writable inside
  one lifetime.
