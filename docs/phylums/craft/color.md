## THINGS
The units this subject is about; other homes named so a cascade can branch.

- THE MATERIAL — the half of every job that talks back: wood splits along its
  grain, steel holds the temper it was given, cloth stretches on the slant,
  clay forgives, glass does not. Its will is set by its insides before the
  tool arrives; also: chemistry (arrangement decides behavior), physics, earth.
- WOOD — a bundle of straws that never stops breathing: swells and shrinks
  across the grain with the room's dampness forever, along the grain almost
  not at all; also: plants, building.
- CLAY — not a substance but a shape: rock weathered to flakes so thin that
  water films between them; they slide while holding together. Fire ends the
  forgiveness permanently; also: earth, building.
- GLASS — a liquid's disorder frozen solid: no melting point, only a long
  slope of softening — the whole reason it can be worked; also: physics.
- METAL AND STEEL — atoms stacked like oranges in a crate, in millions of
  small crystals; most of what happens to a metal happens at the joins between
  them. Steel is iron with a little carbon: carbon sets the ceiling, heat
  treatment picks the spot under it — a file and a spring from one bar; also:
  chemistry, engineering, economy.
- FIBER, THREAD, CLOTH — thin things persuaded to act as one thick thing;
  everything a cloth can be is settled by the fiber's length and fineness
  before a single thread exists; also: plants, animals, farming.
- LEATHER AND STONE — materials keeping their maker's history: the hide dense
  at the back, loose at the belly; the block with the bed it lay in. Both
  record every hole and stroke; also: animals, earth.
- THE TOOL AND THE EDGE — the hand extended: harder, sharper, longer. An edge
  is the line where two flat surfaces meet; a sharp tool is a safe tool,
  cutting where aimed under light force, while a dull one demands muscle and
  skids; also: physics, cooking, medicine.
- THE JIG — remembered accuracy: a guide built once, carefully, so every piece
  after inherits its precision without its effort; also: computing (a program
  is a jig for thought), engineering.
- THE JOINT — where two pieces become one thing and where the thing will
  break; glue, weld, screw, stitch, mortar are each a law about what the
  meeting faces must be; also: building.
- THE WINDOW — the passing state in which the work of the hour is possible:
  clay at leather-hard, glass in its seconds of softness, plaster before it
  goes off. The workshop's schedule is set by the material, not the worker;
  also: cooking, medicine.
- THE HAND AND THE APPRENTICE — the instrument that reads what no bench-price
  gauge reads, and the trade's storage medium: what cannot be written
  transmits by standing next to somebody, priced in years; also: body, mind,
  teaching, people.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query.

- The material has a will and the maker's first job is learning it — split
  along the grain, cut across it, cut the cloth with its arrow. Working
  against the will costs force, and force wounds.
- Strength lives in arrangement, not substance: steel hard or soft from one
  bar, cloth stiff (woven) or springy (knit) from one fiber, a forged hook
  holding where a machined one snaps.
- Measure twice, cut once — and the second measurement must arrive by another
  route (the far end, or the actual part against the actual hole): a repeat by
  the same road repeats the same mistake.
- Fit before force: right parts join with a push and a click. If it needs
  hammering, something is wrong and the hammer makes it wronger — matter
  cannot share space; force wounds a shape, never corrects it.
- Hard and tough pull against each other — hardness is stopping the slip
  inside a material, toughness allowing it; no point on the line is both. And
  hard is not durable: flint shatters, soft limestone takes a hammer all day.
- Stronger is not stiffer: every steel is exactly as stiff as every other; a
  sagging beam is cured by depth or a different material, never by grade.
- One-way doors govern the trade: sawdust never rejoins the board, fired clay
  never softens, a needle hole in leather is forever. Spend lavishly on the
  reversible step, once on the other kind.
- Preparation decides the ceiling, invisibly — wedged clay, cleaned weld
  faces, dry-fitted glue-ups; the effects surface only at the stage where
  nothing can be done.
- Finish is protection wearing beauty: oil, paint, glaze keep water and air
  out; the shine is the visible half of a seal. And the failure plane is
  always the weakest layer in the stack, never the newest — paint holds onto
  whatever it was put on, not onto the wall.
- The last tenth takes half the time: precision is order, paid at rising
  rates. Done-looking is the halfway mark, and the visible step is the short
  one — prep takes the days, paint takes the hour.
- A repair relocates the fault as much as it removes it. So chosen weak points
  are a design act: mortar softer than brick, so the wall cracks where a
  trowel can put it back.
- Mastery is economy — fewer motions, not faster ones: practiced skill sinks
  below deciding and the freed eye watches ahead. And the trade lives in the
  hands, not fully writable: how far to push before the metal goes, what a
  joint sounds like when it seats — a reflex built from thousands of
  corrections has no sentence in it to hand over, which is why every trade
  teaches by standing next to somebody.

## SCIENCE
The math, physics, and chemistry of making, held — not mentioned: LAW to
compute with, RULE to follow, WORKED where it earns it.

### PHYSICS — the edge, the wedge, the difference

LAW: pressure = force ÷ area.
RULE: to cut, shrink the area; to protect, spread it.
WORKED: a 50 N push on a fresh chisel edge touching 25 mm × 0.02 mm = 0.5 mm²:
  50 ÷ 0.0000005 m² = 100,000,000 Pa — 100 MPa, ten times pine's crush
  strength; the edge parts fibers. The same push on a dulled 5 mm² nose:
  10 MPa — at the crush line, so it dents, wedges, and skids. The dull tool is
  dangerous BY THIS ARITHMETIC.

LAW: a wedge multiplies force by length ÷ thickness, and locks itself when its
  half-angle is below the friction angle (tan of friction angle = friction
  coefficient).
WORKED: coefficient 0.18 → arctan 0.18 ≈ 10°. A hammer eye tapered 4° per
  side: 4 < 10, the wedged handle locks for a lifetime; a 15° wedge spits back
  out under vibration. The line is arithmetic, not opinion.

LAW: stress comes from DIFFERENCE, not state — a body changing size together
  does no harm; one part held by a part that already moved is how pots crack
  drying, welds pull frames crooked, mugs split under boiling water.
LAW: thermal stress = stiffness × swell-rate × temperature difference. Glass:
  70,000 MPa × 0.000009 per °C = 0.63 MPa per °C across the piece.
RULE: three cures, one law — thin, low-swell material, even heating.
WORKED: cheap glass, 100°C across: 0.63 × 100 = 63 MPa; a scratched surface
  breaks near ~50 — it splits. Borosilicate (0.0000033/°C): 70,000 ×
  0.0000033 × 100 = 23.1 MPa — it lives. Jam jar versus ovenware, computed.

LAW: metals grow with heat — steel ~0.000012 of size per °C.
WORKED (shrink fit): a 25 mm steel ring heated 100°C grows 25 × 0.000012 ×
  100 = 0.030 mm — enough to pass a shaft 0.02 mm oversize; cold, it grips
  with the full mismatch, forever.

LAW: bending stiffness grows with the CUBE of thickness.
WORKED: a shelf 18 mm versus 12 mm: (18 ÷ 12)³ = 1.5³ = 3.375 — half again
  the wood, three and three-eighths times the stiffness. The same cube is why drape
  belongs to fine yarns and stiffening means depth, not grade.

LAW: a bent bundle loads its outside fibers hardest. WORKED: a 12 mm rope
  knotted round a 12 mm-radius turn stretches its outer fibers 6 ÷ 18 = 33%
  more than the middle; they break first, and the knot costs a third to half
  the rope's strength. A splice keeps the fibers running and keeps nearly all.

### CHEMISTRY AND HEAT — steel, the arrangement trade

LAW: steel hardens only if there is carbon to trap — below ~0.25% nothing
  hardens however hard the quench; ~0.6-1.0% is tool territory; past ~2% it is
  cast iron, hard and unforgeable.
LAW: iron restacks its atoms above ~727°C; a magnet quits at ~770°C — so a
  magnet on a wire is a free furnace instrument: heat until it lets go
  (cherry red, ~780-800°C) and the steel is ready to harden.
LAW: the quench freezes the restack too fast for the carbon to leave — hard,
  strained, brittle, nearly useless as it stands; tempering reheats gently to
  let a CHOSEN amount of strain out.
RULE (the temper ladder, read off the oxide colors the steel paints on
  itself): ~220°C pale straw, ~62 workshop points — razors and scrapers;
  ~240°C dark straw — drills; ~270°C brown-purple, ~57 — chisels and axes,
  five points of hardness down from the razor, sold to survive being struck;
  ~300°C blue, ~50-55 — springs and saws, which must bend all day and never
  shatter. Each ~40°C sells roughly 3-5 points of hardness and buys toughness
  with the proceeds.
LAW: the quench reaches only so far in — heat leaves by the surface, so a
  thin part hardens through, a thick one hardens at the skin and stays soft
  inside. The same steel in two sizes is two different parts.
LAW: annealing is the reset — heat and cool SLOWLY: jammed faults clear,
  fresh crystals grow, the metal works again, and the strength working built
  is erased with the jam. Forward it is work-hardening: every cold bend
  spends the metal's own means of giving way — the coathanger stiffens where
  worked, then snaps there.
LAW: smaller crystals are stronger AND tougher — the one improvement buying
  both; bought by fast cooling, working, or an alloy blocking growth; sold by
  overheating (the coarse ruin beside a careless weld).

### CHEMISTRY — fire and clay, fire and glass

LAW: clay's two waters. Between the flakes: leaves on drying, can be given
  back — unfired clay reclaims forever; the forgiveness. Inside the mineral:
  leaves at ~450-600°C, and from that moment it is not clay and never softens
  in water again.
RULE (the firing ladder): candle below 100°C until bone dry — water becoming
  steam expands ~1,600×, and a damp wall or sealed hollow is a bomb; bisque
  ~950-1,000°C leaves the pot porous so glaze can be drunk on; earthenware
  ~1,000-1,150°C stays porous; stoneware ~1,200-1,300°C closes tight;
  porcelain ~1,300-1,400°C goes nearly to glass. One road, three distances.
LAW: at 573°C the quartz in the body restacks and jumps ~1% in volume all at
  once (under half that in each direction), up AND down — walk the kiln slowly through that band both ways; the
  cooling half cracks the pots, and a perfect firing is ruined by opening the
  door early.
LAW: shrink happens twice — drying and firing — commonly a tenth together.
WORKED: a lid that must finish at 10.0 cm, at 10% total shrink, is thrown at
  10 ÷ 0.90 = 11.1 cm — a size that does not yet exist, computed. Volume pays
  the cube: 0.9³ = 0.729 — a tenth off the length is 27% off the volume.
LAW: glaze fit is one number — the glaze's swell-rate against the body's.
  Glaze shrinks more: stretched, cracks into a net (crazing); less: squeezed,
  peels off edges. A LITTLE squeeze is the aim — glass is strong in squeeze,
  weak in stretch, so a fitted glaze makes the pot stronger than bare.
LAW: glass works ~700-1,000°C on a slope of stiffness, in a window of seconds
  to a minute per visit to the fire; annealed by holding ~510-550°C and
  cooling slowly, or the outside sets while the inside still shrinks and the
  locked disagreement releases days later at a touch. Clay forgives for weeks
  before the fire; glass forgives for seconds, always — the temperament of
  the two crafts, in numbers.

### MATHEMATICS — twist, tolerance, layout

LAW: twist turns friction into unity. Loose fibers side by side carry almost
  nothing — they slide. Twist presses them together so a pull on one is a
  pull on all; the price is that each fiber lies at an angle to the load and
  carries only the cosine of that angle, squared. Strength climbs with twist
  to a peak, then falls as the angle-tax outruns the friction-gain.
WORKED: ~100 cotton fibers, each carrying ~5 g alone. Untwisted: near zero.
  At 20°: cos²20° = 0.94² = 0.88 → 100 × 5 × 0.88 ≈ 440 g held by thread
  thinner than a pin. At 40°: 0.77² = 0.59 — falling while the yarn hardens
  toward cord; the spinner's whole judgment is that peak. A single yarn
  stores its twist as restlessness and kinks; two singles plied the OPPOSITE
  way cancel and lie dead straight — ply is cancellation, the same balance as
  laid rope. Cloth inherits the sum: strength per width ≈ threads per cm ×
  thread strength, taxed ~10-20% by the crimp of weaving.

LAW: fit is a difference of hundredths — clearance slides, interference
  grips, and the decision lives in a band thinner than a hair (~0.07 mm).
WORKED: hole bored 25.00 mm. Shaft 24.98: clearance 0.02 — turns freely.
  Shaft 25.02: interference 0.02 — pressed home, never moves again. Spinning
  to permanent: 0.04 mm, half a hair. Tolerance is the agreement over that
  band, decided before either part exists — why parts made strangers meet.

LAW: the cut has width — a saw eats its slot (kerf ~3 mm), a seam its
  allowance; the join comes from the pieces.
RULE: leave the line on the piece you keep; pick the offcut first.
WORKED: a 2,400 mm board into four equal pieces, three cuts: (2,400 − 3 × 3)
  ÷ 4 = 2,391 ÷ 4 = 597.75 mm — not 600, and the 9 mm the saw ate was
  assigned to somebody before the first stroke.

LAW: errors measured from a moving start add one way; from one origin they do
  not accumulate.
WORKED: ten marks, each ±1 mm. Chained from the last piece, a consistent
  1 mm bias arrives at the tenth as 10 mm — a full centimeter out; from the
  origin, the tenth is ±1 mm like the first. Tenfold difference, by geometry.

LAW: halving beats hunting — each splitting test discards half of what
  remains, so work grows with the doublings, not the size.
WORKED: a dead circuit, 1,000 candidate points: 2¹⁰ = 1,024, ten tests corner
  it; guessing averages 500. Ten versus five hundred is why the old hand
  tests in the middle.

LAW: a torqued bolt is a stretched spring, but stretch is hidden, so twist is
  measured — and friction eats ~90% of it.
WORKED: an M10 bolt at 40 N·m, dry threads (factor ~0.2): clamp = 40 ÷ (0.2 ×
  0.010 m) = 20,000 N — two tonnes from a wrist. Oil the threads (~0.15):
  40 ÷ 0.0015 ≈ 26,700 N — a third more clamp from the same click; the
  proxy's exchange rate moved, the setting did not. Joints settle too —
  crushed high spots spend the stretch — so nuts are retaken, criss-cross,
  in passes.

### THE BODY ARITHMETIC

LAW: cutting stone, fired clay, or concrete dry puts silica in the air, and
  particles below ~4 thousandths of a millimeter are never coughed out — they
  lodge and scar over years, no symptom. Harm is set by particle size, not by
  how dirty the air looks. RULE: cut wet, sweep wet, or wear the fine mask.
LAW: strength and control collapse away from the body's center — a painter on
  proper boards outworks one stretching off a ladder three to one; most bad
  workmanship is good workmanship attempted at arm's length.

## METHODS
How things are done here, with what governs and breaks each. The
prepositioned how-to-build.

- READ THE MATERIAL FIRST: sight the grain before planing (with it, glass;
  against it, tear-out); tap the block and listen (sound stone rings, a
  cracked one answers dead); bend a scrap of the batch. The inside cannot be
  read from the surface — use a channel that reaches inside.
- LAY OUT FROM ONE ORIGIN: one line, one height, every mark from the datum. A
  knife line beats a pencil line — not information but a feature, a tiny wall
  the chisel cannot wander from. In crooked old places, scribe: copy reality
  instead of describing it.
- HOLD THE WORK: bench, vise, clamp — the third worker. Every cutting force
  has an equal one shoving the work away; most bad cutting is a workpiece
  that moved.
- ROUGH TO FINE, EVERYWHERE TOGETHER: bring the whole surface down as one — a
  face finished early fixes the level of the entire block. The abrasive queue
  is law: each grade removes the last grade's scratches; a skipped grade
  hides coarse marks under shine until the finish lights them up. Sharpening
  is the same queue aimed at two faces: flatten both and the edge appears
  free of charge.
- REHEARSE THE IRREVERSIBLE: dry-fit every glue-up, clamps laid out, before
  mixing what sets on a schedule that will not wait — extended to every step
  that starts a clock: the pour, the quench, the kiln door, the scored glass
  (the score's crack is alive minutes only; bend it fresh, never score
  twice).
- CUT TO THE WASTE SIDE: pick the offcut first, leave the line, budget kerf
  and allowance. In leather and coated cloth the material records every
  attempt: clips, not pins; one cut.
- JOIN BY TURNING A WEAK MEETING INTO A STRONG ONE: glue holds long grain to
  long grain and almost nothing else — every traditional joint turns an
  end-grain meeting into long-grain surface plus a lock. A screw needs two
  holes (narrow pilot to bite, wide clearance to pull together); loads go
  ACROSS fasteners, never off their tips. A weld is one pool freezing as a
  single grain, so everything not metal must leave first — paint, rust, oil,
  damp boil into the bead as holes. Solder wants a tight gap (narrowness
  pulls it in); glue wants a thin film (bone-dry clamping starves it).
- TEMPER TO THE JOB: anneal to work, harden to hold an edge, temper to
  survive use — the ladder in SCIENCE, read off the colors. Breaks when
  quenched and left untempered (cracks in the night) or overheated till the
  crystals coarsen.
- WORK INSIDE THE WINDOW: throw with the fewest pulls (water softens the wall
  as you work — the pot has minutes); join clay at MATCHED wetness or the
  drier part tears the joint weeks later; blow glass in planned visits to the
  fire. The design of the object is a plan for what fits in each visit.
- DRY AND FIRE ON THE MATERIAL'S SCHEDULE: even wall thickness (two
  thicknesses is two bodies tied together), slow drying under plastic, vent
  every hollow, bone dry before flame, walk 573°C both ways, cool slower than
  pride wants. Wedge first — air pockets are steam bombs and unaligned clay
  pulls off-center.
- FINISH AS A SEAL: prep is the work — the failure plane lies underneath, so
  the coat's life was settled before it arrived. The seal is re-fought, never
  won: the great bridges are painted end to end, then painted again.
- FIX BY MECHANISM, NOT SYMPTOM: a seam fails three ways — thread breaks,
  cloth tears at the holes, or yarns slide with every stitch intact — needing
  three cures; the commonest trade mistake answers the third with stronger
  thread when nothing broke. Find faults by halving; walk the machine with
  nose, ear, and the back of a hand — a fault is energy going where it should
  not, leaving as heat, noise, or smell.
- MAKE THE JIG WHEN THE COUNT JUSTIFIES IT. WORKED: a jig costing 2 hours
  that saves 10 minutes a piece pays at 120 ÷ 10 = 12 pieces; at three it is
  vanity, at thirty it is the method.
- APPRENTICE THE UNWRITABLE: settings learned by thousands of corrections
  transmit only by watching, doing, being corrected in the moment. Seven
  years at the bench is 7 × 48 weeks × 40 hours = 13,440 hours — the price
  list for hand knowledge; no discount has ever held.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE EDGE TOOLS: knife, chisel, plane, saw — force onto a vanishing area,
  kept sharp because the arithmetic says the dull one crushes and skids.
  Behind them the abrasive ladder: grits in a queue, no grade skipped.
- THE MEASURING SET: rule and tape checked against each other; the square —
  the one reference nature does not give, where plumb and level are gravity's
  free gifts through a string and a bubble; marking knife and gauge; the
  story stick (a measurement kept as a thing, immune to misreading).
- THE FREE INSTRUMENTS: the magnet that quits at ~770°C, the temper colors
  the steel paints on itself, the potter's cones — measuring heat-work
  (temperature × time, the thing actually governing) while the thermometer
  only gossips about the moment.
- THE HOLDERS AND JIGS: bench, vise, clamps (never enough); template,
  stencil, last, pattern cut oversize by the shrink rule. Movable type was a
  jig for words; the assembly line is jigs all the way down.
- THE FIRES AND THEIR ECONOMY: forge (~780-800°C cherry), kiln (mapped by its
  owner — every kiln has a hot shelf and a cold one). Nearly all a firing's
  cost heats the box, not the pots. WORKED: a ~50 kWh firing at $0.15 ≈ $7.50
  for one pot or eighty — $7.50 the pot, or 7.50 ÷ 80 ≈ 9 cents; the
  workshop's rhythm (work waiting on the shelf for company) is this division,
  obeyed.
- THE MATERIALS BY THEIR WILLS: wood (moves forever — give the movement
  somewhere to go), clay (forgives until ~500°C, then never), glass (seconds
  of grace, annealed or armed), steel (holds its temper), fiber (strength by
  twist and angle), leather (records every hole), stone (splits where it lay
  — set it in the wall as it lay in the ground, or frost peels it in sheets).
- THE APPRENTICESHIP: the means of transmission for everything the other
  means cannot carry — 13,440 hours, standing next to somebody.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- MAKE THE NEEDED THING: chair, pot, coat, blade — matter arranged to a want;
  the second-oldest answer to need, after finding.
- MAKE IT FIT — this body, this alcove, this hand: the scribed shelf, the
  tailored dart (flat cloth covers a round body only by shear, wedge, or
  shrink — three moves, nothing else exists), the last carved to the actual
  foot. Fitting is copying reality.
- MAKE IT LAST: temper, finish, bed, lap, drain — every durability is a
  specific enemy named and answered (water, air, salt, sun, rub, repetition).
- MAKE MANY ALIKE: jig, gauge, tolerance — the door through which craft
  became industry; parts that meet though made strangers.
- REPAIR AND KEEP: put strength back where use spent it, knowing the weak
  point moves next door and choosing where it moves to.
- MAKE IT WORTH HANDING ON: beauty as the visible half of care — the finish
  that shows the prep, the fewest motions that show the years.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost.

- Method outran science by millennia, everywhere: smiths chose quench and
  temper a thousand years before anyone knew carbon was in the steel; dyers
  brokered color with metal salts before chemistry had a name; potters walked
  kilns through 573°C without knowing quartz existed. The trades are the
  standing proof that HOW can be held and improved for centuries while WHY
  waits.
- Fired clay was the first material humans MADE that nature had not — and
  became the record of everyone: chemically finished so nothing rots it,
  worthless so nobody melted it down, breaking into pieces of itself. Most of
  what is known of ordinary life survives as broken pots.
- The apprentice chain was the storage medium and the years its cost —
  knowledge below words in living hands, renewed by standing next to
  somebody. Where the chain broke, arts died with the hands (the mirror keeps
  that ledger).
- Measurement breached the wall between one-off and many: before gauge and
  tolerance, every nut fit its one bolt, filed to it, married to it.
  Interchangeable parts — fit decided before either part exists — turned
  fitting from a marriage into an agreement and made the factory possible.
  The jig was the seed all along.
- Each generation the machine took another set of motions — wheel, loom,
  lathe, press — and each time the setup, the reading of the material, and
  the recovery when it disagreed remained (the mirror states the remainder
  exactly). Surpassed methods STAYED for repair, fitting, and the one-off —
  to some query the old method is still the best answer.
- The instruments made settings fileable late: thermometer, gauge, torque
  wrench each turned a thumb's opinion into a number that travels. What was
  filed stopped dying with its holders — and what was never filed (the
  mirror's largest entry) still does.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward.

- PHYSICS: force ÷ area at the edge, the wedge and its friction angle, stress
  from difference, cube-law stiffness, push-back and the third hand, stored
  twist and stored squeeze (tempered glass, plied yarn — one law, two
  crafts).
- CHEMISTRY: arrangement decides behavior (the phylum's founding law);
  one-way reactions (fired clay, set epoxy, quenched steel); oxide skins that
  defend and the wet battery of two touching metals that devours — the little
  one always loses, faster beside a big partner; the mordant as broker
  between dye and fiber.
- MATHEMATICS: cosine-squared twist, tolerance bands, origin versus chained
  error, halving search, the cubes of shrink and stiffness, the geometry a
  crack permits (outside curves only — stained glass is built inside that
  boundary).
- ENGINEERING: tolerance and fit as contract, failure sited on purpose
  (mortar, fuses, crumple), the proxy problem (torque for stretch),
  composites strong only along their fibers.
- BUILDING: the trades in their buried order — pipes before boards, boards
  before plaster; a step out of turn is paid in demolition — load paths, laps
  against wind, the dew point hiding in the wall.
- COOKING: the kitchen is a craft shop — knife law, dough windows, tempering
  chocolate as crystal choice, prep as the meal.
- MEASUREMENT: the instruments that made settings fileable; the second-route
  rule; units defined by procedure (three yarn counts that cannot compare);
  cones measuring heat-work.
- DESIGN: the failure mode chosen at the drawing; ease and allowance put in
  on purpose; the tool's logic left in the product (blown versus cast, forged
  versus machined).
- TEACHING AND MIND: the apprenticeship — watching is not enough, the hours
  are the tuition; skill sinking below deciding; the reflex with no sentence
  in it.
- BODY AND MEDICINE: the hand as instrument, position as half the job, the
  silica arithmetic in the lung; the surgeon as the body's tradesman —
  checklist before the knife, margin cut wide, the count and the dry-fit.
- ECONOMY: batch cost (the kiln's division), cloth layout as price, the
  option kept open (piece-dyed late) paid in depth of color, the gamed metric
  (thread count).
- EARTH, FARMING, PLANTS, ANIMALS: clay's two births (residual pure and
  coarse, river-borne plastic and stained — whiteness and workability
  opposites in nature), stone's bedding planes, the ore behind every metal;
  every fiber, hide, and board grew somewhere.
- COMPUTING: the jig is the ancestor of the program; halving is the
  debugger's search; absolute versus relative addressing is the wall's origin
  line; a rule the machine enforces beats a rule in the manual.
- UPKEEP AND PEOPLE: the seal re-fought, the retightened bolt — the standing
  treaty with disorder, never a victory; and institutions have grain too —
  reformers who cut across it split them, and organizations come apart along
  their real seams, like the carcass and the stone.
- ELECTRONICS: soldering and board-making are craft skills; the maker bench spans both.
- LANGUAGE: trade words name what only hands know; the apprentice learns a vocabulary.
- MOVEMENT: the craftsman's swing and steady hand are trained movement.
- MUSIC: instrument making — the luthier is craft serving sound.
- PLAY: toys and games are made things; whittling is play with an edge.
