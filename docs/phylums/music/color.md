## THINGS
The units this subject is about; other homes named so a cascade can
branch. The raw material of music is not sound — it is expectation.

- SOUND — air rippling; the ear counts the ripples. Also: physics
  (waves add), language, engineering (every machine hums).
- PITCH — vibration speed heard as height: a string shaking 440
  times a second IS the note A. Also: electronics (tuning a radio
  dial is choosing a pitch of light), body (the inner ear is a
  coiled keyboard, one station per speed).
- THE OCTAVE — the same note wearing a higher hat: double any
  vibration and every ear files it as kin; the whole map folds at
  that seam. Also: mathematics (clock arithmetic).
- THE INTERVAL — the ratio between two pitches; it carries a sound
  of its own (a colour) AND a job inside a key (a route) — two
  separate facts. Also: mathematics (ratio survives resizing).
- THE OVERTONE STACK — the quiet multiples inside every real note:
  the string vibrates whole, in halves, in thirds, all at once.
  Also: astronomy (a hot gas emits fixed lines for the same
  whole-number reason), engineering (a shaft's dangerous speeds
  are its multiples).
- TIMBRE — the recipe of those overtones; the mix is the voice, so
  every real note is a chord in disguise. Also: physics (white
  light is a chord of colours; a prism is an ear for it), cooking
  (a stock: one taste from many quiet ones).
- THE SCALE — a chosen handful of pitches per octave, spaced
  unevenly on purpose; the choice is the flavor. Also: language
  (each tongue picks ~40 sounds from the mouth's continuum — same
  fence, same foreign accent when crossed), design (a palette is
  a scale for the eye).
- THE KEY — a home note plus the habit of returning to it; a few
  bars in, the listener holds that note whether they know it or
  not. Also: mind (a reference built from early evidence).
- THE CHORD — intervals piled until several notes fuse into one
  object with a colour. Also: chemistry (an isomer: same parts,
  different property), building (courses that read as a wall).
- THE MELODY — pitch moving through time; the feeling rides the
  steps between notes, not their heights, and what memory keeps
  is the silhouette. Also: mind (a rough shape is cheap to store).
- PULSE, METRE, RHYTHM — the promise, the grid of strong and weak
  seats built on it, and the game played against it. Also: body
  (heart and walk are the first clocks), play (a feint works only
  on a defender taught the rhythm), story (comic timing).
- TEMPO AND DYNAMICS — speed read against the body's resting
  rates, and the loudness curve drawn over time; volume is the
  wave's height, meaning is its shape. Also: language (italics on
  every word are italics on none).
- SILENCE — a position with nothing played in it; a rest is
  played, not skipped: expectation runs on through the gap, so
  the gap carries load. Also: design (negative space), people
  (the pause before the answer says more than the answer).
- THE PHRASE — a breath of music, commonly four bars: music was
  sung long before it was written. Also: language (a sentence
  sized to one pass).
- THE SCORE — instructions, not a recording; it keeps what its
  symbols hold and silently drops the rest. Also: law (a statute
  needing case law), cooking (a recipe missing the judgment).
- THE INSTRUMENT AND THE VOICE — bodies built (or born) to hold
  an overtone stack; a sung vowel is the stack with members
  boosted by the mouth's shape. Also: craft, body (muscles).
- THE REFERENCE PITCH — the agreed number (A = 440); in tune is a
  relation between players, not a property of an instrument.
  Also: people (a standard's value is shared adoption), economy
  (a currency worth what everyone accepts).
- THE GENRE — a bundle of expectations the listener brings
  through the door; it communicates for free. Also: culture,
  story (a genre promises the shape of its ending).
- THE ROOM — the performance's silent partner: echoes blend or
  blur, and size decides which wavelengths it favors. Also:
  building, physics.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
applicability to the query at hand, never absolute.

- Pitch is frequency; the octave is doubling, and nearly every
  culture files the doubled note as the same name (the stretched
  edges are the mirror's).
- Harmony is ratio: pitches agree when their speeds relate by
  small whole numbers — the summed wave repeats quickly — and
  grind when they don't. WHY the tidy sum pleases is only
  part-settled; the open half is the mirror's.
- Consonance and dissonance are stability and wanting-to-move,
  not beauty and ugliness. The ratios rank the intervals into a
  LADDER, not a fence, and every style draws its own line across
  it — what is scandalous is a date, not a fact.
- The intervals were never chosen; they were found already
  sounding inside every note, in the stack's fixed order: octave,
  fifth, fourth, major third — the ranking used ever since.
- A pitch carries two independent facts — its name among twelve,
  and its height — and only the name travels: a man and a child
  singing one tune are singing one tune.
- A scale's steps differ in size ON PURPOSE: uneven spacing gives
  every note a unique address. Equal steps throw the addresses
  away (the mirror holds that corpse).
- A key is built in the listener: prediction appoints a home from
  early evidence and measures everything from it — the same chord
  is arrival or departure depending only on where home is.
- A chord is stacked thirds — the widest step that still fuses
  into one object (stacked seconds fight). Major and minor differ
  by one semitone in the middle note: a large perceived
  difference on a tiny physical one. Add a seventh and the chord
  stops being a place and becomes a direction.
- Turn a chord over and identity never changes but weight always
  does — the lowest voice is the ground the rest is measured
  against.
- Endings come in strengths: full stop (pull chord falls home,
  home note on top), comma (stop ON the pull chord), page-turn
  (anywhere but home). Punctuation, in sound. The pull chord —
  a fifth above home — is the grammar's strongest: two notes
  wanting to move opposite ways, and exactly one chord answers
  both.
- Modulation is new gravity, not new material: move which note
  the ear calls home and every chord is re-priced with none of
  them changing — unnoticed while it happens. Same notes with a
  different home (the modes) is a different world: the distances
  moved, and the distances were where the character lived.
- How often the chords change is a rhythm of its own: two chords
  a bar is urgent, one held eight bars is standing still,
  whatever the notes on top are doing.
- A leap creates an obligation — the ear expects steps back into
  the opened gap. Melodies are mostly steps; leaps are the
  events.
- Play a perfectly even row of clicks and nobody hears one: the
  ear packs them into twos or threes and crowns one of each.
  Metre is added by the listener — and its positions then carry
  weight whatever is played on them: a silence on a strong beat
  is the loudest silence there is.
- A syncopation works only while the beat still runs silently in
  the listener's head; unheld, the ear re-hears the offbeats as
  the beat and the thrill is gone. No surprise without a promise.
- Tempo does most of the emotional work credited to the notes:
  halve a cheerful tune's speed and it is a lament, every note
  unchanged.
- Loudness is not meaning; what carries is loudness's DIRECTION —
  a phrase that grows means what a loud phrase does not, and a
  held level stops being heard: senses report change.
- A sound's identity lives in how it starts and stops: cut the
  first fraction of a second and listeners confuse instruments
  they have known all their lives.
- The ear follows the top and bottom lines and treats the middle
  as filling; two parts in one register blur and the louder wins
  (masking) — mud is a physics outcome.
- Form is a plan for what the listener will still be holding:
  working memory is small, recognition cheap, so structure is
  built from returns, not accumulation.
- Repetition is the converter: on the second hearing the listener
  is predicting, and predicting turns sound into music — repeat a
  spoken sentence enough and it begins to sound sung.
- A groove is small SYSTEMATIC deviation from the grid: measured,
  consistent, information. The same notes exactly on the grid are
  correct and dead.
- A lyric fits when the word's loud syllable lands on the strong
  beat; set against, the singer mangles the word or fights the
  music. Rhyme is matched endings — the same sound from the last
  stressed vowel onward, different before it.
- A melody survives its words: hum the song and it is still the
  song — the tune carried meaning of its own all along.
- Theory is not law composers obeyed; it is what got noticed
  afterwards about music already liked. Practice leads, theory
  follows, then it is taught first — and every rule has famous
  exceptions.
- One mechanism under everything: the listener guesses what comes
  next, and the music arranges to be right, wrong, or late on
  purpose. The guessing goes on after the answer is known — why a
  piece known by heart still moves.

## SCIENCE
The math and physics of music, held — not mentioned: LAWS a query
can compute with, RULES it can follow, WORKED cases proving they
run.

### PHYSICS — the wave itself

LAW: sound travels ~343 m/s in room air; wavelength = speed ÷
  frequency.
WORKED: A 440: 343 ÷ 440 ≈ 0.78 m — arm's length. The piano's
  bottom A (27.5/s): 343 ÷ 27.5 ≈ 12.5 m — longer than the room:
  why bass flows around walls and fills a house while treble dies
  at the door.

LAW: the octave is exact doubling — up ×2, down ÷2.
WORKED: 440 doubled is 880, same letter one fold higher; halved
  stepwise 440 → 220 → 110 → 55 → 27.5 — the piano's bottom A is
  440 halved four times. ✓

LAW: a string fixed at both ends holds only whole numbers of
  half-waves, so its vibrations are the whole multiples of its
  slowest — all sounding at once.
WORKED: a string at 110 carries 110, 220, 330, 440, 550, 660.
  Neighbor ratios: 220:110 = 2:1 (octave), 330:220 = 3:2 (fifth),
  440:330 = 4:3 (fourth), 550:440 = 5:4 (major third), 660:550 =
  6:5 (minor third) — the interval ladder, in the order found,
  inside one note. Nobody chose it.

LAW: frequency doubles when string length halves; tension must
  QUADRUPLE to double pitch (frequency rises with tension's
  square root).
WORKED: fret a 65 cm string at 32.5 cm and 110 becomes 220; the
  same jump by tension needs 4× the pull — why instruments tune
  by tension and change register by length.

LAW: two waves at nearly one speed drift in and out of step; the
  loudness pulses at their DIFFERENCE (beats/s = f₁ − f₂).
RULE: tune by counting the wobble and turning until it stops —
  tuners do not listen to pitch, they remove beats.
WORKED: 440 against 442 wobbles twice a second; against 441,
  once; matched, it vanishes.

LAW: resonance — push a thing at its own speed (or a multiple)
  and small pushes pile into large swings. A bridge has a note,
  so armies break step crossing it; a shaft's dangerous speeds
  are its multiples. Music is the one field where resonance is
  the product, not the hazard.

LAW: pipes — open both ends: fundamental wavelength 2L, all
  multiples sound; closed one end: 4L, odd multiples only.
WORKED: a 39 cm open pipe: 343 ÷ 0.78 ≈ 440 — an A. Closed at one
  end: 343 ÷ 1.56 ≈ 220 — an octave lower from the same wood,
  even overtones missing: the hollow voice. Hence the clarinet
  (closed in behavior) plays deeper and hollower than a flute of
  like length (open).

LAW: real strings are stiff, so overtones run slightly SHARP of
  whole numbers; a piano tuned to pure arithmetic disagrees with
  its own overtones, so tuners stretch the octaves a few cents
  until the instrument agrees with itself.

LAW: a drum skin's overtones are NOT whole multiples (circle
  modes near 1.59×, 2.14×, 2.30×), so the stack never fuses into
  one pitch — the whole reason a drum reads as rhythm, not melody.

### MATHEMATICS — the ratio arithmetic

LAW: the fifth is 3:2 — multiply by 3, divide by 2.
WORKED: above A 440: 440 × 3 = 1,320; ÷ 2 = 660.

LAW: pure fifths and pure octaves cannot meet.
WORKED, digit by digit: twelve fifths = (3/2)¹² = 3¹² ÷ 2¹².
  3¹²: 3⁶ = 729, and 729 × 729 = 531,441. 2¹² = 4,096. So
  531,441 ÷ 4,096 ≈ 129.746. Seven octaves = 2⁷ = 128 exactly.
  Overshoot: 129.746 ÷ 128 ≈ 1.0136 — about a QUARTER OF A
  SEMITONE. A remainder can be moved but never deleted; every
  tuning system is a decision about where it lands (the failed
  decisions are the mirror's).

LAW: equal temperament spreads the remainder evenly — the
  semitone is the twelfth root of 2 ≈ 1.059463.
WORKED: square it three times — 1.122462, 1.259921, 1.587401 —
  and multiply the last two: 1.587401 × 1.259921 = 2.000000 ✓:
  twelve equal steps land exactly on the doubling, every interval
  except the octave now slightly wrong on purpose.
WORKED, the price list: tempered fifth = 2^(7/12) ≈ 1.498307;
  above 440 that is 659.26 against the pure 660 — 0.74/s flat, a
  wobble under once a second, near-inaudible. Tempered major
  third = 2^(4/12) ≈ 1.259921; above 440 that is 554.37 against
  the pure 550 — 4.4/s SHARP, the fast shimmer in every piano
  chord. The bargain: every key playable, every key slightly
  wrong, the thirds paying most of the bill.

LAW: cents — 1,200 to the octave, 100 to the equal semitone; a
  logarithm, so cents ADD where ratios multiply. Melody tolerates
  ~5 cents; beats expose far less; the comma is ~23.5 cents,
  which is why it can never hide.

LAW: the circle of fifths is clock arithmetic — step +7 on a
  clock of 12 and you visit all twelve before returning, because
  7 and 12 share no factor.
WORKED: 0, 7, 2, 9, 4, 11, 6, 1, 8, 3, 10, 5, 0. ✓ Each step
  changes exactly ONE of a key's seven notes — neighbors share
  six of seven, the map of easy and shocking modulations.

LAW: the major scale's steps are 2 2 1 2 2 2 1 semitones and must
  sum to the octave.
WORKED: 2+2+1+2+2+2+1 = 12. ✓ The two 1s are the landmarks that
  give every degree its address; start the pattern from another
  seat and you have a mode — same notes, rotated distances.

LAW: durations are halvings — whole, half, quarter, eighth,
  sixteenth: binary arithmetic older than the word. A bar sums to
  its signature; a dot adds half (dotted quarter = 1½ beats).

LAW: fretting is temperament carved in wood — each fret removes
  1 − 1/1.059463 ≈ 1/17.8 of the string that remains (the old
  "rule of eighteen," slightly flat of true, superseded and
  kept).
WORKED: on a 65 cm scale the first fret sits 65 × 0.05613 ≈
  3.65 cm from the nut; the twelfth at exactly half, 32.5 cm —
  and lands on the octave. ✓

### THE TIME ARITHMETIC (physics + the body, welded)

LAW: seconds per beat = 60 ÷ tempo (beats per minute).
WORKED: at 120 a beat is 0.5 s; a four-bar phrase of four beats
  is 16 × 0.5 = 8 seconds — one comfortable sung breath, WHY the
  four-bar phrase rules: the body set the unit before notation.
  At 60 the same phrase is 16 s — past one breath — so slow music
  breathes in halves or the singer plans thefts of air.

LAW: the body's clocks are the reference — resting heart ~60-80 a
  minute, walking ~100-120 steps. Under the heart is weight, over
  the walk is urgency; nobody is taught the pulse.

LAW: two groupings over one pulse meet at their common multiple.
WORKED: three-against-two — cut the bar into 6; one voice strikes
  every 2 units (0, 2, 4), the other every 3 (0, 3); they agree
  only at 0, and that meeting is the pattern's period. Which grid
  you take as the beat changes what you hear.

LAW: phrases pair — a two-bar ask, a two-bar answer — and pairs
  stack: 2+2 = 4, 4+4 = 8, 8+8 = 16, the sixteen bars most of the
  tradition is built on. The answer works by being clearly the
  same size and clearly not the same content.

### LOUDNESS AND THE EAR (physics + the body)

LAW: loudness is logarithmic — every 10 dB is 10× the energy,
  read roughly twice as loud.
WORKED: conversation (~60 dB) over whisper (~30 dB): 30 dB =
  10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000× the energy, heard as ~8× as loud (three
  doublings). The scale exists because the ear walks a
  trillion-fold energy range comfortably.

LAW: sound thins with the square of distance — double the
  distance, quarter the energy, −6 dB; tenfold, −20 dB.
  Amplification is distance repealed.

LAW: hearing spans ~20 to ~20,000 vibrations per second.
WORKED: 20 doubled ten times → 20,480 — TEN octaves. The piano
  covers just over seven (27.5 to 4,186). The eye gets barely one
  octave of light — why the color wheel can only borrow the
  octave's fold, never earn it.

## METHODS
How things are done here — what governs each, and what breaks it.
The prepositioned how-to-build.

- TUNING BY BEATS: sound two notes, count the wobble, turn until
  it stops (or reaches the tempering's prescribed count). Breaks
  in loud rooms and on intervals whose shared overtones the
  instrument barely makes.
- TEMPERING (allocating the comma): spread evenly — every key
  playable, thirds pay; or pile into far keys (the old ways) —
  near keys purer, far keys broken. The pile is superseded and
  KEPT: music written inside it still answers best in it. The
  mirror holds the wolf.
- BUILDING A SCALE: choose home; lay 2 2 1 2 2 2 1 for major,
  steps uneven so every degree keeps its address. For minor,
  lower the third — then patch the weak pull home (no semitone
  under home): raise the seventh for the cadence, and smooth the
  wide step that patch opens by raising its neighbor on the way
  up. A fix creating a second problem needing its own fix — and
  singers wrote every clause.
- ESTABLISHING HOME: state the home chord early and return; the
  listener's prediction does the rest. Deliberate ambiguity is
  itself a method — for suspense.
- HARMONIZING (the three-role grammar): most chords play home,
  approach, or pull; the tendency runs home → approach → pull →
  home. Not physics — a grammar a culture built, learned before
  speech, working like a word order that feels like a law.
- VOICE LEADING: move each voice as little as possible; leave
  common notes sitting. The ear tracks lines by continuity; a far
  jump reads as a new part starting. Break it only when a new
  object IS the effect.
- ENDING: pick from the cadence menu — full stop, comma,
  page-turn — by how closed the moment must feel.
- MODULATING: find a chord both keys own, let it face both ways,
  cadence in the new key. Done well it is noticed only afterwards,
  like a conversation's topic quietly shifting.
- NON-CHORD NOTES, FORGIVEN: outside notes pass when their exit
  is already predictable — passing, leaning, held over and
  stepping down. The ear charges for the missing indicator, not
  the lane change.
- WRITING A MELODY: mostly steps, few leaps, each leap repaid
  with steps back into its gap; shape a contour memory can keep;
  stay in the range's living band — strain is a spice, not a diet
  (muscles at range's end make force badly and tire fast).
- SETTING WORDS: land the loud syllable on the strong beat; where
  speech and tune disagree, decide which wins — that repeated
  decision is most of what makes a tradition's songs its own.
- DEVELOPING A MOTIF: grow a piece from one idea by operations
  keeping one relation intact — repeat higher, invert, stretch,
  halve, keep rhythm and change notes. Recognition survives
  transformation; the family is heard without work.
- COUNTERPOINT: manufacture independence — one voice leaps while
  the other steps, one moves while one holds. In parallel motion
  the ear fuses them and the second voice is GONE; the rule
  against parallel fifths is that measurement written as law.
- ARRANGING: clearance before colour (one important line per
  register — masking); mind the outer voices (top and bottom are
  most of what is heard); manage the unspent — the last chorus
  feels bigger because something was withheld from the first.
- TEXTURE: declare the figure count before picking notes — one
  line alone, one served, several equal, or a block in one
  rhythm. Attention follows one line well and several badly.
- GROOVE: play the deviations consistently — the pair's second
  note late, snare a hair behind, bass a hair ahead — against a
  kept grid. Quantizing removes the information (the mirror holds
  the corpse). An accent lives in tiny consistent timings.
- SYNCOPATION: strike where the beat is not WHILE something still
  holds the beat; spent against nothing, the promise re-forms
  around the offbeats and the thrill is gone.
- FORM: schedule returns before forgetting — verse and chorus,
  departure and return, theme and variations are one plan: new
  material spent, recognition paid back, sized to a memory that
  holds a few seconds.
- CALL AND RESPONSE: a statement, then an answer the same size
  and not the same content, so the pairing is heard as one.
  Turn-taking set to pitch; the work song's labor sets the phrase.
- TRANSPOSING: shift everything by one interval — tune, harmony,
  form untouched (they were relations) while the task changes
  hands: open strings move, the singer's break falls elsewhere.
  Same music, different work; the capo is this method as hardware.
- IMPROVISING: composing with the editing removed, so it runs on
  prepared material — patterns in the hands, the harmony's map
  shared. It sounds like freedom and is mostly a large vocabulary
  selected from at speed; fluency is stored work.
- DYNAMICS AS A CURVE: plan loudness as a shape over the whole —
  grow, drop, reserve. A speaker who starts shouting has nowhere
  left; the flat line is the only wrong answer.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE VOICE: the free instrument; ~2 octaves reachable, the
  livable band far narrower; the mouth is a filter boosting
  overtone regions into vowels — song and speech share the
  machine, which is why melody can outlive speech in a broken
  brain (medicine holds that door).
- STRINGS: pitch by length, tension, thickness; the fret table is
  temperament carved in wood; a bow is controlled stick-and-slip
  re-feeding the wave every cycle — resonance farmed.
- PIPES: pitch by air-column length, open and closed laws above;
  brass players pick among one pipe's overtone stack with their
  lips — a bugle call is the overtone series played bare.
- DRUMS AND STRUCK THINGS: inharmonic stacks (the 1.59× law) —
  time's instruments; bells, bars, timpani are craft wrestling
  overtones back toward whole numbers.
- KEYBOARDS: temperament frozen into hardware — every key
  playable, nothing bendable; twelve compromises a fine action
  makes invisible.
- THE REFERENCE AND THE METRONOME: fork or signal at the agreed
  440; the click is the promise made audible — practice against
  it builds the grid a groove then plays against.
- NOTATION: fixes pitch exactly, duration roughly, loudness and
  speed as vague words, timbre and groove not at all — two
  faithful readings can differ unrecognizably. A seed, not a
  photograph; its missing half is the mirror's standing want.
- THE ROOM: reverberation blends or smears; meter-long bass
  wavelengths ignore furniture and find the room's resonances —
  every room is an instrument played by whatever sounds in it.
- AMPLIFICATION AND RECORDING: the −6 dB law bought off; presence
  separated from performance; the quiet voice intimate at scale
  (the microphone invented the crooner). A record keeps ONE
  performance perfectly and the piece's openness not at all.
- ELECTRONIC MAKING: any overtone recipe buildable — timbre by
  arithmetic; the attack still carries identity, so a synthetic
  instrument lives or dies in its first fraction of a second,
  same law as the violin.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- MOVE TOGETHER: march, dance, haul, row — a shared pulse is the
  cheapest coordination there is; the work song's beat is the
  labor's clock made audible.
- REMEMBER: metered, rhymed verse carried whole libraries before
  writing — rhyme is a check-digit (a wrong word will not fit),
  meter the case; rhymed rules survived courts that could not
  read.
- BOND: singing together is agreement rehearsed — in tune IS
  consensus, audible; the crowd answering the caller is the
  oldest meeting.
- BE MOVED: the one mechanism — expectation handled skillfully —
  hired on purpose: tension bought, release paid, on time or
  gorgeously late.
- MARK THE OCCASION: ritual runs on return — the same song at the
  same threshold makes the date heavy.
- SAY WHAT WORDS CANNOT: lullaby and lament work below vocabulary
  — contour, tempo, register with no lyric on top; hum the song
  and it is still the song.
- EARN: the player's trade, the maker's craft — economy's door.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- The voice first: nothing to build — pulse from the heart,
  phrase from the breath, pitch from the speech machine.
- Instruments before writing: bone flutes survive from ~40,000
  years back, older than farms. They preserve their scales (the
  holes sit where they sit) but not one tune — the mirror holds
  what stayed lost.
- Ratio heard before physics existed: a plucked string, halved,
  sounds the octave — Pythagoras heard arithmetic, and harmony
  became the first physics written as number. The overtone stack
  behind it was understood millennia later; the hearing outran
  the why the whole way.
- Method before theory, for thousands of years: singers built the
  minor scale's patches, players built the grammar, theory
  noticed afterwards — then was handed down as law. Practice
  leads; the schoolroom inverts the order.
- Notation, about a thousand years back: lines ruled so a note
  could SIT somewhere — music became mailable, buildable in
  layers (counterpoint is notation's child), able to outlive its
  holders. What cannot be written into a symbol cannot be
  inherited — the thermometer's lesson, learned here first.
- The temperament wars, centuries long: everyone heard the comma;
  the fight was where to hide it. Pure-here-broken-there lost to
  slightly-wrong-everywhere as keyboards spread and composers
  demanded all twelve keys. The wolf's record is the mirror's.
- Pitch pinned late: "A" wandered by more than a semitone across
  halls and centuries (surviving forks disagree that widely) —
  brilliance bought by tuning sharper, an arms race without an
  anchor — until 440 was agreed in the recording era.
- Recording and amplification reordered everything: music
  separated from presence for the first time in the species' run;
  the room's grip loosened; the performance itself — not just the
  piece — became keepable. What each keeper drops is the mirror's.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward. Each names
what runs along it.

- PHYSICS: waves adding, the overtone stack, beats, resonance,
  pipes and strings, the inverse square — music is physics worn
  as art, and a prism is an ear for light.
- MATHEMATICS: ratio, the logarithm (cents, decibels), clock
  arithmetic (the circle of fifths), the undeleteable remainder
  (the comma), binary durations, operations that leave a thing
  itself (motif development).
- BODY: the coiled-keyboard ear, heart and walk as first clocks,
  breath as the phrase, voice as muscles, range as joint limits.
- MIND: prediction as the one mechanism, grouping imposed on even
  clicks, contour as cheap storage, working memory sizing the
  form, reference points silently replaced (modulation).
- LANGUAGE: stress seats and word setting, rhyme's matched
  endings, the ~40-sound fence like the scale's, tone of voice as
  what text drops, speech repeated until it sings.
- PEOPLE: in-tune as agreement, turn-taking as call and response,
  conventions becoming expectations heard as laws.
- CULTURE: genre's expectation bundle, each tradition's scale
  picks and dissonance line, ritual's returns.
- STORY: every plot is a long cadence — home, trouble, home; the
  setup and the twisted payoff (comic timing is rhythm's law).
- ENGINEERING: resonance guarded against (bridges, shafts) and
  farmed (every instrument); tolerance allocation = temperament;
  gear teeth mesh in small whole ratios — a gearbox is a chord.
- ELECTRONICS: tuning a dial is choosing a pitch of light; two
  carriers beat like two strings; amplification and synthesis.
- COMPUTING: a small alphabet composing forever, the quantized
  grid and what it kills, timbre by arithmetic.
- TEACHING: metered verse as the first library, spaced review as
  form's twin, copying the master to acquire the parts.
- MOVEMENT: dance and march as the pulse embodied; effort lands
  on the beat or fails; groove as bodies' consistent lags.
- PLAY: the feint on a taught rhythm, the game of expectation,
  the replay watched knowing the score.
- MEASUREMENT: the reference pitch, cents, the metronome, the
  tuner counting beats — music is measurement's oldest customer.
- LAW: rhymed rules outliving illiterate courts, custom hardening
  into statute (theory's biography), the scandalous being a date.
- MEDICINE: rhythm steadying a broken gait, melody carrying words
  past a lesion — receipts and broken claims both in the mirror.
- ECONOMY: the standard as currency, the rounding error that must
  land in some account (the comma's cousin), the player's trade.
- COOKING: appetite built then fed is tension and release; a
  stock is a timbre; a cuisine is a scale.
- DESIGN: negative space as the rest, palette as scale, the
  figure needing a ground.
- CRAFT: instrument-making — wood and metallurgy wrestling
  overtones toward whole numbers; the maker's hand in the attack.
- BELIEF: chant, ritual return, the occasion made heavy by the
  same song sung at it.
- ASTRONOMY: the hot gas's fixed lines — whole-number physics in
  light; the spheres' broken claim and its partial return are the
  mirror's.
- ANIMALS: birdsong and whale song — music's claims meet other composers.
- BUILDING: the hall is an instrument; every room plays what sounds in it.
- PHILOSOPHY: why ordered sound moves us is an old open question.
