## THINGS
The units this subject is about. The soft-looking field with hard
bones: every pleasure here is doing a job.

- PLAY — practice wearing a mask of pointlessness; the
  free-mistake zone where hunting, fighting, and losing are
  rehearsed without their prices; also lives in: life, animals,
  movement, teaching, mind.
- FUN — the wage that gets the repetitions done; not decoration
  but payment, and a wage that stops being counted the moment a
  different wage is paid beside it (see CLAIMS); also: mind,
  teaching.
- THE GAME — a goal made trivially reachable, then fenced by rules
  that forbid the efficient means; the chosen inefficiency IS the
  game; also: movement (sports run games on the body),
  mathematics, computing, conflict.
- THE RULE — the fence that makes freedom interesting: with no
  rules every action is equally available and none is a choice;
  rules prune until choices differentiate; also: law (its solemn
  cousin), governance, mathematics.
- THE TOY — an object licensed to stand for another: the stick
  that is a sword, the simulator that is a cockpit; the license is
  granted by the player and revocable at dusk; also: craft,
  design, story.
- CHANCE — dice, cards, spinners: uniform unpredictability by
  construction, a die's 1/6 an honesty machine; the equalizer
  between unequal players; also: mathematics (probability was born
  at this table — see HISTORY), economy.
- THE JOKE — two readings of one thing held apart, then snapped
  together; something wrong (logic, dignity, expectation) inside a
  frame that is right (no harm, among friends); also: language
  (puns ride grammar), mind, story.
- LAUGHTER — the social sound wearing amusement's clothes: caught
  from strangers, multiplied by company, mostly spent on remarks
  that are not jokes; also: people, body, animals (the cousins
  pant where we ha-ha).
- THE BEAT — timing's unit: the length of the short phrase the ear
  expects next, roughly a second in speech; humor's physics runs
  on it; also: music (the rest before the final chord), story.
- THE FRAME — the safety signal both sides must read before the
  first rough move: the dog's play bow, the child's grin, the
  whistle, "wanna bet?"; no frame, and play is a fight; also:
  people, animals, ethics (consent is the frame grown up).
- BEAUTY — order with a surprise in it: enough pattern to promise
  sense, enough deviation to demand attention; the live zone
  between wallpaper and static; also: design, music, craft,
  mathematics, mind.
- THE SEAM — the almost-too-hard: a task just past comfortable
  skill with instant feedback; too easy bores, too hard frightens,
  and in between, hours vanish; also: teaching (learning runs
  fastest there), movement, craft (the lost afternoon).
- CUTENESS — the pull of infant proportions: big eyes, big head,
  round cheek; a care reflex keyed to shape, steered deliberately
  by cartoonists and toymakers (mechanism part-settled — the
  mirror holds the open half); also: animals, design, body.
- THE PLAYER — the one who chose to be here; choosing is a
  defining condition, not a circumstance (see the obligation law);
  also: people, mind.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Laws here
are tendencies where flagged; iron where stated iron.

- Play is practice with the price removed: the young of every
  clever species rehearse the acts their lives will demand, where
  mistakes cost nothing. Kittens and chess both rehearse wars
  never to be fought.
- A costly universal behavior persists only if it pays. Play burns
  energy, risks injury, exposes the player — and evolution kept it
  in every clever lineage. The persistence is the receipt.
- Fun is the wage. Pay a different wage on top — money, grades,
  mandatory attendance — and the inner wage stops being counted:
  children paid for drawing they already loved drew roughly half
  as much afterward when free to choose (~half; worked in
  SCIENCE). This is WHY play collapses under obligation.
- A laugh marks a rule broken safely: something wrong inside a
  frame that is right. Remove the wrong and it is dull; remove the
  safety and it is an attack. The roast and the insult can be the
  same words — the frame is the whole difference, and it is judged
  by the receiver, not the speaker.
- Timing is humor's physics: expectation built, held one beat past
  comfort, then snapped. The same words early or late land as
  nothing (the beat arithmetic is in SCIENCE).
- A joke explained is a joke dead — iron as fact; the laugh lives
  only in the instant of unassisted crossing (mechanism part-open;
  the mirror holds it, with the arithmetic of why explanation
  starves the snap).
- Laughter is social before it is funny: people laugh on the order
  of thirtyfold more in company than alone (~30×), catch laughs
  from strangers, and spend most laughs on remarks that are not
  jokes. Bonding first; comedy rents the mechanism.
- Beauty is order with surprise: the live zone lies between
  wallpaper (all pattern, no news) and static (all news, no
  pattern). Gardens please between parking lot and jungle; fire
  and surf are watchable forever because order arrives there as
  endless novelty.
- Symmetry pleases, and a slight deliberate break pleases more:
  the near-mirrored face, the pot with one flaw, jazz's beat
  pushed just off center, the letterer's nudge off the
  mathematical middle. The preference is iron; the mechanism is
  the mirror's.
- The seam holds the deepest pull: games build levels to chase it
  as skill grows; climbing, fishing, and work at its best sit in
  it. Where it sits is iron; why it feels like joy is the
  mirror's.
- A game's rules must forbid the efficient means: golf is putting
  a ball in a hole, trivial by hand; the game is the chosen
  inefficiency. Loosen the rules until the easy way is legal and
  the game evaporates.
- A solved game dies into wallpaper: once the best line is known
  to a player, choices stop differentiating and play ends — for
  that player, forever (the counting law prices the shelf life).
- Chance flattens skill and skill rewards practice; every lasting
  game mixes the two, because pure chance bores the skilled and
  pure skill excludes the new.
- Science is play formalized: the sanctioned "what if" with a
  budget; the fire drill and the simulator are play formalized
  for adults.
- Suspense is comic timing played slow: dread and the punchline
  run on the same clock at different speeds.

## SCIENCE
The math of play, held — not mentioned. Each piece is a LAW a
query can compute with, a RULE it can follow, and a WORKED case
proving the rule runs.

### MATHEMATICS — the size of a surprise

LAW: surprise, in bits = log2(1 ÷ p), where p is how expected the
  thing was. The snap of a joke, the twist of a story, and the
  deviation that makes beauty all draw on this account.
RULE: to size a reveal — estimate how expected the second reading
  was; take log2 of one over that.
WORKED: a coin's fall: log2(2) = 1 bit. A die's face: log2(6) ≈
  2.58 bits. A punchline riding a reading the room gave 1-in-100:
  log2(100) ≈ 6.6 bits — a big snap. Repeat the joke: the room
  knows it, p climbs toward 1, and log2(1) = 0 — zero bits, no
  laugh. The worn joke is not a mystery; it is this subtraction.
  The same arithmetic runs the explained joke and the sequel's
  smaller thrill (both filed in the mirror).

### MATHEMATICS — beauty's live zone

LAW: a pattern is a description much shorter than the thing.
  Wallpaper: tiny description. Static: a description as long as
  the thing. Beauty's zone: a short description PLUS deviations
  worth reporting.
RULE: to place a thing on the wallpaper-static line — ask how
  briefly it can be honestly retold, then whether the retelling
  contains any news.
WORKED: a 100-letter strip "ABABAB…" retells as "AB, fifty times"
  — about 6 symbols for 100, ~15:1, no news: wallpaper. A
  100-letter random strip retells only by reciting all 100 — 1:1,
  all news and none worth having: static. A melody: an 8-note
  theme plus three variations each named in a phrase — deep
  compression AND real news at every variation. That is
  theme-and-variation, this law walking naked, and the same zone
  the garden and the surf sit in.

### MATHEMATICS — a game's shelf life (counting positions)

LAW: positions multiply per cell and per move; a game's depth is
  whether a life can exhaust them.
RULE: to price a shelf life — count (or bound) the truly
  different positions and compare to the plays a player will ever
  make.
WORKED: tic-tac-toe, 9 cells of 3 states: at most 3⁹ = 19,683
  boards (3, 9, 27, 81, 243, 729, 2,187, 6,561, 19,683); legality
  and symmetry cut that to well under a thousand truly different
  positions. A child exhausts it in an afternoon — wallpaper for
  the rest of that life. Chess holds roughly 10⁴⁴ legal positions
  (~), go roughly 10¹⁷⁰ (~): no life exhausts either; the seam
  never dies. The count IS the shelf life.

### MATHEMATICS — fairness and the equalizer (expected value)

LAW: a chance game's long-run rate per play = sum over outcomes
  of (probability × payout).
RULE: weight every payout by its chance and add; zero is fair,
  negative is a fee, positive is a job.
WORKED: pay 1 to roll a die, win 5 on a six:
  (1/6)(+5) + (5/6)(−1) = 5/6 − 5/6 = 0 — fair. Pay 1, win 4:
  (1/6)(+4) + (5/6)(−1) = 4/6 − 5/6 = −1/6 ≈ −0.17 per roll —
  about 17 lost per hundred rolls. That standing −1/6 is a house
  edge, and it is why the casino is not playing: it has no seam,
  it has arithmetic. (Economy cashes this line; the mirror files
  the dead systems that tried to beat it, with their numbers.)

### MATHEMATICS — the seam, engineered (matched play)

LAW: between rated players, expected win rate for the weaker ≈
  1 ÷ (1 + 10^(gap ÷ 400)) — the rating scale most ranked games
  run on.
RULE: to keep a room playing, pair inside ~200 rating points;
  past ~400, re-pair or handicap.
WORKED: a 200-point gap: 10^(200/400) = 10^0.5 ≈ 3.16, and
  1 ÷ 4.16 ≈ 0.24 — the weaker player wins one in four: stung,
  hopeful, still at the table. A 400-point gap: 1 ÷ (1 + 10) =
  1/11 ≈ 0.09 — one in eleven: the weak frightened, the strong
  bored, both gone. Handicaps run the law from the other side:
  go's stones and golf's strokes move the seam instead of the
  pairing, so unequal friends can share one game.

### THE MIND'S CLOCK — the timing arithmetic of the reveal

LAW: speech runs ~2-3 words a second; a beat — the short phrase
  the ear expects next — is roughly a second (~). Expectation
  needs time to finish building before it can be broken.
RULE: build the first reading honestly, hold the silence about
  one beat past where the answer was due, then snap. Half a beat
  early, the room hasn't finished predicting — nothing yet to
  break. Two beats late, the prediction has died of old age and
  the reveal arrives ownerless.
WORKED: at 2.5 words a second, a 10-word setup lands its
  expectation in 10 ÷ 2.5 = 4 seconds; the due-point falls at the
  rhythm's next beat; the hold is ~1 second more; then the snap.
  The auctioneer's held silence, the rest before the final chord,
  and the horror film's too-long corridor are this one law at
  three tempos. Numbers rough (~); the shape iron.

### LIFE'S RECEIPTS — the mammal arithmetic

- Rats chirp at ~50 kHz when tickled — human hearing stops near
  ~20 kHz, so the laughter was there all along, above our
  ceiling, until instruments lowered it into range.
- Play is bought at real cost: energy, injury risk, attention off
  predators — a few percent of a young mammal's whole budget (~)
  — and it persists in every clever lineage. By the persistence
  law, it pays.
- The proof runs backward too: rats raised without
  rough-and-tumble misread other rats' signals later (~, lab
  measured). The rehearsal was doing work; remove it and the work
  goes undone.
- The obligation law, measured: children who loved drawing were
  promised a reward for it; afterward, free to choose, they drew
  roughly half as much as unpaid children (~half). RULE: to kill
  a play, wage it; to keep it, keep the choosing in the player's
  hands. This one measurement patrols the border between play and
  work.

## METHODS
How things are done and built here — each with what governs it
and what breaks it.

- MAKING A GAME: pick a goal trivially reachable; forbid the
  efficient means until the remaining ways differentiate; count
  the positions (shelf life); mix skill and chance to set who the
  game is for; playtest at the seam. Breaks when: rules so tight
  one line survives (wallpaper on arrival) or so loose the easy
  way is legal (no game at all).
- TUNING DIFFICULTY: chase the seam as skill grows — levels,
  handicaps, pairing inside ~200 points; feedback must be
  instant, because a delayed score is no seam at all. Breaks when
  the tune lags the player: yesterday's seam is today's boredom.
- TELLING A JOKE: establish the first reading honestly (a setup
  that cheats owes the room a debt the snap can't pay); hold one
  beat; snap to the second reading; among strangers, lay the
  safety frame first. Breaks when: the room got there first (zero
  bits), the frame isn't shared (an attack), the hold runs long
  (the reveal arrives ownerless).
- OPENING PLAY (raising the frame): the play bow, the grin, the
  whistle, "bet you can't" — a signal both sides read before the
  first rough move; dogs, children, and boxers all refuse to
  begin without it. Closing the frame is a method too — "okay,
  I'm done" — and it must be honored instantly or the frame was
  never real.
- MAKING IT BEAUTIFUL: lay a pattern strong enough to promise
  sense; break it once, deliberately, where the eye will find it
  — the potter's one flaw, the swung beat, the varied theme.
  Breaks both ways: perfect the pattern and it is wallpaper;
  multiply the breaks and it is noise.
- PRACTICE DISGUISED: score the drill and it becomes an appetite
  — scrimmage, fire drill, simulator, spelling bee. Governed by
  the obligation law: the game skin must make the practice itself
  the fun; mandate and grade it, and the overjustification
  arithmetic runs (the mirror holds the mandated-fun record).
- HOLDING THE ROOM (compound timing): callbacks — a pattern
  planted early and returned to — get cheaper to set and bigger
  to snap each time, because earlier laughs paid the setup's
  cost. The closer that ties three earlier jokes is the method at
  full stretch.
- HANDICAPPING FRIENDS: stones, strokes, head starts — move the
  seam to the pairing at hand so the game, not the gap, decides
  the evening. Governed by the matched-play law; breaks when the
  handicap is hidden (a discovered secret advantage reads as a
  broken frame, not a kindness).

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- DICE, CARDS, SPINNERS: portable, inspectable chance — six faces
  at 1/6 each is fairness you can hold; a shuffle is a fresh
  unknown every deal. The oldest instruments of the equalizer.
- THE BALL: the cheapest generator of endless novelty — round
  enough to be fair, lively enough to be never twice the same;
  one object, a thousand games.
- THE BOARD: a world pruned to its choices — 64 squares, 361
  points, 9 cells; small enough to hold, deep enough (by the
  counting law) to hold you.
- THE TOY BOX: objects licensed to stand for others; license
  granted by the player, revocable at dusk.
- THE FENCED ZONE: playground, field, ring — the free-mistake
  zone built in timber and chalk: padded ground, agreed edges, a
  referee, a clock. The fence is what makes the mistakes free.
- THE STAGE AND THE MICROPHONE: the beat's instruments — a room
  arranged so one clock (the teller's) holds everyone's
  expectation at once, and the contagion law can run.
- LEVELS, LADDERS, MATCHMAKERS: the seam-chasing engines —
  pairings inside ~200 points, difficulty climbing behind skill;
  the industrial form of the handicap.
- THE WHISTLE AND THE BELL: frame on, frame off — the safety
  signal issued to instruments, so no one reads a face at full
  speed.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- REHEARSE WHERE MISTAKES ARE FREE: the founding purpose — grow
  the skills of hunting, fighting, losing, and getting up, at toy
  prices, before life charges full price.
- GROW SKILL WEARING JOY: the seam is where learning runs
  fastest, and fun keeps the learner in it for hours; play is the
  only teacher whose students stay late.
- BIND THE GROUP: shared laughter is a two-second ritual of
  synchrony; the table game, the team, the running joke — people
  who play together have practiced trusting a frame together.
- SIGNAL SAFETY: the laugh and the frame declare "no harm here"
  among strangers; humor is diplomacy at the speed of a beat.
- REST THAT RESTORES: the off-duty that returns the player
  stronger — unlike idleness, it exercises exactly what it rests.
- MAKE THE ORDINARY WORTH ATTENTION: beauty's want — the garden,
  the well-set table, the swung beat; pattern-with-surprise turns
  the plain world watchable.
- HOST THE WHAT-IF: sanctioned pretending with a budget — the
  simulator, the war game, the thought experiment, science
  itself; the free-mistake zone scaled to civilization.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Play precedes the species: mammals had it long before humanity;
  the free-mistake zone is older than every human thing in this
  fabric. Humanity inherited play, built rules around it, and
  called the fenced kind games.
- Games are among the oldest artifacts: knucklebone dice and
  royal-grave boards go back thousands of years (~5,000 for the
  oldest known boards; go has run continuously for well over two
  thousand). The rules survived where empires didn't —
  inheritance by delight.
- Probability was born at the gaming table: a gambler's dispute
  over a broken-off dice game (the 1650s, two mathematicians
  corresponding) founded the mathematics of chance; risk,
  insurance, and measurement grew from an argument about stakes.
  Play's clearest gift to the serious world.
- The laugh track: honest engineering on the contagion law —
  recorded company for the solitary viewer — worked for decades,
  then aged as rooms learned to hear the can (the mirror holds
  the failure record; the contagion it ran on is still iron).
- The seam was named late: every craftsman knew the lost
  afternoon, every angler the vanishing hours; psychology filed
  it recently and games industrialized it immediately — levels,
  ladders, matchmakers.
- The solved games' funerals: tic-tac-toe dies in every
  childhood; checkers was proved a draw by years of computation
  (~) — a real triumph that ended the game at its highest level,
  exactly as the shelf-life law prices. The funeral notices are
  the mirror's warning shape: some wants consume their object.
- Play science started under a dismissal: "just playing" kept the
  field unfunded for generations while play did the species'
  rehearsal. The correction arrived through instruments — the
  ~50 kHz chirp above our ceiling — and deprivation studies that
  showed the rehearsal's absence as damage. What looked trivial
  was load-bearing.
