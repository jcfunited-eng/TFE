Nobody has been anywhere. Every fact here was got out of arriving
light, arriving particles, and arithmetic — so HOW it is known is
as much of the subject as WHAT is known.

## THINGS
The units this subject is about; other homes named so a cascade
can branch.

- THE TURNING SKY — the vault wheeling once a day because the
  ground turns under it; the oldest clock, compass, and calendar,
  free and overhead; also: measurement (every clock ever built
  copies this wheel), farming, transport, belief.
- THE SUN — the local star: a furnace held steady by its own
  correction for billions of years; also: physics, weather (its
  output drives all of it), farming, earth.
- THE MOON AND PLANETS — the fall that never lands and the
  wanderers against the fixed stars; worlds, not lights; also:
  earth (tides, the comparison underfoot), flight, story.
- STARS — distant suns in every stage of a life no one can
  watch; color their thermometer, mass their whole fate; also:
  physics, chemistry (the element factories).
- GALAXIES — cities of stars so far apart that two cities pass
  through each other without one collision of citizens; also:
  physics, story (the deep past made visible).
- LIGHT — the messenger almost everything rides in; fast but not
  instant, so every look outward is a look backward; also:
  physics, measurement, electronics.
- THE SPECTRUM — light split by wavelength: one faint smear
  carrying elements, amounts, motion, pressure, spin, and
  magnetic field at once; also: chemistry, measurement.
- GRAVITY AND ORBITS — the one engine of every motion out there,
  and the only weighing scale that works at any distance; also:
  physics, mathematics, flight, computing (the map fleet).
- THE DISTANCE LADDER — methods calibrated each on a nearer one:
  geometry at the bottom, the age of the universe at the top;
  also: measurement (its purest large case).
- DUST AND GAS — the thin smoke between stars: nursery,
  light-reddener, and what galaxies actually collide with; also:
  chemistry, weather (haze's big sibling).
- CLUSTERS — many stars born together at one distance from one
  batch: nature's controlled experiment, free; also: measurement,
  teaching.
- THE THREE ENDS — white dwarf (crushed, cooling forever),
  neutron star (a sun's weight in a city, a spinning lighthouse),
  black hole (seen only by its works); also: physics.
- EXOPLANETS — worlds around other stars, found by dimming and
  wobble; the catalogue a portrait of the methods as much as of
  the galaxy; also: life, measurement.
- THE ANCIENT GLOW — the oldest light there is, mottled by one
  part in a hundred thousand; baby picture and geometry ruler in
  one; also: physics, measurement.
- THE INSTRUMENTS — sextant, chronometer, telescope,
  spectrograph, radio array, neutrino tank, ripple detector;
  each opened a sky resembling nothing seen before; also:
  engineering, measurement, craft.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query.

- The earth turns once a day under a sky that holds still, 15°
  per hour, never needing rewinding; the stars wheel around one
  fixed point (Polaris, due north), and the sun's height tells
  hour and season. Clock and compass in one.
- The laws do not vary by address: every star and galaxy shows
  the same ~90 elements under the same physics — helium was
  found in the sun's light (1868) before on earth.
- An orbit is a fall that never lands: the moon drops toward the
  earth every second and its sideways speed makes it forever
  miss; astronauts are falling with their ship. (SCIENCE runs it
  to the millimeter.)
- Brightness lies alone: what arrives is real output ÷ distance²
  — two unknowns welded into one number. The night's brightest
  stars are a mix of close ordinary ones and distant furnaces,
  and the eye cannot tell which.
- Every look outward is a look backward: sunlight ~8 minutes
  old, the nearest star's light ~4 years old. A light-year is
  distance, not time; the past is the only thing anyone has seen.
- Color reports temperature: dull red coolest (~3,000°C), white
  hotter, blue hottest (~30,000°C) — the smith's glow law read
  across trillions of miles.
- Mass decides essentially everything about a star: plotted by
  color against true brightness, nine in ten land on one narrow
  band — a queue joined at birth, not a road. And heavy stars
  die young, not in proportion: ten suns of mass burns thousands
  of times faster — gone in tens of millions of years — while
  the lightest outlive the present universe many times over.
- Stars are born from COLD (collapse wins only where gravity
  beats the pressure holding a cloud open), and star-making
  throttles itself: the first big stars blow the remaining gas
  apart, so only a small fraction of any cloud becomes stars.
- Out of core hydrogen, middle and skin do opposites — the core
  shrinks and heats, the envelope swells and cools: a red giant
  is a fierce heart in a vast thin skin, and the sun will one
  day reach past this planet's orbit. Sun-sized stars then end
  gently, shedding a glowing shell around a bare cooling core;
  heavy stars build ash shells up to iron — where fusion stops
  paying — and the support vanishes in an instant: core falls
  in, rebound tears the star apart.
- The elements came from different factories, split cleanly:
  hydrogen and most helium from the universe's first minutes;
  carbon to iron cooked inside stars; gold and uranium from
  explosions and colliding neutron stars. Every heavier atom in
  a body was inside a star that died.
- Galaxies come in two conditions more than two shapes — flat,
  gas-rich, making blue new stars; or shapeless piles of old red
  ones, nothing new for billions of years: a report of whether
  the gas ran out or was taken. And galaxies collide often while
  their stars essentially never do: what collides is the gas,
  lighting new stars while gravity draws long tails.
- Space is almost perfectly empty — ~1 atom per thimble between
  stars, far emptier between galaxies — which is why light
  crosses billions of years unscattered, and therefore why any
  of this can be known at all.
- Nothing overhead changes on a human timescale except by
  accident: no star's life was ever watched; the life stories
  were assembled from one photograph of a very large crowd.
- The catalogue is shaped by the net: dimming finds big planets
  in tight orbits, wobble finds heavy ones in tight orbits. The
  sample is never the population; every survey must state what
  it could not have seen.
- The band where a planet can hold surface water moves outward
  as its star brightens with age — a world can start inside and
  end outside without moving; around the small cool stars that
  fill the galaxy it lies so close in that anything there keeps
  one face to its sun forever.
- A model that gets a fact as a consequence beats one that must
  add machinery to accommodate it: the planets' backward loops
  cost the earth-centered model an extra circle apiece and come
  free in the sun-centered one — overtaking, seen from a moving
  vehicle. (The mirror holds the fourteen-century receipt.)
- The sun's spots, flares, and eruptions beat on a ~11-year
  cycle — equator outrunning poles winds the field up until the
  tangle lets go; four centuries of counts include ~70 years
  with almost none.
- A meteor shower is a calendar entry, not an event: comet grit
  strung along orbits the earth crosses on the same dates; the
  streaks fan from one point because we fly into them — snow at
  a windscreen.
- Craters are clocks: impacts land at a steady long-run rate, so
  pitted plains are ancient, smooth ones resurfaced; the scale
  was anchored once, on the moon, with samples from counted
  ground.
- Rings sit inside the tearing line and moons outside it; and
  small pushes repeated in step accumulate while random ones
  cancel — simple period ratios empty the asteroid belt's gaps,
  keep Neptune and Pluto apart forever, chain Jupiter's moons. A
  note driving the string tuned to answer it.

## SCIENCE
The math and physics of the sky, held — not mentioned. Each piece
is a LAW to compute with, a RULE to follow, and where it earns
it, a WORKED case run to a real answer.

### MATHEMATICS — finding yourself by the sky

LAW: a triangle with a known side and two angles is fully
  decided. A star's height above the horizon draws a line on the
  earth you must stand on; two lines cross at you.
RULE: latitude = Polaris's height above the horizon, in degrees.
WORKED: Polaris at 40° up: latitude 40°N. Earth's girth ~40,000
  km: one degree of latitude = 40,000 ÷ 360 = 111 km; one MINUTE
  of arc = 111 ÷ 60 = 1.85 km — the definition of the nautical
  mile. A sextant read to one arc-minute fixes you to a couple
  of kilometers, from a star.

LAW: east-west position IS time difference: 360° in 24 hours =
  15° per hour = 1° per 4 minutes of clock.
RULE: carry home's time; note local noon; difference ÷ 4 minutes
  = degrees of longitude from home.
WORKED: local noon at 15:20 home time: 200 ÷ 4 = 50° west. One
  minute of clock error = ¼° = ~28 km at the equator. The
  chronometer that finally held true at sea (1700s) drifted ~5
  seconds in 81 days: 5 ÷ 240 = 0.02° — about 2 km. Longitude
  fell to a clock, not a telescope.

LAW: between fixes the score is arithmetic — distance = heading
  × speed × time — and each error rides into the next: the
  estimate decays from the last known point onward.
WORKED: 8 knots for 6 hours = 48 nautical miles; a heading off
  by 3°: 48 × sin 3° = 48 × 0.052 = 2.5 miles off track — per
  leg, and legs add. Hence the hunger for fixes; hence the map
  fleet correcting its clocks constantly or the maps drift;
  hence forecasting being dead reckoning in the sky.

LAW: a known size at a measured angle gives distance: angle
  (radians) = size ÷ distance.
WORKED: moon, 3,474 km at 384,400 km = 0.0090 rad = 0.52°; sun,
  1.39 million km at 150 million = 0.53°. Both half a degree —
  why total eclipses just barely work; a coincidence of this
  era, not a law.

LAW: a sphere cannot flatten without lying — every flat map
  distorts shape, size, or direction; and zero longitude is pure
  agreement (Greenwich, by treaty, 1884).
WORKED: the sailors' map shows Greenland the size of Africa;
  Africa is ~14× larger (30.4 vs 2.2 million km²). Maps work
  because everyone holds the same fictions.

### PHYSICS — gravity running the worlds

LAW: everything pulls on everything, weakening with distance
  squared: at n times the distance, 1/n² the pull.
WORKED (the founding test): ground gravity 9.8 m/s²; the moon at
  60 earth-radii should feel 9.8 ÷ 3,600 = 0.00272 m/s². Its
  real fall: circling 384,400,000 m out every 27.3 days
  (2,360,000 s) means speed 2π × 384,400,000 ÷ 2,360,000 =
  1,023 m/s, and inward fall v² ÷ r = 1,023² ÷ 384,400,000 =
  0.00272 m/s². The numbers MATCH. The moon falls ½ × 0.00272 =
  1.4 millimeters every second, and its sideways kilometer
  carries it far enough that the ground curves away by the same
  1.4 mm. The miss is perpetual — one law from apple to moon.

LAW: near the ground, orbit begins where the earth's curve falls
  away as fast as you do: the surface drops ~5 m per 8 km, and a
  dropped thing falls 4.9 m in its first second.
WORKED: at 8 km/s sideways you fall 4.9 m per second while the
  ground recedes 5 m per second — fall forever, land never; 7.9
  km/s is in fact every low orbit's speed. A thrown ball's arc
  is a fragment of this, landing only because 30 m/s is not
  8,000.

LAW: farther orbits are slower by a fixed trade — period squared
  grows as distance cubed: T₂ = T₁ × (r₂ ÷ r₁)^1.5.
WORKED: the station, 6,771 km from earth's center, circles in
  92.7 minutes; the moon at 384,400 km: ratio 56.8, 56.8^1.5 =
  428, and 92.7 × 428 = 39,700 min ≈ 27.5 days — the month,
  computed from the station's timetable (measured: 27.3).

LAW: the tearing line — inside roughly 2.4 planet-radii, the
  difference in pull across a body's own width beats its grip on
  itself: rings inside, substantial moons outside, no exceptions
  here. The same difference-in-pull, gentler, is the ocean tide.
LAW: resonance — pushes in step sum, pushes at random cancel;
  simple period ratios (1:2, 2:3) are where "in step" lives.

### PHYSICS — light the messenger

LAW: nothing outruns light, and light takes time: distance =
  300,000 km/s × time. What you see is that old, no exceptions.
WORKED: sun, 150,000,000 ÷ 300,000 = 500 s = 8 min 20 s; moon
  1.3 s. One year of travel: 300,000 × 31,600,000 s = 9.5
  trillion km — the light-year, a distance wearing time's name.
  The nearest star is seen 4.2 years ago; the nearest big galaxy
  2.5 million years ago, before anyone was here to look.

LAW: brightness falls with the square of distance.
RULE: to range a thing of KNOWN true output, measure how bright
  it looks; the dimming factor's square root is the distance
  ratio. Only a few objects announce their true output: certain
  stars by the rhythm of their pulsing (slower beat = brighter
  lamp), and one kind of exploding star with a fixed fuse.
WORKED: a pulsing star matched by rhythm to a calibrated twin at
  10 light-years looks 10,000× dimmer: √10,000 = 100× farther =
  1,000 light-years. Run in the spiral smudges, this arithmetic
  proved other galaxies lie outside our own.

LAW: the bottom rungs are plain triangle, no physics: the
  earth's orbit gives astronomy two eyes 300 million km apart,
  and a nearby star's seasonal jump against the background
  decides its distance while assuming nothing about the star.
WORKED: the nearest star jumps 0.77 arc-seconds (one arc-second
  = a coin seen at 4 km): distance 1 ÷ 0.77 = 1.30 units of 3.26
  light-years = 4.2 light-years. Everything farther is
  calibrated rung by rung on this — and a chain of calibrations
  is worth its worst link.

LAW: a hot body's glow peaks where its temperature says: peak
  wavelength × temperature ≈ 2,900,000 nm·K.
WORKED: the sun peaks at 500 nm: 2,900,000 ÷ 500 = 5,800 K. A
  310 K body peaks at 9,400 nm — deep infrared, why a warm room
  glows only to the right camera; a 3,000 K star peaks just past
  visible red, 30,000 K in ultraviolet. Blunt consequence: most
  of what the universe emits, no eye registers.

LAW: stretched lines report recession — but only because the
  home wavelengths are known from a laboratory bench. Stretch z
  = (observed − home) ÷ home; speed ≈ z × 300,000 km/s; farther
  galaxies recede faster, ~70 km/s per 3.26 million light-years.
WORKED: hydrogen's home line 656.3 nm seen at 662.9: 6.6 ÷
  656.3 = 0.010 → 3,000 km/s; distance 3,000 ÷ 70 = 43 units =
  ~140 million light-years. The whole expanding-universe picture
  rests on a hydrogen atom out there being a hydrogen atom here.

LAW: one spectrum is a stack of readings off the same lines:
  WHICH lines names the elements, DEPTH gives amounts, POSITION
  motion, WIDTH pressure and spin, SPLITTING magnetic field. And
  the same gas reads dark or bright by viewpoint alone: against
  a hot source behind it, it steals its colors out (gaps); from
  the side, those exact colors are the whole picture.

LAW: dust taxes the light — the thin smoke scatters blue more
  than red, so everything behind it looks dimmer AND redder (the
  sunset's reason, at galactic scale).
RULE: correct every distance and color measured through the
  galaxy's plane for dust first; carry the correction as
  uncertainty, never average it away.

### PHYSICS — the star furnace

LAW: mass is frozen energy: E = m × c², c² = 90 trillion joules
  per gram; fusing hydrogen to helium frees 0.7% of the mass.
RULE: the sun's payroll — output ÷ c² = mass converted per
  second; ÷ 0.007 = hydrogen consumed per second.
WORKED: output 3.8 × 10²⁶ W. Converted: 3.8 × 10²⁶ ÷ 9 × 10¹⁶ =
  4.2 × 10⁹ kg — ~4 million tonnes lighter every second; ÷ 0.007
  = 600 million tonnes of hydrogen burned per second. Lifetime:
  usable fuel ~10% of 2 × 10³⁰ kg gives 2 × 10²⁹ × 0.007 × 9 ×
  10¹⁶ = 1.3 × 10⁴⁴ J; ÷ 3.8 × 10²⁶ W = 3.3 × 10¹⁷ s = ~10
  billion years — the arithmetic lands on the sun's actual
  lifespan. The currency: fusing one gram of hydrogen yields 630
  billion J; burning it as fuel, 140,000 J — four million times
  the wage, same worker.

LAW: fusion's first step is a quantum loophole — the core is
  NOT hot enough to force protons together over their repulsion;
  the reaction leaks through the barrier so rarely a given
  particle waits billions of years, and that improbability is
  precisely why the sun lasts.
LAW: the thermostat — squeeze the star and it heats, hotter gas
  pushes back harder, it swells and cools: a correction growing
  with the error is a governor, and a star's gas is one for free.
LAW: fusion pays only downhill toward iron — fusing light nuclei
  pays, splitting heavy ones pays, iron sits at the valley floor
  where neither does. That sets every star's fuel, every heavy
  star's death, why bombs and reactors work, and why nothing at
  all works on iron.

LAW: burn rate climbs savagely with mass — output roughly as
  mass^3.5, lifetime as mass ÷ output.
WORKED: ten suns of mass: output 10^3.5 = 3,200 suns, lifetime
  10 billion × 10 ÷ 3,200 = ~30 million years; nine-tenths of a
  sun: 10 billion × 0.9 ÷ 0.69 = ~13 billion years — a
  first-generation star that size still burns today.
RULE (the cluster clock): a cluster is one age at many masses,
  burning down from the top; the mass just now leaving the band
  has a lifetime that IS the cluster's age.

LAW: a second way to hold a star up needs no heat — squeezed
  electrons refuse further packing (identical particles cannot
  share a state) and push back forever, cold: a white dwarf
  cools for eternity and never collapses. The refusal's ceiling
  is ~1.4 suns: fed past it the dwarf fails at one particular
  size, always — why those explosions come out equally bright
  and became the far galaxies' measuring rod. Past the
  electrons' limit the particles merge: heavier than the sun,
  the size of a city, spinning many times a second, beaming
  radio along a tilted axis — a lighthouse whose pulse rivals an
  atomic clock, found rather than manufactured.

LAW: the messengers that skip the queue — core-made light shoves
  through the crowd for ages, but neutrinos leave directly and
  arrive in 8 minutes, passing through sun and earth without
  noticing: the only live report from inside a star. And mass in
  violent motion shakes space's own geometry outward as ripples;
  catching one meant measuring a change thousands of times
  smaller than a proton across four kilometers of vacuum.

### THE SCALE ARITHMETIC, DONE HONESTLY

RULE: shrink the sun to a grapefruit and carry ONE scale factor
  through everything; never mix scales.
WORKED: sun (1.4 million km) → 14 cm grapefruit; factor 10⁻¹⁰.
  Earth: 1.3 mm, a peppercorn 15 m away; the moon a grain 4 cm
  from it — all human travel fits in that 4 cm. The nearest
  star: another grapefruit 4,000 km away — one fruit in Lisbon,
  one in Moscow, nothing between. Space is named correctly.
WORKED (emptiness): between stars ~1 atom per thimble — emptier
  than any pumped vacuum (the lab's best holds thousands); air
  holds ~10¹⁹. Between galaxies, emptier by a huge factor again.
WORKED (age vs size, no contradiction): the universe is ~14
  billion years old and the farthest visible things now sit
  40-plus billion light-years off — their light traveled 14
  billion years while the space behind it kept growing. One
  journey, three honest lengths: then, traveled, now.
WORKED (the first minutes, predicted then checked): for a few
  minutes the whole universe was a fusion reactor; known rates
  at known temperatures yield one recipe and no other — ~25%
  helium by weight, a trace of lithium, a particular pinch of
  heavy hydrogen — worked out BEFORE anyone checked, and held by
  the oldest gas found. Prediction first is the gold standard.

### PHYSICS — the instruments' laws

LAW: a telescope's size buys two separate goods — light gathered
  grows with the opening's AREA, finest detail shrinks as it
  WIDENS; magnification buys neither.
WORKED: an 8 m mirror vs the eye's 8 mm night pupil: (8,000 ÷
  8)² = one million times the light. Advertised magnification
  delivers a bigger, dimmer, blurrier smudge — enlarging a
  record adds nothing that was not recorded.
LAW: sharpness and depth are separable — two dishes far apart,
  signals combined to interfere, resolve like one dish the size
  of the GAP while collecting only their own two areas.
LAW: the air is a shutter (transparent to visible and radio,
  nearly opaque to all else — those skies waited on flight) and
  a wobbling lens: temperature cells bend starlight, so stars
  twinkle, planets (disks, which average it) do not, and a big
  telescope on an ordinary night is no sharper than a small one.
  Remedy: measure the wander hundreds of times a second and bend
  a mirror to cancel it.

## METHODS
How things are done here, and what breaks each.

- FIXING POSITION BY THE SKY: latitude from a star's height,
  longitude from a carried clock, the lines crossed; descendant,
  timed radio from a fleet of controlled perpetual falls — same
  triangle, clocks corrected constantly or the maps drift.
  Breaks in shut weather; then dead reckoning keeps the decaying
  score until the next fix.
- CLIMBING THE DISTANCE LADDER: geometry to the near rungs,
  pulsing stars calibrated on those, fixed-fuse explosions on
  the pulsers, stretched lines on the fuses. Breaks when any
  lower rung shifts — the whole upper ladder moves with it.
- READING THE SPECTRUM STACK: one faint smear, five measurements
  — what, how much, how fast, how squeezed, how magnetized — on
  anything that shines, untouched.
- WEIGHING BY ORBIT: mass shows only in pull, pull only in
  motion — watch two stars orbit and time them. Nearly all known
  stellar masses come from the partnered half, applied to the
  rest; the caveat is carried openly.
- AGING BY BURN-DOWN: the cluster clock (worked in SCIENCE).
- DATING BY CRATERS: count hits, anchor where samples came home,
  rank every surface against it. Breaks on worlds that recycle
  their skin — this one erases its own record.
- THE CROWD SNAPSHOT: where no individual can be followed, a
  population at one instant substitutes for a sequence in time —
  many examples at different stages, argued into order.
- THE NATURAL EXPERIMENT: a cluster is one distance, one age,
  one recipe, differing only in mass — the other variables held
  still for free.
- THE DIFFERENCE MEASUREMENT: as a planet crosses its star, a
  sliver of starlight passes through the planet's air; subtract
  just-before from during and a signal one ten-thousandth of the
  star's light comes out clean. Difference beats absolute
  wherever background swamps effect.
- COMBINING HALF-MEASUREMENTS: dimming gives a planet's width,
  wobble its mass; only BOTH give density — rock vs gas vs
  ocean. A few with both outweigh hundreds with either.
- FINDING THE INVISIBLE BY ITS WORKS: every known black hole was
  found by effects — a star swinging round nothing, gas at X-ray
  fury queuing to fall in, space ringing at a merger, a shadow
  cut from the glow behind. What emits nothing is still
  locatable if the effects are unmistakable.
- PREDICTION FIRST: work out what the model demands before
  looking (the first-minutes recipe; a planet found within one
  degree of a penciled cross). Fitting old data is cheap;
  predicting new data is the coin. The mirror holds this
  method's one famous failure, with its warning.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE NAKED EYE: ~6,000 stars, half a degree of detail, the
  wheel of the year — builder of calendar, compass, and myth;
  its edge behavior is the mirror's canals entry.
- THE SEXTANT AND CHRONOMETER: an angle to one arc-minute plus a
  time to seconds-per-ocean = a position anywhere on earth; the
  pair that made the sea crossable on purpose.
- THE TELESCOPE: area for faintness, width for detail — the
  instrument that turned lights into worlds.
- THE SPECTROGRAPH: the stack-reader; the most information-dense
  instrument the subject owns.
- THE RADIO ARRAY: dishes spread across continents, sharpness
  bought from spacing; it imaged a black hole's shadow.
- THE FLOWN OBSERVATORIES: above the air's shutter; each new
  band showed a sky resembling nothing seen before.
- THE UNDERGROUND TANK: catches neutrinos — the messenger that
  ignores every wall — reporting the sun's core live.
- THE RIPPLE DETECTOR: four kilometers of vacuum measuring a
  stretch thousands of times smaller than a proton; a whole
  science waited on this one instrument.
- THE BROUGHT-HOME SAMPLE: rocks from counted ground anchored
  the crater clock for every still surface in the system.
- THE KEPT RECORD: four centuries of counted sunspots, millennia
  of eclipse dates — instruments made of patience; the ~70 quiet
  years exist only because someone counted through the boring
  part.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- KNOW WHERE YOU ARE: position from an angle and a timepiece —
  free, global, unjammable; the oldest want, still the backup
  when the electronics die.
- KNOW WHEN YOU ARE: hour, season, planting date, eclipse,
  shower — the master clock every built clock copies.
- KNOW WHAT EVERYTHING IS MADE OF: the spectrum names the
  elements of the untouchable; the atoms in a hand are traced to
  dead stars.
- KNOW WHAT WILL HAPPEN OVERHEAD: the sun's steady billions and
  eventual swelling, the shower dates, the spot cycle — the
  calendar of the certain and the cyclic.
- TEST THE LAWS WHERE NO LAB REACHES: matter at city-sized-sun
  density, gravity at its violent extreme, the first minutes.
- FIND OUT IF ANYONE ELSE IS OUT THERE: the question is open
  (the mirror holds it); the tools — planet catalogue,
  atmosphere fingerprint — are built and working.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost.

- The sky first, before writing: calendar, compass, clock for
  every people; monuments aimed at solstice sunrises; planting
  by the wheel.
- The wrong center held ~1,400 years and fell to measurement,
  not argument: phases, moons, loops, and at last the stars'
  seasonal jump (1838). The receipts are the mirror's largest
  entry — the founding lesson in how long a fitting model can
  survive.
- Longitude fell to a clock (1700s): the problem that sank
  fleets was solved by a chronometer that kept true on a heaving
  ship. Position became arithmetic.
- One law from apple to moon: the inverse-square match (worked
  in SCIENCE) united ground and sky under a single rule.
- Helium in sunlight (1868): an element found in the sun before
  the earth — law's address-independence, demonstrated literally.
- The ladder went up rung by rung; the pulser arithmetic proved
  the spiral smudges are other galaxies, and the known universe
  grew a millionfold in a decade.
- The sun's payroll balanced late: no known fire could run it
  (the mirror holds the failed candidates and their damning
  arithmetic); the answer took mass-as-energy plus a quantum
  loophole — two revolutions to light one star.
- The first-minutes recipe, predicted then found — the strongest
  possible order.
- The skies above the air (mid-1900s on), and the ripples caught
  a century after prediction — new senses added to light, each
  showing a sky resembling nothing seen before.
- Worlds around other stars (1990s on): zero to thousands in a
  generation, the catalogue honestly read as a portrait of the
  methods, bias carried in the open.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths. Each names what
runs along it.

- PHYSICS: nearly everything — gravity and orbits, light's laws,
  the furnace, matter at its limits; astronomy is physics given
  the largest laboratory and the longest exposure.
- MATHEMATICS: the deciding triangle, the square laws, the 1.5
  power, compounding error, one journey with three honest
  lengths.
- MEASUREMENT: the ladder as the purest calibration chain, worth
  its worst link; standards proven undrifted; the anchor sample;
  prediction-first.
- CHEMISTRY: the spectrum as fingerprint-without-touching; the
  element factories; the same ~90 kinds everywhere.
- EARTH: tides from difference-in-pull; craters erased by a
  recycling skin; the seasons as orbit read from inside.
- WEATHER: the sun driving all of it; twinkle and haze as the
  air's tax on seeing; forecasting as dead reckoning in the sky;
  the ~70 quiet years standing in climate's records.
- TRANSPORT: navigation whole — sextant, chronometer, dead
  reckoning, the map fleet overhead; the sea crossable on
  purpose because the sky is a free instrument.
- FARMING: the planting calendar; the water-band sliding like a
  growing belt; the cluster aging like a planted stand, thinning
  from the top.
- ENGINEERING: every instrument in MEANS; the governor loop in
  star's gas and in brass; feedback throttling star-making;
  resonance shaking bridges and clearing asteroid gaps alike.
- COMPUTING: machines cooperating across distance must first
  agree what time it is — longitude's problem reborn in the map
  fleet's corrected clocks.
- FLIGHT: the orbit as flight's outer limit — a fall that keeps
  missing, 8 km/s the price of staying up.
- MUSIC: resonance — the note driving the string tuned to answer
  it — running the belt's gaps and Jupiter's chained moons.
- LAW: one rule for all comers or it is not a rule; zero
  longitude as binding agreement (Greenwich, 1884) — arbitrary
  but held, the treaty the maps and time zones run on.
- ECONOMY: valuation pinned to a comparable pinned to another
  (the ladder's shape in money); a large income spent at a far
  larger rate (heavy stars); a boom whose consequences choke it
  (star-formation feedback).
- MEDICINE: the messenger from the unreachable organ (neutrinos
  as blood test); a disease's course assembled from many
  patients at once (the crowd snapshot); the screening test
  shaping what the disease appears to be (survey bias).
- STORY AND BELIEF: the constellations as the oldest shared
  stories, the sky the first common text — and the mirror holds
  where belief claimed more than the sky delivers.
- MIND: the near voice sounding important (brightness's lie);
  the crash imagined where physics gives a pass-through; pattern
  supplied at the eye's limit (the mirror's canals).
- LIFE: the open question of company (the mirror holds it), and
  the working tools pointed at it — the moving water-band, the
  atmosphere fingerprint.
- ANIMALS: migration steered by stars and the sky's compass senses in birds.
- BUILDING: observatories and aligned monuments — building shaped by the sky's angles.
- ELECTRONICS: every modern eye on the sky is a sensor and amplifier; radio astronomy IS electronics.
- PHILOSOPHY: the sky is where knowing-by-prediction was first proven against knowing-by-story.
- PLANTS: the year the stars announce is the planting year; calendars served fields first.
- SURVIVAL: steering by stars strips down to survival's no-instrument navigation.
