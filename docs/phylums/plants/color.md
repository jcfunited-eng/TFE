# PLANTS — THE COLOR
Built 2026-08-30 to the ratified model (fabric/MODEL.md). Seed
knowledge authored by Claude from
docs/fabric_phylums/117_deep_plants_and_fungi.md; growth from here
must trace to what is written. Mirror: docs/phylums/plants/white.md

Two quiet kingdoms that cannot move. One earns by standing in the
light and paying in water; the other earns by growing through its
own dinner. Almost everything below follows from those two facts.

## THINGS
The units this subject is about; other homes named so a cascade
can branch.

- LIGHT — the income, every food chain's first deposit; also:
  physics, astronomy, weather.
- WATER — the currency every purchase is priced in, hundreds of
  molecules per carbon gained; also: physics, weather, earth,
  farming.
- SUGAR — the payroll: what light becomes and what everything
  else — roots, fungi, nodules, fruit, us — is paid in; also:
  cooking, chemistry, body, economy.
- THE LEAF — a flat solar panel with a wet interior: broad
  because capture scales with area, thin because gas crosses only
  tiny distances; also: engineering (the radiator), design.
- STOMATA — gates with muscles: one valve controlling two flows
  at once (carbon in, water out); also: engineering, economy (the
  shop that trades at a loss or shuts).
- ROOTS — the hidden half: anchor, intake, trading floor; one
  rye plant grew ~600 km of root and ~10,000 km of root hairs;
  also: earth, farming, building.
- XYLEM AND PHLOEM — the two pipes: dead plumbing pulled from
  above, living plumbing pushed by pressure; also: engineering
  (two circuits, two pumps), body (arteries and veins, doing
  unrelated work).
- WOOD AND BARK — last year's plumbing kept as scaffolding, a
  graveyard of used pipework holding the new growth up, wrapped
  in a dead skin made from beneath and shed from outside because
  a widening body must replace what it cannot stretch; thick bark
  is for fire; also: building, craft, upkeep, earth.
- THE SEED — three things in one coat: a paused embryo, a packed
  lunch, and a lock; the lock is the clever part; also: farming,
  economy (a bet with a clock), survival.
- THE FLOWER AND ITS POLLEN — an advertisement addressed to one
  reader (the address written in shape, color, scent, opening
  hours), and the mail itself: by courier, or by leaflet drop in
  grams per tree; also: design, animals, economy, transport.
- FRUIT — a bribe with terms: flesh is payment, seed is cargo,
  ripening is the contract; also: cooking, animals, economy.
- GRASS — the plant that grows from its base, so grazing takes
  the finished blade and leaves the works; why lawns and
  grasslands exist; also: farming, animals.
- THE FUNGUS — a net of threads through soil or timber: a body
  that is almost all boundary, no fixed shape, no center; digests
  outside itself; one Oregon honey fungus spans ~9 km² (~); also:
  life, cooking, medicine, earth.
- THE MUSHROOM AND ITS SPORES — not the fungus: a fruiting body
  up in days, gone in a week, while the organism lives on for
  years; picking one is picking an apple. Spores by the billions,
  light enough to ride every breath anyone takes; also: cooking,
  weather, medicine, upkeep.
- THE NODULE — rented housing a legume builds for bacteria that
  break the air's nitrogen; runs only where nitrogen is the
  shortest supply; also: farming, chemistry, economy.
- THE DEFENSIVE CHEMISTRY — the standing and summoned poisons
  that are also our spices and medicines; nothing separates the
  three but dose and target; also: medicine, cooking, conflict.
- LICHEN — fungus and alga in one crust, fed on air and rain;
  the slow crusts used for dating grow ~a millimeter a decade; a
  wall's hundred-year record of its atmosphere; also:
  measurement, earth.
- YEAST — a single-celled fungus with two ways to burn sugar; our
  bread and drink are its leftovers; also: cooking, chemistry.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query.

- A plant is buying carbon with water: the same pore that admits
  one gas releases the other, so purchase and price are one event
  — hundreds of molecules out per carbon in. Its shape, schedule,
  and the map of where it can live all come out of that rate.
- Plants are green because green is the light they do NOT use —
  the pigment takes red and blue and throws the middle back. The
  light under a canopy is the light the canopy refused; the
  understory lives on rejects.
- Afternoon wilting is usually a decision, not a collapse: the
  shop shutting rather than trading itself dry.
- Big leaves cook and small leaves gather little: a broad surface
  sits in a thicker blanket of still air it has warmed, and still
  air insulates — so wet shade grows enormous leaves and deserts
  grow needles, in unrelated plants, everywhere.
- Nothing pumps water to the top of a tree: the column hangs in
  tension from leaf to root, held by water's grip on itself
  (SCIENCE holds the arithmetic and the ceiling it sets).
- The living transport is a thin cylinder under the bark; the
  wood inside is dead. Ring a trunk and the leaves keep drinking
  for weeks while the roots starve — death from the bottom up,
  months later.
- A stump is a bar chart of a life — wide band for a good year,
  pinched for drought, scarred for fire — and every tree in a
  district shares one weather, so the patterns match and chain
  back thousands of years: a record nobody installed and nobody
  can fake.
- A tree does not answer warmth until it has counted enough cold
  — a lock with two keys turned in order, because a mild January
  week is a forgery. Seeds run the same policy: each demands the
  proof its seedling will need — winter's cold, fire or smoke, an
  animal's gut, or light itself, which tells a dust seed it lies
  at the surface and not buried past its own lunch.
- The ground under any field holds thousands of live seeds per
  square meter (~), waiting decades; disturb bare soil and you
  harvest a crop nobody sowed.
- Dispersal buys landings: wind is cheap, far, random; a gut
  arrives where an animal chose to go; ants file the seed beyond
  fire's reach. Worth = distance × odds the landing is
  survivable. And the fruit pays on delivery — hard, bitter, and
  faintly poisonous until the cargo is worth shipping, then
  sweet, soft, and loud.
- Flowering synchronizes on day length, because the planet's tilt
  makes it exactly repeatable and weather is not; temperature
  adjusts the schedule, the calendar sets it. Move a variety a
  few hundred kilometers in latitude and it flowers on the wrong
  week.
- Wind pollination gives up petals, scent, and nectar and pays in
  bulk — and it flowers BEFORE the leaves open, because a canopy
  in leaf is a filter. That is why hay fever starts before the
  woods turn green.
- Most flowers hedge the partner bet: machinery discouraging
  their own pollen plus a quiet fallback permitting it when the
  season closes — a certain small return held behind an uncertain
  larger one.
- Oaks and beeches starve their seed-eaters with lean years, then
  swamp them all together: a variable supply keeps the eater
  permanently behind where a steady one lets it catch up.
- An annual spends everything on seed and dies; a perennial banks
  its gains and tries again. Which wins depends on how long the
  ground stays available — why the weeds of plowed ground are
  annuals almost without exception.
- Light is taken in order of height — no sharing, no pool. A
  plant is built fast-and-cheap for the open or durable for deep
  shade, never both: one budget, opposite pulls. Some skip the
  race and poison the ground instead — walnut and eucalyptus buy
  standing room with chemistry no growing could win.
- A plant that cannot run makes itself expensive to eat: priced
  at the mouth (thorns), the tooth (silica, fiber), or in the gut
  (toxins, blockers). Much of the arsenal is summoned: bitten
  leaves raise their toxins within hours, untouched leaves on the
  same plant follow, and in several species so do neighbors
  downwind (how much of that is "talking" is bounded in the
  mirror).
- A gall is an insect making the plant build it a house — growth
  instructions hijacked; a dozen insects on one oak, a dozen
  different buildings.
- A plant has no fixed body plan and no indispensable part —
  cuttings, coppicing, and hard pruning work where an animal
  would be dead. The top bud suppresses those below chemically;
  cut the tip and they all break — the entire reason pruning
  makes a plant bushier, not smaller.
- The countable unit and the individual differ: an aspen stand
  can be one organism underground, a bamboo species flowers on
  one clock across continents, a strawberry bed is one plant
  wearing many hats.
- A fungus has no mouth: enzymes out, dinner dissolves where it
  lies, result absorbed — it lives inside its meal, and fungal
  growth and rot are one event described twice. The fuzz on a
  loaf is late news of a net already grown through it; cutting
  mold off works only on hard cheese, where threads cannot
  penetrate.
- Without rot the world stops: everything living things need is
  locked inside things already dead; decomposers are the return
  leg of every cycle.
- Most fungi cannot grow at body heat — the thin wall that keeps
  the kingdom that owns the plant world out of a healthy mammal;
  it fails where the body runs cool: skin, nails, feet.
- An outbreak needs host + pathogen + weather at once — an event,
  not a state. One variety everywhere hands the pathogen both
  gifts: every host susceptible, the next never far. Resistance
  bred on a single change is a lease, not a purchase.
- Many plant diseases cannot travel alone — a virus rides in an
  aphid's needle. The carrier is the bottleneck and the target; a
  spray aimed at the virus is aimed at nothing.
- A tree never heals: it walls damage off, abandons everything
  beyond the wall, and carries the wound inside for life.
- A weed is a career, not a kind: up fast, flowering young, seed
  in huge numbers, seed that waits, a taste for bare disturbed
  ground — the bundle a repeatedly cleared site rewards, and
  farming clears sites for a living.

## SCIENCE
The math, physics, and chemistry of plants, held — not mentioned.
LAW to compute with, RULE to follow, WORKED with the arithmetic
run to the answer.

### CHEMISTRY — sunlight into sugar (the planet's payroll)

LAW: light + 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → sugar + 6 O₂; ~30 g of sugar-stuff
  per mole of carbon; dry plant matter burns at ~16-17 kJ/g. Land
  plants bank ~60 billion tonnes of carbon a year in new growth
  (~), oxygen issued as the receipt.
RULE: harvest priced in sunlight — grams of dry matter × ~17 kJ ÷
  the sunlight that fell on its ground = the field's efficiency.
WORKED, one leaf: a crop leaf in full sun fixes ~20 millionths of
  a mole of CO₂ per m² per second: 0.000020 × 30 = 0.0006 g/s;
  × 3,600 = 2.16 g/hour; a 10-hour day at half peak: 2.16 × 10 ×
  0.5 ≈ 11 g of sugar per m² of leaf. Check: 11 × 16 = 176 kJ
  banked against ~20,000 kJ of sun on that square meter — under
  one percent.
WORKED, one field: a strong maize crop, ~20 t dry matter per
  hectare-year: 20,000,000 g × 17 = 3.4 × 10⁸ kJ. Sun: ~16,000
  kJ/m²/day × 365 ≈ 5.9 × 10⁶ kJ/m² × 10,000 m² = 5.9 × 10¹⁰ kJ.
  3.4 × 10⁸ ÷ 5.9 × 10¹⁰ ≈ 0.006 — half a percent. Theoretical
  ceiling ~5-6%; best season-stretches ~2-3. The living world is
  built on that margin.

### CHEMISTRY + PHYSICS — the water price

LAW: the pore's exchange rate is ~300-600 g of water per g of dry
  matter (ordinary C3 plants), ~150-250 for the concentrators
  (C4: maize, sugarcane, tropical grasses). Two workarounds,
  invented many times: concentrate carbon inside so the gates
  stand nearly shut (C4), or open only at night, store carbon as
  acid, work sealed by day (cactus, pineapple).
RULE: water bill = dry matter × exchange rate; for fields, 1 mm
  of water over a hectare = 10 tonnes.
WORKED: the leaf that earned 11 g spent ~11 × 500 = 5,500 g —
  five and a half liters from one square meter of leaf in a day.
  A maize field adding 200 kg/ha on a peak day (C4, ~250):
  200 × 250 = 50,000 kg = 50 t/ha = 5 mm of rain-equivalent —
  exactly what irrigation engineers measure such a field
  drinking.
LAW + WORKED: each gram evaporated carries off ~2,450 J of heat
  at leaf warmth (the schoolbook 2,257 is the price at a boil;
  cooler water costs more to lift into vapor): 5,500 × 2,450 ≈
  13.5 MJ against the leaf's ~20 MJ of sun — most of a leaf's
  sunlight leaves again as vapor. The water bill IS the cooling
  system: one payment, two services.

### PHYSICS — water lifted without a pump

LAW: suction lifts water only as high as the atmosphere pushes:
  101,325 Pa ÷ (1,000 kg/m³ × 9.81) = 10.3 m — every suction
  pump's ceiling. Trees stand ten times taller, so a tree is not
  sucking: the column HANGS in tension, held by water's grip on
  itself — a rope hauled up a well.
LAW: the pull comes free from dry air: pull ≈ 135 × ln(1 ÷
  humidity fraction) MPa at room warmth — at 50% humidity,
  135 × 0.693 ≈ 94 MPa. Soil holds water at
  ~−0.03 MPa; a working leaf runs ~−1 to −2. The leaf-to-air step
  is ~50 times the whole drop through the plant — the sun and the
  dry sky do all the work; the tree's job is keeping the thread
  unbroken.
RULE: tension at the top = height × 0.0098 MPa/m for gravity,
  roughly doubled for pipe friction.
WORKED, the ceiling: a 100 m redwood: 100 × 0.0098 = 0.98 MPa to
  hang the column; with friction ≈ 2 MPa at the top leaf — what
  instruments read in real redwood crowns (~). Near ~2.5 MPa the
  top cells can no longer hold the pressure they need to grow,
  and stretched columns snap: one bubble kills the pipe. Ceiling:
  2.5 ÷ 0.0196 ≈ 128 m — call it ~125-130; the tallest measured
  tree is ~116 m.
  The sky is forbidden by arithmetic, not ambition. A big oak
  moves hundreds of liters a day (~) through pipes it built years
  ago and no longer lives in.
LAW: the second pipe runs the opposite way — sugar loaded into
  living tubes pulls water in by osmosis, and the pressure pushes
  the flow from where sugar is made to where it is wanted. Pull
  cannot move syrup; push can. Two flows, two mechanisms, two
  plumbings — the ringed trunk's slow death falls out of the
  depths they lie at.

### MATHEMATICS — surface, the underground half

LAW: a thread's surface per unit material = 2 ÷ radius — halve
  the thread, double the surface per gram.
WORKED: fine root ~0.2 mm across, fungal thread ~0.004 mm — 50×
  thinner, 50× the absorbing surface for the same material, and
  it enters pores no root can. That ratio is the whole business
  case for the partnership. The hidden half's size: one
  four-month rye plant, grown in a box of soil and measured
  thread by thread, carried ~600 km of root and ~10,000 km of
  root hairs — surface of the order of six hundred square
  meters (~).
THE MARKET RECEIPTS: most plants do not gather their own minerals
  — fungal threads sheathe or enter the roots and trade reach for
  sugar. Audited: plants pay ~10-20% of everything photosynthesis
  earns to fungal partners (~); the fungi deliver up to
  ~three-quarters of the phosphorus (~). Carbon labeled in the
  leaf shows up in the fungus, phosphorus labeled in the soil
  shows up in the plant, and in controlled trials the fungus
  delivers more to roots that pay more (lab conditions; the
  mirror bounds the metaphor). Ancient, nearly universal,
  entirely unseen.

### CHEMISTRY — the nitrogen bill

LAW: the air is four-fifths nitrogen, welded shut — the triple
  bond costs ~945 kJ/mol; the bacterial enzyme spends ~16 ATP per
  molecule; delivered, fixed nitrogen costs the host ~6-12 g of
  sugar-carbon per gram.
RULE: the nodule pays only where soil nitrogen is the shortest
  supply — and is quietly shut down where the ground is rich.
WORKED: a clover field fixing ~150 kg N/ha-year pays ~150 × 8 =
  1,200 kg of carbon — a seventh of its ~8-9 t annual carbon
  earnings, handed to the tenants. The factory alternative runs
  at ~450°C and ~200 atmospheres and eats ~1-2% of world energy;
  the bacteria do the same chemistry at soil temperature, on the
  plant's tab. Roughly half the nitrogen atoms in a fed human
  body now come through the factory (~); the crop that could skip
  both bills is the mirror's biggest want.

### MATHEMATICS — light in a crowd

LAW: light through a canopy dies exponentially — fraction
  reaching the floor ≈ e^(−k × L), L the leaf layers above,
  k ≈ 0.5.
WORKED: 4 layers: e^(−2) = 0.135 — 13.5% gets through. 6 layers:
  e^(−3) = 5%. 8: e^(−4) = 1.8% — pressing the ~1% floor a
  deep-shade leaf needs to break even. This is why height in a
  crowd is worth more than health, and why the two leaf careers
  are both forced moves.

### MATHEMATICS — the clocks and the bets

LAW (the chill bank): a temperate tree counts hours between
  ~0-7°C; only a filled bank (200-1,200 hours by variety) arms
  the warmth trigger.
WORKED: a 900-hour apple in a 400-hour winter never properly
  wakes; a 300-hour peach in a 1,200-hour winter arms by
  December, opens in the first thaw, and loses its blossom to
  March. Matching number to district is the art of siting an
  orchard.
LAW (the seed bet): an annual weed makes ~10,000 seeds of which,
  long-run, ~1 replaces the parent — 1-in-10,000 odds answered by
  volume, the lock, and the bank spreading tries across decades.
  The clock's reach: a ~2,000-year-old date palm seed has
  germinated; a lotus at ~1,300 (~).
LAW (masting): an eater's numbers track its food with a lag.
  Model: 100 kg of acorns yearly feeds a standing population that
  eats nearly all; the same 400 kg dealt 10, 10, 10, 370 starves
  the eaters three years and swamps the survivors — same total,
  far more seed through.
LAW (the lease): ~10¹¹-10¹² spores a region-season meets a
  one-in-10⁸ mutation somewhere every year, so one-gene
  resistance breaks in ~3-7 years of wide planting (the
  wheat-rust record). Stack two independent genes: 10⁻⁸ × 10⁻⁸ =
  10⁻¹⁶ — beyond a season's tries. The arithmetic IS the breeding
  strategy.
LAW (the outbreak AND): three independent conditions at 1-in-5
  each in a fortnight multiply to 1 in 125 — an outbreak is an
  event, and forecasting plant disease is mostly forecasting
  humidity.

### CHEMISTRY — the fungal kingdom's own

LAW: digestion is the same chemistry in a stomach and a log; the
  container is the only difference.
LAW (lignin): wood's armor has no regular repeat, so no single
  enzyme attacks it — it takes a whole apparatus, barely at a
  profit. Hence leaves vanish in months, logs last years, and in
  a long stretch of the past the undigested backlog became coal
  (the mirror audits that story). The same no-repeat logic is why
  some plastics persist.
RULE (keeping timber): rot needs water — below ~20% moisture the
  digesters stop; dry timber under a roof lasts centuries (stave
  churches ~900 years old still stand).
LAW (yeast's two routes): with air, sugar burns fully for ~30 ATP
  per glucose; sealed, the poor route keeps 2 and leaves 2
  alcohol + 2 CO₂ — by weight 180 g of sugar → 92 g alcohol +
  88 g gas. The alcohol still holds most of the sugar's burnable
  energy (why it lights); bread's rise and beer's kick are one
  organism's two waste products, collected (cooking holds the
  recipes).
LAW (the temperature wall): most fungi stop growing above ~35°C;
  a mammal's 37 is uninhabitable however nutritious. Skin and
  feet run ~30-34 and pay for it.

## METHODS
How things are done here — what governs each, what breaks it.

- SOWING (satisfy the lock, respect the lunch): give the seed its
  proof — damp cold, a scratched coat, smoke, or surface light by
  kind. Depth is priced by the lunch: ~2-3 times the seed's own
  width; a big seed funds a long climb, a dust seed funds none.
- CUTTINGS, DIVISION, RUNNERS (copy the modular body): a plant
  with no indispensable part rebuilds from a piece. Breaks where
  the copy meets a disease that beats one genome — identical
  copies share one weakness (the mirror holds the banana).
- PRUNING AND COPPICING (use the tip's tyranny): cut the top and
  the suppressed buds all break — prune to make bushy, coppice to
  harvest poles forever from one root estate.
- GRAFTING (join the plumbing): set the thin living cylinder of
  one plant against the same layer of another and bind; the pipes
  fuse, one plant's roots feed another's fruit. Breaks when the
  living layers miss or the partners are distant kin.
- MOWING AND GRAZING (cut where the factory isn't): grass grows
  from the base, so cutting takes the finished blade and leaves
  the works; what grows from tips dies of the same treatment.
- TRAINING LIGHT (plant by the exponent): dense planting forces
  straight bare trunks racing up (timber); wide spacing grows low
  spreading crowns (fruit); thinning resets the race on purpose.
- ROTATION WITH THE FIXERS (rent the nodule): grow the legume on
  poor ground, let it pay the nitrogen bill, turn it under, take
  the cereal's crop after — it works BECAUSE the partnership runs
  where nitrogen is short.
- DEFEATING DISEASE (break one leg of three): resistant varieties
  (a lease — rotate and stack), mixed and spaced plantings
  (distance and difference put back), sanitation, vector control
  (kill the courier, not the letter), sprays timed to the humid
  window, not the calendar.
- WOUND CARE FOR TREES (help the wall, not the hole): cut just
  outside the branch collar, paint nothing, leave the rest to the
  tree's own compartments (the century of tar is the mirror's).
- READING RINGS: match a living tree's pattern to older dead
  wood, overlap by overlap, back thousands of years; every
  neighbor holds an independent copy — that is what makes the
  archive trustworthy.
- KEEPING THINGS FROM THE KINGDOM THAT EATS EVERYTHING: deny
  water (dry timber, dry grain), deny warmth (the cold chain),
  deny time (harvest before the spore's weather) — cooking's
  water-denial law, aimed at fungi.
- HARVESTING THE FUNGUS (pick apples, not trees): take the
  fruiting body, leave the organism; grow the edible kinds by
  giving the net its meal and the fruiting its damp, cool
  trigger.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE SUN — ~1,000 W/m² at full noon, ~5 kWh/m²/day where crops
  grow (~); the only income, everything else a cost against it.
- SOIL — the marketplace itself: mineral grain, returning dead
  matter, the water film, the seed bank, the fungal net threading
  it (hundreds of meters of thread per gram, ~).
- WATER — rain or the pipe; a summer maize field drinks ~5 mm a
  day; irrigation is settling the plant's bill for it.
- THE FREE INSTRUMENTS — day length (the calendar no warm spell
  can fake), the chill bank (winter's own counter), the rings
  (the archive nobody installs).
- FERTILIZER — the nitrogen bill paid retail, at ~450°C and 200
  atmospheres; cheap nitrogen is exactly what shuts the nodule
  down.
- THE GREENHOUSE — the conditions moved to the plant: chill, day
  length, and damp made adjustable, every sowing lock picked
  deliberately.
- THE SEED PACKET AND THE VAULT — the bet stored dry and cold to
  multiply its wait; the world's varieties held against the
  monoculture arithmetic.
- THE LABELED ATOM — carbon and phosphorus tagged and traced: the
  instrument that turned the underground market from story into
  receipts.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- FEED EVERYTHING: sunlight into sugar at half a percent, and
  nearly every mouth on the planet paid from it, directly or a
  few steps removed.
- BUILD, CLOTHE, BURN: wood for the frame, fiber for the cloth,
  fuel for the fire; coal draws down the oldest account.
- HEAL AND SEASON: the defensive chemistry cabinet — spice,
  medicine, poison, one shelf sorted by dose and target; and the
  soil's ancient battlefield where the antibiotics were found,
  not invented.
- KEEP THE GROUND AND THE AIR: roots hold soil against rain,
  canopies hold water in a landscape; the oxygen of every breath
  is photosynthesis's receipt.
- READ THE PAST: rings for climate and date, lichens for the air,
  pollen in mud for vanished forests — instruments nobody had to
  install.
- MAKE MORE FROM LITTLE: the grower's door — seed to crop,
  cutting to orchard, the payroll compounded by care.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost.

- The partnership predates the shops: plants came onto land with
  fungi already attached (~400 million years — the threads show
  in the earliest land-plant fossils). The underground market is
  older than roots.
- Wood arrived before its digesters could profit, and the backlog
  became coal — a ledger entry written by a chemical stalemate
  (the mirror holds the modern audit).
- Grasses and grazers made each other, late: the plant that grows
  from its base and the animal that mows it selected each other
  into grasslands and every herd economy.
- Flowers arrived late and took over fast — the timing is the
  mirror's famous open question — and the fruit contract and the
  addressed advertisement rewired the animal world into couriers.
- Farming began as the selection of weed careers: the plow clears
  ground for a living and the weed's bundle was half the crop's
  job description. The detox lore for poisonous wild ancestors
  was paid in poisonings (cooking holds the steps; the mirror
  holds the price).
- The potato famine wrote the monoculture arithmetic in lives:
  one cloned variety across a country, one pathogen, two wet
  summers — ~a million dead, ~a million fled. The vaults and
  mixed plantings are that receipt, institutionalized.
- The nodule was demoted the way salt was: factory nitrogen
  (early 1900s) made the rented fixers optional wherever
  fertilizer is cheap — the method stays, superior again wherever
  the factory's price returns.
- Penicillin, 1928-1940s: a mold's weapon picked off a
  battlefield hundreds of millions of years old; resistance was
  ancient before we arrived, which is why the leases keep
  expiring.
- The rings were chained — living tree to barn beam to bog oak —
  and the chain then calibrated carbon dating itself: one archive
  certifying another.
- The talking-tree arc ran in living memory: ridiculed, proven
  small, over-claimed, audited back down — filed in the mirror as
  the cleanest recent case of a claim finding its true size.

- WEATHER: the outbreak's third leg; drought written into rings;
  wind as pollen and spore courier.
- ASTRONOMY: day length from the planet's tilt — the one signal
  no warm spell can counterfeit.
- EARTH: soil as the marketplace floor; coal as wood's unpaid
  ledger; the carbon cycle's biggest flux runs through the leaf's
  open pore.
- ECONOMY: the phylum's grammar — forced prices, outsourcing for
  reach, in-house vs buying dear, dividend vs reinvestment,
  irregular supply defeating planners, security spending as a
  drag on output.
- ENGINEERING: one valve, two flows; the buffer tank; two
  circuits with different pumps; the governor whose removal frees
  everything; the scaffold kept as structure.
- BUILDING: wood itself; containing a failure instead of
  repairing it; cladding renewed, not maintained.
- ANIMALS: couriers, grazers, pollinators, gall-makers, seed
  thieves — the kingdom this one hires, bribes, poisons, and made
  grasslands with.
- COMPUTING: the gall as a hijacked build program; monoculture as
  one system everywhere catching one cold; mycelium as a network
  with no central node.
- MEASUREMENT: rings, lichens, pollen cores — instruments
  installed by nobody; the labeled atom as auditor.
- CONFLICT: denied ground, imposed cost, feint and mass, the
  resistance arms race.
- GOVERNANCE: apical dominance — the hierarchy where removing the
  head frees every rival at once.
- LAW: define the entity before counting it — the aspen stand and
  the strawberry bed, standing exhibits.
- TRANSPORT: seed dispersal as the parcel service — leaflet drop,
  addressed courier, ant-post to the fireproof archive.
- UPKEEP: rot as processing, not spoilage; the visible patch as
  late news; dry timber; the lichen's slow gauge lagging what it
  gauges.
- STORY: the talking trees — the tales against the receipts; the
  honest split is the mirror's.
- TEACHING: the seed's lock as a prerequisite that genuinely is
  one; the rings as knowledge that outlives its holders because
  it is written.
