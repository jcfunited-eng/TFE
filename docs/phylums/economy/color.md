## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- SCARCITY — the first fact: never enough of everything for
  everyone at once; the whole subject is the management of this;
  also lives in: farming (the harvest is finite), weather (drought),
  life (every organism budgets), physics (nothing from nothing).
- WANTS — the engine: what people reach for, endless where goods
  are not; also: the body (hunger first), culture (wants learned
  from neighbors), medicine.
- GOODS AND SERVICES — what answers a want: bread, shelter, a
  mended tooth, an hour of another's work; also: cooking, farming,
  engineering, the body.
- WORK — human effort sold and bought; the original source of
  everything made; also: the body (calories out), engineering
  (machines multiplying it), culture.
- CAPITAL — tools and stores that make tomorrow's work yield more
  than today's: the plow, the mill, the seed corn deliberately not
  eaten; also: farming, engineering, cooking (the stocked pantry).
- LAND AND NATURE'S GIFTS — what no one made and everyone needs:
  soil, water, ore, fish; also: farming, weather, life, chemistry.
- MONEY — claim tickets on goods; bookkeeping made portable; not
  wealth itself but wealth's receipt; also: mathematics (pure
  number), culture (a shared agreement), chemistry (the metal
  forms and their assay).
- PRICE — the number a trade happens at; news about scarcity
  traveling as a figure; also: mathematics, culture (what a price
  is allowed to say).
- THE MARKET — any place, walled or wireless, where offers meet
  and prices form; also: culture, engineering (the exchange's
  machinery).
- TRADE — the swap both sides walk away from better off; also:
  culture (trust between strangers), engineering (the ships and
  roads that carry it), weather (what each place can grow).
- DEBT AND CREDIT — spending moved backward in time, saving moved
  forward; a promise with a schedule; also: mathematics
  (compounding), culture (the promise's force), medicine (its
  stress is bodily).
- INTEREST — the price of time: what borrowing tomorrow's money
  today costs; also: mathematics (the exponent), life (yeast pays
  the same law in cells).
- THE GRANARY — stored wealth in its oldest form: goods moved
  through time; also: cooking (preservation is its chemistry),
  farming (the harvest it holds), weather (the lean year it
  answers).
- RISK — the maybe that can ruin: fire, drought, shipwreck, the
  price turning; also: mathematics (probability), weather, life,
  medicine.
- INFORMATION — who knows what, and when: the invisible good most
  trades secretly price; also: mathematics, culture.
- THE HOUSEHOLD AND THE FIRM — the two working units: one budgets
  to live, the other organizes work to sell; also: culture, the
  body, engineering.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- Scarcity is the first law: at any moment wants exceed means, so
  every choice spends something unseen — the next-best thing not
  chosen. The real cost of anything is what was given up to get it,
  and it is paid whether or not it is counted.
- Price is a signal, not a decree: a number carrying news about
  scarcity. Changing the number by order changes the messenger,
  never the fact. Condition: the signal is honest only where buying
  and selling are free; a decreed price reports the decree.
- A rising price gives two orders at once with no one commanding:
  it tells users "use less, find substitutes" and tells makers
  "bring more, it pays now" — the same number read from both sides.
- Anything growing by a fixed share of itself doubles on a
  schedule — savings, debts, prices, populations, schemes. The same
  law that raises dough raises a mortgage (see SCIENCE); it does
  not care what it doubles or whom it serves.
- You cannot spend what does not exist: money is claim tickets on
  goods, and printing tickets bakes no loaves. More tickets against
  the same loaves changes only the number on the loaf (the dilution
  arithmetic in SCIENCE).
- A free trade enriches both sides at the moment of the deal, or it
  would not happen: each walks away holding what they value more.
  Conditions: both informed, neither forced; fraud and hidden
  defects break the claim, which is why honest weights and courts
  are economic machinery, not decoration.
- Trade pays even when one side is better at EVERYTHING. The gain
  runs on differing costs-in-goods-forgone, not on who is best —
  the least obvious theorem this subject owns, worked in SCIENCE.
- Dividing work multiplies output — not by a little (the pin
  factory's 240× is worked in SCIENCE). Condition: only where a
  market is wide enough to absorb the output; a village cannot
  support eighteen pin trades.
- Storage is wealth: goods moved through the calendar from glut to
  scarcity. Conditions: only while the good keeps, and only while
  the price gap beats storage plus spoilage — which is why grain
  and salt became money and lettuce never did.
- People do more of what pays them and less of what costs them, at
  the edges, whatever the poster on the wall says. Every rule,
  tax, and subsidy is also a set of incentives, and the incentives
  are what actually ship.
- What is spent is spent: only costs and gains still ahead can
  steer a choice. Money sunk argues loudly and counts for nothing
  — finishing a bad project to honor it spends more.
- Profit above the ordinary calls in imitators until it fades to
  ordinary — the fading is the signal completing its work; the
  extraordinary gain was the wage for arriving first. (Its hard
  mirror — why public prediction eats itself — is in the white.)
- Many small certain payments can replace one crushing maybe: the
  pooling law (worked in SCIENCE). Condition: the blows must land
  independently; what strikes everyone at once cannot be pooled
  from inside the group.
- Falling prices make debts heavier: a debt is fixed in tickets,
  so when tickets grow dearer the debt grows in goods — dilution
  arithmetic run backward, worked in SCIENCE.
- Where a decree forces a good coin and a debased coin to trade as
  equals, the good coin vanishes into hoards and the bad one does
  the shopping. Condition: the decree IS the condition; free to
  price both, people discount the bad one instead.

## SCIENCE
The math of the subject, held — not mentioned. Each piece is a LAW
a query can compute with, a RULE it can follow, and where it earns
it, a WORKED case proving the rule runs.

### MATHEMATICS — compounding, the law that works both ways

LAW: a sum growing at rate r for t periods becomes
  A = P × (1 + r)^t.
  Doubling time ≈ 72 ÷ (rate in percent). The honest number is 69
  (from the logarithm of 2); 72 is used because it divides evenly,
  and it runs truest near 8%.
RULE: to project any steady growth — divide 72 by the percent rate
  for the doubling time; count the doublings that fit; raise 2 to
  that power.
WORKED, the saver's side: 1,000 at 8%: 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years to double
  (exactly: 1.08^9 = 1.999 — the rule's promise kept). Left 36
  years = 4 doublings = 16×: about 15,970. The same 1,000 at 8%
  SIMPLE interest earns 1,000 × 0.08 × 36 = 2,880, ending at
  3,880 — compounding quadruples the simple result because growth
  itself grows.
WORKED, the debtor's side, digit by digit: 1,000 owed at 20% and
  ignored: 1,200 → 1,440 → 1,728 → 2,073.60. Four years, more
  than doubled. Same law, sign of care reversed — this is yeast's
  doubling arithmetic (cooking holds it on dough) running on a
  ledger instead of a loaf.
WORKED, time as the ingredient: a coin saved 40 years at 8% grows
  1.08^40 ≈ 21.7×; saved 20 years, 1.08^20 ≈ 4.7×. The early coin
  outweighs the late one 4.7 to 1 — when you start matters more
  than how much.

### MATHEMATICS — moving value across time

LAW: a future sum is worth less today by the same exponent run
  backward: PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)^t.
RULE: to price a promise — halve it once per doubling-time of the
  going rate between now and its date.
WORKED: 1,000 promised in 9 years, rates at 8%: 1,000 ÷ 1.999 ≈
  500 today. Promised in 18 years: ≈ 250. This is why a pension, a
  bond, and a tree not yet grown all have exact prices today, and
  why a distant promise is cheap to make and dear to keep.

LAW: the asked interest rate is built from parts — payment for
  waiting, expected dilution, and risk — and the honest join is
  multiplication: (1 + real) × (1 + inflation) − 1.
WORKED: a lender wanting 3% real, expecting 5% dilution:
  1.03 × 1.05 = 1.0815 → asks 8.15% (the quick add, 3 + 5 = 8,
  is the rough form). Read backward: a quoted rate minus expected
  inflation reveals what waiting truly pays.

### THE DILUTION ARITHMETIC — inflation held as bookkeeping

LAW: with spending habits and goods unchanged, prices follow the
  ticket count: each ticket buys goods ÷ tickets.
WORKED: an island with 1,000 coins and 500 loaves a season, every
  coin spent once: 2 coins per loaf. Double the coins to 2,000,
  loaves unchanged: 4 per loaf. The loaves did not multiply; the
  tickets did. Condition carried: if loaves grow with the coins,
  no dilution — the matching is the whole difference.
RULE: to find a real change — divide the money change by the price
  change: real = (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1.
WORKED: wages up 5%, prices up 8%: 1.05 ÷ 1.08 = 0.972 — about
  2.8% POORER despite the bigger number on the pay slip.
WORKED, the pension: inflation at 7% doubles prices in 72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10
  years (exactly 1.07^10 ≈ 1.97) — a pension fixed in tickets buys
  half its groceries a decade on. Dilution is a tax on whoever
  holds tickets longest.
WORKED, the law backward (deflation): a farmer borrows 1,000 when
  wheat sells at 1.00 — the debt is 1,000 bushels. Wheat falls to
  0.50: the same paper now demands 2,000 bushels. Nothing was
  signed, and the debt doubled in the only unit the farm can pay.
Receipts of the law at full speed stand in the mirror's ledgers.

### THE TRADE ARITHMETIC — both sides gain, worked to the loaf

LAW: what a thing costs its maker is the other goods the same
  effort would have made. Trade pays whenever those costs differ,
  regardless of who is better at what.
WORKED, complete: a farmer's day makes 8 loaves or 4 fish — her
  fish costs her 2 loaves. A fisher's day makes 2 loaves or 2 fish
  — his fish costs him 1 loaf. She beats him at BOTH trades; he is
  still the cheaper fisherman in loaves forgone.
  · Apart, each splitting the day: she gets 4 loaves + 2 fish, he
    gets 1 loaf + 1 fish. Together: 5 loaves, 3 fish.
  · Arranged — she bakes ¾ of her day (6 loaves) and fishes ¼
    (1 fish); he only fishes (2 fish). Together: 6 loaves, 3 fish.
    One loaf appeared from arrangement alone; no one worked harder.
  · Trading 1 fish at 1½ loaves: she ends with 4½ loaves + 2 fish,
    he ends with 1½ loaves + 1 fish — each half a loaf better than
    alone. The price had to land between 1 and 2 loaves per fish:
    each side's own forgone-cost is its walk-away line, and the
    band between the lines is where every voluntary price lives.
RULE: to find your trade — compare not skills but costs in goods
  forgone; sell what costs you least, buy what costs you most.

### THE DIVISION OF LABOR — the pin factory, worked

LAW: output per worker multiplies when work is split into steps,
  by practice at one motion, no time lost changing tasks, and
  steps simple enough to hand to a machine.
WORKED (the recorded case, 1776): ten workers, the pin split into
  ~18 operations — one draws the wire, another straightens, a
  third cuts, a fourth points — made ~48,000 pins a day: 4,800
  each. Alone, each could make perhaps 20, maybe not one. The
  multiplier: 4,800 ÷ 20 = 240×.
CONDITION, carried with the law: 48,000 pins a day must find
  buyers — division is limited by the market's reach, which is why
  better roads and ships deepen specialization everywhere they
  touch, and why the remote village keeps its one smith who does
  everything.

### THE CROSSING — where price settles, and what a gap costs

LAW: buyers take less at higher prices; sellers bring more. The
  price settles where the two amounts cross. Held above the
  crossing: glut (unsold goods push it down). Held below: shortage
  (empty shelves pull it up) — unless something pins it.
WORKED: a market where buyers want 100 − 2P and sellers bring 3P:
  crossing at 100 − 2P = 3P → P = 20, and 60 units change hands.
  · Price pinned at 25: wanted 50, brought 75 — glut of 25.
  · Price pinned at 10: wanted 80, brought 30 — shortage of 50.
RULE: to read any queue or glut — the line out the door says the
  posted price is below the crossing; the warehouse of unsold
  goods says it is above. The market is read like a thermometer.
WORKED, what a pinned price is paid in: pinned at 10 against a
  crossing of 20, the missing 10 is still paid — in waiting. At a
  wage of 15 an hour, 40 minutes in the queue is 10 paid in time,
  invisible on the sticker. (The decree tried against this law,
  and its receipts, stand certified in the mirror.)

### THE STORAGE ARITHMETIC — the granary as a savings account

LAW: storage profit = (later price − earlier price) × what
  survives − storage cost. Spoilage compounds against the keeper;
  preservation (cooking's chemistry) is what slows it.
WORKED: 1,000 bushels bought at harvest for 2.00 each (2,000
  paid); storage costs 300; damp and pests take 5%, leaving 950.
  Break-even price: 2,300 ÷ 950 ≈ 2.42. Spring price 3.00: revenue
  2,850, gain 550 — the pay for moving grain four months forward.
  And the storer smooths the year for everyone: his harvest buying
  lifts the glut price, his spring selling caps the lean price —
  he is paid for flattening the calendar.
RULE: to judge any keeping — the expected gap must beat storage
  plus spoilage plus the interest the tied-up money forgoes; salt,
  smoke, and cold (cooking's whole preservation shelf) are, in
  this subject's terms, machines for widening what can be wealth.

### THE POOLING LAW — insurance as arithmetic

LAW: expected loss = chance of the blow × size of the blow. Many
  independent maybes average out; the pool's cost per member
  approaches the expected loss.
WORKED: 200 houses worth 10,000 each; one burns a year. Expected
  loss per house: 10,000 ÷ 200 = 50 a year. A premium of ~60
  covers the burn and the bookkeeping. Alone, each family faces
  ruin; together they face arithmetic.
CONDITIONS, hard: blows must land independently — the flood that
  takes all 200 at once collects 200 claims against one year's
  premiums, which is why floods and wars sit outside ordinary
  policies; and the pool must price carelessness or the careless
  fill it (the reckless burn more often than the average charged).

### THE GROWTH LAW — compounding run on a whole people

LAW: output per person compounds like any other sum.
WORKED: over one working life (~40 years), 2% a year grows
  1.02^40 ≈ 2.2× — a doubling of daily bread in a lifetime. At 7%
  (a catching-up country's pace): 1.07^40 ≈ 15×. Children in the
  fast case retire in a different material world than their birth;
  the gap between 2 and 7 is the largest quiet number in the
  subject. (What growth CANNOT be — perpetual, input-free — is
  certified in the mirror.)

### THE ASSAY — chemistry keeping the coin honest

LAW: metals declare themselves by density; mixing cannot hide it.
  Gold ~19.3 g/cm³, silver ~10.5.
WORKED (the bath that caught the goldsmith): a 1,000 g crown of
  pure gold displaces 1,000 ÷ 19.3 ≈ 51.8 cm³ of water. Cut to
  700 g gold + 300 g silver: 700 ÷ 19.3 + 300 ÷ 10.5 ≈ 36.27 +
  28.57 = 64.84 cm³ — thirteen extra cm³ of water, and the fraud is
  a number. Money works only while verifiable; the assay, the
  scale, and honest weights are where chemistry underwrites trust.

## METHODS
How things are done here — each with what governs it, when it
applies, and what breaks it. The prepositioned how-to-build.

- BUDGETING (the household's first method): spending held at or
  under income plus deliberately drawn stores. Governed by the
  bookkeeping claim — you cannot spend what does not exist,
  only borrow it into a debt that compounds. Breaks when: the gap
  is bridged by borrowing at rates above what anything bought
  earns.
- SAVING FIRST (the doubling law run forward): set aside before
  spending, early over large — the 4.7-to-1 arithmetic of the
  early coin. Breaks when: the rate earned sits below dilution
  (the real return is negative and the "saving" quietly leaks).
- BORROWING WELL: match the loan's length to the life of what it
  buys (harvest loans to seasons, house loans to decades); never
  at a rate above what the bought thing earns or saves. Breaks
  when: compounding runs against you unwatched — the 20% ledger
  in SCIENCE is four years from doubling.
- SPECIALIZING AND TRADING: find what costs you least in goods
  forgone, do more of it, trade for the rest — the farmer-fisher
  arithmetic made a habit. Breaks when: the market cannot absorb
  the output, or the single specialty's demand dies (the buggy-whip
  hazard — specialization is exposure, diversify the household
  even when the trade is narrow).
- PRICE-READING: treat prices, queues, and gluts as instruments,
  not opinions — the line says too-cheap, the warehouse says
  too-dear, the widening gap between two towns says a cart can
  earn its road. Breaks when: prices are pinned or decreed
  (the instrument then reports the decree).
- HAGGLING AND AUCTION (price discovery where none is posted):
  the deal lands in the band between the two walk-away lines;
  auction finds the band's edge with many buyers at once. Breaks
  when: one side knows the walk-away line of the other, or bidders
  collude into one buyer.
- STORING AND PRESERVING: buy the glut, hold, sell the scarcity —
  under the granary arithmetic's ceiling (gap must beat storage +
  spoilage + forgone interest). The smokehouse, the cellar, and
  the salt barrel are savings accounts denominated in food.
  Breaks when: the good will not keep, or everyone stores at once
  and the spring gap never opens.
- POOLING AND SPREADING: never carry one ruinous maybe when many
  small independent ones can be carried instead — insure the
  house, vary the crops, split the cargo between ships. Governed
  by the pooling law. Breaks when: the maybes are secretly one
  maybe (all ships in one storm's sea, all savings in one
  employer's stock).
- DOUBLE-ENTRY BOOKKEEPING: every event written twice, where value
  came from and where it went; the columns must agree, so errors
  announce themselves — how commerce scaled past one owner's
  memory. Breaks when: the books record what should have happened
  rather than what did; the method then certifies fiction.
- CONTRACTING: the promise written, witnessed, and enforceable —
  which lets strangers trade across months and oceans, where
  memory and kinship cannot reach. Breaks when: courts are slow,
  bought, or absent; trade then shrinks back to kin and cash on
  the barrel.
- APPRAISING THE FUTURE (the present-value method): bring every
  future gain and cost back to today by the discount rule, add
  them up, act if the sum is positive. Governs wells, orchards,
  educations, and machines alike. Breaks when: the rate is
  guessed to flatter the answer, or the far payoffs are invented
  — the method computes exactly and only what is fed to it.
- THRIFT (stretch, mend, use everything): the method shared with
  cooking's stew-and-stock cookbook — waste nothing, because
  everything not wasted is income that needed no earning. Breaks
  when: the hour spent saving a penny costs a wage above it —
  thrift is also priced.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- MONEY'S FORMS, in the order found: goods themselves (salt,
  grain — money you could eat, spoilage its weakness); metal by
  weight, then coin (the stamp saving the weighing, the assay
  auditing the stamp); paper standing for metal; and ledger
  entries standing for everything — today most money is book
  entries, and the paper is its small change.
- THE LEDGER AND THE BOOKS: the memory of who owes whom; with
  double-entry, a memory that checks itself. The oldest writing
  on earth is this means — tallies of grain and sheep.
- THE MARKETPLACE AND THE EXCHANGE: where offers concentrate so
  prices can form; a bazaar and a wired exchange are the same
  instrument at different speeds — many meeting, numbers forming.
- THE BANK: matchmaker of savers and borrowers — takes deposits,
  keeps a fraction ready (100 depositors of 100 each: holds
  ~1,000, lends ~9,000), lives on the spread. Works while
  withdrawals stay near the average; breaks when all doors are
  tried at once — the run, a pooling failure answered where
  practiced by pooling the banks themselves under a guarantee.
- THE GRANARY, WAREHOUSE, AND SALT BARREL: wealth's containers
  through time; their engineering sets the spoilage rate that the
  storage arithmetic charges.
- ROADS AND SHIPS: distance turned into a cost, and every cost
  drop widens what is tradable — trade happens where the price
  gap between two places beats the carriage between them; cheap
  carriage is why the same goods now cost nearly the same
  everywhere reachable.
- WEIGHTS, MEASURES, AND THE ASSAY: the honesty instruments —
  the scale is truth here as in the kitchen; the density bath
  audits the coin; standard measures are why strangers can trade
  without weighing each other's words.
- TITLE, CONTRACT, AND COURT: ownership written down and
  enforceable. What cannot be proven owned cannot be sold,
  insured, or borrowed against — unwritten wealth is wealth that
  cannot work.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- PROVISION AGAINST SCARCITY: feed, house, and warm from what is
  never enough — the founding purpose, shared with farming and
  cooking at the two ends of the same bread.
- COORDINATE STRANGERS: millions fed daily by no one in charge —
  the price system as the only known machine that runs a
  civilization's provisioning without a commander, moving the
  news of every scarcity to everyone who can act on it.
- CARRY VALUE THROUGH TIME: from harvest to hunger, from working
  years to old age — the granary, the saving, the pension; the
  purpose the storage and compounding laws serve.
- SPREAD THE BLOWS: turn ruin-sized maybes into premium-sized
  certainties — the pooling purpose.
- FUND WHAT DOES NOT YET EXIST: gather scattered savings into a
  mill, a ship, a well — the appraising method's purpose, and the
  door by which the future is built out of the present's surplus.
- MAKE LITTLE GO FAR: thrift's door, shared openly with cooking —
  stretch, keep, waste nothing; the purpose that wrote both the
  stockpot and the ledger.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Writing was born of bookkeeping: the oldest tablets are tallies
  of grain and sheep, not poems — accounting is older than
  literature, and the ledger is the reason letters exist.
- Goods were money first: salt and grain paid workers (the word
  "salary" still carries the salt ration in it); storable,
  divisible, wanted by all — the properties were discovered by
  use, long before they were named.
- The granary state: the first treasuries were grain against the
  lean years — storage as policy is as old as the first cities,
  and the seven-fat-years story is its oldest written manual.
- Coinage, ~600 BC: the stamp that saved every market its weighing
  — and within generations, rulers thinning the metal behind the
  stamp; the assay and the debasement arrived nearly together
  (the mirror holds the debasement ledger).
- Double-entry, spreading through merchant houses by the 1300s and
  codified in 1494: books that check themselves let ventures grow
  past one owner's memory and lifetime — a bookkeeping trick that
  scaled commerce more than any ship.
- The plague repriced labor: with workers suddenly scarce, real
  wages roughly doubled in the decades after the Black Death —
  scarcity pricing demonstrated on the species itself, against
  every decree that tried to hold wages down (the decrees failed;
  the mirror notes why).
- 1776, the pin factory written down: the division of labor was
  ancient practice; the arithmetic of it — 240× — was finally put
  on paper, and with it the market-extent condition.
- 1817, trade's theorem proven: gain from trade even for the
  worse-at-everything partner — the least intuitive law here,
  which is why it was proven late and is re-doubted in every
  generation since.
- The manias and the hyperinflations: the record of the doubling
  law serving greed and ruin lives in the mirror, dated and
  numbered. The color keeps the one lesson that survived every
  episode: the law does not care what it doubles.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward. Each names
what runs along it.

- MATHEMATICS: the exponent (compounding, discounting), ratios and
  proportion, the crossing's algebra, probability under the
  pooling law. The subject is arithmetic wearing work clothes.
- COOKING: preservation as stored wealth — the smokehouse is a
  savings account and cooking holds its chemistry; salt as early
  money; thrift's cookbook shared page for page; the staples as
  civilization's payroll.
- FARMING: the harvest is the original income and the granary the
  original bank; seed corn is capital defined (eating it is the
  oldest bankruptcy); yields, seasons, and famine prices flow both
  ways along this path.
- PHYSICS: nothing from nothing — output needs input here as
  everywhere (the perpetual-yield mirror of perpetual motion is
  certified in this phylum's white); carriage costs energy, and
  transport's physics prices what is tradable.
- CHEMISTRY: the assay and the density bath keeping coin honest;
  preservation chemistry widening what can be stored, therefore
  what can be wealth.
- LIFE: the doubling law is shared flesh and ledger — yeast,
  bacteria, populations, and interest all run 2^(t ÷ doubling
  time); carrying capacity is the biologist's name for the wall
  every unfunded doubling hits.
- THE BODY: work is bodily effort and food its fuel; subsistence
  sets the floor under every wage; hunger is the first want and
  the reason provisioning is the first purpose.
- MEDICINE: plagues reprice labor (the Black Death entry); health
  is productive capacity, so public health is economic
  infrastructure; the pooling law and insurance were built for
  bodies first.
- WEATHER: the harvest gamble — drought and frost move prices
  before any trader does; weather risk fathered both the granary
  and the insurance pool.
- CULTURE: money runs on shared belief and trade on trust between
  strangers; contracts borrow their force from custom before law;
  and the manias (mirror) are crowd psychology keeping a ledger.
- ENGINEERING: machines multiply labor (the pin factory's later
  chapters); roads, ships, and wires shrink carriage and widen
  every market; the granary's walls set the spoilage rate the
  storage arithmetic pays.
