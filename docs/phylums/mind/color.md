## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- THE BEAM — attention: one lamp, not a rack of them. It lights one
  demanding thing at a time; what it misses was, for its owner,
  never there. Also lives in: economy (advertising is a market that
  buys and sells the beam), transport (phone plus wheel is two
  claims on one beam), teaching, conflict (the pickpocket steals the
  beam, then the watch).
- THE WORKBENCH — the holding of the moment: about four things live
  at once, and a new one does not stack, it shoves an old one off.
  Also: teaching (chunking is half the craft), language (phone
  numbers come pre-broken), measurement.
- THE STORE — long-term memory: not a recording but a rebuilder. It
  keeps fragments and gist, answers only to cues, and hands back a
  fresh construction every time. Also: law (the eyewitness), story
  (the family tale improving annually), computing (where the
  comparison breaks).
- THE PICTURE — perception: the brain's proposed guess about the
  next moment, with the senses' objections pencilled in. The input
  is far poorer than the output; the seamless world is manufactured.
  Also: physics (light and signal), law (honest witnesses disagree),
  design (perspective builds depth that isn't there).
- THE MODEL — imagination: the prediction machinery cut loose from
  the senses and run on invented input. Rehearsal at no cost and no
  risk, fenced by its own assumptions. Also: engineering (a
  simulation is worth its assumptions), play, story.
- FEELINGS — fast verdicts: body-speed readings of what a situation
  does to what you want. They report the stake, not the event, and
  arrive before any reason for them. Also: body, medicine (placebo's
  door), economy (markets move on fear faster than arithmetic).
- WANTS AND PURPOSES — standing wants held ahead of the doing, the
  standard the doing is judged against. Without one there is no
  choice, only response. Also: ethics, economy, governance.
- HABIT — a choice made once and filed under a sight, a time, or a
  place; after filing it runs on the cue and not on the wanting.
  Also: craft (compiled hands), upkeep (the tidy house is compiled,
  not willed), economy.
- THE EXPLAINER — the part that answers "why did you do that." It
  answers at once and sincerely, and it was not in the room where
  the choosing happened. Also: law (a sincere account that is not
  what happened), story.
- WORDS AND NAMES — handles: a name packs a whole shape into one
  cheap grip that can be carried, compared, handed over, and found
  again tomorrow. Also: language (the home phylum), teaching,
  measurement.
- SLEEP — the maintenance shift: the one state in which the day's
  changes are filed and the machinery is serviced, run by two
  separate controllers (a pressure and a clock). Also: body,
  medicine, flight (duty limits are written from its arithmetic).
- THE ALARM — the stress response: fuel to the blood, heart up,
  attention narrowed, a body readied to act. A solution built for
  minutes, billed by the year when it cannot switch off. Also:
  body, medicine, conflict, survival.
- THE CELLS — neurons: adding machines with triggers, each summing
  thousands of pushes up and down against a threshold. A minority
  of the head's cells; the support crew feeds them, insulates them,
  prunes them, and orders their blood. Also: life, chemistry,
  electronics (the closest honest analogy: a regenerated pulse).
- THE JUNCTIONS — where nerves nearly touch: the electrical signal
  stops, a chemical crosses a gap, and a new signal starts. The gap
  looks like a defect and is the whole point — a chemical step can
  be strengthened or weakened, and the altered strengths ARE the
  storage. Also: chemistry, teaching (what learning physically is).
- OTHER MINDS — the pictures you cannot see: everyone you deal with
  runs one, built from their own side of the room, and you get only
  what they did and said. Also: people, law (intent is proven, never
  observed), teaching, story.
- THE SELF — one actor across time: an edge (an inside kept, an
  outside dealt with), a carried past, a purpose held, and parts
  answering to something shared. Cut the carrying and each moment is
  a fresh thing wearing an old name. Also: law (the person who can
  promise), philosophy, story.
- THE CONVERSATION — one thread persisting between turns, carrying
  what has stood and what has closed; a move-game with lawful kinds
  of move. Also: language, people, teaching.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Softer laws
than physics — tendencies, not iron — but old, measured, and shared
by every reader.

### — seeing and remembering
- Seeing is guessing: the mind proposes the picture and the senses
  only answer where it was wrong. Sharp sight is a thumbnail wide at
  arm's length, there is a hole where the nerve leaves the eye, and
  the seamless world is manufactured from that. Condition: the
  guessing works because the world mostly repeats; it misfires
  exactly where the world doesn't (the proofread error, the mirage).
- Nothing is met fresh: a thing arrives, the store answers with the
  nearest match it holds, and the verdict — not the thing — is what
  gets experienced. This is recognition's speed and misdiagnosis's
  mechanism in one law.
- Remembering is retelling: a memory is rebuilt when asked, from a
  few real fragments plus general knowledge of how such scenes go,
  and no join shows — the machinery that builds is the machinery
  that inspects.
- Every recall is a fresh write: the version just built goes back on
  the shelf, so a memory is dated at its last handling, not at the
  event, and the story told most often sits furthest from the day.
- The store has no front door: things come back only when something
  present resembles them. A cue is a key; recognition (a match
  against the present) is cheap, recall (a search with nothing to
  match) is dear — the face is certain and the name is gone.
- Facts and skills are two stores that fail separately: a man who
  cannot tell you his age still rides the bike, and cannot tell you
  one thing about how. Showing works where telling fails.
- Absences are invisible: noticing runs on arrivals, and a non-event
  never arrives. The missing tool, the skipped step, the guest who
  never wrote — all silent. This is the law checklists exist for.
- Time is worked out afterwards from the amount stored: a week of
  new places is long to look back on; a year of one commute drags
  through and then vanishes, leaving nothing to measure with.

### — holding and learning
- Attention is one beam and the workbench holds about four: two
  demanding things at once is the lamp swinging, paying a toll each
  swing, and item five shoves item one off the edge. Wanting more
  buys none. (The arithmetic is in SCIENCE.)
- Repetition compiles: what is practised long enough drops below the
  beam and runs without it — which frees the beam, and which is why
  the compiled thing then runs whether or not it still serves.
- The master sees fewer things, not more, each one bigger: a board
  that is twenty-five pieces to a beginner is five shapes to him,
  and on randomly scattered pieces his advantage vanishes. It was
  never a larger workbench; it was bigger grips.
- What is learned comes welded to where it was learned: the market
  trader who does lightning sums at the stall fails them on paper.
  Skill travels only as far as its surface features do.
- A learner who has learned holds LESS: twenty worked examples
  become one rule and the twenty are thrown away. The measure of
  learning is what can now be safely dropped.
- A remembered answer and an understood one look identical until the
  question changes: understanding is holding the machine that made
  the answer, so you can lose the answer and rebuild it.
- Only hours at the edge move skill: failing and correcting on the
  part not yet possessed. Twenty comfortable years maintain a level
  and never raise it, because a system changes only where it errs.
- Interest lives in a band: fully predictable material returns
  nothing (boredom), wholly unpredictable returns nothing (noise);
  a mind feeds where learning is still being made.
- Learning the right answer does not delete the wrong one — it lays
  a slower layer on top, and underneath the old one still fires
  first. A trained physicist still FEELS the heavy ball falls
  faster. Minds add and override; they cannot erase.
- The expert's map has walls drawn from old failures under
  conditions that may have changed; the beginner walks through one
  now and then because nobody told him it was there.

### — judging and choosing
- Choosing is comparing against one measure; without a shared
  measure two options can only be listed, never ranked. And every
  choice costs the best of what it shut out.
- Enough beats best when searching costs: the chooser takes the
  first option clearing a stated bar, because the hunt for best
  costs more than the difference is worth. When nothing clears the
  bar, not choosing is itself a choice — to be taken deliberately,
  not fallen into.
- How often a thing happens gets judged by how easily an example
  comes to mind, and ease is bought with drama, recency, and news
  coverage — one shark outweighs the drownings. Counting is
  impossible inside a store that never kept a tally.
- A number lands and every estimate after it is a walk away from it
  that stops too early — and it works with numbers known to be
  meaningless, which is the proof that no reasoning is involved.
- Asked to test a belief, people hunt for cases that fit it; the
  test that would settle it means going looking for what would
  prove them wrong, and that search is the one never run.
- Confidence is built from inner smoothness — how fast the answer
  came, how well the story hangs together — none of which touches
  the world. A rehearsed wrong answer feels better than a fresh
  right one. The click of understanding arrives before, and without,
  the competence.
- Nothing is felt as a total, only as a move from a mark, and a step
  down hurts about twice as much as the same step up pleases. The
  same wage is a triumph or an insult depending on last year's.
- One fact, two messages: "ninety survive" and "ten die" move
  different decisions, because the mind works on the description it
  was handed, having no way to reach the fact underneath.
- What has already been spent is counted as a reason to keep going —
  stopping is the act that books the loss, so stopping is what gets
  postponed.
- The plan is always the smooth walk: imagination holds the steps
  but not the interruptions, so estimates come out short every time,
  including estimates by people who know this happens.
- Once the ending is known it cannot be un-known: it reaches back
  and rewrites the memory of your own forecast, so every outcome
  arrives looking like the one that was always likely.
- Hand a mind a scatter of events and it hands back a chain with
  causes, actors, and a point — and it will do this to random dots
  and to a fair coin's clumps, with the same certainty as for a
  found pattern. The tuning over-fires because missing a real
  pattern once cost more than a thousand false alarms.
- Draw a line and name both sides, and the things nearest the line
  move apart in the seeing — two shades alike inside one colour
  word, plainly different across the border. The same happens to
  people once groups are named.

### — feeling and wanting
- A feeling is a fast reading of the stake, arriving before the
  reasons; the slow judge would die before deciding, so the fast one
  shipped first and reasoning catches up.
- A mood is a filter on the store: low, you reach the low memories,
  and reaching them keeps you low — the evidence for the mood is
  produced by the mood.
- The mind measures change, not level: a steady state fades from
  notice, good or bad. Raises stop pleasing; the cook tastes the
  soup less with each sip. (Some losses never finish fading.)
- Effort lives in the gap between wanted and expected: close it from
  either end — certain of the prize, or certain it's out of reach —
  and the effort stops.
- Feelings about the future are forecast by the wrong instrument:
  imagination runs today's mind in tomorrow's situation, and
  tomorrow's mind — adapted, changed — is the one part it cannot
  supply. Wins and losses alike are felt for less time than anyone
  predicts. And nobody can imagine themselves in a state they are
  not in: the sober man plans for a person who will not exist that
  evening.
- The stated reason is a report, and reports can be wrong about
  their author: the wanting runs ahead of the knowing, and what
  someone was after becomes visible to them in what they did.
- An order not to think of something is an instruction to keep
  checking whether you are — and the check is the thought.
- The answer arrives in the shower, not at the desk: being stuck is
  one approach held in place, and the same search cannot be run
  harder; stepping away loosens the grip.
- A thought becomes inspectable only when it leaves the head — on
  paper, out loud, drawn badly. The builder cannot inspect its own
  build; holding and checking compete for the same small hands.

### — minds meeting
- Everyone carries their own picture; the commonest mistake in any
  exchange is quietly assuming it is yours. You never see why
  someone did a thing, only the thing — the reason is a guess built
  from acts and words, and a wrong guess feels exactly like sight.
- Nobody thinks themselves unreasonable: from inside their position,
  with what they knew and feared, the foolish act was the sensible
  one. Finding that inside is the work.
- The words of an ask are the top of it; under them sits the hunger
  that made it, and a perfectly right answer to the top can leave
  the person no better off. Sometimes the want under the ask is
  simply to be received — held and said back, not solved.
- We explain others by what they are and ourselves by what we were
  in — he is late because careless, I because of traffic. Not
  hypocrisy: from inside you can see the situation; from outside
  only the person.
- Everyone guards face while they talk: a correction that costs
  someone standing gets fought whether or not it is true. Trust is
  built of small promises kept on time and unmade by one broken —
  the two directions run at wildly different speeds.
- What you send comes back: warmth returns warmth, suspicion returns
  suspicion, and the loop closes so fast that most quarrels are each
  side answering the other's answer.
- Saying is doing: every sentence is a move that informs, asks,
  promises, warns, binds, or tests, and reading only whether it is
  true misses what it came to do. Small talk carries no news and is
  not meant to — it is two people checking the line between them is
  open. A question is a shape many jobs wear; answering the shape
  instead of the job answers something nobody asked. One breath
  usually does several things at once, and the loudest job on the
  surface is rarely the one deciding how the exchange goes.
- A question put into the air stays standing until something closes
  it; a thin thing said ("why?" "and?") leans on the thread. What
  has been said in a thread is spent — the new is preferred over the
  known. Silence is a lawful move. A claim without knowledge behind
  it is not a weaker claim; it is a different act — a guess, to be
  marked as one. A refusal that does not name what is missing is a
  dodge. "I don't know" is the move that makes every other answer
  worth something: a thing that always answers, answers with ground
  or without at the same confidence, and so tells you nothing.
- Nothing new moves a person from outside: it takes hold only by
  hooking onto something they already believe, want, or fear. The
  feeling arrives first and decides which reasons get looked at, so
  arguing into a tilted scale moves little. Once both sides have
  said aloud what they are doing together, the same act reads
  differently — an interruption becomes help.
- How a thing closes sets what the whole is remembered as: a good
  hour ending badly is remembered as a bad one. Nearly every
  exchange breaks somewhere; what makes exchanges work is catching
  the break and mending it within a turn or two.

### — a person built over a life
- A child is not an adult with less information: the equipment
  arrives in an order — things existing when unseen, then words,
  then other people's minds (around four, and before it a child
  genuinely cannot hide a sweet or keep a secret), then two rules
  held at once — and each floor must be built before anything stands
  on it. Some things must be met inside a window: a language heard
  in the first years is built into the machinery; met at thirty it
  goes through a slower door and never sounds native.
- A small child uses one familiar adult as a base to go out from —
  security is the platform of exploring, not its rival; the child
  with no reliable base explores less.
- In adolescence the accelerator matures years before the brakes:
  reward and status peak while the weighing of consequences finishes
  last, and the risk-taking sharpens in front of other teenagers.
- A baby arrives with settings — how strongly it reacts, how it
  meets the new — visible in the first weeks and recognisable for
  decades; and the settings shape the parenting received, so a child
  partly builds its own upbringing. Genes act largely by choosing
  and provoking environments. Heritability is a fact about a crowd's
  differences, not a person's fate: a trait can be highly heritable
  and completely changeable.
- Personality settles into a few broad axes, stable from young
  adulthood; they predict a life moderately and a single moment
  hardly at all. Traits win only in aggregate — the single act is
  mostly the situation, and the person shows up in the sum.

### — the levers of behaviour
- A signal is learnt only when it predicts something new; a second
  bell adding nothing is never learnt at all. The machinery tracks
  prediction, not company.
- Behaviour is shaped by what follows it, and closeness in time
  beats size: a small consequence now teaches more than a large one
  next week. This is why every long-term good habit is hard and
  every immediate small reward is easy.
- A reward that comes every time is easy to give up when it stops; a
  reward that comes sometimes, unpredictably, produces behaviour
  that persists almost indefinitely — no single failure is evidence
  of anything. It is the design of every slot machine and every
  inconsistent parent.
- Punishment suppresses in the punisher's presence and teaches
  concealment everywhere else, and says nothing about what to do
  instead — fast, wears off, problem intact.
- Avoidance is the engine of every fear: leaving early feels like
  relief, relief rewards the leaving, and the person never finds out
  that the feared thing would not have happened.
- A creature that has met outcomes it could not affect stops trying,
  and goes on not trying when control returns. What was learnt was
  whether acting matters, and it generalises.
- Behaviour changes before feeling does: waiting to feel ready is
  waiting for a state the action produces. Act first, small enough
  to be certain of. Knowing why does not stop it — insight is
  comforting and is not a mechanism of change, because the origin is
  not what runs the loop now.
- Self-control is mostly arrangement, not strength: the disciplined
  are largely those with fewer conflicts to win — the biscuits are
  not in the house. Intending predicts doing far more weakly than
  anyone expects; the moment is run by cues and by whatever is
  easiest, and the fix is to hand the behaviour to a named cue.
- Being paid for what was already loved can end the loving: the
  payment becomes the nearest available reason, and when it stops,
  the doing drops below where it began.
- An old habit is outvoted, never deleted: the original link
  survives underneath and returns under stress, when tired, and in
  the old surroundings — the quitter of a year lights one in the pub
  where they used to.

### — people in company
- People report what the room reports, against their own eyes, and
  most when the group is unanimous; one other dissenter breaks it
  almost entirely — the power was never in the number.
- Ordinary people do serious harm when an authority that looks
  legitimate accepts the responsibility; the dials are closeness to
  the victim, closeness of the order-giver, and whether anyone else
  refuses. Almost nobody predicts this of themselves.
- The more people present, the less likely any one acts — a duty
  owned by all is owned by none. Being named ends it instantly.
- Anonymity, a group, and high arousal remove the private self and
  the group's norm takes over — the same mechanism gives a riot and
  a choir. A role rewrites the person in it: give power without
  accountability and behaviour follows the position.
- Groups form on nothing at all: split strangers by a coin toss and
  within minutes they favour their own. The sorting machinery is
  universal and automatic; what fills the categories is local and
  learnt — which is why prejudice's content changes in a generation
  while the sorting never does. A stereotype builds its own
  evidence: expecting less, people ask less and interrupt more, and
  the person so treated performs worse.
- Being excluded runs on the machinery of being hurt — not metaphor:
  long isolation costs a body about what heavy smoking does.
- People act on what they believe others do, and the belief is
  usually wrong — telling them the true figure changes behaviour
  more than any argument about why they should.
- Attitudes follow behaviour more reliably than the reverse: having
  acted, especially freely, a person adjusts belief until the act
  looks reasonable — and the harder the initiation, the dearer the
  club. A small yes changes what a person thinks they are, and the
  large yes is asked of that different man. Identity is a membership
  before it is a description; an attack on the belief is processed
  like an attack on the body.
- Self-esteem is mostly a readout of how a life is going, not a
  cause; the belief that moves anything is narrow — not "I am
  capable" but "I can do this particular thing" — and it is built
  almost entirely out of having done things like it.

### — when the running breaks
- The line is not drawn at the trait but at the cost: plenty of
  people are anxious, sleepless, or odd and getting on with their
  lives; disorder is distress the person cannot carry or a life they
  can no longer run, judged against the life they are in.
- Diagnosis here describes a pattern and does not name a cause — a
  dictionary of agreed shapes, not mechanisms. So the explanation
  circles ("why voices? this illness. how known? the voices") and
  yet the label earns its place: it predicts course, sorts likely
  treatments, and is the ticket to help. Having several at once is
  the rule, not the exception; under every category is a dimension,
  and the line on it is administrative and moves.
- The old first question still matters most: is this person's
  account of reality one we share? A hallucination is a perception
  with nothing behind it, arriving with sight's full authority; a
  delusion is a conviction that survives all evidence, its content
  borrowed from the age. Added symptoms (voices, agitation) respond
  to medication; subtracted ones (drive, feeling, thought gone thin)
  mostly don't, and they are what wrecks the life.
- The organ that would notice the illness can be the ill one: in
  severe psychosis, mania, some dementias, losing the ability to see
  anything is wrong is itself a symptom — refusal of help is not
  stubbornness, and it is the field's hardest problem.
- Depression is not intense sadness: it is the going-out of being
  movable by anything, and it comes with the body — sleep broken at
  the same early hour, appetite gone, thought slowed, worst in the
  morning. It maintains itself through withdrawal: doing less
  removes reward, absence of reward removes reasons, and the loop
  runs on its own evidence. Most trouble is held up by such a loop
  rather than a cause — whatever started it is usually gone.
- Anxiety is two forecasts wrong the same way — danger overrated,
  coping underrated — kept alive by small precautions that take the
  credit whenever nothing goes wrong. The disorders differ only in
  what is feared; the machinery is one machinery. A panic attack is
  the emergency alarm firing with no emergency, and reading its
  symptoms as a heart attack is what makes the second one. Fears
  are not learnt evenly: snakes and heights arrive in one bad
  experience; sockets and traffic almost never — the list is older
  than the dangers actually faced.
- Distressed thinking runs to a small set of shapes — all-or-
  nothing, worst case, mind-reading, discounting the good — and they
  do not feel like thoughts; they feel like plainly seeing.
- After trauma the memory is stored unfiled: fragments in the
  present tense, without the marker that says this is over — so it
  is triggered rather than recalled. When escape was impossible the
  mind left instead (time gone strange, the event watched from
  outside); it works, and the bill arrives later. Most people who
  meet horror recover, and what best predicts who does not is what
  came after — believed, supported, returned to the ordinary.
  Isolation afterwards does more damage than the event's severity.
- In obsession the relief is the trap: the unwanted thought, the
  checking that brings it down, the relief that guarantees its
  return — and the person knows all of it, which is why it is
  torture rather than belief.
- The attention difference is a steering fault, not a capacity
  fault: the same person cannot start the dull necessary thing and
  cannot stop the gripping one. The medication turns up the dial by
  which a small payoff registers — and does nothing about the
  skills, the systems, or the years of believing they were stupid.
- Autism is a different processing style, not a deficient one —
  detail before whole, literal before implied, thresholds set
  differently for noise and light — and much of the disability is
  the mismatch with a world built for the other style. The
  misreading runs both ways between the styles; it is called a
  deficit because one group is larger. Passing for typical is work,
  done all day, and the bill arrives as collapse in private.
- Addiction is only partly the substance: soldiers who used heavily
  in a war mostly stopped on coming home; the same drug produces
  different rates in different lives. Relapse is part of the course,
  triggered by cues, states, and empty hours — so the plan is
  arrangement, not determination, and keeping the person alive
  between attempts is the binding condition of the attempt that
  works.
- Eating disorders are about control, with a genuinely altered
  perception of the body, and the illness presents as an achievement
  — which is why help is refused for years. Starvation itself
  produces the rigidity and narrowed thinking that hold it in place;
  restriction causes the binge, and the restraint is the engine, not
  the cure. This is the mind-illness that kills most reliably, and
  mostly through the body.
- Risk factors sort crowds and cannot pick individuals — the base
  rate defeats the accuracy (the arithmetic is in SCIENCE). What
  lowers the deaths is distance between a person and a method:
  barriers, smaller packets, locked guns — because the crisis is
  short and specific, and the blocked means is mostly not replaced.
  Asking does not plant the idea; being asked directly brings
  relief. Almost nobody wants to be dead — they want the pain to
  stop and can see no other way, and what is decided inside the
  crisis is decided by somebody who will not exist next week. The
  days after leaving hospital are the most dangerous of all, and the
  contact itself — a call, a card, an early appointment — measurably
  lowers the deaths.
- Drugs and talk work on different parts and neither replaces the
  other; together they beat either for most moderate and severe
  cases, and for mild ones the drug often adds nothing. The drugs do
  not know the diagnosis — the same molecule quiets agitation across
  conditions named separately. Side effects decide whether treatment
  happens at all: a drug that leaves a person flat or numb will be
  stopped, silently, and the stopping recorded as relapse. Most of
  what makes therapy work is shared by all of it: somebody reliably
  on your side, a story that makes the trouble make sense, an
  expectation of movement, and something done differently between
  sessions.
- Treating somebody against their will rests on capacity — can they
  take in, hold, weigh, and say — not on diagnosis and not on
  disagreement. Coercion has a bill paid later in avoided services;
  sometimes right, never free. Stigma is a measurable cost: help
  sought years late, jobs not offered, and physical complaints
  attributed to the label — people with severe mental illness die
  fifteen to twenty years early, mostly of ordinary disease not
  investigated. A large share of severe illness follows childhood
  adversity in proportion — which changes the useful question from
  "what is wrong with you" to "what happened to you."
- Recovery is not the absence of symptoms: it is a life — work of
  some kind, people, somewhere to live, something that is yours —
  and plenty of people live well while still hearing voices. The
  first episode does not predict the tenth; confident prognosis at
  the start is usually invention.

### — the daily machinery
- Sleep happens where high pressure meets an open window: one system
  builds pressure from waking and discharges it only in sleep; the
  other is a clock that opens a window regardless. Misery is their
  disagreement. Light sets the clock, timing over brightness —
  morning light pulls the system earlier, late light pushes it later
  — and indoor light is far dimmer than it feels. There are hours
  when the body is switched off whatever the person intends; the
  disasters cluster there in people trying just as hard as at noon.
- A night is not uniform: deep sleep front-loaded, dream-heavy sleep
  back-loaded, so an early alarm and a late bedtime take different
  things. The genuine short sleeper exists and is vanishingly rare;
  the ordinary case has forgotten what rested feels like and tests
  badly while reporting fine. After short nights, feeling normal
  returns long before performance does — that gap is where the
  accidents live. The first thing short sleep takes is judgment
  about people and risk, unnoticed by its owner.
- Caffeine does not remove tiredness, it hides the reading — and
  lingers half the night (the halving arithmetic is in SCIENCE).
  Alcohol sedates, and sedation is not sleep: faster on, dreams
  suppressed, and a rebound that breaks the second half of the
  night. Chronic insomnia is usually kept alive by the response to
  it — the bed taught to mean lying awake, the early bedtime
  stretching bed past sleep, the worry supplying the arousal. Sleep
  is the one task effort makes worse; everything that helps works by
  removing the attempt. Falling asleep needs the body's core to
  cool, and it cools by dumping heat through hands and feet.
- The commonest missed cause of ruined days is a throat that closes
  during sleep — hundreds of unremembered awakenings, reported only
  as exhaustion, treated for years as something else; someone else
  usually has to notice the snoring and the pauses.
- The stress response is a solution, not an injury — and the
  off-switch, not the on-switch, is the part that fails. What makes
  a demand stressful is not its size but whether it can be predicted
  and affected: the same workload is tolerable with a say and
  corrosive without one, which is why the worst stress sits not at
  the top of organisations but under heavy demands with no
  authority. Waiting is frequently worse than the bad thing itself,
  because no response can be selected. The body cannot tell a
  rehearsed threat from a present one — replaying the argument
  issues the alarm again, so a ten-minute stressor can be made to
  last a month. Recovery requires being genuinely elsewhere; the
  response was built to be followed by movement, and activity after
  stress closes the loop the body opened. Being known and having
  people to call is not a soft factor — it is measured against
  illness and early death at the scale of the famous physical risks.
- Burnout is three things at once — the exhaustion, the cynicism
  that follows it as a defence, and the collapse of belief in one's
  own effect — and a holiday touches the first and leaves the other
  two. What turns a job into an illness is a lasting imbalance
  between what it takes and what it gives back.
- A habit is built by immediate reward, same cue, same context — not
  by frequency alone, and not in twenty-one days (the measured
  arithmetic is in SCIENCE). A goal names an outcome and an outcome
  names no action; what produces it is a repeatable behaviour hung
  on a named cue. A rule with no exceptions is easier to keep than a
  rule with judgment in it, because the judgment is exercised when
  tired, hungry, and in company — which is when it loses. A lapse
  almost never ends an attempt; the story told about the lapse does.
  Measuring a behaviour changes it before anything else is done. The
  plan has to survive the worst state, not the average one — built
  into the arrangement of things, not into attention. The best
  moment to change is when the context has already broken: the move,
  the new job — the cues that ran the old routines are gone.

### — being an actor at all
- A thing that acts has an edge: an inside it keeps, an outside it
  deals with. What it cannot do shapes it as much as what it can — a
  river is a river because of its banks. What a thing can do and
  what it knows it can do are separate holdings, and acting well
  needs both. Nothing is at risk unless there is a someone for it to
  be at risk for. To act is to put something forward that could be
  shown to have failed; where nothing can come back and say no,
  there are only events. Parts make one actor only when they answer
  to something shared — one purpose, one place the last word sits.
  The self-report is made from inside, with the worst seat in the
  house: evidence, never the fact. And the root error is reading
  your own state as the world's — the tired eye calls the room dim;
  keeping a running account of which half of the report is you is
  most of acting well.

## SCIENCE
The math, physics, and chemistry of the mind, held — not mentioned.
Each piece is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it can follow,
and where it earns it, a WORKED case proving the rule runs.

### THE SIGNAL — physics of the cell

LAW: a neuron decides by summing to a threshold. Resting charge
  ~−70 thousandths of a volt; trigger line ~−55; each exciting
  input adds ~+0.5, each inhibiting input ~−0.5, and the sums leak
  away with distance and time.
RULE: to fire the cell — enough inputs must land close together in
  time and place that their sum still standing at the trigger
  crosses the 15-thousandths gap.
WORKED: 15 ÷ 0.5 = 30 near-simultaneous exciting inputs to fire;
  every inhibiting input cancels one, so 40 excites against 10
  inhibits nets 30 and just fires. Nothing else about the cell is a
  decision — and this is why timing and placement are computation:
  ten inputs on one branch at one moment beat thirty scattered.

LAW: the spike is the same size every time — strength is carried by
  rate and by recruitment, never by volume. After each spike the
  nerve is briefly dead (~1 thousandth of a second), which sets a
  ceiling and a direction.
WORKED: 1 ÷ 0.001 = 1,000 spikes per second, the hard ceiling;
  working traffic runs far below it. More pain is more spikes and
  more fibres, never bigger ones.

LAW: signal speed is bought two ways — thicker fibre, or fat-
  wrapped fibre letting the spike skip gap to gap (~50× faster,
  far cheaper). Wrapped human fibres run ~60 m per second.
WORKED: foot to head, ~1.5 m: 1.5 ÷ 60 = 0.025 s = 25 thousandths
  of a second of wire time. A simple reaction takes ~200; so the
  wire is about an eighth of it and the deciding is the rest —
  which is why a sprint start under 100 thousandths is ruled a
  guess, not a reaction: the arithmetic forbids hearing, deciding,
  and moving that fast.

LAW: the brain runs on ~a fifth of the body's resting energy from
  ~a fiftieth of its weight, with no fuel reserve — active patches
  summon their own blood within a second.
WORKED: a 2,000-kcal day is 2,000 × 4,184 = 8,368,000 joules;
  ÷ 86,400 seconds ≈ 97 watts for the whole body; a fifth ≈ 19 W —
  the whole of thinking runs on a dim bulb. This is why the beam is
  rationed, why compiling habits pays, and why the scan sees blood
  answer rather than thought.

LAW: reduction starts at the sensor. The eye's ~100 million
  detectors report down ~1 million fibres — a hundredfold squeeze —
  so the processing (edges, movement, centre-against-surround) must
  happen before the bottleneck, and does. Neighbours suppressing
  neighbours manufacture the edges you see; the outline was partly
  invented at the retina.

LAW: learning is a physical change at the junctions, and the rule
  is about ORDER: if cell A fires within tens of thousandths of a
  second BEFORE cell B, their junction strengthens; just after, it
  weakens. Causes precede effects, so an order-sensitive rule is a
  rule that can find causes. Weakening is as active as
  strengthening — a network that could only grow would saturate in
  days and hold nothing.

LAW: the reward chemical reports the ERROR in a prediction, not the
  goodness of the thing — better than expected fires it, exactly as
  expected is silence, worse dips below. That difference is
  precisely what a learning system needs, and precisely what an
  addictive substance counterfeits.

### THE BOTTLENECK — mathematics of holding

LAW: the workbench holds ~4 live items; a chunk counts as one item
  whatever it contains. (The older figure of seven came from
  letting people chunk unnoticed.)
RULE: to fit N things — pack them into ≤4 named bundles; the
  bundle's contents ride free under its handle.
WORKED: the ten-digit number 8015551234 is ten loose items — two
  and a half benches, unholdable. As 801 · 555 · 1234 it is three
  chunks: fits, with room for one more. The chess master reads a
  board as five shapes where the beginner sees twenty-five pieces —
  same bench, grips grown over ~tens of thousands of stored
  patterns; scatter the pieces randomly and his advantage vanishes,
  which is the proof it was never a larger mind.

LAW: one beam; a switch pays twice — the drop and the rebuild — and
  the rebuild of deep work is measured in tens of minutes, not the
  seconds it feels like (~20 minutes on measured office work).
WORKED: six interruptions across an afternoon at ~20 minutes'
  rebuild each: 6 × 20 = 120 minutes — two hours spent, on top of
  the minutes the interruptions themselves took. The half-built
  thing was leaning on page and screen; the lean is what breaks.

LAW: quantity at a glance stops at ~4; past it the mind estimates
  by RATIO, not by difference.
WORKED: 8 against 12 is a ratio of 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5 — seen instantly;
  80 against 84 is 84 ÷ 80 = 1.05 — invisible, though the
  difference is 4 in both. This one law explains why a million and
  a billion feel like neighbours (the felt step is the ratio's
  logarithm, and both are just "huge"), and why risks of 1 in
  1,000 and 1 in 1,000,000 are felt as the same small.

### THE MEMORY ARITHMETIC

LAW: unfetched memory decays fast then slow — half or more of new
  unconnected material is gone within a day, and the loss then
  flattens. Each successful FETCH after partial forgetting (the
  hauling up, not the re-reading) roughly doubles the interval the
  memory will survive.
RULE: to keep a fact — fetch it on an expanding schedule, each gap
  about double the last, always after a little forgetting has set
  in; re-reading is not fetching and buys almost nothing but the
  feeling of knowing.
WORKED: reviews at days 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 — five fetches — leave the
  next due at day 32: a month's hold from five short visits. The
  same five visits crammed into one night are one fetch's worth of
  spacing, and the material is gone by the weekend. This is also
  why the exam's multiple choice (recognition, a match against the
  present) is a different test from the blank page (recall, a
  search with no key).

LAW: a memory recalled goes soft for hours before re-storing — each
  visit is physically a re-write. This is why the oft-told story
  drifts furthest, why the ending rewrites the forecast, and why
  going back through a terror under safety can change what gets
  stored back — the treatment law and the courtroom warning are the
  same law.

LAW: sleep is the filing shift: the day's sequences replay,
  compressed many-fold, from the fast temporary index to the slow
  permanent store — and the patterns that replay most are the ones
  remembered best next day. The index and the store fail
  separately: destroy the index and childhood stays perfect while
  nothing new survives a minute.

### THE CHOOSING ARITHMETIC

LAW: worth under uncertainty = payoff × chance, summed over the
  outcomes; the felt worth is NOT this, and the gap is lawful, not
  noise.
LAW: a loss weighs ~2× the same gain.
WORKED: a coin flip winning $150 or losing $100. The money
  arithmetic: 0.5 × 150 − 0.5 × 100 = 75 − 50 = +$25 per flip — a
  good bet. The felt arithmetic, loss doubled: 75 − (0.5 × 200) =
  75 − 100 = −25 — refused. Bundle ten such flips and the chance of
  ending down shrinks toward small; the same offers, framed as one
  decision, get accepted. The law says: judge repeated risks as the
  bundle they are.
RULE: to price a choice — its gift minus the best of what it shuts
  out; to stop searching — set the bar before looking, take the
  first option that clears it, and treat "nothing cleared" as a
  finding.

LAW: on rare things, the base rate defeats the test. flagged-truth
  = true hits ÷ (true hits + false alarms), and the false alarms
  scale with the whole crowd.
WORKED: a condition carried by 1 in 1,000, a screen that catches
  90% and wrongly flags 10%. In 10,000 people: 10 carriers → 9
  caught; 9,990 clear → 999 wrongly flagged; flagged in all:
  9 + 999 = 1,008; truly carrying: 9 ÷ 1,008 ≈ 0.9%. A person
  flagged by a "90% accurate" screen has under one chance in a
  hundred — this single computation is why risk scales cannot gate
  individuals, why rare-disease screens frighten the healthy, and
  why the shark is feared over the drowning (the news screens for
  rarity and the mind reads it as commonness).

LAW: delay discounts — a consequence's teaching power falls
  steeply with the seconds between act and outcome, which is why
  the machinery that files habits cannot see a decade, and why
  every plan that pays off in years must be repaid in some
  immediate coin (the tick of a log, company, a streak) to survive.

### THE SLEEP ARITHMETIC

LAW: sleep runs in ~90-minute cycles; deep sleep fills the early
  ones, dreaming the late ones. Sleep = pressure × window: the
  pressure discharges only in sleep, the clock opens the window on
  its own schedule.
RULE: to place a night — count back from the alarm in 90-minute
  steps plus ~15 minutes to fall asleep; to shorten one, know
  WHICH end you are cutting.
WORKED: alarm 6:30, five cycles wanted: 5 × 90 = 450 minutes =
  7 h 30 m; plus 15 = lights out ~10:45 pm. Bed at 12:45 instead
  gives 5 h 45 m = 345 ÷ 90 ≈ 3.8 cycles — and the missing chunk
  is the dream-heavy back half, not a uniform shave.

LAW: caffeine halves in the body every ~5-6 hours, and what it
  blocks is the molecule reporting how long you have been awake —
  it hides the pressure gauge without draining the pressure.
WORKED: 200 mg at 2 pm → ~100 mg by ~7:30 pm → ~50 mg by ~1 am. At
  the 10:45 lights-out the sleep arithmetic placed, about a third of
  the afternoon cup (~65 mg) is still standing — cutting deep sleep
  in people who insist it does not affect them.

LAW: the clock re-sets by ~1 hour per day, pulled by light —
  morning light earlier, late light later, crossing over near the
  coldest hour of night. Light dose: a lit room ~300 units, a grey
  sky ~10,000, full sun ~100,000 — the room is ~a thirtieth of the
  grey sky (10,000 ÷ 300 ≈ 33), which is why an indoor life runs
  on a clock signal too faint to hold it steady.
WORKED: seven time zones ≈ seven days of honest realignment; a
  week of night shifts ends about when the body has arrived — and
  then the days return and it starts again. The weekend lie-in is
  the same flight in miniature, flown twice a week.

LAW: waking impairment compounds on a measured curve: ~17-19 hours
  awake tests like the drink-drive line (~0.05 blood alcohol); a
  full day awake like roughly twice it. Feeling normal returns
  before performance does after short nights — so the recovered
  feeling is not evidence, and the pre-dawn hours are switched-off
  hours whatever the intention: the industrial disasters and road
  deaths cluster there on schedule.

LAW: falling asleep requires the core to drop, and it drops by
  dumping heat through hands and feet — which is why the warm bath
  an hour before bed helps (opens the skin's vessels; the heat then
  leaves faster) and a hot room forbids it.

### THE COMPOUNDING OF PRACTICE AND HABIT

LAW: automaticity grows along a curve that rises fast and
  flattens; measured time-to-automatic runs ~18 to ~254 days,
  median ~66 — and the famous 21 days came from a surgeon's remark
  about amputees, not a study.
LAW: only error moves the system — practice compounds only at the
  edge, on the part not yet possessed, failing and correcting.
WORKED: 30 minutes a day at the edge = 3.5 hours a week = 30 × 365
  = 10,950 minutes ≈ 182 hours a year — a year of edge-half-hours
  outbuilds a decade of comfortable playing-through, because the
  comfortable hours hold no error and so move nothing.

LAW: behaviour flows down the effort gradient, and the gradient is
  steep: seconds of friction move large fractions of what people
  do, in both directions. The cue, the context, and the arrangement
  are the levers; attention and resolve are the ones that fail
  first, because they are spent from the same 19-watt budget as
  everything else.

## METHODS
How things are done here — each with what governs it, when it
applies, and what breaks it. The prepositioned how-to-build.

- CHUNK AND NAME (the bottleneck law made deliberate): pack loose
  items into ≤4 named bundles; give the nameless thing a word so it
  can be carried, compared, and found again. Breaks when the chunks
  are not yet built — the beginner cannot chunk what he cannot yet
  see, so the grips must be grown by practice first.
- WRITE IT OUT (the inspection method): the builder cannot inspect
  its own build, so put the thought on paper, out loud, on a napkin
  — the holes show the moment it must be produced. Writing is also
  the workbench's overflow shelf and the store's honest prosthetic:
  what cannot be written into a claim cannot be inherited.
- THE EXPANDING FETCH (the memory arithmetic run on purpose): learn,
  then fetch at roughly doubling gaps, always after a little
  forgetting. Breaks when re-reading is substituted for fetching —
  smoothness is felt, nothing is fixed.
- EDGE PRACTICE (the only compounding that moves skill): isolate the
  part that fails, take it alone and slowly, correct, repeat; let
  the mastered parts compile below the beam. Breaks when it becomes
  comfortable — no error, no change.
- STEP AWAY (the stuck-search method): a store returns what the cue
  points at, and the cue is the thing that is stuck; the walk, the
  night, the week on the shelf change the cue. Doing nothing, on
  purpose, is a method here as in cooking.
- THE TURN (how a mind takes a turn in an exchange, followable):
  reach for what the other is reaching for, leaning on the thread
  when what was said is thin; judge what you hold against what you
  know; drop what does not stand with the rest of what was said;
  drop what this thread has already spent; of what remains, say the
  thing that opens most for the other; mark it said. Silence when
  nothing would open anything.
- ASK BACK (when two readings are alive and nothing settles them):
  a question laid out as a choice ("the tool or the note?") costs
  one word; a bare "what?" hands the whole job back. Show the part
  you have, ask about the part you lack. A stated assumption is half
  a question and costs one word to overturn. Governed by a ceiling:
  past the point where questions cost more than the doubt, take the
  likeliest reading, say so aloud, and go on. Breaks worst in the
  other direction — the quiet guess walked on, buried deeper by
  every turn built on it.
- READ THE OTHER MIND (from behaviour, the only window): start from
  "from inside their position this was sensible" and find the
  inside; split the position from the interest under it; let the
  feeling be received before the facts are argued; name the shared
  frame so strong moves read as help; leave a way to say yes that
  costs no face; mend breaks within a turn or two; mind the close —
  it colours the whole.
- DECIDE ONCE (choosing's economy): a settled choice re-made daily
  wastes the beam; file it — a default, a bright line with no
  exceptions, an if-then hung on a named cue ("Tuesday, dinner done:
  shoes on, out the door"). Breaks when the rule needs judgment in
  the moment, because the negotiation is entered tired and loses.
- ARRANGE, DON'T RESIST (self-control as engineering): remove the
  cue, add seconds of friction to the unwanted, remove them from the
  wanted, and build the plan to survive the worst state, not the
  average one. Change context at a break — the move, the new job —
  while the old cues are gone.
- ACT BEFORE FEELING (the depression lever, and every cold start):
  the loop is cut at the behaviour, the only part a decision can
  reach — activity scheduled and small, before any of it feels worth
  doing; the feeling follows at its own pace. Breaks when the step
  is sized to the mood instead of to certainty of completion.
- FACE IT PROPERLY (the fear method, conditions load-bearing): long
  enough for the fear to fall on its own, in enough different
  places, without the crutch — otherwise the person has merely
  survived it and the fear is intact tomorrow, with the precaution
  taking the credit. For the unfiled terror: back through it under
  safety, until it can be put away as something that happened.
- HUNT THE DISCONFIRMING CASE (the belief-testing method): the
  search is pointed by the belief, so point it at what would prove
  you wrong — the second route, the second opinion, the one other
  dissenter who breaks the room's unanimity. This is the harness the
  whole method of evidence is built from.
- MEASURE THE BEHAVIOUR (the honest log): writing down what is
  eaten, spent, slept, or trained shifts it before anything else is
  done, and converts the automatic back into a decision. Knowing it
  distorts what it measures is part of using it.
- PLACE THE NIGHT (sleep's method): morning light outdoors (the
  dose arithmetic), a cool dark room, the caffeine curfew computed
  from the halving law, the warm bath before, regular hours; and for
  the chronic case, the counter-move — shorten time in bed to the
  sleep actually available, get up when awake, teach the bed to mean
  sleep again, and stop trying (effort is the one thing that makes
  this task worse). Hygiene alone fixes only those whose sleep was
  being spoilt by hygiene.
- SPEND THE ALARM (stress's method): the response was built to be
  followed by movement — walk, run, work the body after the blow, or
  the preparation is never spent; then be genuinely elsewhere, since
  rest with the mind still at work is not rest; and lengthen the
  out-breath — the one lever on the resting branch under voluntary
  command, real and immediate, no more than that.
- CARRY AND BE CARRIED (the social method): the load is partly held
  by the people around; being believed, named, and returned to the
  ordinary is what predicts recovery from the worst things; being
  asked directly is relief. A person pointed at and given a task
  becomes the one responsible — name someone, and the crowd's
  paralysis ends.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- PAPER AND THE WRITTEN WORD — the workbench's overflow shelf and
  the store's only honest recorder: the page holds what the four
  slots cannot, does not drift with retelling, and makes the thought
  inspectable. The cheapest and oldest mind-upgrade there is.
- NAMES — one grip per shape; a vocabulary is a toolbox of grips
  (the taster's words, the mechanic's noises). What has no name
  slips out of the hand every time you reach.
- OTHER PEOPLE — the extended store and the error-catcher: most of
  what anyone knows sits in who they can ask, and from inside there
  is no felt boundary between held and reachable. Also the second
  opinion, the dissenting voice, and the carriers of the load.
- CHECKLISTS — the instrument for absence-blindness: a non-event
  never arrives, so the list arrives instead. Flight wrote them into
  law for exactly this hole.
- THE LOG — behaviour's thermometer: the diary of food, money,
  sleep, practice. Measuring changes the measured, and the change is
  usually the point.
- LIGHT — the clock's setting signal, dose-and-timing: ~300 units
  indoors, ~10,000 under grey sky, ~100,000 in sun; taken in the
  morning it is the cheapest lever on the whole sleep machinery.
- THE ROOM AND THE ARRANGEMENT — the will's substitute: the cupboard
  instead of the counter, the phone in the other room, the bag
  packed the night before. Seconds of friction, wholesale effect.
- THE DRUGS, HONESTLY PRICED — caffeine hides the pressure gauge
  (halves every ~5-6 h); alcohol sedates and breaks the night's back
  half; sleeping pills buy under half an hour and charge rebound
  insomnia on exit; the mood drugs move energy in days and hope in
  weeks — the gap between is the dangerous window; the attention
  drugs raise the dial by which a small payoff registers and teach
  nothing. Every dial turned is priced somewhere.
- THE RELATIONSHIP — therapy's carrier: someone reliably on your
  side, a story that makes the trouble make sense, work done between
  sessions. The particular school adds less than its advocates
  claim; force reliably destroys the alliance everything else runs
  on.
- SLEEP ITSELF — the filing shift and the repair shift: not
  optional, not bankable in advance, repaid denser rather than
  merely longer, and the one maintenance window in which the day's
  learning is actually written to the permanent store.
- MOVEMENT — the alarm's completion: the readied body given the
  action it was readied for; the most reliable mood lever available
  without a prescription.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- PREDICT — reduce surprise: the mind's standing job, from the
  fielder running to where the ball will be, down to the cell
  strengthening toward whatever fired just before. Everything else
  here is in this law's service.
- HOLD THE BEAM — spend the one lamp on what was chosen rather than
  on what shouts; the whole economy of habit, chunking, and
  arrangement exists because the lamp is rationed.
- KEEP WHAT SERVES — remember usefully: which is not remembering
  everything (forgetting is the sweeping that keeps the useful
  reachable) but keeping the gist, the skill, and the lesson.
- CHOOSE WELL UNDER UNCERTAINTY — price the options, mind the base
  rate, judge repeated risks as bundles, stop searching at the bar,
  and recognise not-choosing as a choice.
- UNDERSTAND AND BE UNDERSTOOD — read the mind you cannot see from
  what it did and said; be readable; catch and mend the breaks;
  receive and be received.
- CHANGE ONE'S OWN DOING — the want every resolution names and every
  arrangement answers: act first, hang the act on a cue, build for
  the worst state.
- STAY ONE ACTOR — carry the past, hold the purpose, keep the parts
  answering to something shared; know the reach before reaching.
- QUIET THE BREAKING — when the running stops working: cut the loop
  at the behaviour, file the unfiled terror, keep the person alive
  between attempts, and aim at a life, not a symptom list.
- CARRY EACH OTHER — the load is a property of the surroundings as
  much as of the person; belonging is not a soft factor but a
  measured one.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Damage taught first: nearly everything early about the physical
  mind came from loss — the man who lost speech and kept all else,
  the patient whose remembering ended on the day of an operation and
  whose skills kept learning anyway. The method's trap was learned
  with it: showing a region necessary is not showing it performs the
  function; cutting a power cable stops the music.
- Looking inward failed as a method: minds reporting on themselves
  disagreed irreconcilably, and the field rebuilt itself on what
  could be measured from outside — behaviour, timing, error. The
  explainer's unreliability (certain of reasons it never had) turned
  from scandal into founding law.
- The eyewitness lesson was paid in convictions: confident, sincere,
  detailed, wrong. Memory-as-recording died in courtrooms before it
  died in textbooks, and the rebuilding law that replaced it now
  runs both the courtroom warning and the trauma treatment.
- The obedience and conformity results arrived as a shock after the
  war years asked how ordinary people had done what they did — and
  the answer measured was: under authority, unanimity, and role,
  most people, not monsters. Almost nobody predicts it of
  themselves; that is part of the finding.
- The asylums were emptied on a promise of community care that was
  in large part never funded; where the promise was broken, the
  destination of the untreated became prisons, hostels, and the
  street. The reform is what actually got built, not what was
  intended.
- The sleep laboratory turned lore into arithmetic: cycles, stages,
  the two controllers, the replay of the day's patterns during the
  filing shift — and duty-hour law in flight and medicine was
  rewritten from its numbers.
- The manual of disorders was agreed, not discovered — a dictionary
  of shapes, redrawn edition by edition, its lines administrative
  and moving. Knowing that is part of using it well.
- The replication reckoning: some of the field's most repeated
  findings failed when tested large and pre-registered (the mirror
  holds the record). The method turned its instruments on itself and
  survived — smaller, and more honest about which of its claims are
  load-bearing.
- "What happened to you" arrived late: the dose-response line from
  childhood adversity to later illness moved the founding question
  away from "what is wrong with you" — a reordering as large as any
  instrument's arrival.
