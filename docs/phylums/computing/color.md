## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- THE BIT — the atom of difference: two distinguishable states,
  enough times, hold anything sayable. No bit without a body —
  every 1 is somewhere a charge, a groove, a magnet's lean; also:
  physics, mathematics, life (DNA: four letters, two bits a rung),
  language (Morse).
- THE PROCESSOR — carries out very small orders one after another:
  move, add, compare, jump; nothing else is inside. Its whole sense
  of place is one marker saying "where am I"; also: electronics
  (the switch made fast), engineering.
- MEMORY — the layered store: hand, shelf, cellar, far warehouse,
  each larger and slower; the machine spends much of its life
  waiting on the slow layers; also: physics (distance and size cost
  time), mind (recall versus lookup), economy.
- THE PROGRAM — instructions for a perfectly obedient, perfectly
  literal servant without a drop of common sense: it does what was
  said, never what was meant; also: law (a contract binds by its
  letter), story (every wish-gone-wrong tale).
- THE ALGORITHM — a recipe independent of its cook: same steps,
  same answer, on paper, silicon, or brass gears; the steps are the
  thing, the machine a stage; also: cooking (the recipe, exactly),
  music (one score, any instrument), mathematics.
- THE ARRANGEMENTS — row, chain, table, tree, heap, graph: the
  shapes facts are stored in, each fast at some questions and
  hopeless at others, chosen before a line is written; also: craft
  (arrangement is work stored in advance), design.
- THE FILE AND ITS TREE — a disk holds numbered blocks and nothing
  else; the named thing read start to end is an invention laid on
  top, and the folder tree exists so people can agree where
  anything is; also: language (unambiguous names), law.
- THE NETWORK AND THE PACKET — not a wire but a chain of strangers
  carrying chopped-up messages that may be dropped, delayed,
  doubled or reordered; reliability is built on top, never given;
  also: transport (the post, exactly), physics (light's floor).
- THE DATABASE — the promise-keeper under all the programs: it sees
  every write and enforces what no single program can; also: law (a
  registry beats neighbourly promises), economy.
- THE KEY AND THE LOCK — secrecy from lopsided arithmetic: easy to
  do, hopeless to undo; design public, secret in the key, because
  keys rotate and shipped designs cannot; also: mathematics,
  conflict.
- THE LANGUAGE AND ITS TYPES — no language makes the impossible
  possible; each prices thoughts, and priced thoughts are rare
  thoughts. A type is a claim checked before running, on every line
  including those no test reaches — the one documentation the
  machine keeps honest. A language's real vocabulary is its
  library, its real strength its speakers; also: language, people,
  teaching, measurement.
- THE OPERATING SYSTEM, ITS ROOMS AND WORKERS — the referee: many
  programs, one machine, none trustable to share; it alone touches
  the hardware. Each program lives in a walled room (a crash stays
  home); the workers inside one room share instantly, and one
  worker's mistake corrupts all — there is no wall; also:
  governance (the referee on neither team), building (watertight
  compartments), law (property lines).
- THE SENSOR AND THE ACTUATOR — the loop's eye and muscle: a loop
  holds steady only what it can measure and delivers only what the
  muscle has — the sensor decides the design, the actuator sets the
  ceiling; also: body (temperature, balance), movement.
- THE QUEUE AND THE LOG — the holder of bursts and the memory of
  events: a queue turns a spike into a longer wait; a log is the
  whole evidence afterwards — what was not written did not happen,
  as far as anyone can ever say; also: economy, law (the
  transcript, not the recollection).
- THE CLOCK AND THE RANDOM SOURCE — the hidden inputs: every clock
  drifts, jumps, and is corrected backwards; and a machine is a
  device for doing the same thing every time, so randomness must be
  harvested, not computed; also: measurement (time zones are
  politics), physics (noise), play (the fair shuffle).
- THE LIBRARY — strangers' finished work, pulled in with one
  command, running with your privileges for as long as you live:
  the field's most powerful offer and its widest wound; also:
  economy (a supplier's supplier in your kitchen), conflict.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- Information is surprise: a message carries exactly what its
  receiver could not already predict; the fully expected message
  carries nothing, and the same telegram weighs differently to
  different readers.
- Copying is free and perfect: the copy is the original's equal,
  and giving it away leaves you still holding it. One exception in
  all nature: a quantum state refuses cloning.
- Any computer can imitate any other, given time and memory; a new
  body adds speed, never answers. A machine that reads rules as
  data eats machines — why hardware is frozen once and software
  poured daily.
- How facts are arranged decides how fast they are found: a heap of
  papers and a labeled cabinet hold the same facts, and one hides
  them. Stored work must be maintained — an index is paid at every
  write.
- Cost grows with size in shapes, and the shape — not the machine —
  decides what is feasible (worked in SCIENCE).
- Neighbours are free and strangers expensive: the machine fetches
  whole neighbourhoods, so how data sits can beat the method.
- Keeping an answer costs space, re-deriving costs time; the
  deciding number is how often it is asked before the facts change.
  A kept answer is a fast liar: instantly right about what WAS
  true, told nothing when the world moves.
- Many hands are faster only when the work truly splits. Two hands
  on one value with no agreed order are right nearly always and
  wrong sometimes — the fault lives in the timing, not the lines;
  and two holders each waiting on the other's release wait politely
  forever.
- State is where difficulty lives: with memory inside, the same
  call answers differently for reasons elsewhere and earlier. A
  value that cannot change cannot be spoiled behind your back; a
  call that only computes can be replaced by its answer anywhere —
  why pure pieces are testable, cacheable, parallel-safe.
- A bug is the distance between intent and instruction: the machine
  executed faithfully; the fault lives in the writing.
- A passing test proves the presence of one behavior, never the
  absence of all faults — lanterns in a dark house, one room each.
- On a network, silence and slowness are indistinguishable, so
  every remote wait needs a deadline chosen in advance — it decides
  how long you will not know, never the truth. Lost answers breed
  retries, retries breed doubles: only acts where twice equals once
  survive — and when everyone retries at once, the cure finishes
  off the struggling machine.
- When the link between two halves breaks — and it will — there is
  no third door: answer and risk two honest different answers, or
  refuse until the halves can talk. The usual settlement is
  answer-now-agree-later, and the moment of disagreement is real.
- Machine clocks cannot order events across machines — drift,
  jumps, backwards corrections; order must be carried by the
  messages themselves.
- One machine is up or down; many are mostly up — this one slow,
  that one stale, a third unreachable. Partial failure is the
  weather, not the emergency (arithmetic in SCIENCE). A server
  that remembers nothing between requests can be copied, killed,
  replaced freely; memory is what makes a machine irreplaceable.
- Data outlives every program that touches it, so a stored shape
  may only ever grow: add fields, never reuse or rename, let old
  readers skip the unknown. A mistake in the data model is
  permanent the way no code mistake is.
- Security is a budget, not a wall: every lock claims only that
  breaking costs more than the secret is worth, longer than it
  matters. The opponent picks the door, so the worst-kept part is
  the whole story; the operator is weaker than the machinery, so
  the operator is attacked; physical access ends the software
  argument.
- Deleting removes the name, not the contents — which already sit
  in backups, caches, indexes, and screenshots. "Saved" usually
  means handed to a buffer; truly on disk means asked and waited.
- Who spoke to whom, when, from where, often tells more than what
  was said — and arrives pre-sorted and countable. Behaviour
  identifies: names removed from a rich record is not anonymity,
  because harmless facts combine into a person.
- A controller is one idea forever: look at wanted, look at got,
  act on the difference. Open loop assumes, closed loop checks —
  and open loop is adequate right up until anything differs from
  the day it was set.
- Delay is the enemy of every loop: a correction from old
  information arrives at the wrong moment, and past a certain lag
  it feeds the swing it fights. Every filter buys calm with lag.
- A robot's apparent intelligence is usually the environment made
  simple for it — trays, jigs, marked floors: variability removed,
  not coped with.
- The wrong thing can be built perfectly: a misunderstanding at the
  start survives every test. Wants are found by reaction, not
  interview — people cannot describe what they never used, and
  judge it the instant they see it.
- "Done" is the maker's claim; "shipped" means someone else is
  using it and it holds. The test copy is never the original — it
  proves a thing CAN work; only live proves it does. And once
  someone builds on what you published, reliance turns convenience
  into promise, carried whether you agreed or not.

## SCIENCE
The mathematics and physics of computing, held — not mentioned.
Each piece is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it can follow,
and where it earns it, a WORKED case proving the rule runs.

### MATHEMATICS — counting by twos and naming by halves

LAW: n two-state marks distinguish 2^n messages; naming one of N
  takes log2(N) marks, rounded up.
RULE: double 1 until you pass N; the doublings are the bits.
WORKED: 2^10 = 1,024 — ten bits name any of a thousand; twenty
  name any of a million (1,024 × 1,024 = 1,048,576); one card of
  52 needs six (2^5 = 32 short, 2^6 = 64 covers). A byte is eight
  bits, 256 faces — why a byte is not a letter for most of the
  world's alphabets.

LAW: computers bundle by twos — each place holds nought or one; a
  full two in any place becomes one carried leftward.
RULE: to add — work right to left; at each place count standing
  digits plus carry; set aside every full two, keep the remainder;
  when places run out, write the carry.
WORKED: 1011 (eleven) + 0110 (six): 1+0 = 1; 1+1 — write 0 carry
  1; 0+1+1 — write 0 carry 1; 1+0+1 — write 0 carry 1; carry
  lands: 10001, a sixteen and a one — seventeen. Check in tens:
  11 + 6 = 17. The machine's whole arithmetic is this rule, fast.

LAW: a message's weight is the odds turned inside out — the rarer
  the news, the more it weighs; certainty weighs nothing.
  Compression follows: the predictable part rides free, and what
  refuses to shrink IS the information — compression is prediction
  in a different hat, so how well a thing shrinks measures how
  well it is understood.
RULE: to guess compressibility, ask how much a reader could finish
  for the writer. Speech is roughly half redundant ("yu cn stll rd
  ths"); a random file will not shrink; a shrunk one barely
  shrinks again.

### MATHEMATICS — the shapes of cost (the feasibility law)

LAW: methods grow in shapes — constant, logarithmic, linear,
  n·log n, n-squared, and the whole-thing-again-per-item wall
  (2^n).
RULE: double the data and watch: unchanged, one step more,
  doubled, a bit over doubled, quadrupled, or squared out of
  reach.
WORKED — sorting a million, at a billion comparisons a second:
  · compare-each-with-each (n²): 10^6 × 10^6 = 10^12 → 1,000 s,
    about 17 minutes;
  · halve-and-merge (n·log2 n): log2 of a million ≈ 20, so
    10^6 × 20 = 2 × 10^7 → 0.02 s.
  Same machine, same items: 10^12 ÷ (2 × 10^7) = 50,000× apart. At
  a billion items: n² = 10^18 → 10^9 s ≈ 32 YEARS; n·log n =
  3 × 10^10 → 30 seconds. The machine never decided feasibility;
  the shape did.

LAW: the exponential wall — trying every combination of n choices
  costs 2^n.
WORKED: all subsets of 50 items: 2^50 ≈ 1.13 × 10^15 → 13 days at
  a billion a second; of 60: 2^60 ≈ 1.15 × 10^18 → about 37 years.
  Ten more items cost a thousandfold (2^10 = 1,024) — so a
  thousandfold faster machine wins back exactly ten items. Against
  this shape, hardware only rents.

LAW: halving reaches one from anything in log2 steps.
RULE: ask the middle of ordered ground, discard the losing half,
  repeat.
WORKED: one in a million — 20 questions; one in a billion — 30. A
  fault somewhere in 1,000 changes: 10 builds find it (2^10 =
  1,024), faster than reading code ever is. CONDITION, hard: the
  ground must be ordered — on shuffled ground the halving is not
  slow, it returns a confident wrong answer.

LAW: the comparison floor — no method that learns only by
  comparing two items sorts n of them in fewer than ~n·log2(n)
  comparisons, worst case. Proved: n items arrive in n! orders;
  each yes/no at best halves the orders still standing;
  distinguishing n! cases needs log2(n!) questions.
WORKED: 3 items → 6 orders → log2(6) ≈ 2.6 → 3 comparisons
  minimum; no method sorts every 3-item input in 2. CONDITION:
  comparisons only — digit-sorters (deal by postcode) duck under
  by assuming what keys are.

LAW: doubling a growable store costs a constant per item — the
  move halves in frequency as it doubles in size.
WORKED: growing to 1,024 by doubling from 1: copies at 1, 2, 4 ...
  512 total 1,023 — about one move per item ever held. A fair
  account of throughput, a false account of any one act: the
  unlucky insert still takes the whole hit.

LAW: keep-or-recompute is a count. Keep when (times asked before
  the facts change) × (cost to recompute) exceeds the cost to
  store and maintain.
WORKED: half a second to compute, asked 40 times a day, facts
  changing daily: 40 × 0.5 = 20 s/day against 0.5 s once — keep.
  Asked once a day while facts change hourly: stale before first
  reuse — keeping is the fast liar.

### THE PROVED WALL

LAW: no program can read every program and always foretell whether
  it finishes or loops forever — a cannot minted by proof, not
  physics; no substrate escapes it.
RULE — the construction, followable: suppose a truthful foreteller
  F. Build the contrary T: given any program text P, T asks F
  "does P, fed its own text, finish?" and does the opposite — F
  says finishes, T loops; F says loops, T stops. Feed T its own
  text: whichever answer F gives, T makes it false. F never
  existed.
CONDITIONS: the foreteller is itself a program — and by
  universality, every machine a machine can describe is one. The
  same self-reference is Gödel's unprovable truths in another
  costume. KIN: every question about what all programs will DO
  (ever print this, equal that, behave as malware) hits the same
  wall. The only universal oracle is run-and-wait, maybe forever;
  a deadline decides how long you will not know, never the truth.
  The white indexes the escape: give up loops and generality, and
  checkability returns.

### PHYSICS — memory, distance, and light

LAW: memory is layered, each layer a hundred to a thousandfold
  slower: the processor's own store ~1 nanosecond, main memory
  ~100 ns, fast disk ~100 microseconds, spinning disk ~10 ms,
  across an ocean ~100 ms.
WORKED, scaled to a human clock — call the nanosecond one second:
  memory is a two-minute walk, fast disk a day's wait, spinning
  disk about four months, a question across the ocean roughly
  three years. The processor lives at the first speed and waits at
  the others.
RULE: count passes over the data, not arithmetic — one sweep doing
  twice the sums beats three doing half. Corollary: fetches come
  as neighbourhoods (~64 bytes), so a row of boxes beats a chain
  of scattered links even when the step-count says otherwise.

LAW: a question that must go and come back costs at least twice
  the distance over the speed of light, and faster machines have
  not moved geography.
WORKED: London to New York ~5,600 km; light in fiber ~200,000
  km/s; one way 5,600 ÷ 200,000 = 0.028 s — a 56 ms round trip,
  floor forever. The cure is asking fewer times, never faster: one
  full letter beats twenty notes.

LAW: a fixed store holds a fixed count of states, so machine
  numbers wrap — one past the largest rolls quietly to small or
  negative.
WORKED: an 8-bit counter tops at 255, then 0. A signed 32-bit
  counter of seconds tops at 2,147,483,648 ≈ 68 years of seconds —
  a fuse lit at the naming of a field. RULE: before trusting any
  counter, ask what it does on the day it is full.

LAW: machine fractions are a ruler whose marks spread as numbers
  grow — precision is relative, and most decimals are endless in
  base two (one tenth in twos repeats forever): a tenth plus two
  tenths is not quite three tenths, tiny numbers vanish into huge
  ones, and the same list summed in a different order totals
  differently.
RULE: exact quantities — money, counts, doses — are stored as
  whole numbers of the smallest unit, rounding rule written in the
  specification. And a number with no unit is a rumour: a
  spacecraft was lost when one team's pound-force met another's
  newton.

### THE ARITHMETIC OF MANY MACHINES AND MANY HANDS

LAW: with many parts, the chance all are well at once falls by
  multiplication.
WORKED: 1,000 machines each 99.9% healthy: 0.999^1000 ≈ 0.37 — the
  fleet is all-up barely a third of the time. Build for partial
  failure or be surprised hourly.

LAW: redundancy repairs only when copies fail independently;
  enough of it lets majority rebuild certainty.
WORKED: a channel flipping 1 bit in 100 — say each bit three ways,
  keep the majority: wrong only when 2 of 3 flip, chance
  3 × (0.01² × 0.99) + 0.01³ ≈ 0.0003 — three in ten thousand,
  ~33× surer for 3× the length. CONDITION, hard: independence is a
  claim about the world — two disks from one batch on one power
  strip are one disk that cost twice as much.

LAW: connections among n people grow as pairs, n(n−1)/2.
WORKED: a team of 5 holds 10 lines of talk; of 10, 45. Adding
  people to a late project makes it later: newcomers are taught by
  exactly the people already behind, and each head adds a line to
  everyone. Also why parts with few, thin couplings survive:
  change travels along the lines.

LAW: a rare failure repeated often enough is a frequent event —
  judge rates per hour, not per attempt.
WORKED: 19 successes in 20, one attempt every 3 minutes: 20
  attempts an hour — one failure every hour, forever, with
  somebody standing there for it. The demo and the deployment are
  separated by this arithmetic, not by polish.

### FEEDBACK — the mathematics of acting in the world

LAW: a controller acts on the error, e = wanted − measured. The
  proportional answer pushes in proportion — and needs an error to
  push at all, so it settles short.
WORKED: a room losing 200 W per degree above outdoors, outdoors
  0°C, wanted 20°C, heater at 2,000 W per degree of error. Steady
  when heat in = heat out: 2,000e = 200 × (20 − e) → 2,200e =
  4,000 → e ≈ 1.82°C — the room holds 18.2°, never 20. Check:
  heater 2,000 × 1.82 ≈ 3,636 W; loss 200 × 18.18 ≈ 3,636 W —
  balanced, short. Double the gain: 4,200e = 4,000 → e ≈ 0.95° —
  droop shrinks, never zero; chasing it with gain buys
  oscillation.
RULE: the integral term is patience — summing the error, so a tiny
  lasting gap accumulates any size of push: how the loop lands
  exactly, and how it learns to be late. The derivative term is a
  brake on fast approach — and it listens hardest to noise, which
  is nothing but fast change. Tuning is a position on the line
  from sluggish to unstable — a statement of which you fear more.

LAW: the loop must look faster than the world moves — at least
  twice per wiggle, comfortably ten times — or it has already
  lost; the rule is the world's pace, not the computer's clock.
LAW: an accumulating term plus a maxed-out muscle is windup — the
  sum piles undeliverable demand; when the jam frees, the machine
  acts on the mountain. Cure: stop summing at the limit.
RULE: feedback waits for the mistake; feedforward spends knowledge
  — push in advance for the load you know is coming, let the loop
  keep the leftovers. Fast loops nest inside slow: torque inside
  speed inside position, each layer quicker than the one above.

LAW: a moving mass carries energy by the square of speed,
  E = ½mv².
WORKED: a 10 kg arm at 2 m/s carries ½ × 10 × 4 = 20 J; at 1 m/s,
  5 J — halve the speed, quarter the harm. Working beside machines
  is bought with lighter limbs and lower speeds; and a stiff limb
  turns tiny position errors into huge forces (force = stiffness ×
  bend), so deliberate give is what makes contact survivable.

### SECURITY — locks made of math

LAW: a key's strength is the size of the space it was drawn from;
  attacker's cost = space ÷ guesses per second. Length multiplies
  space faster than any cleverness.
WORKED: eight random lowercase letters: 26^8 = 208,827,064,576 ≈
  2 × 10^11 — about 21 seconds at ten billion guesses a second.
  Four random words from a 7,776-word list: 7,776^4 ≈ 3.7 × 10^15
  — about 4 days at the same rate. Store only a deliberately slow,
  salted fingerprint (each guess a millionfold dearer): ~12,000
  years.
  Salt makes a million accounts a million separate jobs; slowness
  prices each guess.
RULE: bounding attempts is cheaper than bounding the secret — a
  4-digit code is 10,000 possibilities, and behind three tries and
  a lockout the thief's chance is 3 ÷ 10,000 = 0.03%. Why a bank
  card works at all.

LAW: the one-way door — easy forward, hopeless backward.
  Multiplying primes is a blink: 61 × 53 = 3,233; recovering them
  from the product has no known fast road, and at working size
  (~600-digit products) the sun dies first. UNSURE at bedrock,
  stated honestly: undefeated, not proven — the bet the locks lean
  on (the white holds the open question and the quantum
  condition-mover).
RULE: the two-part lock — publish a lock anyone can snap shut,
  keep the only key: strangers who never met now keep secrets. Run
  backwards it proves origin instead of hiding content — sealed
  with the private half, checked by anyone, bound so tightly to
  the exact text that one changed letter breaks it.

LAW: a fingerprint (hash) maps anything to a short fixed tag —
  same input same tag, one letter changed an unrelated tag, no
  road back: check, compare, chain things without holding them.
  CONDITION: the fast kind (spreading keys in tables) and the
  forgery-proof kind (identifying content) are different tools
  wearing one name; the fast one where the strong belongs is a
  whole class of breach.

RULE: secrecy lives in the key, never the design — assume the
  method published, because it will be; a key rotates in a second,
  a design in a million devices never. Trust a cipher only because
  many skilled strangers attacked it in public for years and
  failed. And a key must be cancellable — the hard half is
  reaching every door that still honors it before the thief does.

## METHODS
How things are done and built here — each with what governs it,
when it applies, and what breaks it.

- BREAK THE TASK DOWN: split until every step is an act already
  known — the breaking IS the design, and a step that will not
  break names exactly what knowledge is missing. Order is part of
  the procedure: the same steps rearranged are a different one,
  usually wrong. Every repetition needs a stopping test that must
  someday answer yes — a loop is a promise held each turn plus
  something moving toward an end; break either half and it never
  stops.
- CHOOSE THE ARRANGEMENT BY THE QUESTIONS: the row for reaching by
  number, the chain for splicing, the table for instant finding by
  key (order surrendered), the tree for order kept while finding,
  the heap for only-the-most-urgent, the graph for what the facts
  really are. Breaks when: a tree fed sorted input becomes a chain
  wearing a tree's name; a table's keys are chosen by an enemy to
  collide; order is bought where no question needs it.
- SORT FIRST, THEN EVERYTHING IS EASIER: in ordered ground,
  finding is halving, duplicates are neighbours, grouping is one
  pass, two sets merge in one walk. No sort wins everywhere —
  insertion on tiny or nearly-ordered data; merging steady,
  tie-keeping, working past memory; the quick method fastest in
  practice and worst on hostile input; toolkits switch mid-run.
  Ties keep their order only if the sort was stable.
- DIVIDE, CONQUER, JOIN: split into like pieces, solve each the
  same way, join; the heaviest of the three steps decides
  everything. Breaks when the join eats the gain.
- TAKE THE GREEDY STEP only where the best-looking step can never
  become the regretted one — there, no search is needed. Breaks
  without the property: coins of 1, 3, 4 make six greedily as
  4+1+1 — three coins where two threes did, and nothing in the run
  reports anything wrong.
- WRITE ANSWERS DOWN when the same small problem repeats inside
  the big one: the impossible collapses into a table. CONDITION:
  the best whole must build from best parts — shortest routes do,
  longest simple routes do not, and no table fixes that.
- TRY, UNDO, PRUNE: walk a choice; at the dead end undo exactly
  and take the next — a full search holding only the path. What
  makes it finish is the abandoning: prove a branch empty and one
  test deletes millions of futures — a cut near the root removes a
  product, not a sum. Watch the stack: thousands of frames, not
  millions — recursion per item dies on the real file.
- RANDOMIZE THE CHOICE where a chooser could aim your worst case:
  the bad input still exists but cannot be pointed at you.
- MEASURE, THEN MAKE FAST: nearly all the time hides in a tiny
  part, and nobody guesses which correctly. Make it work, make it
  right, then speed the one place the measurement condemned.
  Breaks reversed: complexity bought early for guessed speed is
  paid at once and forever.
- VALIDATE AT THE DOOR, FAIL LOUD AND NEAR: check what enters
  once, where outside meets inside; past the wall all is known
  good. A fault caught at birth names its own cause; swallowed, it
  surfaces far away as nonsense. Absence is a real answer needing
  its own shape. Everything opened must be closed on the failing
  path too — precisely the path nobody rehearses.
- MAKE THE WRONG STATE UNSAYABLE: the strongest check is a shape
  that cannot express the fault — a plug fitting one way, a result
  that will not open without answering both cases. Nothing to
  remember, nothing to drift.
- TEST AS LANTERNS, SCAR EVERY FIX: every fault fixed leaves the
  test that would have caught it — the system's memory of a pain
  suffered, so the wound cannot reopen. A piece hard to test is
  the shape complaining: too much world, too many jobs, hidden
  inputs — the clock, the random source, the network are inputs
  even unnamed; hand them in or no test can pin the piece.
- DEBUG AS AN EXPERIMENT: reproduce first — a fault you cannot
  summon you cannot prove fixed. Strip everything that leaves it
  standing; most bugs confess at the cut that stops them. Believe,
  test the cheapest thing that could prove you wrong, cut, repeat.
  If it worked before, the fault has a birthday: halve the
  history. Know which you hold: a wrong line in a right shape is
  fixed where found; a right line in a wrong shape grows new bugs
  at every patch, each patch correct.
- KEEP EVERY CHANGE WITH ITS HISTORY: any mistake can be walked
  back; delete dead code freely — history keeps it, and dead code
  still costs reading, belief, and mistaken edits. Branches are
  private futures that drift: short ones merge, old ones fight —
  join the shared line constantly, let a machine build and test
  every change. Review's larger work: a second head now knows this
  corner exists.
- SHIP SMALL, BORING, REVERSIBLE: the smallest complete thing that
  runs end to end, then grown — working large systems are grown
  from working small ones. Releases small, frequent, automatic;
  wake the new thing for a few first (faults announce in minutes —
  the question is the blast radius); a switch separates shipping
  from turning on; the last good version is one rehearsed step
  away. Data changes wall off the exit: stage them — add new,
  write both, move readers, remove old. Stamp what runs, so "what
  is live" has an exact answer.
- WATCH WHAT RUNS: a live system shows only what it was built to
  show — design the showing in; logs are the entire evidence
  afterwards. Wake a person only for what a person must fix now —
  false alarms teach everyone to sleep through the true one. Write
  the three-in-the-morning procedure for a tired stranger. After
  failure, hunt the cause, not the culprit — blame teaches people
  to say less — and the review's product is a change, or the
  outage is bought again at full price. A backup never restored is
  a hope with a filename: restore on schedule, timed.
- GUARD BY BUDGET AND DEPTH: least privilege (each permission is
  also the intruder's), rights to roles not persons, layers each
  assumed to fail, the patch clock (publishing the fix publishes
  the flaw), the count of secret-holders tracked, time-to-notice
  watched hardest — incident and catastrophe differ by how long
  the intruder walked unseen. Breaks when security obstructs the
  work: people route around it, and the system is less safe than
  before.
- CONTROL BY LOOP: close the loop wherever the world varies from
  setup day. Command position OR force knowingly — position is
  precise and blind and will crush what is in the way; force is
  gentle and cannot hold a place. Home at power-on: a relative
  reading means nothing until tied once to a known mark. Plan
  paths smooth in speed and acceleration too — a sharp corner
  demands infinite force; the smooth machine looks slower and is
  faster. Structure the environment before making the machine
  cleverer. Safety never rests on the program being right: a
  separate circuit, a fence, a stop the software cannot argue with
  — and a watchdog outside the program to notice its silence,
  because a faulty system cannot detect its own fault.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE PROCESSOR'S LOOP: fetch, decode, do, move the marker —
  billions a second. The world interrupts mid-loop; the machine
  saves its place, handles, resumes — how a thing that only
  marches forward also reacts.
- THE MEMORY LADDER, priced in SCIENCE: hand, shelf, cellar,
  warehouse. The craft of speed is mostly staying near the top.
- THE OPERATING SYSTEM: referee and door — programs ask for
  anything real by knocking and being carried across; turns handed
  out so fast the smoothness is made entirely of turns. One level
  up, the same trick: packaged worlds (containers, virtual
  machines) carrying their surroundings, so a program meets the
  same world everywhere.
- THE LANGUAGES AND THEIR BILLS: translate-in-advance buys tight
  code and complaints before running; translate-as-you-go buys a
  fast first answer and complaints from users; the third road
  watches what runs hot and translates that, live. The checker
  must refuse some good programs — a gate that turns nobody away
  is a hole. Memory's end is decided by hand (fast; yours are the
  corpses), by tally (prompt; cycles never die), by sweeper (safe;
  it pauses you), or by proof before running (free at runtime;
  paid in what you may not write). Concurrency's three houses —
  shared memory with hand-taken turns, message rooms with nothing
  shared, values handed down a pipe with ownership moving — each
  decides what CAN go wrong. Failure returns as a flying exception
  (clean path, invisible forks), a returned code (visible,
  ignorable), or a box that must be opened both ways — the
  settlement most new languages reach. Null, the value answering
  every claim falsely — its inventor's own billion-pound
  accounting; absence as its own case is the repair. Little
  languages that cannot loop are the safe ones: what cannot be
  written cannot be got wrong.
- VERSION CONTROL: every hand's work kept whole, every change with
  author and why, two futures joined deliberately.
- THE DATABASE'S TRANSACTION, four promises: all or none; the
  rules still hold at the end; two at once behave as if one went
  first; confirmed survives the power cut. What lets money move by
  machine.
- THE HONEST INSTRUMENTS: the profiler (where time actually goes),
  the log that left the machine that wrote it (an editable log is
  not evidence), the test suite, the reproducible build — same
  source, same program, any machine, or nobody knows what runs.
- SENSORS AND MUSCLES: the sensor decides what the machine can be
  good at; everything unmeasured drifts free. Fusion is not
  averaging — believe each sensor where it is strong; two that
  disagree need a rule, because the average of a right answer and
  a wrong one is wrong. Motors rate how hard AND how long: a joint
  holding a heavy arm still draws full current doing nothing —
  many robots' most demanding act is stillness, and they overheat
  doing it. Backlash and stiction are dead zones where commands do
  nothing; everything flexes, and a loop pushed near the
  structure's own rhythm feeds it.
- THE RANDOM SOURCES: harvested physical noise, seeded with care —
  a predictable draw shrinks the key space to one.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Rule-following preceded electronics: bureaucracies computed on
  paper (their cruelties are bugs run faithfully), looms read
  cards, the player piano performed a score complete enough to
  need no pianist. Logic mechanical at the bottom was in hand
  before any machine could exploit it.
- The wall was proved before the machines were built: the halting
  cannot was minted by pencil, from self-reference, in the same
  stroke that showed one machine could imitate all. The field knew
  a hard limit before it had a working engine — its walls are
  proved into place, not discovered by bumping.
- The free jump was surrendered: early programs leapt anywhere,
  and a program full of jumps has no shape a reader can hold.
  Three shapes — sequence, choice, repetition — cost nothing in
  power (proved) and bought readability; the discipline was the
  feature.
- The null convenience was adopted everywhere and later named by
  its own inventor the billion-pound mistake: a value fitting
  every claim falsely, crashing programs from a distance for a
  human lifetime. Its repair — absence as its own case, forced
  open by the checker — is the model lesson: a shape beats a rule
  everyone must remember.
- Secrecy moved out of the design and into the key, paid for in
  broken wartime traffic: systems safe by hiddenness died the day
  one machine was captured. Publish the method, rotate the key,
  trust only what public attack has failed to break — that record
  compressed to a rule.
- Copying became free and law met it unprepared: property built
  for loaves fits copies badly; songs needed new law where bread
  never did. The strain is still live (the white holds it).
- The machines got a millionfold faster and the distances did not
  move: the light-floor arithmetic inverted the craft from saving
  arithmetic to saving trips — caches, batches, queues, and the
  one-letter-not-twenty rule are that inversion written out.
- Robots mastered the factory and stalled at the doorstep: the
  mechanism has been excellent for a long time; what defeated the
  machines was the world's refusal to repeat itself. The factory
  answer — make the world repeat — works by narrowing, and the
  unnarrowed world is still mostly unclaimed (the white holds the
  record).

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward. Each names
what runs along it.

- MATHEMATICS: the father — counting in twos, the growth shapes,
  log's halvings, n! and the comparison floor, probability for
  keys and failures, induction for loops, self-reference for the
  wall. Every SCIENCE law above is mathematics in work clothes.
- PHYSICS: the landlord — no bit without a body, finite stores
  wrap, distance costs time and light sets the floor, energy by
  the square of speed. What the landlord signs binds every tenant
  machine.
- ELECTRONICS: the substrate — the two-state switch made cheap and
  fast, sensor noise, the stalled motor's heat. The laws would
  hold on brass gears; electronics is why they run a billion times
  a second.
- ENGINEERING: shared method — margins, interlocks, the separate
  safety circuit, failure modes chosen or defaulted to physics,
  the weakest point (here, searched for on purpose).
- LANGUAGE: meaning by agreement — the same bits are a photograph
  or gibberish by convention; protocols are grammar between
  strangers; names are claims readers believe over the code; a
  byte is not a letter, and alphabetical order is each language's
  own decision.
- MEASUREMENT: a quantity is a number AND a unit; the profiler
  against the guess; repeatability is not accuracy; a stated error
  bound is what makes an approximation an answer.
- LAW: computation on paper — registries beating goodwill,
  permissions as rights attached to persons and things, published
  interfaces binding like reliance, logs as evidence only when the
  actor cannot edit them.
- ECONOMY: every trade priced — space against time, nines against
  money, shortcuts compounding as debt, security as budgets,
  features paying rent forever.
- BODY: the original loops — temperature, balance, blood sugar;
  two kidneys as redundancy; skin as the validation boundary;
  reflexes nested under decisions as fast loops nest in slow.
- MIND: attention as the scarce resource syntax taxes; recognition
  over recall (wants found by reaction); the interleaving
  explosion no head can hold; vigilance decaying exactly as
  automation improves.
- LIFE: DNA as code with error correction built in — each strand
  the other's backup; every cell carrying the whole rulebook with
  different pages running — the universal machine, found by
  biology first.
- MUSIC: the score as program, the player piano, one melody on any
  instrument as universality, notation as syntax nobody reinvents.
- COOKING: the recipe as the algorithm's oldest costume; mise en
  place as the structured environment; counter-cupboard-cellar as
  the memory ladder.
- CONFLICT: the adversary who chooses the door — worst-case
  judgement, randomization as defence, traffic analysis, the
  operator attacked instead of the mathematics.
- GOVERNANCE AND PEOPLE: the referee pattern, roles over persons,
  blameless review because information flows only where safe to
  give, coordination rules worth exactly their followers.
- TEACHING: the newcomer's first safe change as the honest measure
  of a design; review as mutual instruction; the familiar form
  freeing the mind for content.
- CRAFT AND DESIGN: arrangement as stored work, one tool one job,
  the jig doing the thinking, boring consistency read faster than
  scattered brilliance.
- TRANSPORT AND MOVEMENT: packets as parcels, queues as sorting
  offices, legs against wheels priced in watts and pavement,
  jerk-limited motion as ride quality.
