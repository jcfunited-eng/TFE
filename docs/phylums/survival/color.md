## THINGS
The units this subject is about; other homes named so a cascade can
branch.

- THE RESERVES — the body's margins in four sizes: a breath of
  air, a night of core heat, days of water, weeks of fat. The
  whole subject is managing these accounts; also: body, medicine,
  economy.
- THE BODY'S FURNACE — ~100 watts at rest, several hundred working
  or shivering; everything else defends this small fire; also:
  body, physics, cooking (the same first law).
- FIRE — the wilderness multi-tool: one skill yields heat, light,
  dry clothes, safe water, cooked food, a signal, and courage;
  also: chemistry, cooking, story (the hearth, the first address).
- WATER, WILD — streams, dew, rain, snow, the low places;
  drinkable only after the boil or by luck; also: cooking, earth
  (drainage), weather, life (what swims in it).
- SHELTER — trapped still air between skin and sky: clothing,
  bedding, roof, windbreak — one thing at four distances; also:
  building, physics, animals.
- THE LAND — organized by gravity around its drains: slope,
  trickle, stream, river — the map always with you; also: earth,
  transport (rivers collect people).
- THE SKY — sun and stars as the clock and compass nobody can
  take away; also: astronomy, measurement, weather.
- THE HANDRAIL — any reference that does not move: river, ridge,
  coast, held bearing; progress is real only along one; also:
  measurement (a standard is a handrail).
- TRACKS — the ground's memory: weight, direction, gait, and time
  pressed into soil and snow; also: animals, earth (strata are
  tracks with deep ages), law (a fingerprint is a track indoors),
  computing (logs are tracks).
- THE MIND UNDER FEAR — the first tool and the first casualty;
  panic's opening command is always "move," and it is almost
  always wrong; also: mind, teaching (drills rewrite it).
- THE KIT — knife, container, cordage, cover, fire-starter: five
  small masses standing in for a house; also: craft, design.

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query.

- The rule of threes orders the dying, so it orders the working:
  ~3 minutes without air, ~3 hours without shelter in killing
  weather, ~3 days without water, ~3 weeks without food. Rough
  spans, exact order — the numbers move with conditions (cold
  water shrinks three hours toward thirty minutes); the sequence
  does not.
- Shelter before thirst, thirst before hunger: the cold night
  kills tonight, the dry throat this week, the empty belly next
  month. Build, then drink, then hunt — hunger is the loudest
  account and the longest.
- Lost means stop: sit down the moment you know. Walking
  multiplies the error, spends the heat and water budgets, and
  moves you off the searchers' map (the square law in SCIENCE).
- Wet plus wind outkills bare cold: water strips heat ~25× faster
  than still air, and wind carries off the warm layer as fast as
  skin rebuilds it. Dry and windless beats warmer-but-wet.
- Stillness chosen is strategy; stillness suffered is death
  arriving. Stopping when lost, freezing before a motion-keyed
  predator: chosen, correct. The unchosen freeze against a clock
  (cold, fire, water) spends the deadline — the mirror's record.
- Water runs downhill, and so does the way home: trickle to
  stream to river; rivers collect water from uphill and people
  from downhill.
- A rolling boil is a certificate you can see: by the roll, the
  killing work on what lives in the water is done — at any
  altitude a walker can reach (SCIENCE holds the margin).
- Navigation is handrails: position exists only against fixed
  things, and the walker carries no fixed point inside. Unguided
  legs veer; the mirror holds the circling receipts.
- Tracks read back for hours or days: pressed matter stays
  pressed, and sun, wind, and rain erase slowly enough that the
  fading itself tells the age.
- Ration sweat, not water: the canteen does no cooling; only
  drunk water does. Sipping to save the bottle dehydrates the
  body to protect it.
- Signals work by threes and contrast: three blasts, three fires
  — nature makes no deliberate threes, few straight lines, and
  searchers see motion. Fire signals to yourself too: fear
  shrinks in firelight, and a lit camp changes what can be
  planned.

## SCIENCE
The math and physics of staying alive, held — not mentioned: LAW,
RULE, and WORKED with real numbers run to a real answer.

### THE TRIAGE ARITHMETIC (the rule of threes, worked)

LAW: the four reserves in one unit (minutes): air ~3 · core heat
  in killing weather ~180 · water ~4,320 (3 × 1,440) · food
  ~30,240 (21 × 1,440). Rung to rung: 60×, 24×, 7× apart.
RULE: fight the nearest deadline; spend hours only from accounts
  that hold them.
WORKED: one hour of shelter-and-fire work, priced per account:
  against the 3-hour heat account, 60 ÷ 180 = a third of the
  budget — it must buy the night back; against the food account
  (21 × 24 = 504 h), 1 ÷ 504 ≈ 0.2% — nearly free. An evening on
  shelter and none on food is not a mood; it is this division.

### PHYSICS — the body's heat budget

LAW: cooling = mass × heat-capacity × degrees dropped (cooking's
  Q = m·c·ΔT run backward on a person); body c ≈ 3.5 J/g°C;
  the furnace makes ~100 W at rest, ~300-500 W shivering.
RULE: time to serious cold = joules from core 37°C to impaired
  32°C ÷ NET drain (loss minus make).
WORKED: 70 kg: 70,000 × 3.5 = 245,000 J per degree; five degrees
  = 1,225,000 J. Soaked in wind (lose ~400 W, make 100, net 300):
  1,225,000 ÷ 300 ≈ 4,083 s ≈ 68 min. Dry, still, clothed (net
  ~50 W): 1,225,000 ÷ 50 = 24,500 s ≈ 6.8 h. Same night, same
  cold — wet-and-windy is six times faster; "three hours" sits
  between these numbers, which is why it says "in killing
  weather."

LAW: the three thieves, with multipliers — touch: water conducts
  ~25× better than still air, and cold ground under a sleeper is
  the same thief; flow: wind replaces the warmed layer at the
  skin — 0°C air at 30 km/h strips heat like still air at ~ −6°C;
  evaporation: each gram drying off skin or cloth carries ~2,400
  J — the thief the body HIRES as sweat.
RULE: block them in order of size — dry, out of wind, off the
  ground, then loft.
WORKED: a liter of sweat left in clothing, dried by body heat:
  1,000 × 2,400 = 2,400,000 J — at net 100 W, 24,000 s ≈ 6.7
  hours of the entire resting furnace spent drying laundry.
  "Vent before you sweat" is this number, avoided.

### PHYSICS AND CHEMISTRY — fire

LAW: fire is a triangle — heat, fuel, air — dead when any side is
  removed. Wood ignites near ~300°C, burning as the gas it gives
  off when heated.
RULE: build a ladder of sizes — tinder (gram-scale fluff),
  kindling (finger-thick), fuel — each stage must heat the next
  to ~300°C with the energy it has.
WORKED: 1 g of dry tinder from 20°C to ignition: 1 × 1.7 × 280 ≈
  476 J — one match's gift. A 100 g stick needs 100× that; no
  match holds it, only burning kindling does. The ladder is
  arithmetic, not custom.
WORKED (damp tinder refuses): at 30% moisture each gram carries
  0.3 g water — heating it to boil costs 0.3 × 4.18 × 80 ≈ 100 J,
  boiling it off 0.3 × 2,257 ≈ 677 J: a 777 J water tax on the
  476 J dry bill, 2.6× the price, and the match dies mid-payment.
  So: carry tinder dry, or cut to the dry heart of standing dead
  wood.

### THE WATER ARITHMETIC (finding, spending, making safe)

LAW: the body spends ~2-3 L/day at rest in shade, ~1 L per hour
  walking hard in heat; losing ~10% of body weight in water
  disables, ~15% kills.
WORKED: a 70 kg person's 10% line is 7 L. Resting in shade at
  2.5 L/day: 7 ÷ 2.5 = 2.8 days — the rule's "three days" is this
  division. Walking desert noon at 1 L/h, the same 7 L lasts 7
  hours: sit by day, move at night is the same liters at two
  rates.
LAW: safety is cooking's pasteurization trade outdoors — the
  wild's stomach-wreckers die in under a minute by ~70°C; the
  rolling boil is the visible certificate, no thermometer.
RULE: bring to a rolling boil; at the roll, done.
WORKED (altitude margin): the ceiling falls ~1°C per 300 m; at
  3,000 m water rolls at 100 − (3,000 ÷ 300) = 90°C — still 20
  degrees above the kill line. The certificate holds on any
  mountain a walker meets.
WORKED (fuel price): a liter from a 10°C stream to the roll:
  1,000 × 4.18 × 90 = 376,200 J. Dry wood ~16 MJ/kg, ~10%
  reaching the pot → 376,200 ÷ 1,600,000 ≈ 0.24 kg. A double
  handful of dry wood buys a safe liter — fire and water are one
  problem.
RULE (finding): walk downhill — gravity has organized the land
  around its drains; game trails converge on water at dusk and
  dawn; dew collects by morning; green in dry country flags
  water beneath.

### MATHEMATICS — navigation and search

LAW: an unguided walk spreads; search area grows with the radius
  SQUARED: area = π × (speed × time)².
WORKED: a lost walker at 3 km/h: after one hour, π × 3² ≈ 28 km²;
  after four, π × 12² ≈ 452 km² — sixteen times the ground for
  four times the walking. Sitting still holds the circle at a
  point. "Lost means stop" is this square, refused.
LAW: the sun crosses at 15° per hour (360 ÷ 24).
RULE (shadow-stick compass): plant a stick, mark the shadow tip,
  wait 15 minutes, mark again — the line between marks runs
  west→east (first mark west, everywhere on Earth); with west on
  your left you face north in either hemisphere.
WORKED: 15 minutes moves the sun 15 × (15 ÷ 60) = 3.75° — enough
  to walk the tip a hand's width and draw the line.
RULE (night's fixed point): the Big Dipper's two outer bowl stars
  point at the North Star, five of their spacings away; it alone
  does not wheel, and its height is your latitude — at 40°N,
  four fists at arm's length up (a fist ≈ 10°).
LAW (pricing a walk): flat ground ~5 km/h; each 600 m of climb
  adds about an hour.
WORKED: 10 km with 600 m climb: 10 ÷ 5 = 2 h, + 1 = 3 h. Plan
  daylight with this sum, not hope. Distance is counted, not
  felt: double paces ~1.5 m each → 1,000 ÷ 1.5 ≈ 667 per km.

## METHODS
How things are done here — what governs each, what breaks it.

- STOP, THEN PLAN (before all others): sit, breathe until
  arithmetic returns, take stock, observe, plan. Breaks when
  skipped — every method run in panic is run badly.
- SHELTER BUILD (dead air, in thief order): off wet, out of wind,
  off the ground, then loft — insulation below twice as thick as
  above; roof before any furniture. Breaks when walls get the
  effort while wind pours through, or a fine roof covers bare
  ground.
- THE FIRE LADDER: gather three sizes before one match; shield
  from wind. Breaks when stages are skipped or fuel is damp (the
  water tax, computed above).
- THE BOIL: any vessel, a rolling roll, done. A single-wall metal
  bottle boils directly; no vessel — hot stones dropped in a
  bark or hide hollow do the same work slowly.
- FINDING WATER: downhill, the green, converging game trails at
  dusk; catch rain on spread cover; sweep dawn dew from grass
  with a cloth and wring it.
- NAVIGATING BY HANDRAILS: pick a reference that cannot move and
  check it often; cross open ground by leapfrogging two aligned
  marks — held in line they make the straight walk legs alone
  cannot. Breaks when the handrail is traded for a shortcut.
- WALKING OUT DOWNHILL: when walking is chosen at all, follow
  water down — rivers collect people. Breaks in gorge-cut
  country; then ridges and handrails serve.
- SIGNALING: threes, contrast, motion — smoke by day (green
  boughs on flame), bright fire by night, a mirror flash, big
  straight symbols stamped in open ground.
- TRACK READING: weight from depth, direction from toe-push, gait
  from spacing, age from crumbling edges. Runs backward as
  self-rescue: your own tracks are a handrail home while the
  ground holds them.
- RATIONING BY SWEAT: shade by day, work at dawn and dusk, move
  at night in hot country; drink when the body asks and spend
  EFFORT slowly.
- LAYERING AND VENTING: dress in shells of trapped air and open
  them BEFORE work heats you — sweat banked in clothing is the
  6.7-hour bill computed above.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE KNIFE — cooking's force-÷-area law outdoors: dry tinder
  from wet wood's heart, cordage from bark, every other means
  from the land.
- THE CONTAINER — the boil needs a vessel; a single-wall metal
  bottle is canteen and kettle. No container, no cheap safe
  water: it outranks food in any kit.
- CORDAGE — the shelter's joints; bought, or twisted from inner
  bark at a real cost in hours.
- COVER — a tarp is a roof in one minute against a bough hut's
  three hours: same shelter, two prices, chosen by the heat
  account's balance.
- FIRE-STARTERS, REDUNDANT — matches, lighter, spark rod: two
  ways minimum; grams of cost against the account they defend.
- WOOL vs COTTON — wool keeps most of its trapped air soaked; wet
  cotton keeps almost none (~90% of insulation gone) and dries
  at the wearer's expense. "Cotton kills" is a materials claim,
  and this is its phylum.
- THE WHISTLE AND THE MIRROR — a whistle outlasts a voice by
  hours; a flash carries tens of kilometers in sun: the two
  cheapest grams in the kit.
- THE BODY ITSELF — always present: ~100 W of heat, pace-counting
  legs, hands that make, a mind that plans. Every other means is
  a lever on this one.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- GET THROUGH THE NIGHT: the three-hour account defended —
  shelter, fire, dry.
- DRINK SAFELY: the three-day account refilled without buying
  sickness that spends it faster (the boil; the mirror holds the
  false shortcuts).
- BE FOUND, OR GET HOME: visible-and-patient, or handrails and
  drainage walked out — two strategies, chosen once and early.
- KEEP THE FURNACE FED AND SHIELDED: the heat budget managed;
  food is the slow log on this fire.
- KEEP THE MIND IN COMMAND: fear as advisor, not driver — the
  fire's second job, and teaching's door.
- READ THE LAND AND ITS TRAFFIC: water, direction, food, danger —
  from slope, sky, green, and tracks.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost.

- The species' oldest curriculum: every law here was paid for
  before writing, and the failed learners left no notes, only
  absence. Fire is older than our species; the hearth was the
  first address, and cooking was born on it.
- Star and land navigation carried people across every ocean and
  desert before instruments — coast, then stars, then clocks:
  the whole history of navigation is better handrails.
- Armies wrote the first manuals in blood: mass war put city men
  into wild places and counted what killed them; the world wars'
  lifeboat ledgers turned "don't drink seawater" from proverb
  into measured law (the mirror holds the numbers).
- Search-and-rescue records turned lore into statistics — where
  lost people actually walk, how the circle grows; stay-put
  stopped being an opinion when the data was compiled.
- The children's version proved the structure teachable: "hug a
  tree" — stop, stay, be findable — cut child deaths by
  installing one claim in six-year-olds. A core that fits a
  frightened child and still holds for an army rests on real law.
- The false chapters were expensive and are kept: cut-and-suck
  kits, seawater rations, freeze-or-flee guesses — each tried
  for decades, measured, and moved to the mirror WITH receipts,
  where a failed method becomes knowledge.
