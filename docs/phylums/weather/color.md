## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- THE SUN — the engine of everything below; every wind, cloud, and
  storm is sunlight being redistributed; also lives in: physics
  (radiation), farming (the crop's paymaster), life.
- AIR — the working fluid: four-fifths nitrogen, one-fifth oxygen,
  a trace of water vapor that does most of the interesting work;
  also: physics (gas law), cooking (leavening), the body (breath).
- WATER IN THREE COATS — vapor, drop, and ice, changing coat
  constantly and carrying huge energy in each change; also: cooking,
  chemistry, life, the body.
- PRESSURE — the weight of all the air overhead; the number the
  barometer reads and storms are built of differences in; also:
  physics, cooking (the boiling ceiling), engineering.
- WIND — air leveling a pressure difference, bent sideways by the
  planet's spin; also: physics, economy (the trade winds carried
  the trade), engineering (loads on everything built).
- CLOUDS — floating water, condensed where rising air crossed its
  dew point; each shape is a diagnosis of the air that made it;
  also: physics (phase change), mathematics (the forecast's hardest
  part — see the mirror).
- FRONTS — the boundaries where unlike air masses meet and fight;
  most mid-latitude weather is the news from these borders; also:
  physics (fluids).
- STORMS — thunderstorm, hurricane, winter cyclone: engines that
  cash in stored evaporation energy fast; also: physics, economy
  (the loss ledger), engineering (what must stand).
- THE OCEAN — the planet's flywheel: slow to heat, slow to cool,
  smoothing every swing the land would otherwise suffer; also:
  life, economy (shipping), chemistry (dissolved CO₂).
- MOUNTAINS — the sky-wringers: they force air up, take its water
  on one side, and send it down dry on the other; also: farming
  (which valley is green), culture (why cuisines differ across one
  ridge).
- ICE AND SNOW — the bright lids: they reflect sunlight the dark
  ground would drink, cooling their own neighborhood — a feedback
  running both ways; also: physics, the ice ages, farming.
- THE SPIN — Earth's rotation, once a day: invisible until air
  travels far, then it bends every long journey and organizes
  storms into spirals; also: physics (momentum), mathematics.
- SEASONS — the tilt's yearly consequence (23.4°), not distance:
  Earth is nearest the sun in northern winter; also: farming, life
  (migration), culture (every calendar).
- CLIMATE — the long average of weather at a place: the standing
  answer that writes crops, houses, and cuisines; also: farming,
  economy, culture, cooking (the preservation map).
- LATENT HEAT — the hidden cargo: energy banked when water
  evaporates, paid back when it condenses; the currency every storm
  is paid in; also: physics, cooking (the steam burn), the body
  (sweat).

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- The sun heats the planet unevenly — equator more than poles, land
  faster than sea, day more than night — and ALL wind is the
  leveling of that unevenness. It is kitchen convection at planet
  size: the same rising-warm, sinking-cool loop as a heated pot,
  with continents for pot walls.
- Warm air rises because it is lighter (the density arithmetic is
  in SCIENCE), and rising air cools by expansion: ~10°C per km of
  climb while clear, ~6°C per km once cloud forms — the wet rate is
  gentler because condensing vapor pays its stored heat back.
- Warm air holds more water — capacity roughly doubles per 10-11°C.
  This one law explains why warm storms are wetter, why cold
  places are dry even by the sea, and why heated winter rooms
  parch (the worked numbers are in SCIENCE).
- Rain needs LIFT. Four lifters exist: sun-heated ground
  (convection), mountains, fronts, and winds converging. No lift,
  no rain — the great deserts sit under air that is sinking.
- Mountains wring the sky: the windward side takes the water, the
  lee side gets the rain shadow, and the descending air arrives
  warm and dry (the chinook/foehn — the same air, minus its water,
  plus its returned heat). One ridge can separate ~2,500 mm of
  yearly rain from ~250.
- Pressure falls with height on a logarithm: halves about every
  5.5 km, never reaching zero. Near sea level, 1 hPa of fall ≈ 8 m
  of climb — the barometer doubles as an altimeter.
- Wind does not run straight from high to low pressure: the spin
  bends it (rightward north of the equator, leftward south), so
  air CIRCLES pressure centers — counterclockwise into northern
  lows. Rule of thumb that follows: back to the wind in the north,
  the low is on your left.
- The bending vanishes at the equator; hurricanes cannot organize
  within ~5° of it and never cross it.
- Hurricanes run on warm sea water: they need ~26.5°C or better,
  tens of meters deep, and die over land or cold water within
  days — cut off from the evaporation payroll.
- Mid-latitude weather travels west to east (the jet stream
  steers it); tropical weather travels east to west (trade winds).
  This is why "red sky at night" works in the mid-latitudes — a
  clear west means clear is coming — and fails in the tropics,
  where tomorrow comes from the east.
- The ocean smooths the land's swings: coasts get mild winters and
  cool summers, continental hearts get savage of both. In January,
  western Norway sits near freezing while deep Siberia at the same
  latitude sits near −40°C — same sun, different flywheel.
- The hottest part of the year lags the longest day — by roughly a
  month over land, longer over sea — because temperature follows
  the running BALANCE of heat, not the day's income; the flywheel
  arithmetic in SCIENCE prices the lag.
- Snow needs the whole air column below freezing; a warm layer
  aloft melts the flakes and delivers freezing rain instead —
  the worse weather, from one buried degree.
- Fresh snow is mostly air: ~10 cm of snow melts to ~1 cm of
  water (fluffy cold snow up to 20:1, wet snow near 5:1) — the
  shovel's weight and the reservoir's arithmetic in one ratio.
- The daily forecast is real and bounded: skill has gained about a
  day of range per decade for forty years, and dies entirely past
  about two weeks — arithmetic, not engineering; the certificate
  with its numbers is the mirror's crown entry. The AVERAGE
  escapes the bound: climate stays predictable when days are not,
  as a die's average is knowable and its next roll is not.
- Climate writes the ground rules of living: what grows (farming),
  what keeps (cooking's preservation map), what houses look like,
  when anything is planted or sailed. Move the average two degrees
  and every one of those answers is re-asked.

## SCIENCE
The math and physics of weather, held — not mentioned. Each piece
is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it can follow, and where
it earns it, a WORKED case proving the rule runs.

### PHYSICS — the uneven fire

LAW: sunlight arrives at ~1,361 W/m² at the top of the air (the
  solar constant). Spread over the whole spinning sphere it
  averages a quarter of that, ~340 W/m²; after ~30% reflects off
  cloud, ice, and dust, ~238 W/m² is kept.
LAW: a tilted surface catches beam × cos(tilt from overhead).
RULE: to price a latitude or a season — take the noon sun's angle,
  cosine it against overhead, and that fraction is the heating per
  square meter.
WORKED: at 60° latitude the noon sun in the annual mean stands
  ~60° from overhead: cos 60° = 0.5 — half the equator's heating
  per square meter, every day, for the age of the planet. That
  standing surplus at the equator and deficit at the poles is the
  entire wind system's job description: move the difference.
WORKED (seasons): at 45° latitude, midsummer noon sun stands 68.4°
  above the horizon, midwinter 21.6° (45 ± the 23.4° tilt). The
  ground catches by the sine: sin 68.4° ≈ 0.93 against sin 21.6°
  ≈ 0.37 — 2.5× the heating per square meter, before the longer
  day is even counted. The seasons are this cosine arithmetic,
  nothing else; distance runs the WRONG way (nearest sun in
  northern winter) and loses.

LAW: everything warm radiates heat as the fourth power of its
  temperature: out = 5.67×10⁻⁸ × T⁴ W/m² (T in kelvin). A planet
  settles where radiated out = absorbed in.
WORKED: absorbed ~238 W/m² → T⁴ = 238 ÷ 5.67×10⁻⁸ = 4.2×10⁹ →
  T ≈ 255 K ≈ −18°C. That is the bare-rock answer. The measured
  average is ~+15°C. The 33-degree difference is the greenhouse
  blanket — water vapor first, CO₂ second — absorbing outgoing
  heat and returning a share. The blanket itself is measured fact;
  the exact warming per ADDED blanket is the mirror's bounded
  unknown, filed there with its bounds.

### PHYSICS — the buoyancy engine

LAW: air's density follows pressure over temperature:
  ρ = P ÷ (287 × T), kg/m³, pascals, kelvin.
RULE: to find which air floats — compute both densities; the
  lighter column rises, the heavier slides under it.
WORKED: sea-level air at 15°C (288 K): 101,325 ÷ (287 × 288) =
  101,325 ÷ 82,656 ≈ 1.23 kg/m³. The same air over sun-baked
  ground at 45°C (318 K): 101,325 ÷ 91,266 ≈ 1.11 kg/m³ — about
  10% lighter. That 10% is the whole vertical economy: thermals,
  cumulus, thunderstorms, the sea breeze, the monsoon — every one
  is this subtraction, run at a different size.

LAW: rising air cools by expansion at ~9.8°C per km while clear
  (call it 10); ~6°C per km once condensing, because every gram of
  vapor that turns to droplet pays back its stored ~2,450 J.
RULE: cloud base sits where rising air cools to its dew point —
  height ≈ (temperature − dew point) × 125 m per °C.
WORKED: surface air 30°C, dew point 18°C: (30 − 18) × 125 =
  1,500 m. Every fair-weather cumulus over that countryside bottoms
  at ~1,500 m — the flat cloud bases all at one height are this
  multiplication drawn across the sky, because the whole field
  shares one dew point.
WORKED (what a cloud weighs): a modest cumulus of 1 km³ carries
  ~0.5 g of liquid water per m³: 10⁹ m³ × 0.5 g = 5×10⁸ g = 500
  tonnes — airborne, held up by an updraft of a few m/s. The sky
  routinely suspends the mass of a laden train.

LAW: instability is a race of cooling rates. Air forced up cools
  at 10 (clear) or 6 (cloudy) per km; if the surrounding air gets
  colder with height FASTER than the rising parcel does, the
  parcel stays warmer than its surroundings and keeps rising —
  self-propelled. That is a thunderstorm's permission slip.
RULE: steep environmental cooling + moist surface air = storms;
  a warm layer aloft = a lid (the parcel arrives cooler than the
  lid and stops — stable haze, no storm until the lid breaks).

### PHYSICS — the water cycle's bank (latent heat)

LAW: evaporating water banks ~2,450 J per gram at sea and skin
  temperatures; condensing pays it all back. (Cooking holds the
  same law at the boil, where it is 2,257 — same physics, warmer
  branch of the curve; both numbers are right at their own
  temperature.)
RULE: to price a storm — grams of rain it drops × ~2,450 J each;
  all of that was collected earlier, silently, by sunshine
  evaporating sea and soil.
WORKED: a single ordinary thunderstorm drops 2 cm of rain on a
  5 km × 5 km patch. Water: 25×10⁶ m² × 0.02 m = 5×10⁵ m³ =
  5×10⁸ kg = 5×10¹¹ g. Energy: 5×10¹¹ × 2,450 ≈ 1.2×10¹⁵ J.
  A large 1-gigawatt power station would need 1.2×10¹⁵ ÷ 10⁹ =
  1.2×10⁶ seconds ≈ 14 DAYS to deliver what that one afternoon
  storm cashes in an hour. A hurricane runs the same ledger
  ~1,800 storms wide (that hour-long storm averaged 3.4×10¹¹ W;
  6×10¹⁴ ÷ 3.4×10¹¹ ≈ 1,800): latent heat released at ~6×10¹⁴ W,
  roughly 200× humanity's entire electric output — the number
  that certifies the mirror's storm-control entries.

LAW: warm air holds more vapor — saturation, in grams per cubic
  meter: 0°C → ~4.8 · 10°C → ~9.4 · 20°C → ~17 · 30°C → ~30.
  Roughly doubling per 10-11°C.
RULE: cool saturated air below its dew point and the excess MUST
  leave as liquid — subtract the two capacities.
WORKED (the rain machine): saturated 20°C sea air (17 g/m³) forced
  up a mountain and cooled to 10°C (capacity 9.4): every cubic
  meter sheds 17 − 9.4 = 7.6 g as cloud and rain. A moist wind
  delivers millions of cubic meters a second, hour after hour —
  the rain shadow is this subtraction, industrialized. The lee
  side receives the same air minus its 7.6 g and plus its returned
  latent heat: warm, dry, and thirsty.
WORKED (backwards — the winter room): 0°C outdoor air (4.8 g/m³)
  brought inside and heated to 20°C (capacity 17) now holds 4.8 of
  a possible 17 = 28% relative humidity. Nothing was removed; the
  capacity grew. Cracked lips, static sparks, and split furniture
  are this division, and the humidifier is its refund.

### PHYSICS — the logarithm of thin air

LAW: pressure is the weight of the air overhead, and it halves
  roughly every 5.5 km of climb: P ≈ P₀ × 2^(−height ÷ 5.5 km).
  It thins forever and never reaches a hard top.
WORKED (what the column weighs): sea-level pressure 101,325 Pa ÷
  9.8 = ~10,300 kg pressing on every square meter — over ten
  tonnes, unfelt only because it pushes equally from inside.
RULE: to price altitude — count the halvings: height ÷ 5.5, then
  2 to that power.
WORKED: Everest's summit, 8.85 km: 8.85 ÷ 5.5 = 1.61 halvings;
  2^1.61 ≈ 3.0 — one-third of sea-level pressure, one-third of the
  oxygen in every breath. The summit does not starve climbers by
  cold; it starves them by this exponent.
WORKED (the cooking ceiling, priced from this side): with less
  pressure overhead, water boils sooner — the ceiling falls ~1°C
  per 300 m. Denver (1,600 m): boils at ~95°C. Everest base camp
  (5,300 m): ~82°C — tea never gets properly hot. The summit:
  ~71°C — a "boiled" egg is a 71°C egg and the quick-kill safety
  numbers in cooking's ladder are out of reach wet. Cooking holds
  this law from its side; weather is WHY the law is true.

### PHYSICS — the spin

LAW: Earth turns once a day, so the ground at the equator rides
  east at 40,075 km ÷ 24 h ≈ 1,670 km/h, and slower toward the
  poles by the cosine of latitude. Air keeps its eastward speed
  when it travels — and outruns or lags the ground it arrives
  over. Result: paths bend right in the north, left in the south.
WORKED: equatorial air carried to 30° latitude arrives over
  ground moving 1,670 × cos 30° = 1,670 × 0.866 ≈ 1,446 km/h. The
  air still carries ~1,670 — it runs ~220 km/h eastward past the
  ground beneath. (The full momentum accounting adds more; the
  simple difference already gives the direction and the size.)
  That eastward river is the jet stream's seed — the 100-250 km/h
  high road that steers mid-latitude weather and hands eastbound
  airliners an hour.
RULE: the bending is why pressure differences don't just fill in:
  moving air curves until it circles the center — counterclockwise
  into northern lows, clockwise out of highs, mirrored south. And
  it is why the great surface winds are belts: trades blowing
  west in the tropics, westerlies blowing east in mid-latitudes.
LAW (the desert belt): equatorial air rises, rains itself dry,
  travels poleward aloft, and SINKS at ~30° latitude — arriving
  dry and warming on the way down (the 10°/km law in reverse).
  The Sahara, the Kalahari, the Australian interior, the great
  hot deserts of both hemispheres sit in that belt of used air —
  geography written by a convection cell.

### PHYSICS — the ocean flywheel (worked against land)

LAW: heat stored per square meter per degree = depth engaged ×
  density × heat capacity. Water: c = 4.18 J/g·°C, stirred tens of
  meters deep by wind and waves. Dry ground: c ≈ 0.8, and heat
  crawls in barely 2 m over a season.
WORKED: ocean, 50 m engaged: 50 m³ = 5×10⁷ g per m²; × 4.18 =
  2.1×10⁸ J per m² per °C. Land, 2 m of soil at 1,600 kg/m³:
  3.2×10⁶ g; × 0.8 = 2.6×10⁶ J per m² per °C. Ratio ≈ 80. The sea
  needs ~80× the heat to move one degree — so the same summer sun
  that drags a continent's interior through 30-40°C seasonal
  swings moves the sea surface a few degrees. Every mild coast,
  every savage continental winter, every lag of August behind the
  June solstice is this ratio.
RULE (the daily version — sea breeze): by afternoon the land is
  hot and the sea is not; the land's air column is lighter (the
  buoyancy subtraction above: ~1.17 vs ~1.21 kg/m³ for a 10°C
  difference), it rises, and sea air slides in underneath — the
  onshore afternoon wind of every coast on Earth. At night the
  race reverses and the breeze turns around.
RULE (the yearly version — monsoon): the same race at continent
  size. Asia's interior heats through spring (small flywheel),
  pulls in ocean air all summer (the wet monsoon), then cools
  fast in autumn and exhales dry land air all winter. A billion
  farms are scheduled by the flywheel ratio.
- The ocean also carries heat sideways: warm currents run poleward
  along the western sides of oceans (the Gulf Stream among them),
  part of why northwestern Europe winters green while Labrador,
  across the same ocean at the same latitude, freezes.

### THE DROPS AND STONES (how water actually falls)

LAW: a cloud droplet (~0.01 mm radius) falls ~1 cm/s — any breath
  of updraft holds it; that is why clouds float. A raindrop (~1 mm
  radius) falls ~6.5 m/s. The radius ratio is 100, so the VOLUME
  ratio is 100³ = a million: every raindrop is the merger of
  ~a million cloud droplets.
RULE: rain is therefore a two-step problem — making cloud is easy
  (lift and cool), making the million-to-one merger is the hard
  step, helped by ice crystals aloft and collisions in deep
  clouds. Thin clouds can sit all day and never rain: no depth,
  no mergers. (What governs the merger's slowest step is a named
  absence in the mirror.)
LAW: a hailstone grows only while an updraft can carry it, so the
  stone is a measurement of the wind that made it: fall speed ≈
  what the updraft had to beat. A 2 cm stone falls ~20 m/s — the
  storm that grew it was blowing 70 km/h straight UP. Golf-ball
  hail testifies to ~30 m/s updrafts. Read the stone, know the
  engine.

### MATHEMATICS — the forecast

LAW: tomorrow's weather is computable: carve the air into boxes,
  apply the laws above (motion, heat, moisture) to each box, step
  forward minutes at a time, repeat. This is not a metaphor — it
  is what every forecast center does, and skill has gained about
  one day of useful range per decade for forty years.
LAW: but small errors in the starting picture DOUBLE every ~2-3
  days as they ride the flow. The doubling makes a hard horizon at
  ~two weeks for daily particulars — the arithmetic is run in
  full in the mirror, as a certified cannot-be with conditions.
RULE (the method chaos forced — ensembles): run the model ~50
  times from slightly-jiggled starting pictures. Where the runs
  agree, trust; where they fan apart, report the odds. The
  forecast is a probability ON PURPOSE; a rain chance is the fan's
  width, not a hedge.
RULE (the free baselines): "tomorrow = today" (persistence) and
  "the date's long average" (climate) cost nothing; any forecast
  that cannot beat both is decoration. Climate is itself the
  second baseline — the average stays computable long after the
  particulars die, which is why crop planning outlives the
  ten-day forecast that serves it.

## METHODS
How things are done here — each with what governs it, when it
applies, and what breaks it.

- READING THE SKY (the oldest method, still sound): clouds are
  diagnoses. Flat sheets = stable air lifted gently (a warm front
  a day out — the high ice-halo is its first page); towers =
  instability (the race of cooling rates, running); an anvil top =
  the tower hit the stratosphere's lid and spread; cloud bases
  lowering and pressure falling = the low is coming. Breaks when:
  read in the tropics with mid-latitude proverbs (the steering
  reverses).
- READING THE BAROMETER AND WIND: a fall of ~2-3 hPa in three
  hours announces a low; back to the wind (north hemisphere), the
  low lies on the left — a bearing on the storm from a porch.
  Governed by the spin's circling law; breaks near the equator
  where the circling law quits.
- MEASURING THE COLUMN: thermometer, barometer, hygrometer at the
  ground; the radiosonde balloon carrying them upward twice a day
  from ~900 stations worldwide — the world agreeing to probe the
  sky at the same two hours is what makes a shared starting
  picture possible at all.
- SEEING THE WHOLE BOARD: radar reads rain by echo and wind by
  Doppler shift; satellites watch every ocean basin — since the
  mid-1960s, when coverage became global, no hurricane anywhere
  has arrived unannounced. Weather data
  crosses even hostile borders by standing agreement: the starting
  picture is indivisible, so hoarding spoils it for the hoarder.
- FORECASTING BY ARITHMETIC: the boxes-and-laws method above.
  Governed by the doubling law; run as ensembles; degrades
  gracefully — day 1 sharp, day 5 useful, day 10 tendencies,
  day 15 climate. Breaks when: sold as certainty at ranges where
  the fan is wide (the mirror holds the broken-trust record).
- FORECASTING THE SEASON (the flywheel road): the ocean's slowness
  is memory — a warm or cool tropical Pacific (El Niño / La Niña)
  tilts the odds of whole seasons months ahead. Delivers ODDS on
  averages, never dates; strongest near the tropics, weak in
  continental interiors. Breaks when: asked for a particular day
  — that is the certified wall.
- WARNING (the lives method): watch = conditions ready; warning =
  it is happening — a two-step so the first word is early and the
  second is sure. Hurricanes give days (evacuate), tornadoes ~13
  minutes (shelter — the short fuse is a mirror entry), floods
  hours. Each is tuned to what its physics allows.
- READING PAST CLIMATE (the archive method): tree rings (a rain
  gauge per year), ice cores (bubbles of the actual old air — CO₂
  read directly back 800,000 years), corals, sediments. Governs
  "this decade against the ages"; breaks when a proxy's own
  biology is misread — hence several proxies, agreeing, or the
  claim waits.
- BUILDING TO THE STATISTICS: engineering sizes drains, roofs, and
  dams to the rare worst — the "hundred-year" rain read off the
  record's tail. Governed by climate-as-statistics; its standing
  crack (the statistics have started moving) is in the mirror.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE INSTRUMENTS: barometer (Torricelli, 1643 — air has weight
  was a DISCOVERY), thermometer, hygrometer, anemometer, rain
  gauge. Cheap, old, and still the ground truth every satellite
  is calibrated against.
- THE BALLOONS: radiosondes to ~30 km twice daily worldwide; the
  vertical column they read (the two cooling rates, the lids, the
  moisture) is the storm forecast's raw material.
- THE RADARS: rain echo + Doppler wind; the means behind the
  tornado warning's minutes and the downpour's county-by-county
  map.
- THE SATELLITES: first frames 1960, the whole planet in every
  frame since the mid-1960s;
  infrared reads cloud-top heights (colder = taller = stronger)
  by the 6°/km law run backwards.
- THE OCEAN'S OWN THERMOMETERS: ships, moored buoys, and ~4,000
  drifting floats profiling the upper kilometers — reading the
  flywheel directly; the seasonal forecast's memory lives here.
- THE COMPUTERS: the boxes-and-laws arithmetic outran human hands
  by millions (the by-hand attempt and its failure are the
  mirror's cleanest drain story). Boxes shrink as machines grow;
  clouds still fall between them — a named absence in the mirror.
- THE SHARED PICTURE: one starting field assembled from all the
  above, exchanged worldwide twice daily — the largest standing
  scientific cooperation there is, and the quiet precondition of
  every forecast anyone sees.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- KNOW TOMORROW: shelter, sow, sail, or stay home — the oldest
  question asked of the sky, now answered usefully out to about a
  week, in odds to two.
- WARN OF THE KILLER: hurricane, tornado, flood, heat wave,
  freeze — days of notice where the physics is slow, minutes where
  it is fast; the unwarned centuries' death tolls (the mirror
  keeps that ledger) are the measure of this purpose won.
- CHOOSE WHAT AND WHERE TO GROW: climate is farming's standing
  answer — frost dates, growing-season heat, rain's timing. The
  crop map IS a climate map (farming's door).
- ROUTE THE SHIP AND THE PLANE: winds as roads — the trade winds
  that carried centuries of commerce, jet stream riding, storm
  avoidance.
- MANAGE THE WATER: dam, well, irrigation schedule, flood plain
  map; snowpack as the summer's water read in spring (the 10:1
  ratio cashed).
- HOLD THE LONG BOOK: the climate record as the planet's ledger —
  what is drifting, how fast, and what that re-prices (farming,
  economy, engineering, culture all enter by this door).

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Lore first: proverbs as compressed statistics — red sky, halo
  before the warm front's rain. The ones that encoded real fronts
  survive testing; the ones that encoded wishes are in the mirror.
  Lore was the forecast for all but the last two centuries.
- The barometer (1643): air found to have weight, then the falling
  glass found to PRECEDE the storm — the first instrument reading
  of tomorrow; prophecy becoming measurement.
- The map (1850s): weather outruns a horse but not a wire — the
  telegraph made the same-hour map possible, and storm-warning
  services followed within years (their founder's story, paid in
  full, is in the mirror).
- The physics (1900s-1920s): the Bergen school named fronts —
  trench-war language for the borders where air masses fight — and
  weather became fluid mechanics with a geography.
- The arithmetic dream (1922): one man computed a forecast by hand
  — six weeks of sums for six hours ahead, and wrong. The failure
  carried the method: his boxes-and-laws IS the modern forecast,
  waiting for machines (the mirror's cleanest drain).
- The satellite (1960): the whole board seen at once; hurricane
  landfalls stopped being ambushes where the pictures reached.
- Chaos (1963): a rerun from rounded-off numbers wandered into
  different weather entirely — the two-week wall found by
  accident, certified in the mirror, answered in the color by the
  ensemble method: the fan admitted on purpose.
- The long ledger (1958-): CO₂ measured rising year over year
  against the ice cores' 800,000-year book (180-300 ppm for all of
  it; ~425 now); the greenhouse arithmetic moved from curiosity to
  the century's re-pricing. The exact sensitivity stays
  bounded-not-pinned — the mirror holds it honestly.

## RELATIONS
First-class connections — the cascade's paths outward. Each names
what runs along it.

- PHYSICS: the whole engine room — radiation and T⁴ balance,
  buoyancy, gas law, latent heat, the spin's momentum, expansion
  cooling. Weather is physics run on a spinning, wet, unevenly
  heated ball; nearly every LAW above is physics wearing weather's
  clothes.
- COOKING: the boiling ceiling travels this ribbon (altitude sets
  cooking's wet thermostat — worked in both phylums, each in its
  own terms); climate wrote the preservation map (what dried,
  what salted, what fermented, by what the local air allowed);
  the solar oven's condition is weather's to state.
- FARMING: climate picks the crop before the farmer picks the
  seed; frost dates bracket the season; rain's timing is worth
  more than rain's total; drought is the harvest subtracted.
  The season forecast (odds, months) and the frost warning
  (hours) both travel this ribbon.
- LIFE: every species is a standing bet about its climate;
  migration rides the seasons; the rain shadow decides forest or
  scrub on each side of the ridge.
- BODY: sweat runs on the latent-heat law (~2,400 J carried off
  per gram evaporated) — humid air blocks that only radiator,
  which is why the heat INDEX is the honest number and a wet-bulb
  reading near ~35°C is unsurvivable outdoors at any fitness;
  wind chill is the same physics run cold.
- MEDICINE: heat waves and cold snaps kill on schedule; winter's
  dry indoor air (the 28% arithmetic above) and indoor crowding
  carry the infection season; pollen rides the wind's calendar.
- ECONOMY: harvests are weather monetized; insurance prices the
  storm tail; heating and cooling demand track degree days;
  shipping followed the trade winds so faithfully that wind and
  commerce came to share the name (the sailors' word first meant
  a steady track); the forecast itself is among the cheapest
  high-return public goods ever built.
- CULTURE: the gods of rain and thunder came first (the mirror
  holds the rituals' record); the calendar is the seasons written
  down; cuisines remember their climate (through cooking); weather
  remains the small talk that bonds strangers — shared, blameless,
  new daily.
- ENGINEERING: everything built stands in the statistics — wind
  loads, drain sizes, dam heights read off the record's tail (the
  moving-statistics crack is in the mirror); the instruments and
  satellites are engineering's gifts back.
- MATHEMATICS: the forecast IS applied mathematics — fluid
  equations in boxes, probability as the honest output, statistics
  as climate's definition, and the doubling law that certifies
  the horizon; chaos was DISCOVERED here and given to mathematics.
- CHEMISTRY: the greenhouse gases' absorption bands (why CO₂ and
  water vapor blanket), ozone made and unmade aloft, acid rain —
  a broken sky-chemistry relation measured, priced, and largely
  repaired by treaty within one generation: the standing proof
  that some sky problems close.
- ANIMALS: migration rides the seasons; beasts read pressure before instruments.
- ASTRONOMY: the seasons are astronomy's shadow on the ground.
- BUILDING: wind load, rain, and frost — buildings are weather's opponents.
- CONFLICT: winter defeated armies that generals could not.
- EARTH: mountains make rain shadows; the ground shapes the sky's habits.
- ELECTRONICS: lightning and the forecast's computers — mutual traffic.
- FLIGHT: weather is flight's daily judge — wind, ice, and storm.
- MOVEMENT: outdoor sport is played against the sky's conditions.
- PHILOSOPHY: the two-week forecast wall is a lesson in knowable limits.
- PLANTS: climate writes what grows where; frost dates rule gardens.
- SURVIVAL: exposure kills faster than hunger — weather is survival's clock.
- TRANSPORT: storms close ports and passes; the schedule bends to sky.
- UPKEEP: weathering is decay's outdoor form; paint and gutters fight it.
