## THINGS
The units this subject is about. Each sits in many phylums at once;
its other homes are named so a cascade can branch.

- SUNLIGHT — the only payer; every calorie anyone has ever eaten
  was first caught by a leaf; also lives in: physics (radiant
  energy), weather, life, cooking (the food it became).
- SOIL — not dirt but a living bank: minerals, dead matter, and a
  workforce of billions per gram running the deposits and
  withdrawals; also: life, chemistry, engineering (foundations),
  weather (dust).
- WATER — the crop's bloodstream and its biggest single cost;
  measured in millimeters that are secretly tonnes; also: weather,
  physics (phase change), cooking, the body.
- SEED — a lunchbox with instructions: stored energy plus the plan,
  and the compounding instrument of the whole subject; also: life
  (heredity), economy (the sowing reserve), mathematics
  (compounding made flesh).
- NITROGEN — the wage every protein is paid in; four-fifths of the
  air and almost none of it spendable, because the two atoms hold
  each other with a triple grip; also: chemistry, the body
  (protein), economy (the fertilizer trade), weather (lightning
  fixes a little).
- THE GRASSES THAT FEED THE WORLD — wheat, rice, maize: three
  domesticated grasses carry most of humanity's energy; also:
  cooking (the staples), economy (the price of bread is the price
  of order), culture, history.
- LEGUMES — pea, bean, clover, alfalfa, soy: the plants that hire
  bacteria to mine the air for nitrogen and pay them in sugar;
  also: life (symbiosis), chemistry, cooking.
- LIVESTOCK — animals as converters: grass and scraps humans
  cannot eat, into food and work and manure they can use; also:
  the body, economy, culture, medicine (shared diseases).
- PASTURE AND GRASS — the crop that harvests itself into the
  animal; grows from the base, so grazing is a haircut, not an
  execution — unless the crown is torn; also: life, weather.
- MANURE — the withdrawal partly returned; the oldest fertilizer
  and the proof the books can be rebalanced; also: chemistry,
  economy (once fought over), medicine (also a disease route).
- PESTS, WEEDS, AND BLIGHTS — every other eater at the table; a
  weed is any plant whose payroll you don't collect; also: life
  (evolution runs in them fastest), medicine, economy.
- SOIL MICROBES AND RHIZOBIA — the unseen staff: decomposers
  releasing last year's wages, root-partners trading nitrogen for
  sugar; also: life, chemistry, medicine (antibiotics came from
  soil).
- POLLINATORS — the unpaid workforce fruit and seed depend on;
  wind serves the grasses, bees serve the orchards; also: life,
  economy (hives trucked between crops), weather.
- THE SEASON — a window with two frost walls and a light budget;
  the scheduling law nothing on the farm escapes; also: weather,
  physics (the tilted axis), culture (the calendar is farming's
  invention).
- THE GRANARY — the harvest half-won; food across time instead of
  space; also: cooking (same keeping laws), economy (stored grain
  is stored wealth), engineering, history (writing began as its
  ledger).
- LAND — area as the multiplier on everything above; the one input
  not manufacturable; also: economy (rent), culture (inheritance),
  history (most wars).

## CLAIMS
What holds, written to be tested by a crossing query. Worth is
never absolute — it is applicability to the query at hand.

- All food is captured sunlight. The farm is a factory whose only
  supplier is the sky; soil, water, and nitrogen decide how much of
  the delivery gets caught (see the payroll arithmetic in SCIENCE).
- Yield obeys the scarcest input, not the sum of inputs — the
  barrel fills to its shortest stave. Pouring on more nitrogen when
  water is the shortage buys nothing. Condition: find the stave
  before spending.
- The nutrient books must balance. Every cart leaving the field
  carries nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium off the farm; what
  leaves must come back — from air (legumes), rock, bag, or manure
  — or yields fall on schedule. The quantities are in SCIENCE.
- Selection answers only what is heritable. Feed the parent, and
  the seed learns nothing; choose the parent, generation after
  generation, and any heritable trait moves. Every crop and breed
  on Earth is this claim, run for centuries (the wolf-to-dog story;
  the equation is in SCIENCE).
- Traits move only if they are seen. Selection improves what is
  measured and lets the unmeasured drift — the shadow side of the
  claim above; the mirror holds its receipts (lost flavor).
- Water is measured in millimeters but spent in tonnes: one
  millimeter of rain on one hectare is ten tonnes of water. In dry
  country, yield tracks rain almost line for line (the formula is
  in SCIENCE).
- Heat is a budget, not a date. A crop needs its sum of warm days
  (degree days), and the variety must fit the window between the
  frosts — planting by the calendar alone fails whenever the year
  runs cold or the seed traveled north.
- Some crops read the clock by the night, not the thermometer:
  day length triggers their flowering. Move such a variety far
  north or south and it blooms on the wrong date regardless of
  warmth. Condition: latitude is part of the variety.
- Rotation starves the specialist. A pest or disease that must
  find its host every year dies in the year the host is elsewhere;
  a field in the same crop forever compounds its own enemies.
- Uniformity trades resilience for convenience. One variety across
  a region is one lock for every door — whatever picks it, takes
  everything. The mirror holds the paid receipts (potato, banana,
  corn blight).
- Dry grain keeps, wet grain rots: below ~14% moisture and cool,
  the granary's microbes and insects are locked out of free water
  — the same law the kitchen's jam and jerky run on, at field
  scale.
- Grass grows from the base: graze the blade and it returns; rip
  the crown by overgrazing and the pasture dies. Rest is part of
  the harvest.
- Soil is destroyed faster than it is made — plowed slopes can
  lose in one year what nature builds in ten or more (quantities
  in SCIENCE). Keeping soil covered is keeping the bank.
- Animals lose most of the energy passed through them: roughly
  nine parts in ten of the grain fed to cattle never reaches the
  eater. Condition on the claim's use: animals on land or scraps
  that could not feed people directly are addition, not loss.
- One farmer feeding many is the precondition of every other
  trade. Every scribe, smith, and stonemason eats a surplus
  somebody else grew; the depth of that ratio is the width of a
  civilization.

## SCIENCE
The math, physics, and chemistry of farming, held — not mentioned.
Each piece is a LAW a query can compute with, a RULE it can follow,
and where it earns it, a WORKED case proving the rule runs.

### PHYSICS AND CHEMISTRY — the payroll of light

LAW: photosynthesis stores sunlight as sugar.
  6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂.
  Dry plant matter holds ~17 MJ per kg; grain as eaten ~14 MJ/kg.
  Field crops catch ~1% of the season's sunlight as biomass (the
  theoretical ceiling, ~4-6%, lives in the mirror as a named gap).
RULE: to size a hectare's payroll — season days × sunlight per
  square meter per day × 10,000 m² × the ~1% catch, then divide by
  17 MJ/kg for biomass; halve biomass for grain (modern varieties
  put ~half their weight into seed — the harvest index).
WORKED: temperate season, 150 days at ~18 MJ/m²/day:
  150 × 18 = 2,700 MJ/m²; at 1%: 27 MJ/m², × 10,000 =
  270,000 MJ/ha stored; ÷ 17 ≈ 16 tonnes of dry matter; × 0.5 ≈
  8 tonnes of grain — which is exactly what a good wheat or maize
  hectare yields. The arithmetic and the field agree.

LAW: a person runs on ~10 MJ/day, ~3,650 MJ a year.
RULE: to count who a field feeds — grain mass × 14 MJ/kg ÷ 3,650.
WORKED: the 8-tonne hectare: 8,000 × 14 = 112,000 MJ ÷ 3,650 ≈ 30
  person-years of energy on a grain diet. Route it through feedlot
  beef at ~10:1 energy loss and the same hectare feeds ~3. The
  trophic toll is a division, and it is this one.

LAW: energy in equals energy out for animals too — a working horse
  delivers ~750 W (one horsepower, by definition of the unit); a
  laboring person ~100 W sustained.
WORKED: a 100 kW tractor = 100,000 ÷ 746 ≈ 134 horses ≈ 1,000
  human laborers of raw power on one seat. This single division is
  the emptying of the countryside, computed.

### THE WATER ARITHMETIC

LAW: 1 mm of rain on 1 hectare = 10 m³ = 10 tonnes of water.
  (0.001 m × 10,000 m² = 10 m³.)
RULE: to turn any forecast into tonnage — millimeters × 10 per
  hectare.
WORKED: a 25 mm storm delivers 250 tonnes to every hectare; a
  season of 400 mm delivers 4,000 tonnes. No pump on the farm
  moves what one honest rain does.

LAW: in water-limited country, grain yield follows transpired
  water: yield ≈ (season rain − ~110 mm lost to bare evaporation)
  × ~20 kg grain per hectare per mm (wheat's measured rate).
RULE: to price a dry season before it happens — subtract the ~110,
  multiply by 20; that is the ceiling water sets, whatever else is
  spent.
WORKED: 400 mm season: (400 − 110) × 20 = 5,800 kg/ha — call it
  5.8 tonnes. And the water bill: 290 mm transpired = 2,900 tonnes
  of water for 5.8 tonnes of grain — ~500 tonnes of water per
  tonne of grain through the plant alone, ~700 counting the
  field's whole 400 mm (4,000 ÷ 5.8), and nearer 1,000 in the
  world's books once irrigation losses join. A loaf is a bathtub,
  prepaid.

LAW: lifting water costs energy = mass × 9.8 × height (joules,
  kg, meters).
RULE: to price irrigation from a well — tonnes × 9.8 × meters of
  lift, in kJ; divide by 3,600 for kWh; real pumps roughly double
  it.
WORKED: 3,000 tonnes lifted 10 m: 3,000,000 × 98 J = 294 MJ ≈ 82
  kWh at perfection, ~160 kWh in practice. Deep water is expensive
  water, and this is the meter.

LAW: irrigation methods waste by their nature — of water sent,
  the crop's root zone receives roughly: flood ~50-60%, sprinkler
  ~75%, drip ~90%.
WORKED: to deliver 300 mm to the crop: drip pumps 300 ÷ 0.9 =
  333 mm (3,330 t/ha); flood pumps 300 ÷ 0.5 = 600 mm (6,000 t/ha)
  — nearly double the water for the same crop.

### THE SOIL BANK AND THE NITROGEN WAGE

LAW: a hectare's plow layer (20 cm at ~1.3 t/m³) weighs ~2,600
  tonnes. Organic matter is ~5% nitrogen; the living staff
  release (mineralize) ~1-3% of the organic store per year.
RULE: to read a field's own nitrogen paycheck — soil mass × %
  organic matter × 0.05 × the release rate.
WORKED: 3% organic matter: 2,600 × 0.03 = 78 t of organic matter;
  × 0.05 = 3,900 kg of nitrogen in the vault; at 2% release =
  ~78 kg of nitrogen per year paid out free. That is most of a
  modest crop's wage — and why organic matter IS the bank balance.

LAW: protein is ~16% nitrogen (multiply nitrogen by 6.25 to get
  protein, divide to go back). Wheat grain at ~12% protein is
  therefore ~2% nitrogen.
RULE: to compute what the cart removes — tonnes of grain × 20 kg
  nitrogen; add ~4 kg phosphorus and ~5 kg potassium per tonne.
WORKED: the 8-tonne harvest carries off 8 × 20 = 160 kg of
  nitrogen. The field's free 78 falls short by ~82 — the gap must
  come from clover, manure, or the bag, or next year's ceiling
  drops. The books, balanced in public.

LAW: legumes fix air's nitrogen through hired bacteria in root
  nodules — a good clover or alfalfa year banks ~150-250 kg
  nitrogen per hectare; fresh cattle manure carries ~0.5%
  nitrogen, about half spendable in year one.
WORKED: 20 t/ha of manure = 100 kg nitrogen, ~50 available now —
  so manure alone balances a small crop, and clover balances a
  large one. Rotation (see METHODS) is this law made schedule.
LAW (the factory route): N₂ + 3 H₂ → 2 NH₃ at ~450°C and
  hundreds of atmospheres over an iron catalyst — air's triple
  grip broken by brute conditions. Roughly half the nitrogen in
  living human bodies came through this reaction.

LAW: erosion outruns formation. Nature builds ~1 t/ha of soil a
  year (~0.08 mm); 1 mm of soil over a hectare weighs ~13 tonnes.
WORKED: a plowed slope losing 13 t/ha/yr loses a millimeter a
  year: the 2,600-tonne, 20 cm bank is spent in ~200 years while
  formation replaces ~1/13th of the loss. Cover, roots, and
  terraces are the only arithmetic that closes this ledger.

LAW: soil acidity is a logarithm (each pH step is 10×); most
  crops bank best near pH 6-7; ground limestone neutralizes acid
  at roughly 2-5 tonnes per hectare per pH step (texture decides
  within the range).

### THE SCHEDULING LAW — seasons and degree days

LAW: crops ripen on summed warmth, not elapsed days.
  Degree days = Σ over days of max(0, (Tmax + Tmin)/2 − Tbase);
  maize counts from base 10°C, wheat from ~0°C. A maize variety
  carries a budget (~1,100-1,500 degree days by maturity class).
RULE: to fit a variety to a place — sum the place's typical
  degree days between frosts; buy a variety whose budget fits
  inside, with room for a cold year.
WORKED: a day of high 28°C, low 16°C: mean 22, minus base 10 =
  12 degree days. A 1,400-degree-day hybrid at that pace: 1,400 ÷
  12 ≈ 117 days. If the frost-free window is 130 days, it fits;
  if the region only averages 10/day, it needs 140 and does NOT —
  the shorter-season variety is not a preference but a subtraction.

LAW: frost kills by ice in the cell; blossom dies around −2°C.
  Water freezing releases 334 J/g — the same number the kitchen
  pays to melt ice, running in reverse as a shield.
RULE: to hold an orchard through a radiation frost — sprinkle
  continuously; as long as water keeps freezing on the bud, the
  ice-water mix sits pinned at 0°C, above the kill line. Stop
  early and evaporation drives it below air temperature: the
  method's condition is "until thaw," not "until dawn."

### SELECTION — the wolf-to-dog arithmetic

LAW: response to selection = heritability × selection gap.
  R = h² × S: keep parents better than the average by S; the next
  generation moves by h² of it (h² commonly 0.2-0.6 by trait).
RULE: to breed anything — measure the trait, keep the top for
  seed, repeat; gains per generation are small and never stop.
WORKED: save seed from ears 20 g above the field's mean, trait
  heritability 0.4: next year's mean rises 0.4 × 20 = 8 g. Modest
  — until compounded (next law).

LAW: gains compound. gain over n generations ≈ (1 + r)ⁿ.
WORKED: maize, ~1.6 t/ha in the 1930s to ~11 t/ha ninety years
  on is ~7×; at 2% a year, 1.02⁹⁰ ≈ 5.9× — the record ran a shade
  above 2%, compounded without a single miracle year. Teosinte to
  maize is the same law with a bigger n: a hard-cased spike of
  ~5-12 kernels became a 500-kernel ear over ~9,000 years of
  farmers keeping the best. Every crop is a wolf-to-dog story;
  the equation above is the whole plot.

LAW: the seed return ratio prices civilization.
WORKED: medieval wheat returned ~4 grains per grain sown — a
  quarter of every harvest was owed back to the ground, before
  anyone ate. Modern wheat: sow ~180 kg/ha (at ~45 mg a seed
  that is 4,000,000 seeds — 400 per m²), reap ~7,200 kg: 40:1,
  seed claim 2.5%. The freed 22.5% between those two worlds is
  cities.

### STORAGE — the harvest half-won

LAW: keeping is denying free water and warmth — the kitchen's
  preservation law restated at field scale. Grain below ~14%
  moisture and cool neither molds nor heats; each ~10°C colder
  roughly halves the speed of everything that eats stored grain.
RULE (the drying mass balance): water to remove = wet mass −
  (dry matter ÷ target dry fraction); evaporating it costs
  2,257 kJ per kg — the same latent price the kitchen pays to
  reduce a sauce; real dryers pay roughly double.
WORKED: 1 tonne of maize at 25% moisture = 750 kg dry + 250 kg
  water. Dried to 15%: total = 750 ÷ 0.85 = 882 kg, so remove
  118 kg of water; floor cost 118 × 2,257 ≈ 266 MJ per tonne.
WORKED (hay, the same law with the sign of sweat): 1 tonne of
  fresh-cut grass at 80% moisture is 200 kg feed + 800 kg water;
  at 15%: total 200 ÷ 0.85 = 235 kg — 765 kg of water must leave,
  ~1,700 MJ if a dryer pays it. The sun pays it free, once, in
  fair weather: "make hay while the sun shines" is a latent-heat
  invoice folded into a proverb.

LAW: pests in storage compound like the kitchen's spoilage —
  doubling on doubling in warm damp grain, near-stopped cold and
  dry. The granary lost roughly one sack in five across much of
  history;
  the difference between that and near-zero is these two numbers
  (14%, cool) held all year.

## METHODS
How things are done here — each with what governs it, when it
applies, and what breaks it. The prepositioned how-to-build.

- PLOWING AND TILLAGE (turn the soil, bury the weeds, warm the
  seedbed): governed by the erosion ledger — bare turned soil on
  slopes or dry plains is the bank left open (the mirror holds the
  Dust Bowl receipt). Condition-moved modern form: no-till and
  cover crops keep the surface armored and give up the buried-weed
  benefit for the kept-soil one.
- SOWING BY DRILL (even depth, even spacing): seed planted at
  ~2-3× its own width — deep enough to drink, shallow enough that
  the lunchbox lasts until light; the drill's even stand replaced
  broadcast's wasted third. Breaks when: sown into cold wet soil
  (the lunchbox rots before it earns).
- ROTATION (the schedule that pays the bank and starves the
  specialist): the classic four-course — wheat, roots, barley,
  clover — feeds grain crops on the clover's fixed nitrogen and
  breaks every one-host pest's year. Governed by the nitrogen
  ledger and the rotation claim. Breaks when: the paying crop is
  dropped for two cash years in a row and the books quietly open.
- MANURING AND COMPOSTING (returning the withdrawal): manure's
  numbers are in SCIENCE; compost trades mass for stability.
  Governed by the balance law. Breaks when: spread on frozen or
  saturated ground — the wage washes to the river instead.
- LEGUME LEY (a year of clover as deposit): 150-250 kg of
  nitrogen banked while the field rests from grain and the
  pasture feeds stock — three purposes, one method.
- LIMING (paying the acid): tonnes per hectare per pH step;
  governed by the logarithm — a field twice as acid needs the
  step priced, not guessed.
- IRRIGATION (rain by appointment): efficiencies and lift costs
  in SCIENCE; governed by the water arithmetic. Breaks when:
  applied without drainage in hot country — evaporation leaves
  its salts behind, and the field is slowly seasoned to death
  (the mirror holds the salted empires).
- DRAINAGE (the inverse method): roots drown in standing water;
  buried channels turned Europe's marshes into its richest fields.
  Governed by: water obeys slope, always.
- SELECTION AND SEED SAVING (the compounding method): keep the
  best for seed, every year, forever — the breeder's equation run
  by hand; the method that made every crop from every weed.
- CROSSING AND HYBRIDS (borrowed vigor): cross two inbred lines
  and the first generation outyields both parents; the vigor does
  NOT breed true — F1 seed is bought yearly, and resowing it
  scatters the gain (the law of segregation, cashed annually).
- GRAFTING (heredity by carpentry): fruit trees do not come true
  from seed; a chosen branch spliced onto a hardy root copies the
  exact tree for another lifetime. Every named apple on Earth is
  one original, extended by knife.
- FALLOW AND REST (time as an input): a year unplanted stores a
  dry country's rain in the ground and breaks pest years; what
  rest does NOT restore is in the mirror (the mineral books don't
  refill from nothing).
- INTERCROPPING — THE THREE SISTERS (maize pole, bean fixing and
  climbing, squash shading the weeds): three payrolls on one
  plot, each covering another's shortage; the oldest worked
  example of complementarity.
- TERRACING (trading labor for slope): the erosion arithmetic
  answered with stone; hillsides farmed for two thousand years
  stand on it.
- PEST SCOUTING AND THE THRESHOLD (spend only above the line):
  count the pest; treat only when expected loss exceeds the
  treatment's cost. Governed by compounding — count EARLY, because
  the doubling law never scouts late kindly. Breaks when: spraying
  by calendar instead — cost without count, and resistance bred
  free of charge (the mirror holds the DDT ledger).
- ROTATIONAL GRAZING (the haircut schedule): move stock so every
  paddock's grass regrows from the base before the next bite;
  governed by the grass claim. Breaks when: stock stay until the
  crowns are torn — then the pasture obeys the erosion law
  instead.
- ENSILING (preserving wet feed by acid, not dryness): pack green
  feed airtight; the kitchen's kraut bacteria acidify it below pH
  ~4.2 and it keeps all winter — the fermentation fence, built
  barn-sized. Breaks when: air leaks in and mold takes the wage.
- HARVEST TIMING AND DRY-DOWN (the last bet of the year): cut too
  early, pay the dryer (266 MJ/tonne, in SCIENCE); too late, the
  weather and shattering tax the standing crop. Governed by the
  storage law and the sky.
- SEASON EXTENSION (glass and plastic against the frost walls):
  trap the sun's glow, block the wind, gain weeks on both ends;
  the frost-sprinkler method in SCIENCE holds the other flank.

## MEANS
What the methods run on, with the numbers that matter.

- THE SUN — the payer; ~18 MJ/m²/day in season, no invoice. Every
  other mean exists to catch, keep, or not waste what it sends.
- THE PLOW AND ITS HEIRS — moldboard, disc, and the no-till
  drill that unlearned it where the erosion ledger demanded.
- THE SEED DRILL — even depth and spacing; the quiet machine that
  turned a scattered gamble into rows that can be hoed.
- THE HARVEST LADDER — sickle, scythe (a strong hand cuts ~0.4
  ha/day), reaper, combine (~3-5 ha/hour: one seat doing a
  village's autumn). Each rung stays: to some field and some
  purse, the surpassed rung is still the right answer.
- MUSCLE TO MOTOR — ox and horse (~750 W) to tractor (100 kW ≈
  134 horses): the wattage ladder in SCIENCE, parked in the barn.
- THE FERTILIZER BAG — three numbers, N-P-K, the percent of
  nitrogen, phosphate, and potash inside: the balance law as a
  product. Governed by the ledger in SCIENCE, not by hope — more
  than the shortest stave needs is money to the river.
- THE WELL, PUMP, AND CANAL — water moved against slope at 9.8
  joules per kg per meter; the drip line that wastes only a tenth.
- THE GRANARY AND SILO — dry, cool, sealed: the storage law as
  architecture; the silo is the ensiling method poured in
  concrete.
- THE HONEST INSTRUMENTS — the rain gauge (mm are tonnes), the
  soil test (read the bank before spending), the moisture meter
  (14% is a measurement, not an opinion), the thermometer summing
  degree days. Farming ran on proverbs until these; the proverbs
  that survive are the ones the instruments vindicated.
- THE SEED BANK — varieties kept alive in the vault against the
  uniformity claim's bad day; insurance measured in generations.
- FENCE AND HEDGE — the grazing schedule made of wire; also the
  windbreak that slows the erosion law at the field's edge.
- THE GREENHOUSE — glass that lets the sun's light in and keeps
  its warmth: bought degree days, priced per pane.

## PURPOSE
The wants this subject answers — the doors a query enters by.

- FEED THE MANY: the payroll purpose — turn sunlight, water, and
  the bank's wages into ~30 person-years per good hectare; every
  other purpose stands on this one.
- STEADY THE YEAR: the harvest comes in weeks, the eating takes
  twelve months; storage and the granary move food across time —
  the purpose the storage law serves.
- FREE THE HANDS: every surplus percent releases someone to build,
  heal, write, or forge; the seed-return arithmetic (4:1 to 40:1)
  is this purpose measured. Civilization is farming's compound
  interest.
- KEEP THE LAND FOR THE CHILDREN: balance the books — nutrients,
  soil, salt, and water table — so the bank outlives the banker;
  the purpose the erosion and balance laws serve.
- CLOTHE, WARM, AND BUILD: fiber, wool, hide, timber, and fuel —
  the payroll spent on more than dinner.
- MAKE IT GOOD: variety, flavor, and the kitchen's supply — the
  purpose that rises as the others are tamed, and the one the
  mirror shows was left off the scoresheet for a century.

## HISTORY
How the knowledge arose, in what order, at what cost — the order
carries why.

- Domestication first, partly by accident: the wild grains that
  shattered and fell resowed themselves; the mutants that held
  their seed waited for the harvester — and were harvested, and
  resown. The first selection ran before anyone knew they were
  selecting. Cost: millennia, and paid before writing existed to
  record it — writing itself then arrived as the granary's
  ledger.
- The plow and the draft animal second: muscle beyond the human
  hundred watts; fields grew to the ox's endurance, not the
  arm's.
- Rest and rotation, learned by exhaustion: fallow years, then
  the medieval three-field, then clover's four-course — each step
  found by fields that failed without it, centuries before
  nitrogen had a name. Method outran why by a thousand years,
  farming's standing proof alongside cooking's bread.
- The nutrient why arrives: mineral doctrine — plants eat salts,
  not soil's spirit — turned manure from custom into chemistry
  and made the balance law computable. The guano rush proved it
  by exhaustion (the mirror holds that receipt).
- Mendel's peas, ignored for 34 years: heredity's arithmetic
  published in 1866 to silence, rediscovered in 1900 — the
  breeder's equation had been running blind for ten thousand
  years and now had eyes. Cost of the delay: a generation of
  breeding done by lore that could have been done by count.
- Air's grip broken: the nitrogen factory (~1913) let the wage be
  manufactured; the payroll ceiling moved for the first time
  since clover. Roughly half of living humanity is downstream of
  that one reaction — with its bill still being computed (the
  river's share, the fuel's share; the mirror watches).
- Hybrid vigor cashed (1930s): maize yields left their century
  plateau and began the 2%-a-year climb the compounding law in
  SCIENCE tracks to this day.
- The dwarf wheat bet (1950s-60s): shorten the straw so fertilizer
  becomes grain instead of a stem that falls over; harvest index
  climbed from ~0.3 toward ~0.5 and yields in hungry countries
  doubled inside a decade — the largest famine-that-didn't-happen
  in the record.
- The soil's lesson, paid in dust: plowed dry plains blew away in
  a decade (the mirror holds the full portrait); no-till, cover,
  and windbreaks are the color's answer, written after the loss.

## RELATIONS

- COOKING: everything on the board grew here; the staples, the
  seasons of the pantry, and the same preservation law (deny free
  water — the granary and the jam jar are one sentence); drying's
  2,257 kJ/kg is paid in both kitchens and barns; breeds and
  varieties are flavor decisions made decades before the meal.
- WEATHER: the season's window, the frost walls, rain in tonnes,
  degree days — farming is placed bets on this phylum; forecasts
  run along this line in one direction and harvest prayers in the
  other.
- LIFE: selection and heredity (the breeder's equation is
  biology's law worn as a tool), symbiosis (rhizobia, pollinators),
  pests evolving back — farming is evolution with a steering
  wheel, and the passenger seat is occupied.
- CHEMISTRY: photosynthesis, the nitrogen triple bond and its
  factory breaking, N-P-K, pH's logarithm and lime, fermentation
  in the silo.
- PHYSICS: light as energy, latent heat in the dryer and the
  frost sprinkler, water lifted at 9.8 J/kg/m, the tilted axis
  that writes the seasons.
- MATHEMATICS: compounding (yields, pests, herds), the balance
  ledger, ratios (seed return, feed conversion), the degree-day
  sum, millimeters-to-tonnes — the farm is arithmetic with mud on
  it.
- THE BODY: the 10 MJ/day it exists to supply; protein's nitrogen;
  the trophic toll of routing food through animals.
- MEDICINE: nutrition and its deficiencies, diseases shared with
  livestock, antibiotics born in soil microbes and dulled by use
  in feed — a two-way street with traffic in both lanes.
- ECONOMY: surplus as the first wealth, grain as the first money,
  land and rent, the fertilizer and futures trades; the price of
  bread as the temperature of politics.
- CULTURE: the calendar, the harvest festival, land inheritance,
  the village's shape — farming's schedule became humanity's.
- ENGINEERING: every mean above — plow, drill, combine, pump,
  dam, drain, silo, greenhouse; terraces holding hillsides where
  the erosion law would take them.
- ASTRONOMY: the planting calendar was astronomy's first paying job.
- BELIEF: harvest festivals and rain prayers; the field taught dependence.
- BUILDING: barns, terraces, and granaries — farming builds its own landscape.
- CONFLICT: land is what wars are for; the farm feeds the army.
- CRAFT: plows, scythes, and fences — farming runs on made tools.
- EARTH: soil IS ground rock plus life; the farm mines it slowly.
- GOVERNANCE: land title, water rights, and grain taxes built early states.
- MEASUREMENT: the field's area, the seed rate, the yield count — farming was measured first.
- PLANTS: the crop is the plant domesticated — one phylum farming the other.
- TEACHING: farm knowledge passes parent to child; extension services teach at scale.
- TRANSPORT: harvest moves or rots; the granary and the road are one system.
- UPKEEP: drainage, fencing, and fallow — the farm is maintenance with seasons.
