# THE FABRIC MODEL — ratified in conversation, 2026-08-30

## THE TWO SHEETS

HIS: two fabrics, complementary, needing each other to have purpose.

THE COLOR — the possibles and the knowns. All the phylums of the
known. Split light: distinct, placed, usable.

THE WHITE — the impossibilities and the unknowns. All things at
once, undifferentiated, where discoveries emerge. HIS vision, named
in an earlier session: white because it is every color before the
split. A discovery is a color separating out of the white when new
knowns give it something to separate against.

The white is an anti-mirror of the color (HIS): same structure,
opposite sign. The knowledge of what didn't work goes there — and
it is knowledge, filed with the same care as what did.

How much white stands behind a piece of color depends on how many
tries the success took (HIS: think Dorian Grey — the color keeps
the clean working answer; its portrait in the white absorbs every
scar it cost). A success stops its own trying — and opens the next
question (HIS: flight didn't stop at balloons). So neither sheet
ever finishes growing, and growth is NOT proportional: the ratio at
any spot is the map of how hard that territory was, or still is.

THE SHIMMER (HIS): what is impossible one day is still unknown in
general. Every impossibility is certified UNDER CONDITIONS, and
outside them it is an unknown, alive. Filed with its why in
quantities and conditions, an old impossibility recomputes the
moment new knowns move a condition — wings on arms fail at Earth's
gravity and work at a third of it. The white shapes the next
discovery; that is why it is the interesting side.

## THE PHYLUM — universal structure

One subject's full standing knowledge, on both sheets at once.
THE STRUCTURE IS A MIRROR: both halves have exactly the same
parts; only the contents differ by sign.

The parts, identical on both sides:

  THINGS     color: the units the subject is about
             white: units wondered about or attempted that are not
                    yet real, or cannot be
  CLAIMS     color: what holds, written so a crossing question can
                    test it
             white: what didn't hold — with WHY in quantities and
                    conditions — and what is still unsettled
  SCIENCE    color: the applying math, physics, chemistry and other
                    associated knowledge, written IN, in this
                    subject's terms, with working quantities
             white: the science the subject still lacks, named; and
                    where applied science broke
  METHODS    color: how things are done and built here, followable
                    — the prepositioned how-to-build
             white: what was tried and didn't work, carrying its
                    conditions
  MEANS      color: the tools and materials the methods run on
             white: means that fell short, and means not yet found
  PURPOSE    color: the wants this subject answers
             white: the wants it cannot yet answer
  HISTORY    color: how each known arose, in what order, at what
                    cost in tries
             white: the record of the trying itself
  RELATIONS  color: its connections to other phylums, first-class
             white: connections that broke, or are suspected and
                    unproven

HIS rulings on the life of it: knowledge is neither right nor
wrong — worth is applicability and utility TO THE QUERY. Purpose
can change. History evolves. Relationships grow. Methods that work
become inferior — and STAY, because to some query the surpassed
method is still the best answer. New science reopens old entries on
both sheets. The query is the richer for knowing all the nuances.
Nothing surpassed is discarded; only what never held goes white.

Each phylum holds its own science AND all its relationships to
other phylums. Every phylum starts as this skeleton — a seed — and
self-evolves from its own queries, external queries, and the
programs and artifacts the fabric creates.

## RIBBONS

HIS: ribbons are queries (internal and external), programs created
by the fabric, and data flowing along the ribbon. The fabric
provides the knowledge; the ribbon queries BECOME the programs,
built by ribbons; ribbons gently interact to pass along data and/or
other programs.

The fabric is not a CPU: it never stores the flowing data. What
stands is knowledge; what is made is the program; what moves is the
data. Only the first is stored. The bridge is condensing: when a
crossing finishes, what the outcome MEANS (held / didn't hold /
under what conditions) files as knowledge; the particulars are
gone, the lesson remains. Programs worth keeping condense as METHOD
entries — re-assemblable knowledge, never stored cargo.

Ribbons work on both sheets: add to the color (new knowns, kept
methods), add to the white (tries with their why, exposed
unknowns), subtract from the white (drains when conditions move),
and — rarely — subtract from the color (a claim that never held).

## LIVING SEARCHES (HIS name, plural deliberate)

The search concern is real only if phylums are built wrong. A query
shapes its own boundaries: it picks its entry point on initial
contact, cascades through the phylum along relations, and spawns
sub-queries — each its own living search, entering at its own
point, dying where nothing grips, braked wherever the white says
closed. Many searches alive at once, passing findings gently
between them.

Cost follows the shape of the answer, not the size of the fabric.
Growth buys reach instead of costing speed. And the white is the
speed: every filed closure is search never repeated.

BUILD STANDARD for every phylum: a query must be able to ENTER it
(purpose and things are the doors), CASCADE through it (relations
are the paths), and LEAVE with what gripped. A phylum that fails
this standard is prose, not fabric.

## THE ANSWER RULE (HIS, verbatim intent)

It never says "I don't know." It says: NOTHING IS CERTAIN HERE —
BUT HERE IS A POSSIBILITY, and here is what would settle it. A
crossing with no certain path assembles the best possibility from
what does grip and offers it as such. The offered possibility is
where discoveries come from.

## THE SELF-AWARE PHYLUM (ratified by Joe)

The fabric holds a phylum OF ITSELF: its structure, its history —
its own life story — and its own white (what it does not know about
itself). It knows ALL the ribbons (HIS): who is asking, what
programs were built, what is in flight, what each crossing returned
— knowing OF them, their existence, shape, state, and record. Never
their cargo. The moment it stored flowing data it would be a CPU's
memory again.

## THE RIBBON ORDER (HIS)

FIRST RIBBON — communication, and all its means: syntax, cognition,
thinking, discernment, self-awareness, creativity. Nothing can be
told what to build until it understands the telling. Without this
the sheets are files, and the machine degrades into a turn-based
chatbot doing weighted averages on best hits like an LLM. The ten
language walls in FIRST_RIBBON.md govern its build.

SECOND RIBBON — the hands that build: takes an understood want,
crosses the building phylums, assembles the thing, and emits it OUT
— a world to walk, a program that runs, a document that stands.
What the hands make feeds back to both sheets.

The targets — worlds, any programs, self-discovery — are the same
act at different depths: an assembly that survives constraints. The
condition is HIS bet, stated verbatim in intent: if the knowledge
of how to build is prepositioned, it can build anything. The
phylums are the real work; the engine is the smaller half.

## THE ENGINE

One engine, no per-task programs (standing law from DIRECTION.md).
Its own rulebook stays small: how a crossing finds what applies,
how a claim kills what it forbids, how survivors condense, how
outcomes file, mirror addressing, no stored data, everything
traceable to something written. A fattening engine rulebook is the
failure signal — knowledge leaking into machinery.

Host language: Python while proving the design; the hot core moves
to Rust when measurement says so. The ribbons' programs are in no
human language — assembled structures the engine runs.
