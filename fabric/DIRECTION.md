# THE DIRECTION — read before building anything here

Joe, 2026-08-28: "I care about the direction."

1. The fabric MAKES things. A want goes in — make a world, make a
   recipe, work something out — and the thing comes out. It is not
   an index that answers "what do you know about X". A build that
   answers instead of makes is off the heading.
2. The knowledge IS the computation. Every entry is a constraint
   that permits or forbids. Possibilities are staged; every
   constraint that grips kills what it forbids; what survives is
   the answer. Nothing is looked up.
3. One engine. No per-task programs. World, recipe, puzzle,
   arithmetic — same machinery, different constraints. If a task
   needs its own program, that is the failure signal.
4. The unknown is what survives that nobody placed — never a walk
   aimed at a claim already held (see 90_minted_by_walking.md).
5. It runs on its own, grows the possible and the impossible, and
   proposes new areas when its records keep pointing at territory
   not yet written.

6. THE OBSERVER IS THE FOURTH PART, AND IT IS CURRENTLY A PERSON.
   The two sheets are inert; laws and possibilities only sit
   there. The ribbon — the observer — is the only thing that acts,
   and what it does is see applicability and utility. The machine
   holds laws, stages possibilities, and invents bearers. It does
   NOT judge worth, and no version of it should pretend to. Joe's
   own description of how we work is the architecture: one side
   invents without regard for sanity, the other sees what has use
   and applies it toward the outcome. Build for that pairing;
   do not try to replace it quietly.

Working proof of the principle, both in this repository's history:
the taught-families world (a landscape and city composed from a
typed name in 30-80 ms, recovered to city_world.html) and the
constraint solving of coins and seating, including one proven
impossibility.

The detour to avoid repeating: word-matching retrieval over the
entries, and per-task code. Entries are inert until they act as
constraints.
