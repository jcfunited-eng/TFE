ASKING: why does bread go stale

Answer — from my knowledge:
  (deep kitchen chemistry) bread going stale is not drying — cooked starch, jumbled soft by the oven, slowly recrystallizes back toward order and firms the crumb even inside a sealed bag; a brief reheat melts the young crystals and the loaf turns briefly fresh again.
  (deep kitchen chemistry) the room's air holds one level of dampness and every open food walks toward it from its own side — a chip is drier than the air, drinks, and goes limp; a cut loaf is wetter, exhales, and dries hard on top of its crystal-staling.
