ASKING: why does a kite need a tail

I have no certified answer — this is recorded as an open question. But a question asks what is possible, so here is me looking across everything I know and playing with the unknown:
  What I know that touches this:
    (physics) a thing keeps doing what it is doing unless pushed. Change of motion needs a push, and the push works both ways — you cannot shove without being shoved back. (F = m·a; action = reaction)
    (cooking) oil and water refuse each other; an emulsion is a forced truce needing a mediator whose molecule loves both sides (egg yolk, mustard).
    (chemistry) every reaction has a price of admission — even a downhill reaction needs a starting shove before it runs. (activation energy)
  What I know cannot be, nearby:
    (physics) no acceleration without force. No recoilless push. You cannot lift yourself by your own belt.
    (cooking) no stable mayonnaise from oil and water alone. Every unmediated mixture separates — the refusal always wins in the end.
    (chemistry) no fire without ignition. Gasoline and air can sit mixed forever — no spark, no burn. Nothing reacts merely because reacting would profit it.
  Unproven play — nothing I know forbids these combinations, and the pieces are real:
    maybe: a bubble is price detached from thing TOGETHER WITH a bubble is obvious only afterward because while it inflates the best evidence
    maybe: oil and water refuse each other; an emulsion is a forced truce needing a mediator whose molecule loves both sides (egg yolk, mustard). TOGETHER WITH grease and water refuse each other, and soap is the paid mediator
    maybe: a coin has no memory TOGETHER WITH probability is ignorance made precise
