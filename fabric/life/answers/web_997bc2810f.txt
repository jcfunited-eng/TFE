ASKING: why does a kite need a tail

I have no certified answer — this is recorded as an open question. But a question asks what is possible, so here is me looking across everything I know and playing with the unknown:
  Territory 1 your words touch:
    known: (medicine body) the body was never designed — it is renovations stacked on a fish, and the seams show: the food pipe crosses the air pipe (choking), the eye is wired backward and keeps a blind spot, the tailbone remembers a tail.
    known: (chance risk) a coin has no memory — past flips leave nothing on the next one, so "due" is a fiction; five heads running make the sixth exactly even money.
    known to fail: no fresh starts — every generation must be a working body, so no redesign may pass through a broken middle, ever. The bad wiring stays because each step away is worse before better.
    unproven play: maybe a coin has no memory TOGETHER WITH probability is ignorance made precise
  Territory 2 your words touch:
    known: (flight fluids) lift is deflection — a wing meets air at a slight tilt and bends the flow downward; the air, thrown down, throws the wing up, and the pressure difference across the wing IS that throw written in fluid. (one force, two descriptions)
    known: (flight fluids) a fluid pushes on everything it touches, from all sides at once, and harder with depth — pressure is the weight of fluid above, shared as squeeze in all directions.
    known to fail: no lift without turning air downward. No wing works in vacuum. And nothing flies free — lift is bought with drag and paid in fuel, every minute aloft.
    unproven play: maybe lift is deflection TOGETHER WITH everything that moves through fluid moves by throwing fluid the other way
