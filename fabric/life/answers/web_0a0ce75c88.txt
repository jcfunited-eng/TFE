ASKING: why does a kite need a tail

I have no certified answer — this is recorded as an open question. But a question asks what is possible, so here is me looking across everything I know and playing with the unknown:
  What I know that touches this:
    (flight fluids) lift is deflection — a wing meets air at a slight tilt and bends the flow downward; the air, thrown down, throws the wing up, and the pressure difference across the wing IS that throw written in fluid. (one force, two descriptions)
    (medicine body) the body was never designed — it is renovations stacked on a fish, and the seams show: the food pipe crosses the air pipe (choking), the eye is wired backward and keeps a blind spot, the tailbone remembers a tail.
    (chance risk) a coin has no memory — past flips leave nothing on the next one, so "due" is a fiction; five heads running make the sixth exactly even money.
  What I know cannot be, nearby:
    (flight fluids) no lift without turning air downward. No wing works in vacuum. And nothing flies free — lift is bought with drag and paid in fuel, every minute aloft.
    (medicine body) no fresh starts — every generation must be a working body, so no redesign may pass through a broken middle, ever. The bad wiring stays because each step away is worse before better.
    (chance risk) chance cannot owe you. No streak creates a debt the next trial must repay — the universe keeps no such ledger.
  Unproven play — nothing I know forbids these combinations, and the pieces are real:
    maybe: a coin has no memory TOGETHER WITH probability is ignorance made precise
    maybe: a coin has no memory TOGETHER WITH the mind is a pattern-finder tuned to over-fire
    maybe: a coin has no memory TOGETHER WITH chance has laws
